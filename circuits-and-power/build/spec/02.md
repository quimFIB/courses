## Why this unit exists, and what it must not become
Units 00 and 01 worked with fields. From unit 03 on, the course works with circuits: a finite list of voltages and currents. This unit says when that replacement is legitimate, derives Kirchhoff's two laws from Maxwell's equations under stated hypotheses, defines the three ideal elements and the two ideal sources from the fields, introduces circuit diagrams and the passive sign convention, and shows — with a famous demonstration — what goes wrong when one hypothesis fails.

The learner has never seen a circuit diagram, and has never been taught **inductance** (MR never defines it) or **capacitance** beyond a passing use in MR 24's capacitor (MR 23 explicitly left capacitance coefficients out) or **dielectrics** (MR 23 left constitutive theory out). All three are taught here, from energy, as far as the course needs and no further.

It must not become: network analysis (no incidence matrices, no solving networks — unit 03 onwards; a circuit here is solved only when it is one loop); transients or phasors (unit 05 — the element laws are stated, not yet solved); transformers (unit 07 — mutual inductance is defined and bounded, nothing more); transmission lines (unit 08 — the coaxial L′ and C′ are computed and L′C′ = μ₀ε₀ noted, and the unit stops there); a dielectrics course (one slide: what a linear dielectric does to a capacitor, cited; no polarisation theory beyond a sentence).

## Physics and mathematics, in order

**What a circuit model is.** A circuit is a set of *elements*, each with two *terminals*, joined at *nodes* by ideal wires. Each element carries one current i (through it) and one voltage v (across it). Circuit diagrams: the symbols for resistor (IEC rectangle), capacitor, inductor, ideal voltage source, ideal current source, and wire; drawn once on a symbol slide. The **passive sign convention**: the current arrow enters the + terminal, so p = vi > 0 means the element absorbs power — this is unit 01's theorem, now a convention for bookkeeping; sources therefore show p < 0 when delivering (AUTHORING.md). The kettle from unit 00 as the first diagram: an ideal 230 V source, two wire resistances (6.72 mΩ/m × 10 m each), the 23 Ω element.

**The three hypotheses, and Kirchhoff's laws derived.**
- (H1) No changing magnetic flux through any loop drawn *outside* the elements — changing flux is confined to inductors.
- (H2) No net charge accumulates on any element or on the wires and nodes — charge may separate *inside* an element (a capacitor's plates carry ±q), but each element's total stays zero.
- (H3) The circuit is small against the wavelength c/f of anything happening in it, so retardation (MR 24) can be ignored and the fields respond instantaneously.
KCL: enclose a node by a closed surface cutting only wires; continuity gives ∮**J**·d**A** = −dQ/dt, and (H2) makes Q constant, so Σ i = 0. KVL: (H1) makes ∮**E**·d**l** = −dΦ/dt = 0 round any loop outside the elements, so **E** is a gradient there, the voltage across each element is well defined (path-independent outside it), and Σ v = 0 round any loop. (H3) is what licenses (H1) and (H2) as approximations: name, with an order-of-magnitude argument, the error each neglects (radiation, retardation, stray capacitance and inductance). Then the numbers: at 50 Hz the wavelength is about 6000 km, so a building's wiring is lumped by about five orders of magnitude (six for the 10 m kettle cable); at 2 GHz it is 15 cm in vacuum and shorter in a circuit board's insulation, so a phone's board is not — and unit 08 is what replaces the lumped model there.

**The element laws, from fields.**
- *Resistor*: v = Ri (unit 00).
- *Capacitor*: two conductors with charges ±q. Linearity of Laplace's equation plus uniqueness (MR 23) makes the potential difference proportional to q: q = Cv defines C. Its energy is ½Cv² = (ε₀/2)∫E² (MR 23's field energy). With i = dq/dt: i = C dv/dt. Parallel plates, C = ε₀A/d with fringing neglected (say so and what it neglects); coaxial, C′ = 2πε₀/ln(b/a) per metre.
- *Dielectrics, one slide*: filling the space between the conductors with a linear insulator multiplies C by its relative permittivity ε_r (cited, Griffiths ch. 4); polyethylene ε_r = 2.25 (Pozar). Coax per metre with polyethylene: 90.3 pF/m against 40.1 pF/m in vacuum for b/a = 4. One sentence on why: the insulator's molecules polarise and partly cancel the field for given charge. No **D**, no bound-charge theory.
- *Inductor*: a loop carrying i; Biot–Savart is linear in i, so the flux through the loop is Λ = Li (flux linkage, with N turns counted N times). Its energy is ½Li² = ∫B²/2μ₀ (MR 24's magnetic energy). Faraday's law with the passive sign convention gives v = L di/dt — derive the sign carefully, with a figure. Coaxial cable: L′ = (μ₀/2π) ln(b/a) for the field between the conductors, plus μ₀/8π per metre inside a solid inner conductor with uniform current (derive from energy; note that at high frequency the current is not uniform — branch 10). For b/a = 4 in vacuum: 277 nH/m external, 50 nH/m internal. The honest caveat: for an infinitely thin wire the self-inductance diverges (the field energy near a line current is infinite), so a real L depends on the wire's radius.
- *Mutual inductance*: two loops; with the vector potential in Coulomb gauge (MR 23), Λ₁₂ = ∮**A**₂·d**l**₁, giving Neumann's formula M = (μ₀/4π)∮∮ d**l**₁·d**l**₂/|**r**₁ − **r**₂| — symmetric, so M₁₂ = M₂₁ (reciprocity, again in unit 03). Total energy ½L₁i₁² + Mi₁i₂ + ½L₂i₂² = ∫B²/2μ₀ ≥ 0 for all currents, so the inductance matrix is positive semi-definite and M² ≤ L₁L₂ (proof). Unit 07 calls M/√(L₁L₂) the coupling coefficient; here only the bound.
- *Ideal sources*: a voltage source fixes v whatever i (unit 01's EMF with zero internal resistance); a current source fixes i whatever v. A real battery as an ideal source plus a resistor (unit 01's internal resistance) — named, not analysed (unit 04).
- *LC′ = μ₀ε₀*: for the coax, L′C′ (external L′) = μ₀ε₀ = 1/c² exactly, whatever b/a; with a dielectric, ε_rμ₀ε₀. Noted, and left to unit 08.

**The running example: Lewin's circuit.** A ring: two resistors R₁ = 100 Ω and R₂ = 900 Ω joined in a loop that encircles a long solenoid; the solenoid's flux changes so that the EMF round the ring is 1 V. Current I = 1 mA. Two voltmeters attached to the same two junction points, one with its leads on the R₁ side, one on the R₂ side, read 0.1 V and 0.9 V — and with the same orientation, opposite signs. Both are right: a voltmeter reads ∫**E**·d**l** along the path of its own leads and meter (Romer; or derived), and with changing flux inside the ring that integral depends on which side of the solenoid the path passes. (H1) has failed: the changing flux is not inside any element. Threaded:
- overview: the demonstration as a puzzle;
- the hypotheses section: the ring breaks (H1); show which loop encloses the flux;
- the element-laws section: the right lumped model — put the changing flux *inside* an element by treating the solenoid–ring coupling as a mutual inductance, and the ring becomes a loop with an EMF M di_s/dt; then KVL holds round the ring, and each voltmeter's leads form a loop of their own that does or does not enclose the solenoid, which the model must include;
- closing: the two readings recomputed from the corrected model; the moral, that "the voltage between two points" means something only when (H1) holds, and the lumped approximation is exactly the decision to draw circuits where it does.
Before stating 100 Ω, 900 Ω and 1 V as Lewin's values, confirm them (Sources); otherwise present them as chosen here.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Ten to twelve problems; at least one proof and several numbers, all computed with python3.
- **When is it lumped?** (routine): wavelengths at 50 Hz, 100 kHz, 2.4 GHz; which of a house's wiring, a wireless charging pad (a few cm), a phone's board is lumped at each.
- **KCL with a capacitor inside the surface** (medium): a closed surface that cuts through a capacitor's gap — continuity still holds, KCL fails at that "node", and the displacement current restores the balance (MR 24); why (H2) is phrased per element.
- **Lewin both ways** (medium): the two readings, then a third voltmeter whose leads loop once round the solenoid; what it reads.
- **The voltmeter's path** (medium, proof): a voltmeter reads ∫**E**·d**l** along its leads; two readings between the same points differ by the rate of change of flux between the two lead paths.
- **Coax L′ and C′** (medium): from field energy; L′C′ = μ₀ε₀ for any b/a; with polyethylene, C′ = 90.3 pF/m for b/a = 4.
- **Internal inductance** (medium): μ₀/8π per metre for uniform current in a round wire, from ∫B²/2μ₀ inside it.
- **Parallel plates** (routine): C for 1 cm² plates 1 mm apart (0.885 pF), and the energy stored at 100 V.
- **Neumann's formula is symmetric, and M² ≤ L₁L₂** (medium, proof): from the energy's positivity.
- **A hairpin of leads** (medium, number): two parallel wires of radius 0.25 mm, 2 mm apart (centre to centre), 1 cm long, joined at one end, with the end effects neglected: L ≈ (μ₀l/π) ln((d − r)/r) ≈ 7.8 nH for the field between the wires (plus about 1 nH internal); the voltage L di/dt when 10 A switches off in 10 ns, ≈ 8 V. State the approximation (two long parallel wires, field between them only) on the page.
- **The diverging self-inductance** (medium): the field energy of a line current per unit length diverges logarithmically at the wire; the finite radius cures it.
- **The kettle, lumped** (routine): solve the one-loop circuit with the wire resistances; the current, the power lost in the wires and delivered to the element.
- **Sign convention drill** (routine): for a given diagram with marked polarities and arrows, which elements absorb and which deliver power; check that the total is zero (unit 01's theorem, summed — unit 03 will prove this sum in general as Tellegen's theorem; here it is checked, not proved).
