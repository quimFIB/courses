## Why this unit exists, and what it must not become
Unit 03 used no element law. This unit adds the simplest one, Ohm's law on every edge, and finds that a resistive network is a discrete version of MR 23's electrostatics: a Laplacian, a Dirichlet problem with a unique solution, a maximum principle, and a variational principle (Thomson's) that is MR 23's minimum-energy property in disguise. From those come the engineer's working tools — Thévenin's theorem, series and parallel, star–delta — as theorems rather than recipes, and the maximum-power theorem with the efficiency it costs. Unit 01's notes left two questions here ("what load takes the most power from such a source"; unit 02's real battery "Unit 04 analyses this"); make both land.

It must not become: AC (no impedance, no phasors — unit 05; every edge weight here is a positive real conductance, and the slide that introduces L says unit 13 will reuse it with complex weights); a graph-theory course (the matrix-tree theorem, spectral graph theory beyond the kernel and positive semi-definiteness, random walks — Doyle & Snell's random-walk view gets one sentence and the reading list, no more); motors (the starter motor is modelled as a resistance while stalled, and the slide says the back-EMF of a turning motor is unit 12's subject).

## Physics and mathematics, in order

**The Laplacian.** Ohm's law on every edge, i = G v with G = diag(G_e), G_e = 1/R_e > 0; the voltage law v = Aᵀφ; the current law with injections Ai = s. Together: Lφ = s with L = AGAᵀ, the *weighted graph Laplacian*. Entries: L_kk = sum of conductances at node k, L_kj = −G_e for an edge between k and j, row sums zero. This is the matrix of unit 03's nodal analysis: say so, and show it for unit 03's bridge.
- Symmetric; positive semi-definite, since φᵀLφ = Σ_e G_e (φ_tail − φ_head)² = the power dissipated (physics and algebra the same line); kernel exactly the vectors constant on each component (from that sum). Rank n − c, consistent with unit 03.
- **Existence and uniqueness**: Lφ = s is solvable iff s ⊥ ker L, i.e. the injected currents sum to zero on each component (charge does not accumulate — unit 02's (H2) again), because im L = (ker L)^⊥ for a symmetric matrix. The solution is unique up to adding a constant on each component; grounding one node per component makes it unique. Then the *discrete Dirichlet problem*: fix the potentials at some boundary nodes (sources), require KCL with no injection at the interior nodes; the interior block L_II is positive definite (proof: if each interior component touches the boundary, φ_Iᵀ L_II φ_I = 0 forces φ_I = 0), so the solution exists and is unique — the circuit version of MR 23's uniqueness theorem.
- **Maximum principle**: at an interior node the potential is the conductance-weighted average of its neighbours' (harmonic), so the extremes are on the boundary — MR 23's again, one slide with the proof. Used below for the metric and in problems.

**Thomson's principle.** Among all flows j with Aj = s (the same injections), the dissipated power Σ R_e j_e² is minimised by the actual currents i, and only by them. Proof from unit 03: write j = i + c with Ac = 0; the cross term Σ R_e i_e c_e = vᵀc = 0 by Tellegen; so P(j) = P(i) + Σ R_e c_e² ≥ P(i). Name the continuous analogue — the electrostatic field minimises energy among fields with the same sources — and prove it in the notes, since MR 23 does not. Its dual, *Dirichlet's principle* (the actual potentials minimise φᵀLφ given boundary values), in the notes or a problem.
- **Effective resistance**: for a unit current in at a and out at b, R_eff(a, b) = φ_a − φ_b = (e_a − e_b)ᵀL⁺(e_a − e_b) = the power dissipated by that unit flow. By Thomson, R_eff(a, b) = min over unit flows of Σ R_e j_e²: any flow gives an upper bound.
- **Rayleigh monotonicity**: raising any edge's resistance cannot lower any effective resistance (proof: the old optimal flow is admissible in the new network at no lower power). *Cutting* (R → ∞) raises, *shorting* (R → 0) lowers. Series and parallel as the two cases that are exact.

**Thévenin's theorem as an affine map.** A network of linear resistors and ideal sources, seen at two terminals: the port voltage is an affine function of the port current, v = V_th + R_th i (passive convention at the port — fix the signs with a figure). Proof: drive the port with a current source i; the solution exists and is unique (above), and is linear in (i, sources) jointly, so v is affine in i. V_th is the open-circuit voltage, R_th the effective resistance between the terminals with every source set to zero (voltage sources shorted, current sources opened — explain why: that is what "zero" means for each). Norton's form (current source in parallel) as the same line solved for i. Positivity: R_th ≥ 0 is an effective resistance. Revisit unit 03's bridge: seen by the detector, V_th = 20/21 V, R_th = 5000/21 Ω ≈ 238.1 Ω, so i_g = (20/21)/(5000/21 + 50) = 2/605 A — unit 03's answer in one line.

**Kron reduction.** Eliminate interior nodes (no injection) by block elimination: with L partitioned into boundary B and interior I, L_red = L_BB − L_BI L_II⁻¹ L_IB, the Schur complement. Theorem: L_red is again a Laplacian (symmetric, zero row sums, non-positive off-diagonal entries) of a network on the boundary nodes alone that is indistinguishable from the original at those nodes. Prove symmetry and zero row sums on the slide; the sign of the off-diagonals via the maximum principle (the interior potentials for a unit potential at one boundary node lie in [0, 1]) in the notes. It is Thévenin at many ports at once. Star–delta is the special case of one interior node with three neighbours: G_ab = G_aG_b/(G_a + G_b + G_c). Name that unit 13 reduces a grid's buses this way.

**Effective resistance is a metric.** Symmetric and positive are immediate; the triangle inequality: with x = e_a − e_b and y = e_c − e_b, R(a, c) = R(a, b) + R(c, b) − 2xᵀL⁺y, and xᵀL⁺y = ψ_a − ψ_b for the potential ψ of a unit current in at c and out at b; by the maximum principle b is ψ's minimum, so ψ_a − ψ_b ≥ 0. (Equality iff every path from a to c passes through b — a cut vertex; in the notes.)

**Foster's theorem.** For a connected network, Σ_e R_eff(e)/R_e = n − 1, where R_eff(e) is the effective resistance between edge e's endpoints. Proof: G_e R_eff(e) = G_e a_eᵀL⁺a_e with a_e the column of A, so the sum is the trace of P = G^{1/2}AᵀL⁺AG^{1/2}; P is symmetric and P² = P (one line, using LL⁺AG^{1/2} = AG^{1/2} because the columns of A lie in im L), so it is the orthogonal projection onto im(G^{1/2}Aᵀ) — the cut space in the conductance-weighted inner product — whose dimension is rank A = n − 1. Physical reading: R_eff(e)/R_e is the fraction of a unit current injected across e that goes through e itself.

**Maximum power, and its price.** A source (ℰ, R_s) — by Thévenin, any linear resistive source — feeding a load R_L: P_L = ℰ²R_L/(R_s + R_L)², maximised at R_L = R_s with P_max = ℰ²/4R_s, and the efficiency η = R_L/(R_s + R_L) is then exactly 50%. Efficiency rises monotonically to 1 as R_L/R_s → ∞, at falling power: maximum power and maximum efficiency are different design points (the curve of P_L and η against R_L/R_s, a figure). Unit 06 makes this conjugate matching; unit 15 meets the distinction again.
- **The real system**: why a power station refuses the matched point. A grid's sources have internal resistance far below their loads', so they run at high efficiency far from the matched point; at the matched point half of everything generated would heat the generator and lines — twice the fuel per delivered kilowatt-hour. Matching is right where the source's energy is free and its received power is scarce: a radio receiver taking the signal from its antenna (branch 16 returns to this). **Correct the curriculum card**, which said "an audio amplifier accepts" the matched point: an audio amplifier is designed with an output resistance far *below* its loudspeaker's — it is not matched — so do not use it as the example. ## What the problem sheet exercises
Eleven to thirteen problems; at least three proofs and several numbers, all computed with python3.
- **The cube two ways** (medium — the checkpoint's): 5R/6 by symmetry and by Kron reduction; then adjacent corners 7R/12 by symmetry and superposition.
- **The infinite square grid, adjacent nodes** (hard): the superposition argument (1 A in at a spreading as ¼ A per edge, 1 A out at b, superposed: ½ A through ab, so R/2) — and why it is not yet a proof: in two dimensions a unit current spreading to infinity has infinite dissipated power (Σ over rings of radius r of about (1/8r)² × 8r diverges), so "the potential at infinity" is undefined. Make it a proof on the N × N torus, where the symmetric argument works exactly with the extraction spread uniformly over all N² nodes: R_eff = (R/2)(1 − 1/N²); check it independently by Foster (2N² equivalent edges, n − 1 = N² − 1). Then the finite N × N squares: each embeds in a larger torus (as the torus with edges cut), so by Rayleigh R_square(N) ≥ (R/2)(1 − 1/M²) for every M > N, hence ≥ R/2; and R_square(N) decreases with N (Rayleigh again). That the decreasing limit is exactly R/2 is *cited* (Doyle & Snell, the infinite lattice) and shown numerically in the solution (python3, central adjacent pair: N = 5, 11, 21, 41 give 0.5245R, 0.5046R, 0.5012R, 0.5003R) — the solution states which parts are proved and which cited.
- **Star–delta as a Kron reduction** (routine): G_ab = G_aG_b/(G_a + G_b + G_c), and the inverse map.
- **Thomson by Lagrange multipliers** (medium): minimise Σ R_e j_e² subject to Aj = s; the multipliers are (twice) the node potentials and stationarity is Ohm's law.
- **Rayleigh bounds** (medium, number): the 4 × 4 square grid (16 nodes, 24 unit resistors), corner to opposite corner. Shorting the anti-diagonal layers x + y = k gives a lower bound Σ 1/c_k = 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/4 + 1/2 = 11/6 R ≈ 1.833R; two disjoint boundary paths of length 6 in parallel give an upper bound 3R (find a better flow for a tighter one); the exact value is 13/7 R ≈ 1.857R (computed, given in the solution for comparison).
- **Effective resistance is a metric** (medium, proof): the triangle inequality via the maximum principle; when equality holds.
- **Foster's theorem** (hard, proof — the checkpoint's): the projection argument in full, then checked on the bridge of unit 03 with its five resistors — the source is not a resistor (compute the five R_eff, sum the five ratios, get 3 = n − 1).
- **Maximum power and 50%** (routine, proof — the checkpoint's): the theorem, the efficiency, and the curve.
- **The headlights** (routine, number): the dimming from 114.2 W to 79.5 W via Thévenin; then what a thicker starter cable (R_c = 2 mΩ) does to the lamps.
- **The power station** (routine, number): a source with R_s/R_L = 0.01 against the matched point — efficiency 99.0% against 50%, and the delivered power in each case per unit of ℰ²/R_s; the fuel per delivered kWh compared.
- **Dirichlet's principle** (medium): given fixed boundary potentials, the actual potentials minimise φᵀLφ; the dual of Thomson; together they bracket R_eff from both sides.
- **The interior block is invertible** (medium, proof): L_II positive definite whenever each interior component touches the boundary; a counterexample when one does not (a floating island of resistors has no defined potential).
