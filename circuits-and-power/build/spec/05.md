## Why this unit exists, and what it must not become
Units 03 and 04 solved networks with no memory. Unit 02 gave the capacitor and the inductor their element laws and stopped ("unit 05 solves them"; "unit 05 counts these as the circuit's state"; "Unit 05's ignition coil spends this" — of the inductor's continuous current). This unit solves them: first in the time domain, as a linear ODE system whose state is the energy stores; then in the sinusoidal steady state, where d/dt becomes multiplication by iω and a network of R, L and C becomes unit 04's network with complex weights. Its central theorem — every network of positive R, L and C has a positive-real impedance — is proved from unit 03's Tellegen theorem with complex phasors, which unit 03's notes promise in so many words. Land every forward reference listed under "Forward references to land".

The learner knows MR 06's oscillator and MR 20's transforms well, including MR 20's own slide on the Laplace transform. Do not re-teach either: cite them, and prove only what this unit adds (below). The learner has never seen a phasor, an impedance or a time constant.

It must not become: AC power (unit 06 — RMS values are defined here because phasors need a normalisation, but P, Q, S, power factor and matching are 06's; the one power fact here is the capacitor-charging loss, which is a transient result); transformers (unit 07 — the ignition coil's secondary is left open and named, and its voltage is unit 07's problem); network synthesis (Foster's reactance theorem is proved and the Foster partial-fraction form written; Brune, Darlington and the Bott–Duffin construction are out, named at most in the reading); filters as a design subject; state-space theory beyond the order count and one worked state equation.

## Physics and mathematics, in order

**Memory, and the state.** A network of R, L, C and sources is unit 03's Ai = s and v = Aᵀφ plus the element laws, now with derivatives: a linear system of ODEs with constant coefficients. Its *state* is the capacitor voltages and inductor currents — the quantities unit 02 showed are continuous whenever the other variable is finite, and which carry the stored energy ½Cv², ½Li². The *order* is the number of independent energy stores: one per capacitor and inductor, less one for each loop made only of capacitors (and voltage sources) and each cut made only of inductors (and current sources). State it, prove the simple direction (each such loop or cut is a linear constraint on the state by KVL or KCL), and cite Desoer & Kuh for the general count. Examples: two capacitors in parallel are order 1; the series RLC is order 2.

**First order: the time constant.** RC and RL circuits switched at t = 0: v(t) and i(t) approach their final values as e^{−t/τ}, τ = RC or L/R, from MR 00's first-order equation (cite, solve in one line). The 63%-per-τ and the 5τ ≈ 99.3% rules computed, not asserted. Dimensions: Ω·F = s, H/Ω = s.
- **Charging a capacitor through any resistance wastes exactly half the energy drawn** (the checkpoint's first half): from a fixed voltage V through R to full charge, the source supplies CV², the capacitor stores ½CV², the resistor dissipates ½CV² — whatever R. Proof by integrating i² R with i = (V/R)e^{−t/RC}, and a second proof needing no exponential (∫v_R i dt with v_R = V − v_C and i dt = C dv_C). The limit R → 0 is singular (the loss stays ½CV² while the current becomes an impulse): one slide on what this says about lumped models (the loss goes into whatever the model left out — radiation, the wire's inductance), qualitatively.

**The running example, part 1: the ignition coil switched on and off.** The primary: 12 V, R = 3 Ω, L = 6 mH. Closed at t = 0: i = 4(1 − e^{−t/τ}) A, τ = 2 ms; final current 4 A; stored energy ½Li² = 48 mJ. Current fraction after 1, 1.5, 2, 3 τ: 0.632, 0.777, 0.865, 0.950; energy fraction (its square): 0.400, 0.604, 0.748, 0.903. **High revs**: at 6000 rpm a four-cylinder four-stroke engine fires 200 times a second, every 5 ms; with the contact closed for 60% of that (assumed), 3 ms = 1.5τ, so the current reaches 77.7% and the energy 60.4% — 29.0 mJ against 48 mJ (43.3 mJ at 3000 rpm, full 48 mJ at 1000 rpm). Opened: the current cannot stop instantly; with no capacitor, stopping 4 A in 1 μs would need L di/dt = 24 kV across the opening contact — it arcs instead (qualitative; no arc physics).

**Second order: the series RLC is MR 06's oscillator.** Lq̈ + Rq̇ + q/C = v(t): γ = R/L, ω₀ = 1/√(LC), Q = ω₀L/R = (1/R)√(L/C). Under-, critically and over-damped, with MR 06's names and results cited, not re-derived. The *characteristic resistance* √(L/C) appears as the ratio of peak voltage to peak current in the free oscillation (energy: ½LI² = ½CV²).
- **The running example, part 2: the capacitor across the contact** ("condenser" if the source confirms the name). When the contact opens, the coil's current flows into C instead: a series RLC (L, R, C, with the battery's constant voltage only shifting the equilibrium — say so). √(L/C) = 173.2 Ω, so the capacitor voltage rises to about I√(L/C) = 693 V (lossless estimate; 684 V with R = 3 Ω, at 54 μs), rings at f₀ = 4.59 kHz with Q = 57.7. A few hundred volts across a contact that has had 54 μs to open, instead of 24 kV in 1 μs: that is the snubber's job. Unit 07 puts the coil's secondary back and finds the spark voltage. Energy check: ½CV² = 48 mJ at the lossless peak.

**The Laplace transform, as used here.** Cite MR 20's slide for the definition, the region of convergence, the Bromwich inversion, and L[ḟ] = sF − f(0). Prove here only what the unit adds: L[∫₀ᵗ f] = F/s; the convolution theorem for the one-sided transform (from MR 20's Fourier convolution, or directly by Fubini); and inversion of a rational F by partial fractions and residues (MR 20's residue theorem, closing the Bromwich contour to the left — cite Jordan's lemma from MR 20). Then the *s-domain element laws*: V = RI, V = sLI − Li(0), I = sCV − Cv(0) — initial conditions as sources. The ignition coil's switch-on in one line this way, as a check.
- **Network functions**: with zero initial state, every response is H(s) × input, with H rational in s with real coefficients (Cramer's rule on unit 04's nodal system with weights G, sC, 1/(sL)). The impulse response is the inverse transform of H; the circuit is *stable* (every bounded input gives a bounded output, and the free response decays) exactly when every pole of H has Re s < 0 — the partial-fraction terms t^k e^{pt}. Connect to MR 06 / MR 20: MR 06 (drive e^{+iωt}, so s = iω) puts the poles of χ in the *upper* half ω-plane, while MR 20 (f̂ = ∫ f e^{+iωt} dt, Laplace with s = c − iω) puts them in the *lower* half-plane; both are the *left* half s-plane — state both translations explicitly with a figure of the planes; it is a sign trap (corrected 2026-10-28 after the writer checked MR 20).

**The sinusoidal steady state: phasors.** e^{st} is an eigenfunction of d/dt; on the imaginary axis, e^{iωt}. A stable linear circuit driven at ω settles to a response at ω, whose complex amplitude is H(iω) times the drive's (the transient decays because the poles are in the left half-plane). MR 06's complex-drive trick is the licence (cite it). **Phasors are RMS** (AUTHORING.md): v(t) = Re(√2 V e^{iωt}). Define the RMS value of a periodic signal as √(mean of v²) and show that a sinusoid of peak V̂ has RMS V̂/√2, so 230 V mains peaks at 325.3 V. Say once that engineers write j for i, and that this course keeps i as Mechanics to Relativity does. Say why RMS: the mean power in a resistor is V²/R with no ½ — and that unit 06 develops AC power.
- **Impedance and admittance**: Z = V/I for an element or a one-port: R, iωL, 1/(iωC); Y = 1/Z = G + iB. Unit 04's nodal analysis carries over verbatim with complex weights (Kirchhoff's laws hold for phasors because they are linear with real coefficients) — so do series and parallel, star–delta, Thévenin and Norton (land unit 04's "Thévenin's theorem holds verbatim for phasors"). What does *not* carry over: Thomson's principle and the maximum principle (complex weights have no order) — one sentence.
- **Phasor diagrams**: the series RLC drawn as arrows, at below, at and above resonance. Resonance: Z real and minimal at ω₀; the current's half-power width is Δω = R/L = ω₀/Q (MR 06's width γ, cited). A tuned-circuit number for the problems: L = 200 μH tuned to 1 MHz needs C = 126.7 pF; with R = 10 Ω, Q = 125.7 and bandwidth 7.96 kHz.
- **Unit 02's "unit 05 handles AC properly" lands here**: the kettle's 50 Hz circuit is resistive, so its phasors are real and in phase, and unit 02's treatment of mains as steady was exact for RMS values.

**Positive-real: what passivity means for an impedance.** Definition: a rational function Z(s) with real coefficients is *positive-real* if Re Z(s) ≥ 0 whenever Re s > 0. **Theorem (the checkpoint's second half)**: the driving-point impedance of any network of positive R, L and C is positive-real. Proof from unit 03's Tellegen theorem with complex quantities: drive the port with current I at complex frequency s; the branch phasors V_e obey KVL and the conjugates Ī_e obey KCL (the incidence matrix is real), so Σ_e V_e Ī_e = 0, i.e. Z|I|² = Σ_R R|I_e|² + Σ_L sL|I_e|² + Σ_C |I_e|²/(sC); for Re s > 0 every term has non-negative real part (Re(1/s) = Re s/|s|²). On the axis s = iω: Re Z(iω) = Σ R|I_e|²/|I|² ≥ 0 — the mean power absorbed is non-negative, and unit 06 will call it P. Land unit 03's and unit 04's promises ("unit 05 extends this to Re Z ≥ 0").
- Consequences, proved: a positive-real function has no poles in Re s > 0; poles on the imaginary axis are simple with positive real residues (state, prove the residue sign from the local behaviour Z ≈ k/(s − iω₀) near the pole). The converse — every positive-real rational function is the impedance of some RLC network — is *cited* (Brune 1931), not proved; say what it means (passivity is exactly positive-realness).
- Tests for the problems: (s + 2)/(s + 1) is positive-real; (s − 1)/(s + 1) is not (at s = 1/2 it is −1/3); s/(s² + 1) is (an LC tank).

**Lossless networks: Foster's reactance theorem.** For a network of L and C only, Z(iω) = iX(ω) is purely imaginary, and dX/dω > 0 wherever X is finite: reactance rises with frequency. Proof via Tellegen and the derivative (Desoer & Kuh / Guillemin): dX/dω = (Σ L|I_L|² + Σ |I_C|²/(ω²C))/|I|² > 0 — the sum of the stored energies, so it is energy that forces the slope. Consequence: poles and zeros of X alternate along the ω axis, all simple, with a pole or zero at 0 and at ∞. The Foster partial-fraction form Z(s) = k_∞s + k₀/s + Σ 2k_i s/(s² + ω_i²) with all k ≥ 0, and its reading as a series chain of parallel LC tanks — one realisation, drawn. Example for the problems: Z(s) = (s² + 1)/(s(s² + 4)) (pole 0, zero 1, pole 2 in ω) and its realisation.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Eleven to thirteen problems; at least three proofs and several numbers, all computed with python3.
- **Time constants** (routine): RC and RL drills, with the 63% and 99.3% figures; dimensions.
- **Half the energy, whatever R** (medium, proof — the checkpoint's): both proofs; then charging in N equal voltage steps loses ½CV²/N (compute and prove), the reason adiabatic charging exists.
- **The order of a network** (medium): count the state for several drawn networks, including a capacitor loop and an inductor cut; write one state equation ẋ = Ax + Bu.
- **The coil at speed** (routine, number): the rpm table, and the dwell needed to store 90% of the energy: current √0.9 = 94.9% of final, dwell 2.97τ = 5.94 ms — longer than the whole 5 ms between sparks at 6000 rpm, so it cannot be done at that speed with this coil.
- **The snubber** (medium, number): choose C so the contact sees at most 400 V (C = L(I/400 V)² = 0.6 μF, compute exactly) and what that does to the ringing frequency; add a series resistor in the capacitor's branch and find the value that makes the switch-off critically damped (total 2√(L/C) = 346.4 Ω, so 343.4 Ω added) — and why that defeats the purpose: the contact sees I·R = 1374 V the instant it opens.
- **Q and bandwidth against MR 06** (routine): the tuned circuit's numbers; check that R/L is MR 06's γ and the half-power width.
- **Laplace with initial conditions** (medium): an RLC network switched with a charged capacitor; solve by s-domain sources and check the energy balance at t → ∞.
- **Stability from poles** (medium): a network with a negative resistance (a defined element) whose pole crosses into the right half-plane; the threshold value.
- **Is it positive-real?** (medium): decide for (s + 2)/(s + 1), (s − 1)/(s + 1), s/(s² + 1), (s² + s + 1)/(s² + 1) — with a witness point when it is not.
- **Every RLC impedance is positive-real** (hard, proof — the checkpoint's): the Tellegen proof in full, including why the conjugated currents still obey KCL.
- **Foster's reactance theorem** (hard, proof): dX/dω > 0 and the alternation of poles and zeros.
- **A Foster form** (medium): realise Z(s) = (s² + 1)/(s(s² + 4)) as a chain of LC tanks (and check by recomputing Z).
- **Phasors and the kettle** (routine): unit 02's kettle circuit at 50 Hz with a 1 μH lead inductance added: the phase shift it causes (1.4 × 10⁻⁵ rad) — why unit 02 was right to ignore it.
