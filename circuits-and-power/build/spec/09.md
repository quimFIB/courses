## Why this unit exists, and what it must not become
Unit 08 solved lines in the time domain and stopped at one sinusoidal formula. This branch takes the sinusoidal steady state seriously, because that is how radio, microwave and every RF engineer works: a terminated line of length d transforms the load impedance into a different input impedance, the forward and reflected waves beat into a standing wave, and a mismatch costs delivered power. Then the mathematics that makes it all visible: the reflection coefficient Γ = (z − 1)/(z + 1) is a Möbius map, it sends unit 05's right half-plane of passive impedances onto the unit disc, moving along a line rotates the disc, and the Smith chart's whole grid is circles because Möbius maps send circles to circles. Unit 06 promised "matching over a band is a harder problem (unit 09 begins it, on a line)" and unit 05 that "unit 09's Smith chart is a map of the positive-real right half-plane"; land both.

It must not become: a microwave-engineering course (no multi-section transformers beyond naming them, no double stubs, no lumped L-networks beyond one problem, no S-parameters beyond Γ); antennas (branch 16 — the "antenna" here is a load impedance of stated value, nothing more); loss on the line (lossless throughout, said so once, with |Γ| shrinking along a lossy line as one sentence).

## Physics and mathematics, in order

**A terminated line in sinusoidal steady state.** Phasors (unit 05) on unit 08's lossless line, with z measured toward the generator as d from the load: V(d) = V₊(e^{iβd} + Γ_L e^{−iβd}), I(d) = (V₊/Z₀)(e^{iβd} − Γ_L e^{−iβd}), β = ω/u. Define Γ(d) = Γ_L e^{−2iβd} and the input impedance
Z_in(d) = Z₀ (Z_L + iZ₀ tan βd)/(Z₀ + iZ_L tan βd)
(derive; check the limits: d = 0, a matched load, a short giving iZ₀ tan βd — a lossless reactance rising with frequency, as unit 05's Foster theorem demands; say so). Units and energy: the power delivered is |V₊|²(1 − |Γ_L|²)/Z₀, the same at every d — forward minus reflected, constant along a lossless line (prove from the conjugate-Tellegen idea of unit 06, or directly).
- **Standing waves**: |V(d)| oscillates between |V₊|(1 + |Γ|) and |V₊|(1 − |Γ|) with period λ/2; the *standing-wave ratio* VSWR = (1 + |Γ|)/(1 − |Γ|). A figure: |V| and |I| against d for the running example. MR 18's standing waves on a string (cite) are the |Γ| = 1 case.
- **The quarter-wave line**: at d = λ/4, Z_in = Z₀²/Z_L — it inverts impedances about Z₀; so a section of impedance Z_q = √(Z₀Z_L) matches a resistive load to a line. The half-wave line repeats the load.

**The running example: matching 12.5 Ω to 50 Ω.** z_L = 0.25, Γ_L = −0.6, VSWR = 4 — the checkpoint's number; with a matched source, 1 − 0.36 = 64% of the available power reaches the load and 36% returns. Two matches:
- *Quarter-wave transformer*: Z_q = √(12.5 × 50) = 25 Ω, a quarter-wavelength long — at 100 MHz in the polyethylene cable, λ = 1.999 m and λ/4 = 0.500 m (a 25 Ω polyethylene coax needs b/a = e^{25·2π√2.25/376.7} — compute: about 1.87).
- *Single shunt stub*: move along the line until the normalised admittance has real part 1, then cancel the imaginary part with a short-circuited stub in parallel. y_L = 4. Two solutions: d = 0.0738λ, where y = 1 − 1.5i, cancelled by a stub of length 0.4064λ (input susceptance +1.5); or d = 0.4262λ, y = 1 + 1.5i, stub 0.0936λ. At 100 MHz: d = 0.147 m and 0.852 m. Found on the chart (the circle r = 1 in the admittance chart) and checked by formula.
- *Bandwidth* (unit 06's promise): the match is exact at one frequency only. VSWR ≤ 1.5 (|Γ| ≤ 0.2) holds for the quarter-wave transformer from 0.825 to 1.175 of the design frequency (35% bandwidth), for the first stub solution from 0.948 to 1.037 (8.9%), for the second from 0.965 to 1.037 (7.3%) — computed numerically (the solution does not depend on the tool; the curves are drawn).

**Möbius maps (the mathematics).** A Möbius transformation is z ↦ (az + b)/(cz + d), ad − bc ≠ 0, on the extended plane.
- Every Möbius map is a composition of translations, dilation-rotations and the inversion z ↦ 1/z (prove).
- **Theorem (the checkpoint's proof)**: a Möbius map sends generalised circles (circles and lines) to generalised circles. Proof: true for the first two kinds trivially; for 1/z, write a generalised circle as A|z|² + Bz̄ + B̄z + C = 0 with A, C real, and substitute — the form is preserved with A and C swapped.
- Γ = (z − 1)/(z + 1) maps Re z > 0 onto |Γ| < 1, the imaginary axis onto |Γ| = 1, z = 1 to the centre (prove: |z − 1| < |z + 1| iff Re z > 0). So every passive impedance (unit 05: Re Z ≥ 0) lands in the closed disc — land unit 05's sentence.
- **The Smith chart**: the images of the lines Re z = r (constant resistance) and Im z = x (constant reactance) — circles by the theorem; compute their centres and radii: r-circles centred at (r/(1 + r), 0) with radius 1/(1 + r), x-circles centred at (1, 1/x) with radius 1/|x|. Draw the chart (computed, lean), with the running example's z = 0.25 at Γ = −0.6.
- **Moving along the line is a rotation**: Γ(d) = Γ_L e^{−2iβd}, clockwise ("toward the generator") by 4π/λ per unit length, one full turn per half wavelength. The quarter-wave line is a half-turn, and a half-turn about the centre is z ↦ 1/z in normalised impedance: the quarter-wave inversion seen on the chart.
- **The admittance chart** is the same chart rotated by 180° (y = 1/z ↔ Γ ↦ −Γ, prove), which is why the stub is located on it.

**The real system: the antenna's matching network.** At the end of a radio's feed line, an antenna whose impedance is not Z₀ is matched by a network (a stub, a quarter-wave section, or lumped L and C — the last as one problem with unit 05's reactances). Why a mismatched transmitter heats up — say only what follows from the physics: the reflected power returns to the transmitter; a source matched to the line (Γ_s = 0) absorbs it as heat, and a source that is not re-reflects part of it, so the transmitter's output stage sees a load other than the one it was designed for. No figures about real transmitters, and nothing about protection circuits unless a source is confirmed.
