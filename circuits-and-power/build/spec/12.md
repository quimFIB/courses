## Why this unit exists, and what it must not become
Unit 11 made a rotating magnetic field out of three stationary coils and named this unit as where the field becomes torque. Unit 04 left a stalled starter motor modelled as a resistance and promised: "turning, it also develops a back-EMF (unit 12)". Unit 06 said motors are inductive because they draw magnetising current, "unit 12 builds the machines". Unit 04 also said power stations turn fuel into electrical energy "in a generator (unit 12)". This unit keeps those promises from one principle. For a lossless electromechanical coupling, the force or torque is the derivative of the *co-energy* with respect to position at constant current. That is proved by an energy balance, and recovered from a Lagrangian with charge and angle as generalised coordinates (MR 12). Everything else follows: reluctance torque, the back-EMF and its constant (the same constant as the torque's, by energy conservation), the synchronous machine locked to unit 11's field, and the induction motor slipping behind it.

It must not become a machine-design course. Leave out:
- winding factors, slot harmonics, d–q (Park) transforms and transient reactances;
- saturation, beyond one sentence;
- permanent-magnet materials;
- starting methods and speed control, beyond one sentence each.

The synchronous machine is the round-rotor model at steady state, an EMF behind a synchronous reactance. Its dynamics (the swing equation) are unit 13's, named in the closing. The DC machine appears only as the simplest case of back-EMF, to land unit 04's starter, on one slide and in one problem.

## Physics and mathematics, in order

**Energy, co-energy and force.** A lossless coupling with one electrical port (flux linkage λ, current i) and one mechanical coordinate (angle θ, or x for a plunger). Flux linkage is written Λ, as unit 02 established; the λ below is that Λ.
- Energy balance: the electrical energy in, i dλ, equals the stored-energy change, dW, plus the mechanical work out, T dθ. So dW(λ, θ) = i dλ − T dθ, and T = −∂W/∂θ at constant λ.
- Co-energy W′(i, θ) = iλ − W, the Legendre transform (MR 14 has it properly; here one line). Then dW′ = λ di + T dθ, so **T = +∂W′/∂θ at constant i**. Prove that the two expressions give the same torque at the same state.
- For a linear system, W′ = ½L(θ)i², so T = ½i² dL/dθ. Several coils: W′ = ½ iᵀL(θ)i, and T = ½ iᵀ (dL/dθ) i.
- Units check: H·A²/rad = J/rad = N·m.
- Energy check on a slide: at constant current, half the electrical input goes into stored energy and half into mechanical work, for a small displacement. Derive this; it is the linear case's signature.

**The Lagrangian route (MR 12).** Take charge q (with q̇ = i) and θ as generalised coordinates. L = ½L(θ)q̇² + ½Jθ̇², with the source as a generalised force on q. The Euler–Lagrange equations give v = d(L(θ)i)/dt for the circuit and Jθ̈ = ½i² L′(θ) for the rotor. The torque term is the co-energy's derivative. The magnetic energy of a current plays the role of *kinetic* energy, which is why its θ-derivative enters with a plus sign. Prove this, and note it is the same sign as the co-energy result.

**Reluctance torque.** An iron rotor in a stator coil: the inductance depends on angle as L(θ) = L₀ + L₂ cos 2θ (an assumed form; derive the period π from the rotor's symmetry). Then T = −i² L₂ sin 2θ. At constant current the rotor aligns with the coil's axis, where inductance is greatest.
- With sinusoidal current i = Î cos ωt and the rotor turning synchronously, θ = ωt − δ, the mean torque is (Î²L₂/4) sin 2δ (derive by averaging).
- So a reluctance motor runs only at synchronous speed, with a maximum mean torque at δ = 45°.
- Example: L = 0.8 + 0.4 cos 2θ H and Î = 2 A give a mean torque of at most 0.40 N·m, against 1.6 N·m peak static torque at 2 A DC.
- This example is in the checkpoint.

**Back-EMF, and the one constant (land unit 04).** The simplest case is a DC machine with a commutator, whose armature flux linkage changes with angle at the constant rate ∂λ/∂θ = k. Turning at ω, it generates an EMF e = kω (Faraday). By the energy balance, the torque is T = ki with *the same k* (prove from co-energy: W′ = ki·θ + ½Li², so ∂W′/∂θ = ki). Electrical power ei equals mechanical power Tω: the conversion is exact for the lossless coupling.
- Unit 04's starter: stalled, the current is 210 A (12.6 V over 60 mΩ in total).
- Turning, with back-EMF e, the current is (12.6 − e)/0.060: 110 A at e = 6 V and 60 A at e = 9 V.
- This is why a motor's current falls as it speeds up, and why the stalled starter of unit 04 was a resistance "only at the instant of engagement".
- The commutator itself is named, with a figure: a mechanical switch that keeps the torque one-signed. No design detail.

**The synchronous machine.** A rotor carrying a DC field winding (or magnets), turning in unit 11's rotating field.
- *Synchronous speed*: a machine with p poles locks to a field rotating at ω/(p/2) mechanical radians per second, so n = 120f/p rpm. Derive this from unit 11's field with p/2 pole pairs: the field pattern repeats p/2 times around the bore, so it advances 2/p of a turn per electrical cycle.
  - 2 poles: 3000 rpm. 4 poles: 1500 rpm. 40 poles: 150 rpm (the checkpoint's).
  - A steam turbine turns fast and a water turbine slowly, so their generators differ in pole count. That is the real system; keep it qualitative beyond the formula.
- *The model*: the rotor's field induces an EMF E in each stator phase (Faraday, unit 02). The stator's own rotating field adds a voltage proportional to the stator current, which is a reactance X_s, the synchronous reactance. Per phase: V = E − iX_s I (generator convention, said so, and related once to the passive convention of AUTHORING.md). State that this round-rotor model neglects resistance and saturation, cited to Fitzgerald ch. 5. Derive what can be derived: the phasor diagram, and the EMF from the rotating flux.
- *Power–angle*: P = 3EV sin δ/X_s (per phase times three, with E and V as phase values), where δ is the angle of E ahead of V.
  - Example per unit: E = 1.2, V = 1.0, X_s = 1.0 gives P_max = 1.2. P = 0.8 needs δ = 41.8°.
  - Reactive power Q = 3(EV cos δ − V²)/X_s. Raising the field current raises E and makes the machine supply Q: the generator as unit 06's power-factor corrector, in one slide.
  - Unit 13 uses exactly this P(δ), so name it.
- Energy: mechanical power in equals P out (lossless), and the torque is P/ω_mech.

**The induction motor.** A rotor of shorted conductors (a cage, shown in a figure) in the rotating field.
- If the rotor turns at the field's speed, no flux changes through its loops, no current flows and there is no torque. So it must *slip*: s = (n_s − n)/n_s.
- The rotor currents are induced at frequency sf.
- *Equivalent circuit (derive the key step)*: refer the rotor to the stator as a transformer (unit 07) whose secondary frequency is sf. Dividing the rotor's voltage equation by s turns it into a circuit at the supply frequency with rotor resistance R₂′/s. Split R₂′/s = R₂′ + R₂′(1 − s)/s: the first part is rotor copper loss, and the second is mechanical power (prove the split from the air-gap power P_ag: rotor loss is sP_ag, mechanical power (1 − s)P_ag).
- *Torque–slip*: T = 3V_th²(R₂′/s)/(ω_s[(R_th + R₂′/s)² + (X_th + X₂′)²]) with the stator side replaced by its Thévenin equivalent (unit 04, verbatim with phasors). The maximum is at s_maxT = R₂′/√(R_th² + (X_th + X₂′)²), and is T_max = 3V_th²/(2ω_s[R_th + √(R_th² + (X_th + X₂′)²)]). Prove this by maximising over R₂′/s, which is unit 04's maximum-power theorem on the magnitude of an impedance (land that).
- **Running example, the pump motor** (computed, assumed values above):
  - V_th = 222.0 V and Z_th = 0.462 + 1.161i Ω.
  - Maximum torque 164.1 N·m at s = 0.187 (1219 rpm).
  - Starting torque at s = 1 is 66.1 N·m, with a starting current of 91.2 A.
  - At s = 0.03 (1455 rpm): 57.7 N·m, 8.80 kW mechanical, 16.4 A, power factor 0.834, efficiency 92.9% counting only copper losses (say so). Rotor copper loss there is 272 W = sP_ag.
  - Stable operation lies on the low-slip side of the maximum (prove from dT/ds against a load torque that rises with speed). Above that, the motor stalls.
- The magnetising branch is why the motor draws lagging current: land unit 06's "motor loads are inductive" with this circuit's power factor (0.834 at the operating point, assumed values).

**The real system.** A two-pole steam turbo-generator at 3000 rpm beside a many-pole hydro generator turning slowly, both locked to the same 50 Hz: the grid's clock is the speed of every synchronous machine on it, which is unit 13's starting point. And the induction motor in every pump: robust because its rotor has no connections, and slipping a few per cent at full load. No real machine's ratings. A figure: the machines' cross-sections, drawn schematically (2-pole and 40-pole rotors).

## What the problem sheet exercises
Ten to twelve problems. At least two proofs and several numbers, all computed with python3.
- **Energy and co-energy** (medium, proof): T = −∂W/∂θ|_λ = +∂W′/∂θ|_i. The half-and-half split at constant current.
- **A plunger** (routine): a solenoid with L(x) = L₀/(1 + x/g), assumed; the force at given current and gap; which way it pulls.
- **The Lagrangian** (medium, proof; MR 12): Euler–Lagrange with q and θ; recover the circuit equation and the torque.
- **Reluctance motor** (medium; the checkpoint's): mean torque at synchronous speed; why it cannot start on its own (with one coil the field pulsates: the mean torque is zero unless the rotor turns at ±ω, and at standstill there is only a static aligning torque; prove).
- **Back-EMF** (routine): k from a measured no-load speed; unit 04's starter at speed.
- **Synchronous speed** (routine; the checkpoint's): 120f/p for 2, 4 and 40 poles; a 60 Hz machine's speeds for comparison.
- **The synchronous generator's P and Q** (medium): the power–angle curve; the field current that makes Q zero at P = 0.8 pu.
- **Rotating field to torque** (medium): unit 11's (3/2)B̂e^{iωt} acting on a rotor's field; constant torque at synchronous speed, zero mean torque otherwise.
- **The induction equivalent circuit** (medium, proof): the R₂′/s step; the split into rotor loss and mechanical power.
- **Torque–slip maximum** (medium; the checkpoint's): s_maxT and T_max by the maximum-power theorem; the pump motor's numbers.
- **Stability of the operating point** (medium): against a load torque ∝ n²; which intersection is stable (MR 02's idea, one dimension).
- **Doubling R₂′** (routine): the maximum torque is unchanged and moves to twice the slip (prove from the formula); why wound-rotor machines once used that for starting (qualitative, no history claim).
