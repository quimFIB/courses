## Why this unit exists, and what it must not become
Unit 01 promised this unit as the place where the conveyor-belt picture has nothing to run on: "Unit 15 — no belt at all. A phone on a charging pad: energy crosses an air gap with no conductor and no electrons crossing." Units 04 and 06 each promised that maximum efficiency and maximum power would part company again, "with numbers". Unit 07 handed over its coupled pair "at k far below 1, where the ideal limit is useless and resonance does the work instead", and promised that k would be computed from Neumann's formula and found to fall as the cube of the distance. Unit 02 said M "falls faster than 1/D — unit 15 finds the rate". This unit keeps all of them. Four ideas carry it:
- a loosely coupled pair, untuned, mostly sloshes reactive power; tuned, it is MR 07's two coupled oscillators, and power crosses at ωM|I₁||I₂| sin θ, largest when the currents are in quadrature, which resonance arranges;
- the efficiency depends on one number, U = k√(Q₁Q₂), through η_max = U²/(1 + √(1 + U²))², reached at R_L = R₂√(1 + U²), which is not the conjugate match R₂(1 + U²);
- k falls as 1/d³ once d ≫ a, so every doubling of the distance costs a factor 8 in U, which only Q can buy back;
- the energy crosses the gap as a Poynting flux, entirely in the cross term between the two coils' fields, through an annulus over the windings.

It must not become any of the following:
- **Coupled-mode theory.** Kurs et al.'s κ and Γ appear once, translated into U (derived), and nothing more. The circuit equations carry the unit.
- **A Qi design guide.** No communication protocol, no power-control loop, no rectifier, no ferrite design. The WPC's statements are quoted where cited (Sources) and nowhere extended.
- **Antennas or radiation.** Radiation is neglected throughout and said so once; λ/2π is used only to say the pad, Kurs's coils and an electric-car pad are all in the near field. Branch 16 owns radiation.
- **Coil design.** The coils are a stated model (two identical concentrated loops), chosen so that M can be computed exactly. Flat spirals, ferrite shields and misalignment are named, not modelled.
- **An eddy-current solver.** The foreign object gets one derived formula, its validity condition (derived), and one computed figure (labelled computed).

## Physics and mathematics, in order

**1. The pad is a lumped two-port at k well below 1.**
- **Lumped.** The coils are 40 mm across at 140 kHz, where λ = 2141 m: ℓ/λ = 1.9 × 10⁻⁵ (unit 02's (H3); its slide put "the pad at 100 kHz" five decades below its wavelength). The gap is threaded by changing flux, so unit 02's (H1) puts both coils inside **one boundary**: the pad and the phone together are one two-port element, unit 07's coupled pair. Land unit 02's "a pair of coils that must be drawn inside one boundary".
- **The equations** are unit 07's, quoted: V₁ = iωL₁I₁ + iωMI₂, V₂ = iωMI₁ + iωL₂I₂, passive marking at both ports, so a load gives V₂ = −Z_LI₂ and Z_in = iωL₁ + ω²M²/(Z_L + iωL₂).
- **The model's numbers.** L = μ₀aN²(ln(8a/b) − 2) = **9.47 μH** (approximate formula, assumed); ωL = 8.331 Ω; R = ωL/Q = **0.0833 Ω**; the resonating capacitor C = 1/(ω²L) = **136.5 nF**. At d = 5 mm, M = 3.776 μH and k = M/L = **0.399** (section 6 computes it).
- **Against unit 07.** The street transformer had k ≈ 0.9999 (*Worked: how ideal is the street transformer?*). The WPC says a Qi system typically has k of 0.5 or below (cited). Unit 07's ideal limit needed k → 1; here 1 − k² = 0.84, and the limit is useless — land unit 07's sentence.
- **Honesty about "far below 1".** At 5 mm the model's k is 0.40, which is well below 1, not far below; at 20 mm it is 0.10, at 40 mm 0.030. The slide should say exactly this.

**2. Untuned: the secondary hardly changes the primary, and the drive is mostly reactive.**
- With no capacitors and a resistive load R_L, the secondary loop is Z₂ = R₂ + R_L + iωL₂, and the primary sees Z_in = R₁ + iωL₁ + ω²M²/Z₂. Compute at 20 mm (k = 0.104) with the best R_L: Z_in = 0.129 + 8.286i Ω, against the bare coil's 0.083 + 8.331i Ω. The reactive power the driver must carry is **64 var per watt** (Q/P = 64.4). At 5 mm: 10.4; at 40 mm: 95.7. That is the card's "most of the drive circulates as reactive power" (unit 06's Q = 2ω(⟨W_m⟩ − ⟨W_e⟩): the coil's field energy sloshing).
- **The best untuned load** (derive; problem). η = ω²M²R_L / [R₁|Z₂|² + ω²M²(R₂ + R_L)], minimised in 1/η over R_L at
  R_L* = √(R₂² + (ωL₂)² + ω²M²R₂/R₁) ≈ ωL₂,
  which is unit 06's *When only the resistance can change* (R_L = |Z_s|) applied to the secondary's own reactance. Numbers: 8.97 Ω at 5 mm, 8.38 Ω at 20 mm, 8.34 Ω at 40 mm.
- **The closed form** (derive; it is the clean way to compare with section 5): with U = ωM/√(R₁R₂) (defined in section 3),
  η_untuned,max = U² / (U² + 2 + 2√(1 + U² + Q₂²)).
  Section 5's tuned result is U²/(U² + 2 + 2√(1 + U²)): **tuning the secondary removes Q₂² from the square root**, the reactance that a resistive load cannot cancel. For Q₂ ≫ U it is ≈ k²Q₁/(k²Q₁ + 2): roughly one Q instead of two.
- **Tuning the primary does not change η at all** (prove in one line: η depends only on I₂/I₁, which the secondary fixes). It removes the reactive power from the drive. So: tune the secondary for efficiency, the primary for the driver.

**3. Tuned: MR 07's two coupled oscillators.**
- Put C₁, C₂ in series with the coils, each resonating its own coil at ω₀ = 1/√(LC). Charges q₁, q₂ as coordinates: the energy is ½q̇ᵀ𝓛q̇ + ½qᵀKq with 𝓛 the inductance matrix [[L₁, M], [M, L₂]] and K = diag(1/C₁, 1/C₂). **The inductance matrix is the mass matrix, and it is not diagonal**: MR 07's *A mass matrix that is not diagonal*, with unit 02's M² ≤ L₁L₂ as its positive-definiteness. The generalised eigenvalue problem Kv = λ𝓛v (MR 07) for identical coils gives modes (1, 1) and (1, −1) (MR 07's P4 by symmetry) at
  ω∓ = ω₀/√(1 ± k)
  (derive). At 5 mm: **118.4 kHz and 180.5 kHz**; at 20 mm: 133.2 and 147.9 kHz; at 40 mm: 137.95 and 142.15 kHz. Draw the two modes as two loops with current arrows: together (lower), opposed (upper).
- **Beats** (MR 07's P5): energy put into coil 1 alone passes to coil 2 in t* = π/Δω, Δω = ω₊ − ω₋ ≈ kω₀, so in about 1/(2k) cycles: **8.0 μs (1.1 cycles) at 5 mm, 34 μs (4.8) at 20 mm, 119 μs (16.7) at 40 mm**. The coils' energy e-folds in Q/ω₀ = 114 μs (Q/2π = 15.9 cycles; MR 06's "radians per e-fold"). Transfer time over loss time ≈ π/(kQ). MR 07's P5 found "complete transfer is a tuning condition": two oscillators at different natural frequencies never exchange all their energy. That is the time-domain reason to tune both coils to the same frequency.
- **The figure of merit.** Define U = k√(Q₁Q₂) = ωM/√(R₁R₂) (prove the equality: Q = ωL/R). Physically, U is the rate at which energy crosses against the rate at which it is lost. The model: **U = 39.9 at 5 mm, 10.4 at 20 mm, 3.0 at 40 mm.** Dimensionless ✓.
- **The steady state at resonance.** Driven at ω₀ with load R_L in series with coil 2, the secondary loop impedance is real, Z₂ = R₂ + R_L, so
  I₂ = −iωMI₁/(R₂ + R_L) and Z_in = R₁ + ω²M²/(R₂ + R_L),
  **real**: the tuned pair draws no reactive power from the driver (unit 06's tuned circuit across a port). The reflected resistance ω²M²/(R₂ + R_L) = U²R₁R₂/(R₂ + R_L).
- **The currents are in quadrature**: I₂ lags I₁ by exactly 90° (the factor −i). Section 4 shows why that is the best phase.

**4. Where the energy crosses: ωM|I₁||I₂| sin θ, and the Poynting flux across the gap.** (The energy-flow thread's station; give it at least three slides and two figures.)
- **From the circuit equations.** Circuit 2's voltage law, 0 = iωMI₁ + Z₂I₂, times I₂*, real part: the power absorbed by everything in circuit 2 is Re(Z₂)|I₂|² = Re(−iωMI₁I₂*) = ωM Im(I₁I₂*), i.e.
  **P₁→₂ = ωM|I₁||I₂| sin θ, θ = arg I₁ − arg I₂.**
  In the time domain (a second derivation, for the notes): circuit 2 receives −M i₂ di₁/dt; with i₁ = √2|I₁|cos ωt, i₂ = √2|I₂|cos(ωt − θ), the mean is ωM|I₁||I₂| sin θ. At θ = 90° the instantaneous power into circuit 2 is 2ωM|I₁||I₂|sin²ωt ≥ 0: it never reverses — contrast unit 06's *The flux runs backwards*. Units: s⁻¹·H·A² = W ✓.
- **Quadrature is optimal.** For given |I₁|, |I₂| (given copper loss in each coil) the transfer is largest at θ = 90°, and resonance puts the currents exactly there. At θ = 0 or 180° no mean power crosses at all: only reactive power sloshes. The untuned pair at its best load is not far off in phase — θ = 132.6° at 5 mm, 134.6° at 20 mm (sin θ ≈ 0.71–0.74, computed) — its trouble is magnitude: the secondary's own reactance throttles its current to |I₂|/|I₁| = 0.270 at 5 mm and 0.073 at 20 mm, against 0.975 tuned at 5 mm.
- **As a Poynting flux (derive; the central result).** Split the fields by their sources: E = E₁ + E₂, H = H₁ + H₂, coil 1's charges and currents making the first, coil 2's the second (superposition, MR 24). Take a closed surface Σ: the plane midway between the coils, closed by a hemisphere at infinity round coil 2. Assumptions, said: quasi-static (∇ × H = J, radiation neglected — branch 16), and magnetic coupling only (the capacitors' electric fields stay inside the capacitors, so outside them E = −iωA). Then, with RMS phasors, the mean power into Σ is −Re∮(E × H*)·dA, and:
  - the hemisphere contributes nothing (each field falls as 1/r³, so |S|·area ~ r⁻⁴);
  - **neither self term carries mean power**: for coil 1's fields alone, the divergence theorem with ∇ × E₁ = −iωμ₀H₁ and J₁ = 0 inside Σ gives −iωμ₀∫|H₁|², purely imaginary; for coil 2's alone the mean flux out through Σ is its radiated power, zero here. On the mid-plane the self terms vanish **point by point**: E_φ = −iωA_φ and H_ρ are in phase quadrature for one current;
  - **the cross term carries it all**: ∇·(E₁ × H₂* + E₂ × H₁*) integrated over the inside gives −2iωμ₀Re∫H₁·H₂* (imaginary) − ∫E₁·J₂*, so the mean power in is Re∫E₁·J₂* dV = Re(−iω I₂*∮A₁·dl₂) = Re(−iωMI₁I₂*) = ωM Im(I₁I₂*), with ∮A₁·dl₂ = MI₁ by Stokes and Neumann (unit 02's proof). The same number as the circuit.
  
  So: **the energy that reaches the phone crosses the gap in the product of the pad's field with the phone's, and in nothing else.** On the mid-plane, S̄_z = −Re(E_φH_ρ*), and integrating its cross term over the plane returns M exactly (checked numerically to 10⁻⁹: an identity worth a line in the notes).
- **The figure (computed).** S̄_z against ρ on the mid-plane, at the 5 W maximum-efficiency point of section 5 (I₁ = 1.258 A, I₂ = 1.227 A, in quadrature), d = 5 mm. Draw it as a radial profile and as arrows across a side view of the two coils (the deck's main energy-flow figure; unit 01's third sketch, now computed). Numbers:
  - the integral is **5.125 W** = 5 W to the load + 0.125 W lost in coil 2 = ωM|I₁||I₂| ✓;
  - S̄_z peaks at **0.74 W/cm²** at ρ = 19.9 mm, over the windings; **88%** of the power crosses in the annulus 15 mm < ρ < 25 mm; 0.4% within 10 mm of the axis;
  - S̄_z > 0 everywhere on the plane (checked at 5, 20, 40 mm): no backflow;
  - normalised profiles at 5, 20 and 40 mm for a second panel (the shape is independent of the currents): the share in 15–25 mm falls from 88% to 52% to 30%, and the share beyond ρ = 40 mm rises from 0.5% to 5.6% to 21%. The energy spreads out as the gap opens.

**5. The efficiency, and the one number.** (The checkpoint.)
- Write a = R_L/R₂. At resonance the load takes R_L/(R₂ + R_L) = a/(1 + a) of circuit 2's power, and circuit 2 takes R_refl/(R₁ + R_refl) = U²/(1 + a + U²) of the input. So
  **η(a) = aU² / [(1 + a)(1 + a + U²)].**
- **Theorem.** η is largest at a* = √(1 + U²), i.e. **R_L = R₂√(1 + U²)**, where **η_max = U²/(1 + √(1 + U²))²**. Proof: 1/η = (1 + a)(1 + a + U²)/(aU²); with s = √(1 + U²) the numerator is a² + 2a + s², so 1/η = (a + s²/a + 2)/U² ≥ (2s + 2)/U² by AM–GM (unit 04's and unit 06's tool), equality iff a = s; and U² = (s − 1)(s + 1) gives η_max = (s − 1)/(s + 1) = U²/(1 + s)². Both forms on the slide.
- **The checkpoint numbers:** η_max(1) = 3 − 2√2 = **0.1716 (17%)**; η_max(10) = **0.8190 (82%)**. The U each efficiency needs: **2.108 for 40%, 2√2 = 2.828 for 50%, 8.944 for 80%, 18.97 for 90%** (invert: U = 2√η/(1 − η), derive).
- **Limits.** U → 0: η → U²/4. U → ∞: η → 1 − 2/U. Check at U = 39.87: 1 − 0.050 = 0.950 against 0.951 ✓.
- **The input is matched too** (a proof problem): at the optimum, R_in = R₁ + U²R₁/(1 + s) = R₁s, so R_in/R₁ = R_L/R₂ = √(1 + U²): seen from either end, the link looks like √(1 + U²) times the coil's own resistance. At 5 mm: R_L = R_in = **3.322 Ω**.
- **Kurs's optimum is the same statement** (derive the dictionary): coupled-mode κ = ωk/2 (half the mode splitting of section 3) and Γ = ω/(2Q) (amplitude decay rate, half MR 06's γ), so κ/√(Γ_SΓ_D) = k√(Q₁Q₂) = U, and Γ_W/Γ_D = R_L/R₂ (each Γ is R/2L). Their Γ_W/Γ_D = [1 + κ²/(Γ_SΓ_D)]^{1/2} is R_L/R₂ = √(1 + U²). Their "strong coupling", κ²/(Γ_SΓ_D) > 1, is U > 1.

**6. Maximum efficiency is not maximum power: different design points, again.** (Lands units 04 and 06.)
- **The conjugate match at the secondary port.** Seen from the load, the tuned link is a Thévenin source whose internal impedance is R₂ + ω²M²/R₁ = R₂(1 + U²) (reactances cancelled by tuning; derive by unit 07's reflected impedance turned round). Unit 04's and unit 06's caveat, now with numbers: the equivalent's ½ is not the real network's efficiency, because the equivalent's internal resistance stands for losses in two coils.

**7. How k falls with distance.** (Lands units 02 and 07.)
- **Exact, from Neumann.** Reduce unit 02's double integral by symmetry to the one-dimensional integral above (derive); the table of section 5 is its value (computed). Units: H/m × m²/m = H ✓.
- **Far field, derived from Neumann** (unit 02's P11 promised the rate). Expand 1/√(d² + 2a²(1 − cos u)) in a²/d²: the zeroth term integrates to zero against cos u (∮dl = 0, unit 02's observation); the first gives
  **M ≈ μ₀πN²a⁴/(2d³) · (1 − 3a²/d² + …)** (carry the second term; the coefficient −3 is checked numerically: exact/leading = 0.955 at d = 8a, against 1 − 3/64 = 0.953).
  A second derivation for the notes: the on-axis field of a loop from Biot–Savart (MR 23), B = μ₀NIa²/(2(a² + d²)^{3/2}), times the receiving coil's Nπa² when d ≫ a.
- **The 1/d³ law**: k ∝ (a/d)³ once d ≫ a, which is Kurs's "D⁻³ dependence characteristic of dipole-dipole coupling" (cited).
- **Where it fails (show it).** The leading term against the exact value: at 160 mm (d = 8a) 0.00081 against 0.00078 (5% high); at 40 mm 74% high; at 5 mm it gives **k = 26.7**, which unit 02's M² ≤ L₁L₂ forbids outright. The correction 3a²/d² is 48 at 5 mm: the series is meaningless there, and the phone sits in the regime where only the exact integral will do.
- **Scale invariance** (prove): scaling every length (a, b, d) by λ scales M and L by λ, so k depends only on the shape, d/a and a/b (checked: k(5 mm) = k(50 mm) for coils 10 times larger). An electric-car pad across a gap set by ground clearance needs coils of comparable size to the gap to keep k: the phone's pad scaled up (derived from the scaling; no claim about real pad dimensions).
- **Why metres need very high Q.** At fixed geometry U = kQ, and k ∝ d⁻³ far away: doubling d divides U by 8. The Q both coils need for 80% (U = 8.94): **299 at 40 mm, 1630 at 80 mm, 11 500 at 160 mm**; for 50% (U = 2.83): 94, 515, 3640. Kurs's coils had Q = 950 (cited), against our assumed 100.
- **Kurs, by U** (inferred and labelled). ~40% needs U ≈ 2.11; with Q = 950 that is k ≈ 0.0022 at about 2 m. A crude single-loop model of their coils (radius 0.3 m, wire radius 3 mm, the turns concentrated) gives k = 0.00106 at 2 m (U = 1.0, η = 17%) and 0.00063 at 2.4 m (U = 0.59), where the paper reports κ/Γ > 1. Present this as the crude model's limit: a helix 20 cm tall carrying a distributed current is not one loop. Do NOT present it as matching Kurs.
- **Near field.** All of this is the quasi-static, 1/d³ field; it holds for d ≪ λ/2π. λ/2π is **341 m at 140 kHz**, 561 m at 85 kHz, **4.82 m at Kurs's 9.9 MHz** (so their 2 m is still near field: their word is "nonradiative"), and 1.99 cm at 2.4 GHz. **8. Hardware: Litz wire, and a scrap of foil.**
- **Litz wire (one paragraph, works with or without branch 10).** Copper's skin depth at 140 kHz is δ = √(2/ωμ₀σ) = **0.174 mm** (the formula stated in unit 07's eddy-current notes and proved in branch 10). Strands thinner than δ carry uniform current (1.0002 for 0.1 mm strands, branch 10), and twisting them so that each takes every position in the bundle makes them share the current equally. If you took branch 10, this is its *A charging coil at 150 kHz* and *Twisted, so every strand takes every place*; if not, take the two ratios as stated. Proximity effect between strands and turns is named, not computed. **Why it matters here, in U** (computed): if both coils' resistance rose by 3.13, U would fall by 3.13 and η_max at 20 mm from 0.826 to 0.553 (at 40 mm from 0.519 to 0.161). - **A foil scrap on the pad (the foreign object; derived).** A thin aluminium disc, radius R, thickness t, in a uniform axial field B̂ cos ωt.

Say it in those words, as a result of the model.
