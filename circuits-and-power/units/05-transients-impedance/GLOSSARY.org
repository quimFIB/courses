The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: a car's ignition coil. A 12 V battery drives the coil's
primary winding, \(L = 6\) mH in series with \(R = 3\ \Omega\), through a
contact; a capacitor \(C = 0.2\ \mu\mathrm F\) sits across the contact. All
values are assumed for the example. Switched on: time constant
\(L/R = 2\) ms, final current 4 A, stored energy 48 mJ (29.0 mJ at 6000 rpm,
where the dwell is 1.5 time constants). Switched off: the current rings into
the capacitor, about 693 V lossless (684 V with \(R\), 695 V with the
battery), at 4.59 kHz with \(Q = 57.7\), instead of the 24 kV a bare contact
would face. Seen from the contact, \(Z(s) = (R + sL)/(LCs^2 + RCs + 1)\),
which is positive-real.

* The running example

- ignition coil :: Two windings on a common iron core: a primary of few turns
  fed from the battery and a secondary of many turns connected to the spark
  plug. Current built up in the primary is interrupted, and the collapse of
  the flux induces a high voltage in the secondary, which fires the plug (unit
  07 finds how high it can rise; the spark itself is not modelled). This
  unit models the primary alone, as an inductance in series with a
  resistance. /Slide: How twelve volts makes a spark./
- contact :: The switch that interrupts the coil's primary current once per
  spark: a pair of points opened by a cam in older systems, a transistor in
  later ones. A capacitor (the condenser) is connected across it. /Slide: How
  twelve volts makes a spark./

* Memory, and the first-order circuit

- state :: The capacitor voltages and inductor currents of a network. They
  are continuous whenever the other variable is finite, the stored energy is a
  function of them alone, and with the sources from \(t_0\) on their values at
  \(t_0\) fix everything after. /Slide: Kirchhoff's laws with derivatives./
- order :: The number of independent state variables: the number of
  capacitors and inductors, less one for each independent capacitor loop and
  each independent inductor cut. Proved here as an upper bound; that it is
  attained is stated, not proved (Desoer and Kuh, ch. 12, give the general
  construction of state equations). /Slide: The order: counting energy stores./
- capacitor loop :: A cycle of the graph made only of capacitors and voltage
  sources. KVL round it fixes one capacitor voltage in terms of the others, so
  it lowers the order by one. /Slide: The order: counting energy stores./
- inductor cut :: A cut made only of inductors and current sources. KCL across
  it fixes one inductor current in terms of the others, lowering the order by
  one. /Slide: The order: counting energy stores./
- time constant :: In a circuit with a single energy store and constant
  sources, the \(\tau\) in \(x(t) = x(\infty) + (x(0^+) - x(\infty))e^{-t/\tau}\):
  \(\tau = RC\) or \(L/R\), with \(R\) the Thévenin resistance the store sees.
  After \(\tau\) a quantity has covered 63.2% of its change, after \(5\tau\)
  99.3%. /Slide: One store, one time constant./
- dwell :: The time the ignition contact stays closed in each cycle, during
  which the coil's current builds up. Taken as 60% of the interval between
  sparks, an assumption. /Slide: High revs: the coil does not fill./

* The series RLC

- characteristic resistance :: \(\sqrt{L/C}\), the ratio of peak voltage to
  peak current in the lossless free oscillation of an inductor and a
  capacitor, from \(\tfrac12L\hat\imath^2 = \tfrac12C\hat v^2\). The quality
  factor of a series RLC is \(Q = \sqrt{L/C}/R\). /Slide: Free ringing, and
  the characteristic resistance./
- snubber :: An element placed across a switch to give an inductive current a
  path while the switch opens, so that the voltage across the opening switch
  rises slowly instead of all at once. The ignition condenser is one. /Slide
  notes: Worked: the switch-off, in time./

* The Laplace transform

- s-domain :: The circuit redrawn for Laplace transforms: the same graph with
  \(V_e(s)\) and \(I_e(s)\) on the edges, impedances \(R\), \(sL\),
  \(1/(sC)\) in place of the elements, and each nonempty energy store
  replaced by an empty one plus a source, \(Li(0)\) or \(v(0)/s\).
  /Slide: Initial conditions become sources./
- network function :: The ratio \(H(s)\) of the transform of a response to
  the transform of the input, with every store empty at \(t = 0\). It is a
  rational function of \(s\) with real coefficients, by Cramer's rule on the
  nodal equations. /Slide: Network functions, and what stable means./
- impulse response :: The inverse Laplace transform \(h\) of a network
  function: the response to a unit impulse input. Every response from rest is
  \(h\) convolved with the input. /Slide: Network functions, and what stable
  means./
- stable :: Of a linear circuit: every bounded input gives a bounded output.
  For a proper rational network function this holds exactly when every pole
  has \(\operatorname{Re}s < 0\). /Slide: Network functions, and what stable
  means./
- natural frequencies :: The exponents \(p\) of a network's free motions
  \(e^{pt}\): the zeros of the determinant of its s-domain equations. Every
  pole of every network function is one. /Slide notes: Network functions, and
  what stable means./

* Phasors and impedance

- sinusoidal steady state :: The state a stable linear circuit settles into
  under a sinusoidal input at \(\omega\): every quantity is a sinusoid at
  \(\omega\), with complex amplitude \(H(i\omega)\) times the input's.
  /Slide: The sinusoidal steady state./
- RMS value :: The root of the mean square of a periodic signal,
  \(\sqrt{\tfrac1T\int_0^Tv^2\,\mathrm dt}\); for a sinusoid of peak
  \(\hat V\), \(\hat V/\sqrt2\). Mains 230 V is RMS, 325.3 V peak.
  /Slide: RMS, and the phasor./
- phasor :: The complex number \(V\) with \(v(t) = \operatorname{Re}(\sqrt2Ve^{i\omega t})\):
  its modulus is the RMS value, its argument the phase. Phasors obey
  Kirchhoff's laws, and \(\mathrm d/\mathrm dt\) acts on them as
  multiplication by \(i\omega\). /Slide: RMS, and the phasor./
- impedance :: \(Z = V/I\) for an element or two-terminal network in the
  sinusoidal steady state, \(Z = R + iX\); for a resistor \(R\), an inductor
  \(i\omega L\), a capacitor \(1/(i\omega C)\). It is the network function
  from current to voltage at \(s = i\omega\). /Slide: Impedance and
  admittance./
- admittance :: \(Y = 1/Z = G + iB\): conductance plus \(i\) times
  susceptance. Admittances in parallel add. /Slide: Impedance and admittance./
- reactance :: The imaginary part \(X\) of an impedance: \(\omega L\) for an
  inductor, \(-1/(\omega C)\) for a capacitor. /Slide: Impedance and
  admittance./
- susceptance :: The imaginary part \(B\) of an admittance: \(\omega C\) for
  a capacitor, \(-1/(\omega L)\) for an inductor. /Slide: Impedance and
  admittance./
- resonance :: For a series RLC, the frequency \(\omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{LC}\) at
  which the reactance vanishes: the impedance is \(R\), real and least, and
  the inductor's and capacitor's voltages, each \(Q\) times the drive, cancel.
  /Slide: The series RLC as arrows./
- phasor diagram :: The phasors of a circuit drawn as arrows in the complex
  plane, so that Kirchhoff's laws become closed polygons of arrows.
  /Slide: The series RLC as arrows./
- half-power bandwidth :: The distance between the two frequencies at which
  the power in a resonant circuit falls to half its peak. For a series RLC it
  is exactly \(R/L = \omega_0/Q\), MR 06's \(\gamma\). /Slide: Resonance, Q
  and bandwidth./

* Positive-real

- positive-real function :: A rational function \(Z(s)\) with real
  coefficients and \(\operatorname{Re}Z(s) \ge 0\) whenever
  \(\operatorname{Re}s > 0\). Every RLC impedance is one (proved from
  Tellegen's theorem), and every one is the impedance of some passive network
  (Brune, 1931, cited; his networks may include ideal transformers). The
  term is Brune's. /Slide: Positive-real: the
  definition./
- driving-point impedance :: The impedance seen at one pair of terminals of a
  network, as a function of \(s\). /Slide: Positive-real: the definition./
- tank :: An inductor and a capacitor in parallel. Its impedance
  \(\frac{s/C}{s^2 + 1/(LC)}\) is infinite at its resonance: a parallel
  resonance blocks current. /Slide: The converse, and three tests./

* Lossless networks

- Foster's reactance theorem :: For a network of inductors and capacitors
  only, \(Z(i\omega) = iX(\omega)\) with \(X\) odd and
  \(\mathrm dX/\mathrm d\omega = (\sum L I_e^2 + \sum I_e^2/(\omega^2C))/I^2 > 0\)
  wherever finite: twice the mean stored energy per squared ampere. Poles and
  zeros of \(X\) alternate. R. M. Foster (1924). /Slide: Foster's reactance
  theorem./
- lossless network :: A network of inductors and capacitors with no
  resistors; it stores energy and dissipates none. /Slide: Foster's reactance
  theorem./
- Foster form :: The partial-fraction form of a lossless impedance,
  \(Z = k_\infty s + k_0/s + \sum 2k_is/(s^2 + \omega_i^2)\) with every
  \(k \ge 0\), read as an inductor, a capacitor and a chain of parallel tanks
  in series. /Slide: The Foster form, as a chain of tanks./
