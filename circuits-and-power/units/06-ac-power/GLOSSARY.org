The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: a workshop drawing 20 kW at power factor 0.7 lagging from a
single-phase 230 V, 50 Hz supply through 50 m of two-core \(35\ \mathrm{mm^2}\)
copper cable (\(R = 48.0\ \mathrm{m\Omega}\)), all assumed. \(|S| = 28.57\)
kVA, \(Q = 20.40\) kvar, \(I = 124.2\) A; \(p(t)\) runs from −8.57 to +48.57
kW and is negative for 25.3% of every cycle; the cable loses 741 W. A 1.228 mF
capacitor at the intake corrects it to unity: 86.96 A and 363 W, a factor
\(0.49 = 0.7^2\).

* Instantaneous power at 50 Hz

- instantaneous power :: \(p(t) = v(t)\,i(t)\), the rate at which energy
  enters a two-terminal element at each instant, under the passive sign
  convention; by unit 01's theorem it is the Poynting flux into any surface
  round the element. For sinusoids with RMS phasors its mean is
  \(\operatorname{Re}(VI^*) = |V||I|\cos\varphi\). /Slide: The mean of a
  product of two sinusoids./
- real power (active power) :: \(P = |V||I|\cos\varphi = \operatorname{Re}S\),
  the mean of \(p(t)\) over a cycle, in watts: the rate at which a load does
  work or makes heat. For an impedance it is \(R|I|^2\), and for a passive
  load it is never negative. /Slide: \(p(t)\) splits in two./
- reactive power :: \(Q = |V||I|\sin\varphi = \operatorname{Im}S\), in var:
  the amplitude of the part of \(p(t)\) that averages to zero. Positive for an
  inductive load, negative for a capacitive one. For a network of R, L and C
  it equals \(2\omega(\langle W_m\rangle - \langle W_e\rangle)\). Defined here
  for sinusoids only. /Slide: \(p(t)\) splits in two./

* Real, reactive, apparent, complex

- complex power :: \(S = VI^* = P + iQ\) for RMS phasors \(V\), \(I\) under
  the passive convention; \(\arg S = \varphi = \arg V - \arg I\). For an
  impedance \(S = Z|I|^2 = |V|^2/Z^*\). It does not depend on where \(t = 0\)
  is put. /Slide: Complex power./
- apparent power :: \(|S| = |V||I|\), in VA: the product of the RMS voltage
  and current, whatever their phase. It sets the current a load draws at a
  given voltage. Not conserved: \(|\sum S_e|\le\sum|S_e|\). /Slide: Complex
  power./
- var :: The volt-ampere reactive, the name of the unit of reactive power. It
  is dimensionally \(\mathrm{V\,A} = \mathrm W\); W, var and VA name which
  product of \(V\) and \(I\) is meant. /Slide: Complex power./
- inductive load :: A load whose current lags its voltage, \(\varphi > 0\),
  so that it absorbs positive reactive power: it stores more magnetic energy
  than electric on average. A capacitive load is the opposite. /Slide: For an
  impedance: the resistance takes \(P\), the reactance \(Q\)./
- power triangle :: \(S = P + iQ\) drawn in the plane: legs \(P\) and \(Q\),
  hypotenuse \(|S|\), angle \(\varphi\) at the origin, so \(|S|^2 = P^2 + Q^2\)
  and \(Q = P\tan\varphi\). /Slide: The power triangle, and the power
  factor./
- power factor :: \(\cos\varphi = P/|S|\), the share of the apparent power
  that is real. At fixed \(P\) and voltage the current is
  \(P/|V|\cos\varphi\). Stated with "lagging" or "leading", since the cosine
  does not say the sign of \(\varphi\). /Slide: The power triangle, and the
  power factor./
- lagging :: Said of a power factor when the current lags the voltage,
  \(\varphi > 0\), \(Q > 0\): an inductive load. /Slide: The power triangle,
  and the power factor./
- leading :: Said of a power factor when the current leads the voltage,
  \(\varphi < 0\), \(Q < 0\): a capacitive load, or an over-corrected one.
  /Slide: The power triangle, and the power factor./

* Complex power is conserved

- conservation of complex power :: In any network at one frequency,
  \(\sum_eV_eI_e^* = 0\) over every element, sources included: Tellegen's
  theorem, since the conjugated currents still obey the current law (the
  incidence matrix is real). Real and reactive power therefore balance
  separately; apparent power does not. /Slide: The conjugate currents obey
  the current law./

* Correcting the power factor

- power-factor correction :: Connecting a capacitor across a load so that it
  supplies the load's reactive power locally: \(Q_C = -\omega C|V|^2\), and
  \(C = Q/\omega|V|^2\) for unity. The supply current falls by \(\cos\varphi\)
  and the loss in a resistive supply cable by \(\cos^2\varphi\). To a target
  \(\cos\varphi_2\): \(-Q_C = P(\tan\varphi_1 - \tan\varphi_2)\). /Slide: A
  capacitor across the load./
- reactive-energy charge :: In Spain's access tariffs (CNMC Circular 3/2020,
  art. 9.5), a term in €/kvarh billed to all supplies except low-voltage ones
  of up to 15 kW contracted, in every tariff period but period 6, on the
  reactive energy in excess of 33% of the active energy over the billing
  period. For a steady load the threshold is \(\tan\varphi = 0.33\),
  \(\cos\varphi = 0.9496\). /Slide: How far to correct: 0.95, and a tariff's
  33%./
- over-correction :: Correcting with more capacitance than the load's \(Q\)
  needs, so that the supply sees a leading power factor. The supply cable's
  loss depends on \(Q_s^2\), so it rises again: twice the right capacitor
  restores the uncorrected current and loss. /Slide: Over-correction is also
  a loss./
- capacitor bank :: Several capacitors connected across the supply at an
  installation's intake, switched in and out in steps by a controller that
  follows the reactive power drawn, so that a varying load is corrected
  without being over-corrected at light load. /Slide: Capacitor banks in the
  switch room./

* Conjugate matching

- conjugate match :: For a source \(V_{\mathrm{th}}\) behind \(Z_s = R_s +
  iX_s\), the load \(Z_L = Z_s^*\), which takes the most power of any passive
  load, \(|V_{\mathrm{th}}|^2/4R_s\), at exactly 50% efficiency: the load's
  reactance cancels the source's, and its resistance matches. If the load must
  be a resistance, the best is \(R_L = |Z_s|\). /Slide: The conjugate-matching
  theorem./
