AC power: real, reactive, complex — and conjugate matching

the slides · the curriculum

Checkpoint. For the workshop — 20 kW at power factor 0.7 lagging from 230 V, 50 Hz — you compute the capacitor that brings the power factor to 1, about 1.2 mF, and the factor by which it cuts the supply cable's losses, 0.49, and say why that factor is \(\cos^2\varphi\).

Work each problem on paper first. Under it, Hints opens a ladder one rung at a time — each rung appears only once the one before it is open, and the last gives the first line of the working — and Worked solution has the whole thing: the idea, the working, then a check. Read the solution after your attempt, including when you got it right. F folds everything back; D switches dark and light.

Twelve problems, about four hours. P6 is the checkpoint's; P2, P3, P9 and P11 are proofs. Conventions as in the deck: RMS phasors, \(v(t) = \operatorname{Re}(\sqrt2Ve^{i\omega t})\); the passive sign convention; \(\varphi = \arg V - \arg I\); complex power \(S = VI^* = P + iQ\), with \(Q > 0\) for an inductive load; \(Z = R + iX\), an inductor \(i\omega L\), a capacitor \(1/(i\omega C)\); \(\omega = 2\pi\cdot50 = 314.16\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\) unless stated.

The workshop (all values assumed): 20 kW at power factor 0.7 lagging from 230 V, 50 Hz, single-phase, so \(|S| = 28.57\) kVA, \(Q = 20.40\) kvar, \(|I| = 124.2\) A, \(\varphi = 45.57^\circ\). Its supply cable: 50 m of two-core \(35\ \mathrm{mm^2}\) copper, \(R = \rho\cdot100\ \mathrm m/35\ \mathrm{mm^2} = 48.0\ \mathrm{m\Omega}\) with \(\rho = 1.68\times10^{-8}\ \Omega\,\mathrm m\); its reactance is neglected unless a problem gives one.

Instantaneous power, and what \(Q\) is

P1 \(p(t)\) for three loads routine

exercises: the decomposition of \(p(t)\), \(P\) and \(Q\) from \(\varphi\), the energy check on a store

Three loads are connected in turn to 230 V, 50 Hz. Each draws 10 A RMS: (a) a resistor \(R = 23\ \Omega\); (b) an inductor \(L = 73.21\) mH; (c) a capacitor \(C = 138.4\ \mu\)F.

  1. For each, give \(\varphi\), write \(p(t)\) in the form \(P(1 + \cos2\omega t) + Q\sin2\omega t\), and give \(P\), \(Q\) and the range of \(p\).
  2. For (b), check that the peak stored energy \(\tfrac12L\hat I^2\) equals \(|Q|/\omega\), and explain why it must.
  3. Unit 01's problem P13 put a \(10\ \mu\)F capacitor on the same supply and found that its power swings with amplitude 166 W. Find its \(Q\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Each answer is a pair of numbers \((P, Q)\) in W and var, read off from \(\varphi = \arg Z\). First find each element's impedance at 50 Hz.

Rung 2

\(\omega L = 314.16\times0.07321 = 23.0\ \Omega\) and \(1/\omega C = 23.0\ \Omega\): the three have the same \(|Z|\), and \(\varphi = 0\), \(+\pi/2\), \(-\pi/2\). For part 2, \(Q = 2\omega\langle W_m\rangle\) and the peak of \(\tfrac12Li^2\) is twice its mean.

Rung 3

First line: \(|V||I| = 230\times10 = 2300\) VA for all three, so \(P = 2300\cos\varphi\) and \(Q = 2300\sin\varphi\).

Worked solution

Idea. All three have \(|S| = |V||I| = 2300\) VA; only the angle differs, and the angle sorts the power into its two parts.

1. (a) \(\varphi = 0\): \(p = 2300(1 + \cos2\omega t)\) W, \(P = 2300\) W, \(Q = 0\); \(p\) runs from 0 to 4600 W, never negative. (b) \(Z = i23\ \Omega\), \(\varphi = +\pi/2\): \(p = 2300\sin2\omega t\), \(P = 0\), \(Q = +2300\) var; \(p\) from \(-2300\) to \(+2300\) W. (c) \(Z = -i23\ \Omega\), \(\varphi = -\pi/2\): \(p = -2300\sin2\omega t\), \(P = 0\), \(Q = -2300\) var; the same range, opposite phase.

2. \(\hat I = \sqrt2\times10 = 14.14\) A; \(\tfrac12L\hat I^2 = \tfrac12(0.07321)(200) = 7.32\) J. And \(Q/\omega = 2300/314.16 = 7.32\) J ✓. It must: \(Q = 2\omega\langle W_m\rangle\) (the deck's stored-energy theorem), and \(\tfrac12Li^2\) swings between 0 and twice its mean, so its peak is \(2\langle W_m\rangle = Q/\omega\). In the inductor's quarter cycle of charging, \(p = 2300\sin2\omega t\) integrates to \(2300/\omega = 7.32\) J — the same energy, delivered and then returned.

3. \(Q = -\omega C|V|^2 = -314.16\times10^{-5}\times52900 = -166.2\) var. Unit 01 computed \(\tfrac12C\omega\hat V^2\) with the peak \(\hat V\); since \(\hat V^2/2 = |V|^2\), it is the same number. Unit 01's "swinging part" is this unit's reactive power.

Check. Units: \(\Omega\cdot\mathrm A^2 = \mathrm W\) for every line ✓. Limit: in (a) the mean of \(p\) is unit 00's \(VI\) with RMS values ✓. Energy: in (b) and (c) the stores are periodic, so \(P = 0\) is forced ✓.

P2 Proof: \(Q\) from stored energy medium

exercises: Tellegen's theorem with conjugated currents, RMS means of stored energy, the stored-energy theorem

A one-port network of positive resistors, inductors (no mutual inductance) and capacitors is driven at angular frequency \(\omega\); its port phasors are \(V\), \(I\) under the passive convention.

  1. Prove that \(Q = 2\omega(\langle W_m\rangle - \langle W_e\rangle)\), where \(\langle W_m\rangle\) and \(\langle W_e\rangle\) are the time-averaged energies stored in all the inductors and all the capacitors.
  2. Deduce that a series LC at its resonant frequency \(\omega_0 = 1/\sqrt{LC}\) draws no reactive power, and that so does any network whose mean magnetic and electric energies are equal.
  3. For the workshop, find \(\langle W_m\rangle - \langle W_e\rangle\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

The object to compute is \(VI^*\), as a sum over the network's branches. Which theorem turns a port quantity into a sum over branches?

Rung 2

Tellegen's theorem with \(V_e\) from the network and \(I_e^*\) from its conjugate: \(\sum_eV_eI_e^* = 0\) over every branch, the driving source included. Why do the \(I_e^*\) obey the current law? Then use each element's law \(V_e = Z_eI_e\).

Rung 3

First line: \(VI^* = \sum_RR|I_e|^2 + \sum_Li\omega L|I_e|^2 + \sum_C|I_e|^2/(i\omega C)\).

Worked solution

Idea. Tellegen's theorem expresses the port's complex power as a sum of each element's; each element's imaginary part is \(\pm2\omega\) times its mean stored energy.

1. The branch phasors obey Kirchhoff's laws: \(V = A^{\mathsf T}\Phi\), \(AI = 0\). Since the incidence matrix \(A\) is real, \(AI^* = (AI)^* = 0\), so the \(I_e^*\) are a current distribution on the same graph. Tellegen: \(\sum_eV_eI_e^* = \Phi^{\mathsf T}AI^* = 0\). The driving source's branch carries \(-I\) under the passive convention and contributes \(-VI^*\), so \(VI^* = \sum_{e\ne\text{source}}V_eI_e^*\). With \(V_e = Z_eI_e\), each term is \(Z_e|I_e|^2\):

\[VI^* = \sum_RR|I_e|^2 + i\omega\sum_LL|I_e|^2 - \frac{i}{\omega}\sum_C\frac{|I_e|^2}{C}.\]

Take the imaginary part. On a capacitor \(I_e = i\omega CV_e\), so \(|I_e|^2/(\omega C) = \omega C|V_e|^2\). Hence \(Q = \omega\sum_LL|I_e|^2 - \omega\sum_CC|V_e|^2\). Finally \(\langle\tfrac12Li^2\rangle = \tfrac12L\langle i^2\rangle = \tfrac12L|I_e|^2\), because the mean square of a sinusoid is the square of its RMS value; likewise \(\langle\tfrac12Cv^2\rangle = \tfrac12C|V_e|^2\). So \(Q = 2\omega(\langle W_m\rangle - \langle W_e\rangle)\). \(\square\)

2. Series LC at \(\omega_0\): one current \(I\) through both; \(\langle W_m\rangle = \tfrac12L|I|^2\) and \(\langle W_e\rangle = \tfrac12C|I|^2/(\omega_0C)^2 = \tfrac12|I|^2/(\omega_0^2C) = \tfrac12L|I|^2\). Equal, so \(Q = 0\). In general \(Q = 0\) iff the two means are equal, by part 1.

3. \(Q/2\omega = 20404/628.32 = 32.5\) J.

Check. Units: \(\mathrm s^{-1}\cdot\mathrm J = \mathrm W\) ✓. Limits: one inductor gives \(Q = \omega L|I|^2\), the definitions slide's value ✓; one capacitor gives \(-\omega C|V|^2\) ✓. The real part of the same identity is \(P = \sum_RR|I_e|^2\): unit 05's \(\operatorname{Re}Z\ge0\), the energy balance for the mean ✓.

P3 Proof: complex power is conserved medium

exercises: Tellegen with conjugates, why the conjugate and not the plain product, the triangle inequality for apparent power

  1. For a network of linear elements and sinusoidal sources at one frequency, prove that \(\sum_eS_e = 0\) over all its elements.
  2. The plain sum \(\sum_eV_eI_e\) also vanishes. Show that it is nevertheless not a power: shifting the origin of time by \(t_0\) changes each \(V_eI_e\), but no \(V_eI_e^*\).
  3. A 230 V source feeds a \(23\ \Omega\) resistor in parallel with a capacitor of reactance \(-23\ \Omega\). Find every \(S_e\) and show that the apparent powers of the loads do not add to the source's.
  4. Prove that for absorbing elements \(|\sum_kS_k| = \sum_k|S_k|\) if and only if all the \(S_k\) have the same argument.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Part 1 is unit 03's Tellegen theorem applied to a particular pair of vectors; part 4 is a statement about adding complex numbers.

Rung 2

The incidence matrix is real. For part 2, shifting time multiplies every phasor by the same \(e^{i\omega t_0}\). For part 4, equality in the triangle inequality for complex numbers.

Rung 3

First line of part 1: \(AI^* = (AI)^* = 0\), so \(\sum_eV_eI_e^* = (A^{\mathsf T}\Phi)^{\mathsf T}I^* = \Phi^{\mathsf T}AI^* = 0\).

Worked solution

Idea. Conjugation keeps a current distribution a current distribution because the graph's matrix is real; the bilinear Tellegen pairing does the rest. Apparent power is a length, and lengths of vectors do not add.

1. As in the first line. Every element, sources included, appears once in the sum under the passive convention. Taking real and imaginary parts, \(\sum P_e = 0\) and \(\sum Q_e = 0\) separately. \(\square\)

2. Replace \(t\) by \(t + t_0\): every phasor becomes \(V_ee^{i\omega t_0}\), \(I_ee^{i\omega t_0}\). Then \(V_eI_e\to V_eI_ee^{2i\omega t_0}\), which changes, while \(V_eI_e^*\to V_eI_e^*e^{i\omega t_0}e^{-i\omega t_0} = V_eI_e^*\). A physical property of an element cannot depend on where the clock's zero is put, so only the conjugate product can be a power. (Its real part is the mean of \(p\), by MR 06's averaging rule.)

3. Resistor: \(S_R = 230^2/23 = 2300\) W. Capacitor: \(S_C = |V|^2/Z^* = 52900/(+23i) = -2300i\) VA. Source, passive convention: \(S_s = -(2300 - 2300i) = -2300 + 2300i\) VA — it delivers 2300 W and absorbs 2300 var, the reactive power the capacitor supplies. Sum: \(2300 - 2300i - 2300 + 2300i = 0\) ✓. Apparent powers: loads \(2300 + 2300 = 4600\) VA; source \(|2300 - 2300i| = 2300\sqrt2 = 3253\) VA. Not equal.

4. For complex numbers, \(|\sum z_k|\le\sum|z_k|\), with equality iff all non-zero \(z_k\) are positive multiples of one another: square both sides, \(|\sum z_k|^2 = \sum_{j,k}\operatorname{Re}(z_j\bar z_k)\) and \((\sum|z_k|)^2 = \sum_{j,k}|z_j||z_k|\), and \(\operatorname{Re}(z_j\bar z_k)\le|z_j||z_k|\) with equality iff \(\arg z_j = \arg z_k\). For loads, \(\arg S_k = \varphi_k\): equal power factors of the same sense. \(\square\)

Check. Units: VA throughout ✓. Energy: in part 3 the real column, \(2300 - 2300 = 0\), is unit 03's power balance averaged over a cycle ✓. Limit: with the capacitor removed, all \(S\) are real and positive for the load, and \(|S|\) does add, as part 4 says ✓.

P4 A three-load balance medium

exercises: complex power load by load, the line's real and reactive share, the source's voltage and \(S\), conservation as a check

A bus held at 230 V (take it as the reference phase) feeds three loads: a motor of 8 kW at power factor 0.8 lagging, lighting of 2 kW and a heater of 3 kW, both at unity power factor (all assumed). The bus is supplied through a line of impedance \(Z_\ell = 0.05 + 0.04i\ \Omega\).

  1. Find each load's \(S\) and current magnitude, and the bus's total \(S\).
  2. Find the line current \(I\), the line's \(S_\ell\), the source voltage \(V_s\) and the source's \(S\) and power factor.
  3. Check that every \(P\) and every \(Q\) balance, and compare the sum of the loads' apparent powers with the bus's.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Everything is a complex number in VA, A or V. Work from the bus outwards: the loads fix the current, the current fixes the line, the line fixes the source.

Rung 2

The motor's \(Q = P\tan\varphi\) with \(\cos\varphi = 0.8\). The line current is \(I = (S_{\text{bus}}/V)^*\). The line absorbs \(Z_\ell|I|^2\), and \(V_s = V + Z_\ell I\).

Rung 3

First line: \(S_m = 8000 + 8000\times0.75\,i = 8000 + 6000i\) VA, so \(S_{\text{bus}} = 13000 + 6000i\) VA.

Worked solution

Idea. Conservation lets the loads be added as complex numbers at the bus; the line is one more element whose \(S\) is \(Z_\ell|I|^2\); the source supplies the total.

1. Motor: \(\tan\varphi = 0.75\), \(S_m = 8000 + 6000i\), \(|S_m| = 10\) kVA, 43.48 A. Lighting: 2000 VA, 8.70 A. Heater: 3000 VA, 13.04 A. Bus: \(S = 13000 + 6000i\) VA, \(|S| = 14318\) VA.

2. \(I = (S/V)^* = 56.52 - 26.09i\) A, \(|I| = 62.25\) A, \(|I|^2 = 3875.2\ \mathrm{A^2}\). Line: \(S_\ell = (0.05 + 0.04i)(3875.2) = 193.8 + 155.0i\) VA. \(V_s = 230 + (0.05 + 0.04i)(56.52 - 26.09i) = 230 + (2.826 + 1.043) + i(2.261 - 1.304) = 233.87 + 0.96i\) V, \(|V_s| = 233.87\) V, \(0.23^\circ\) ahead of the bus. Source, passive convention: \(S_s = -V_sI^* = -(13193.8 + 6155.0i)\) VA, \(|S_s| = 14559\) VA; it delivers at power factor \(13193.8/14558.8 = 0.906\) lagging.

3. Real: \(8000 + 2000 + 3000 + 193.8 - 13193.8 = 0\) ✓. Reactive: \(6000 + 0 + 0 + 155.0 - 6155.0 = 0\) ✓. Apparent: loads \(10000 + 2000 + 3000 = 15000\) VA against the bus's 14318 VA. The currents tell the same story: \(43.48 + 8.70 + 13.04 = 65.22\) A of magnitudes against 62.25 A in the line.

Check. Units ✓. Energy: the source's 13193.8 W is the loads' 13000 W plus the line's 193.8 W ✓. Limit: with \(Z_\ell\to0\), \(V_s\to230\) V and \(S_s\to-S\) ✓. A number: \(V_sI^*\) computed directly, \((233.870 + 0.957i)(56.522 + 26.087i) = 13193.8 + 6155.0i\) ✓.

Power factor, and correcting it

P5 Power factor of an inductive load routine

exercises: \(\varphi = \arg Z\), \(S = |V|^2/Z^*\), building an impedance from \(P\) and a power factor

  1. A load is modelled as \(R = 3\ \Omega\) in series with \(L = 10\) mH (assumed values) on 230 V, 50 Hz. Find its power factor, current, \(P\) and \(Q\).
  2. Find the series \(R\) and \(L\) that draw 20 kW at power factor 0.7 lagging from 230 V, 50 Hz — the workshop seen from its terminals.
  3. Find instead the parallel \(R_p\) and \(L_p\) that do the same, and say why both models are right.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Answers are an angle and three numbers, then two pairs of element values. The angle of the load's \(S\) is the angle of its \(Z\).

Rung 2

Part 2: \(Z = |V|^2/S^*\), so \(|Z| = |V|^2/|S|\) and \(\arg Z = \varphi\). Part 3: in parallel the voltage is common, so each element's power is \(|V|^2\) over its own resistance or reactance.

Rung 3

First line of part 1: \(Z = 3 + i(314.16)(0.010) = 3 + 3.142i\ \Omega\), \(|Z| = 4.344\ \Omega\).

Worked solution

Idea. \(S = Z|I|^2\): the resistance carries the real power and the reactance the reactive, and the power factor is \(R/|Z|\).

1. \(\cos\varphi = 3/4.344 = 0.691\) lagging; \(|I| = 230/4.344 = 52.95\) A; \(P = 3\times52.95^2 = 8410\) W; \(Q = 3.142\times52.95^2 = 8807\) var.

2. \(|S| = 28571\) VA, so \(|Z| = 52900/28571 = 1.8515\ \Omega\); \(R = |Z|\cos\varphi = 1.296\ \Omega\), \(X = |Z|\sin\varphi = 1.322\ \Omega\), \(L = 1.322/314.16 = 4.21\) mH.

3. \(R_p = |V|^2/P = 52900/20000 = 2.645\ \Omega\); \(X_p = |V|^2/Q = 52900/20404 = 2.593\ \Omega\), \(L_p = 8.25\) mH. Both models present the same impedance \(1.296 + 1.322i\ \Omega\) at 50 Hz: \(1/(1/2.645 + 1/(2.593i)) = 1.296 + 1.322i\). A measurement at one frequency cannot tell them apart; at another frequency they differ, and neither is claimed to be what is inside the workshop.

Check. Units: \(\mathrm V^2/\mathrm{VA} = \Omega\) ✓. Energy: \(P = R|I|^2 = 1.296\times124.22^2 = 20.0\) kW ✓. Limit: \(L\to0\) gives power factor 1 ✓.

P6 Sizing the capacitor routine

exercises: \(Q_C = -\omega C|V|^2\), full and partial correction, the \(\cos^2\varphi\) law for losses — the checkpoint's computation

  1. Find the capacitor that corrects the workshop to unity power factor, and the supply cable's current and loss before and after.
  2. Find the capacitor that corrects it to 0.95 lagging instead, and the cable's current and loss then.
  3. Show that full correction cuts a resistive cable's loss by exactly \(\cos^2\varphi\), and that correction from \(\cos\varphi_1\) to \(\cos\varphi_2\) cuts it by \((\cos\varphi_1/\cos\varphi_2)^2\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

A capacitance in mF and two ratios. The capacitor must supply reactive power equal and opposite to what is to be removed.

Rung 2

At fixed \(P\), \(Q = P\tan\varphi\) before and after; the capacitor supplies the difference, \(\omega C|V|^2\). The cable's current is \(|S_s|/|V| = P/(|V|\cos\varphi)\).

Rung 3

First line: \(C = Q/\omega|V|^2 = 20404/(314.16\times230^2)\).

Worked solution

Idea. The capacitor changes what the cable carries, not what the workshop takes; the cable's loss is \(R\) times the square of the current it carries, and that current is \(P/(|V|\cos\varphi)\).

1. \(C = 20404/(314.16\times52900) = 1.228\) mF. Before: 124.2 A, \(0.048\times124.22^2 = 741\) W. After: \(20000/230 = 86.96\) A, \(0.048\times86.96^2 = 363\) W.

2. \(\tan\varphi_2 = \tan(\arccos0.95) = 0.3287\); the capacitor supplies \(20000(1.0202 - 0.3287) = 13.83\) kvar; \(C = 13830/(314.16\times52900) = 0.832\) mF. Current \(20000/(230\times0.95) = 91.5\) A; loss \(0.048\times91.53^2 = 402\) W.

3. The cable's loss is \(R|I_s|^2 = RP^2/(|V|^2\cos^2\varphi_s)\) at fixed \(P\) and \(|V|\). Going from \(\varphi_1\) to \(\varphi_2\) multiplies it by \(\cos^2\varphi_1/\cos^2\varphi_2\); to unity, by \(\cos^2\varphi_1\). For the workshop: \(0.7^2 = 0.49\) ✓ (363/741), and to 0.95, \((0.7/0.95)^2 = 0.543\) ✓ (402/741). \(\square\)

Check. Units: \(\mathrm{var}/(\mathrm s^{-1}\mathrm V^2) = \mathrm F\) ✓. Energy: the supply now provides \(20000 + 363\) W instead of \(20000 + 741\) W ✓. The two common slips: using \(P\) instead of \(Q\) (1.203 mF here — close only because \(\tan\varphi = 1.02\)), and the peak 325.3 V instead of 230 V (0.614 mF, half the right value).

P7 Over-correction routine

exercises: a leading power factor, the cable loss as a function of \(C\), why the sign of \(Q\) does not matter to copper

  1. The workshop is "corrected" with 2.456 mF, twice the right value. Find the supply's \(S\), power factor, current and cable loss.
  2. Write the cable's loss as a function of \(C\), and show that it is a parabola symmetric about \(C_1 = Q/\omega|V|^2\).
  3. Find the current and loss for \(C = \tfrac12C_1\) and \(C = \tfrac32C_1\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

The supply's reactive power is \(Q - \omega C|V|^2\); it can have either sign.

Rung 2

\(|I_s|^2 = (P^2 + Q_s^2)/|V|^2\). Write \(Q_s = \omega|V|^2(C_1 - C)\).

Rung 3

First line: with \(C = 2C_1\), \(Q_s = Q - 2Q = -20.40\) kvar.

Worked solution

Idea. Copper is heated by \(|I|^2\), and \(|I|^2\) depends on \(Q_s^2\): reactive current flowing the "other way" heats it equally.

1. \(S_s = 20 - 20.40i\) kVA: power factor 0.7 leading; \(|S_s| = 28.57\) kVA; 124.2 A; 741 W — as if nothing had been done.

2. \(R|I_s|^2 = \dfrac{R}{|V|^2}\big[P^2 + \omega^2|V|^4(C_1 - C)^2\big]\): a parabola in \(C\), least (\(RP^2/|V|^2 = 363\) W) at \(C = C_1\), and unchanged under \(C\mapsto2C_1 - C\).

3. \(C = 0.614\) mF and \(1.842\) mF: \(|Q_s| = 10.20\) kvar either way, \(|I_s| = \sqrt{20000^2 + 10202^2}/230 = 97.6\) A, loss \(0.048\times97.62^2 = 457\) W. The first is lagging, the second leading.

Check. Units ✓. Limits: \(C = 0\) and \(C = 2C_1\) both give 741 W ✓. The leading case is backwards for \(|\varphi|/\pi = 25.3\%\) of every cycle, as the deck's theorem says for either sign of \(\varphi\).

P8 Where the capacitor goes medium

exercises: which conductors a correction relieves, conservation at a node, the voltage dependence of \(Q_C\)

The workshop is at the end of its 50 m cable; at the other end is the building's fuse box, fed from upstream.

  1. The 1.228 mF capacitor is installed at the workshop's intake. What does the cable carry, and what does it lose?
  2. It is installed instead at the fuse box, 50 m upstream. What does the cable carry and lose now, and what has the capacitor achieved?
  3. At the fuse box the voltage is not 230 V. Find it (cable resistive, workshop held at 230 V), and the capacitance there that makes the upstream network see unity power factor.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

A capacitor changes the current only in the conductors between it and the supply. Draw the current law at the node where it is connected.

Rung 2

In part 2 the cable is downstream of the capacitor. In part 3, \(V_{\text{fuse}} = 230 + RI\) with the workshop's current \(I = 86.96 - 88.71i\) A, and a purely resistive cable absorbs no reactive power, so the reactive power arriving at the fuse box is still 20404 var.

Rung 3

First line of part 3: \(V_{\text{fuse}} = 230 + 0.048(86.96 - 88.71i) = 234.17 - 4.26i\) V.

Worked solution

Idea. A correction relieves exactly the conductors that lie between it and the source; everything downstream still carries the load's quadrature current.

1. 86.96 A, 363 W (P6).

2. The cable now lies between the workshop and the capacitor, so it carries the workshop's full 124.2 A and loses 741 W, as before. What the capacitor relieves is everything upstream of the fuse box — the distributor's lines and transformer — which now carry only the in-phase current.

3. \(|V_{\text{fuse}}| = 234.21\) V. To cancel 20404 var there: \(C = 20404/(314.16\times234.21^2) = 1.184\) mF. With the original 1.228 mF at the fuse box instead, \(Q_C = -\omega C|V_{\text{fuse}}|^2 = -21158\) var, so the upstream would see \(20740.7 - 754i\) VA: slightly leading, power factor 0.9993. The difference is the 3.7% by which \(|V|^2\) has grown.

Check. Units ✓. Energy: the fuse box delivers \(20000 + 741 = 20741\) W in part 2 ✓. Limit: as the cable's resistance goes to zero, the two positions become one and the two capacitances coincide ✓. The general rule, which the deck's capacitor-bank slide states: correct as close to the load as the conductors you want relieved.

Conjugate matching

P9 Proof: conjugate matching medium

exercises: maximising over two real variables in turn, unit 04's theorem reused, the AM–GM bound

A source is \(V_{\mathrm{th}}\) behind \(Z_s = R_s + iX_s\), \(R_s > 0\); it feeds a passive load \(Z_L = R_L + iX_L\), \(R_L\ge0\).

  1. Prove that the load's power is largest when \(Z_L = Z_s^*\), find the maximum, and show that the efficiency there is exactly \(\tfrac12\).
  2. Prove that if \(X_L = 0\) is imposed, the best \(R_L\) is \(|Z_s|\), and that it delivers less than the conjugate match unless \(X_s = 0\).
  3. Show that at \(R_L = |Z_s|\) the efficiency exceeds \(\tfrac12\) whenever \(X_s\ne0\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Write \(P_L(R_L, X_L)\) explicitly; it is a ratio with \(X_L\) in the denominator only.

Rung 2

For fixed \(R_L\) the \(X_L\)-dependence is \((X_s + X_L)^2\ge0\); after removing it, unit 04's theorem applies. For part 2 write \(P_L = |V_{\mathrm{th}}|^2/f(R_L)\) and minimise \(f\).

Rung 3

First line: \(P_L = |V_{\mathrm{th}}|^2R_L/[(R_s + R_L)^2 + (X_s + X_L)^2]\), from \(I = V_{\mathrm{th}}/(Z_s + Z_L)\) and \(P_L = R_L|I|^2\).

Worked solution

Idea. First cancel the reactance — it only ever reduces the current — then match the resistance.

1. For any \((R_L, X_L)\): \(P_L(R_L, X_L)\le P_L(R_L, -X_s) = |V_{\mathrm{th}}|^2R_L/(R_s + R_L)^2\le|V_{\mathrm{th}}|^2/4R_s\), the second step by unit 04 (\((R_s + R_L)^2 - 4R_sR_L = (R_s - R_L)^2\ge0\)), with equality throughout iff \(X_L = -X_s\) and \(R_L = R_s\). Efficiency: the real power the EMF delivers is \(\operatorname{Re}(V_{\mathrm{th}}I^*) = (R_s + R_L)|I|^2\), of which \(R_L|I|^2\) reaches the load: \(R_s/2R_s = \tfrac12\). \(\square\)

2. With \(X_L = 0\): \(P_L = |V_{\mathrm{th}}|^2/f(R_L)\), \(f(R) = [(R_s + R)^2 + X_s^2]/R = R + 2R_s + |Z_s|^2/R\). By AM–GM, \(R + |Z_s|^2/R\ge2|Z_s|\), equality iff \(R = |Z_s|\). So \(P_L\le|V_{\mathrm{th}}|^2/2(R_s + |Z_s|)\), and since \(|Z_s|\ge R_s\) with equality iff \(X_s = 0\), this is \(\le|V_{\mathrm{th}}|^2/4R_s\), strictly unless \(X_s = 0\). \(\square\)

3. \(\eta = R_L/(R_s + R_L) = |Z_s|/(R_s + |Z_s|) > \tfrac12\) iff \(|Z_s| > R_s\) iff \(X_s\ne0\). \(\square\)

Check. Units: \(\mathrm V^2/\Omega = \mathrm W\) ✓. Limit: \(X_s = 0\) gives back unit 04's theorem and its 50% ✓. Energy: at the match the loop's reactive power \((X_s + X_L)|I|^2 = 0\) — the source's inductance and the load's capacitance exchange their stored energy, and the EMF supplies none ✓.

A source is 10 V (RMS) behind \(Z_s = 4 + 3i\ \Omega\) at 1 MHz (values chosen for the arithmetic).

Powers in watts from \(P_L = R_L|V_{\mathrm{th}}|^2/|Z_s + Z_L|^2\); components from \(X = \omega L\) and \(X = -1/\omega C\).

P11 Proof: the backwards flux medium

exercises: the compact form of \(p(t)\), measuring where a cosine falls below a level, unit 01's flux as \(p(t)\)

  1. From \(p(t) = VI[\cos\varphi + \cos(2\omega t - \varphi)]\), prove that for \(|\varphi|\le\pi/2\) the power is negative — the Poynting flux in the supply cable points back towards the supply — for exactly the fraction \(|\varphi|/\pi\) of each cycle.
  2. Tabulate the fraction for \(\cos\varphi = 1, 0.95, 0.9, 0.8, 0.7, 0.5, 0\), and sketch it against \(\cos\varphi\).
  3. For the workshop, find how long each backwards stretch lasts in milliseconds, and the largest backwards flow.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

The answer is a fraction of a period. In \(\theta = 2\omega t - \varphi\), which runs through \(2\pi\) twice per cycle of the supply, when is \(\cos\theta\lt-\cos\varphi\)?

Rung 2

\(-\cos\varphi = \cos(\pi - \varphi)\). On a circle, \(\cos\theta\lt\cos(\pi - \varphi)\) on an arc of \(\theta\) centred at \(\pi\).

Rung 3

First line: \(p\lt0\iff\cos\theta\lt\cos(\pi - |\varphi|)\iff\theta\in(\pi - |\varphi|,\ \pi + |\varphi|)\bmod2\pi\).

Worked solution

Idea. The negative part of \(p\) is where a full cosine dips below a fixed level; the arc it occupies has length \(2|\varphi|\) out of \(2\pi\).

1. \(p\lt0\iff\cos\theta\lt-\cos\varphi = \cos(\pi - |\varphi|)\). Since \(0\le\pi - |\varphi|\le\pi\), and cosine decreases on \([0, \pi]\) and is symmetric about \(\pi\), this holds exactly for \(\theta\) in the open arc \((\pi - |\varphi|, \pi + |\varphi|)\), of length \(2|\varphi|\). \(\theta\) advances uniformly in time, so the fraction of time is \(2|\varphi|/2\pi = |\varphi|/\pi\). By unit 01's theorem the flux through the cable's cross-section is \(p(t)\) at every instant, so it points back towards the supply for exactly that fraction. \(\square\)

2. \(\cos\varphi = 1\): 0; 0.95: 0.101; 0.9: 0.144; 0.8: 0.205; 0.7: 0.253; 0.5: 0.333; 0: 0.5. The curve \(\arccos(c)/\pi\) falls from \(\tfrac12\) at \(c = 0\) to 0 at \(c = 1\), steeply near \(c = 1\) (its slope \(-1/\pi\sqrt{1 - c^2}\) diverges there): even 0.95 runs backwards a tenth of the time.

3. Each stretch lasts \(\varphi/\omega = 0.7954/314.16 = 2.53\) ms, twice per 20 ms cycle: 5.06 ms in 20, i.e. 25.3% ✓. The largest backwards flow is \(|P - |S|| = 8.57\) kW, at the middle of each stretch.

Check. Limits: \(\varphi = 0\) gives no backwards flow, the resistor ✓; \(\varphi = \pi/2\) gives half the time, the pure reactance with mean zero ✓. Energy: over a cycle \(\int p\,\mathrm dt = P\times20\) ms \(= 400\) J for the workshop, whatever the backwards stretches carry ✓.

P12 The tariff routine

exercises: reactive energy as \(\int Q\,\mathrm dt\), a real regulation's threshold read as \(\tan\varphi\)

CNMC Circular 3/2020 (art. 9.5) bills reactive energy, for supplies like the workshop's, in every tariff period but one, on the excess of reactive energy over 33% of the active energy in the billing period. Suppose (an assumption) that the workshop runs at its full 20 kW and power factor 0.7 for 8 hours a day on 22 days a month, all in periods to which the term applies, and draws nothing otherwise.

  1. Find the month's active and reactive energy, their ratio, and the reactive energy that is billed.
  2. Repeat after correction to 0.95 lagging, and to unity.
  3. What is the lowest steady power factor that incurs no charge?
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Energies in kWh and kvarh: power times hours. The billed quantity is a difference.

Rung 2

176 hours. Reactive energy is \(Q\times176\) h with \(Q = P\tan\varphi\). The allowance is 33% of the active energy.

Rung 3

First line: active energy \(20\times176 = 3520\) kWh; reactive \(20.404\times176 = 3591\) kvarh.

Worked solution

Idea. For a steady load the ratio of reactive to active energy is \(\tan\varphi\), so the 33% threshold is a power factor in disguise.

1. 3520 kWh and 3591 kvarh: ratio 1.020, i.e. 102%. Allowance \(0.33\times3520 = 1161.6\) kvarh; billed excess \(3591.1 - 1161.6 = 2429.5\) kvarh. (The price per kvarh is set separately from article 9.5 and is not used here.)

2. At 0.95: \(20\times0.3287\times176 = 1157.0\) kvarh, under the 1161.6 allowance: nothing billed. At unity: none at all.

3. \(\tan\varphi = 0.33\): \(\cos\varphi = 1/\sqrt{1.1089} = 0.9496\).

Check. Units: kW·h = kWh, kvar·h = kvarh ✓. The ratio needs no hours at all: \(\tan(\arccos0.7) = 1.0202\) ✓. A caution the regulation itself implies: for a load whose \(P\) and \(Q\) vary through the month, the test is on the ratio of the integrals, which is not \(\tan\) of any single angle.
