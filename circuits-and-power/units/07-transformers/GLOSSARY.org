The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: the distribution transformer at the end of a street, 20 kV to
400 V, 400 kVA, modelled as one single-phase transformer of that rating and
ratio: \(n = 50\), rated currents 20 A and 1000 A. Its losses are the EU Tier 2
limits for 400 kVA (Regulation 548/2014, Annex I, Table I.1): no-load
\(P_0 = 387\) W, load \(P_k = 3250\) W. Short-circuit impedance 4% (IEC
60076-5, Table 1); no-load current 0.5% of rated, assumed. From its two tests:
\(R_c = 413\ \Omega\), \(X_m = 81.5\ \Omega\) on the 400 V side; \(R = 8.13\
\Omega\), \(X = 39.2\ \Omega\) on the 20 kV side. Most efficient at 34.5% of
rated load, 99.44% at unity power factor.

* The running example

- distribution transformer :: The transformer that takes a distribution
  network's medium voltage (here 20 kV) down to the low voltage houses use
  (here 400 V between lines). In this unit it is modelled as one single-phase
  transformer of the same rating and ratio; unit 11 gives it its three
  phases. /Slide: A grey box at the end of the street./

* Two coils on one core

- winding, windings :: A coil of insulated wire wound round a core; a
  transformer has (at least) two. /Slide: Unit 02's pair, with its dots./
- core :: The closed path of magnetic material (laminated steel) on which the
  windings sit, and through which their shared flux runs.
- dot convention :: A dot on one terminal of each winding, placed so that
  currents entering both dotted terminals drive flux the same way round the
  core. With both reference currents into the dots, \(M > 0\).
- primary :: The winding fed from the source. A role, not a winding: either
  winding can be the primary.
- secondary :: The winding that feeds the load.
- coupling coefficient :: \(k = M/\sqrt{L_1L_2}\), dimensionless, with \(|k| \le
  1\) by unit 02's bound \(M^2 \le L_1L_2\). \(k = 1\) means every line of one
  winding's flux threads the other. /Slide: The coupling coefficient./
- relative permeability :: \(\mu_r\): the factor by which a linear magnetic
  material multiplies the flux density that a given free current makes,
  \(B = \mu_r\mu_0H\). For a toroid of \(N\) turns, \(L = \mu_r\mu_0N^2A/l\).
  Cited from Griffiths ch. 6 and Fitzgerald ch. 1. /Slide: Iron, in one slide./
- saturation :: The end of a ferromagnet's linear range: beyond it \(B\) rises
  far more slowly with \(H\) than \(\mu_r\mu_0H\).
- turns ratio :: \(n = N_1/N_2\). For windings sharing all their flux, \(L_1 =
  N_1^2\mathcal P\), \(L_2 = N_2^2\mathcal P\), \(M = N_1N_2\mathcal P\), so
  \(\sqrt{L_1/L_2} = n\) and the voltages are in the ratio \(n\). /Slide: Two
  windings on one core: the turns ratio./

* The ideal transformer, as a limit

- magnetising current :: \(i_m = i_1 + i_2/n\): the part of the primary current
  that makes the flux, fixed by the voltage (\(L_1\,\mathrm di_m/\mathrm dt =
  v_1\) at \(k = 1\)) and not by the load. It lags the voltage by a quarter
  cycle. /Slide: At k = 1 the voltage ratio is exact, and the currents are
  not./
- ideal transformer :: The two-port \(V_1 = nV_2\), \(I_1 = -I_2/n\) (currents into
  the dots). Proved here as the limit of coupled inductors with \(k = 1\) and
  \(L_1 \to \infty\) at fixed \(L_1/L_2 = n^2\); the leakage must vanish first.
  It passes complex power unchanged and stores nothing. /Slide: The ideal
  transformer./
- reflected impedance :: An impedance \(Z_L\) on the secondary of an ideal
  transformer is seen at the primary as \(n^2Z_L\); its phase, and so the power
  factor, is unchanged. /Slide: Power passes, nothing is stored, and impedance
  scales by n²./
- referring :: Moving an element across an ideal transformer: multiplying an
  impedance by \(n^2\) to bring it to the primary side, dividing to bring it
  to the secondary.

* The real transformer

- T equivalent circuit :: An exact equivalent of the coupled pair for any
  \(a > 0\): series \(L_1 - aM\), shunt \(aM\), series \(a^2L_2 - aM\), then an
  ideal transformer \(a : 1\). The natural choice is \(a = N_1/N_2\). /Slide:
  The T equivalent circuit./
- leakage inductance, leakage inductances :: The series elements of the T
  circuit with \(a = n\): \(L_1 - nM\) on the primary and \(n^2L_2 - nM\) for
  the secondary referred to the primary, each \(N_1^2\) times the permeance of
  one winding's leakage path.
- magnetising inductance :: The shunt element of the T circuit with \(a = n\),
  \(nM = N_1^2\mathcal P_m\): the flux path through the core, threading both
  windings.
- leakage flux :: Flux of one winding that closes through the air round that
  winding without threading the other. It is why \(k < 1\). /Slide: Leakage
  flux and magnetising flux./
- copper loss :: The loss \(I^2R\) in the windings' resistance, growing as the
  square of the load. Named for the metal whatever the winding is made of.
  /Slide: Copper loss and core loss./
- core loss :: The loss in the iron — hysteresis and eddy currents — modelled
  as a resistance \(R_c\) across the magnetising branch, so \(\propto |V|^2\) and
  nearly independent of load.
- B–H loop :: The closed curve a ferromagnet's flux density \(B\) traces against
  \(H\) over one cycle; traversed anticlockwise, so its area \(\oint H\,\mathrm
  dB\) is positive. /Slide: Hysteresis: the loss per cycle is the loop's area./
- hysteresis :: The dependence of a ferromagnet's \(B\) on the history of \(H\),
  not only its present value. The energy it absorbs per cycle per unit volume
  is the area of the B–H loop, so the loss is that area times \(f\) times the
  volume.
- eddy current, eddy currents :: Current driven round a changing flux inside a
  conductor by Faraday's law. In a steel sheet of thickness \(d\) with uniform
  \(B = \hat B\cos\omega t\), the classical loss is \(\sigma\omega^2\hat B^2d^2/24\) per
  unit volume. /Slide: Eddy currents, and why the core is laminated./
- lamination, laminations :: The thin sheets, insulated from each other, of
  which a transformer core is built, so that eddy-current loss, \(\propto d^2\),
  stays small.

* Measuring it

- open-circuit test :: Rated voltage on one winding, the other open; the
  meters read the shunt branch: \(R_c = |V|^2/P\), \(X_m = |V|^2/Q\). /Slide: The
  open-circuit test./
- no-load loss :: \(P_0\): the power a transformer draws at rated voltage with
  no load — its core loss. 387 W for the street transformer.
- short-circuit test :: One winding shorted, the other raised to rated current
  at reduced voltage; the meters read the series branch: \(R = P/|I|^2\), \(|Z| =
  |V|/|I|\). /Slide: The short-circuit test./
- short-circuit impedance :: The voltage needed to drive rated current with the
  other winding shorted, as a fraction of rated voltage; equally, the drop
  across the series impedance at rated current. 4% for the street
  transformer, which then delivers \(1/0.04 = 25\) times rated current into a
  short.
- load loss :: \(P_k\): the loss at rated current, measured by the
  short-circuit test — the copper loss at full load. 3250 W for the street
  transformer.

* Efficiency

- load fraction :: \(x\): the load as a fraction of the rated apparent power.
  The copper loss is \(x^2P_k\); the efficiency peaks at \(x^* =
  \sqrt{P_0/P_k}\), where copper loss equals core loss. /Slide: Efficiency
  against load./

* What stops the voltage rising

- surface field :: The electric field at a line conductor's surface. In the
  thin-wire picture, \(E_s = \hat V/(2r\ln(D/r))\) for peak voltage \(\hat V\)
  between two conductors of radius \(r\) at spacing \(D\). /Slide: The field at a
  line conductor's surface./
- corona :: Breakdown of the air next to a conductor where the surface field is
  too high: a glow, and a continuous loss of power into the air. Peek's visual onset for
  smooth parallel wires, \(g_v = 29.8(1 + 0.301/\sqrt r)\) kV/cm peak (\(r\) in cm), tends
  to about 3 MV/m for large radii; for a stranded cable he multiplies it by 0.72
  to 0.82. /Slide: Corona: when the surface field
  ionises the air./
- bundle :: Several subconductors held a fixed distance apart and connected
  together as one phase. Each carries a share of the charge, so each sees a
  smaller surface field. /Slide: Bundles: four thin conductors acting as one
  thick one./
- equivalent radius :: The radius of the single conductor a bundle acts as,
  seen from far away: the geometric mean of the distances from one
  subconductor to all of them, its own radius included; \((NrR^{N-1})^{1/N}\)
  for \(N\) subconductors of radius \(r\) on a circle of radius \(R\).
- insulator, insulators :: The non-conducting supports from which a line's
  conductors hang; they lengthen as the voltage rises. /Slide: Insulation,
  clearance, and what high voltage costs./
- clearance, clearances :: The air gap a conductor must keep to towers, the
  ground and other conductors; it widens as the voltage rises. Set by standards
  for overhead lines; no figure is given in this unit.
