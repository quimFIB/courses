The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: a 12 V battery with a switch and a bulb (12 V, 6 W, taken as
a fixed \(24\ \Omega\)) stand 1 m apart; from each terminal a wire runs
300 000 km out and back, so the circuit is two two-wire lines, each joined at
its far end. Wires of radius 1 mm in air (assumed): \(Z_0 = 828.4\ \Omega\).
Within about 3.3 ns, 7.14 mA flows (1.22 mW in the bulb); after each 2 s round
trip the current steps up, approaching 0.5 A with the lumped \(L/R = 69\) s.

* From Maxwell to the telegrapher's equations

- telegrapher's equations :: The pair \(\partial_zv = -L'\partial_ti\),
  \(\partial_zi = -C'\partial_tv\) for the voltage and current along a
  two-conductor line, derived from Faraday's law and charge conservation over a
  length \(\mathrm dz\). With loss, \(R'i\) and \(G'v\) are added.
  /Slide: Faraday over a length dz: the first equation./
- transmission line :: Two parallel conductors of uniform cross-section in a
  uniform insulator, long enough that voltage and current vary along them.
  /Slide: A long line, and the hypothesis that fails./
- TEM :: Transverse electromagnetic: fields with \(E_z = B_z = 0\), lying in
  each cross-section. Then each cross-section holds a static field, so a voltage
  and a current are defined there.
  /Slide: A long line, and the hypothesis that fails./

* Waves, speed, and the characteristic impedance

- forward wave :: A solution \(v_+(t - z/u)\), moving towards \(+z\) at
  \(u = 1/\sqrt{L'C'}\), with current \(v_+/Z_0\).
  /Slide: d'Alembert, and the characteristic impedance./
- backward wave :: A solution \(v_-(t + z/u)\), moving towards \(-z\), with
  current \(-v_-/Z_0\) counted along \(+z\).
- characteristic impedance :: \(Z_0 = \sqrt{L'/C'}\): the ratio of voltage
  to current in a forward wave. A real resistance for a lossless line, though
  nothing dissipates; \(\sqrt{(R' + i\omega L')/(G' + i\omega C')}\) with loss.
- impedance of free space :: \(\eta_0 = \sqrt{\mu_0/\varepsilon_0} =
  376.7\ \Omega\). A coaxial line has \(Z_0 = (\eta_0/2\pi\sqrt{\varepsilon_r})
  \ln(b/a)\). /Slide: Z0 for a coaxial cable and for two wires./
- wave front :: The leading edge of a wave switched on at one instant: behind
  it voltage, current and field; ahead of it none. It moves at \(u\), and the
  current flows to it, charging the next length of line.
  /Slide: Switching on: the front./

* Reflection

- reflection coefficient :: \(\Gamma = v_-/v_+ = (R_L - Z_0)/(R_L + Z_0)\) at
  a resistive load; \(-1\) for a short, \(+1\) for an open end, \(0\) for a
  matched load. /Slide: A load at the end: the reflection coefficient./
- matched load :: A load equal to the line's \(Z_0\): \(\Gamma = 0\), nothing
  returns, and the line behaves as if infinitely long.
- transmission coefficient :: At a junction of lines \(Z_1\) and \(Z_2\), the
  ratio of transmitted to incident voltage, \(1 + \Gamma = 2Z_2/(Z_1 + Z_2)\).
  /Slide: Open, short, matched: MR 18's ends./

* The bounce diagram

- bounce diagram :: The \((z, t)\) plane with each wave drawn as a diagonal
  line labelled by its amplitude, and each reflection as a corner. The voltage
  at a point is the sum of the waves that have passed it.
  /Slide: Waves as lines in the (z, t) plane./

* Long lines, and loss

- propagation constant :: \(\gamma = \sqrt{(R' + i\omega L')(G' + i\omega C')}
  = \alpha + i\beta\); a forward sinusoid varies as \(e^{-\gamma z}\).
  /Slide: Loss: the propagation constant./
- attenuation constant :: \(\alpha = \operatorname{Re}\gamma\), in nepers per
  metre: the amplitude falls as \(e^{-\alpha z}\).
- phase constant :: \(\beta = \operatorname{Im}\gamma\), in radians per metre;
  \(2\pi/\beta\) is the wavelength and \(\omega/\beta\) the phase speed.
- dispersion :: Dependence of the phase speed on frequency, so that a pulse's
  components drift out of step and the pulse spreads (MR 20).
- distortionless condition :: \(R'/L' = G'/C'\), Heaviside's condition. Then
  \(\alpha = \sqrt{R'G'}\) and \(\beta = \omega\sqrt{L'C'}\) at every frequency,
  and a pulse arrives smaller but the same shape.
  /Slide: Heaviside's condition: a lossy line that keeps its shape./
- loading coils :: Inductors inserted at intervals along a telephone line to
  raise its \(L'\) towards the distortionless condition.

* Lightning

- stepped leader :: The first leader of a stroke lowering negative charge to
  ground: it grows down from the cloud in steps, depositing negative charge
  along its path (Rakov and Uman, ch. 1).
  /Slide: Before the stroke: a charged channel above the ground./
- return stroke :: The discharge that runs up the leader's channel from ground
  towards the cloud once the leader connects, at typically a third to a half of
  \(c\) (Rakov, 2007), carrying a median peak current of 30 kA in first strokes
  (Berger et al., 1975).
