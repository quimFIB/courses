Transmission lines: telegrapher's equations, characteristic impedance, and reflection

the slides · the curriculum

Checkpoint. You derive the telegrapher's equations from Maxwell's for a coaxial line, compute \(Z_0 = 50\ \Omega\) for polyethylene with \(b/a = 3.49\), and draw the bounce diagram for a 12 V source with 10 Ω internal resistance driving a 150 Ω load through 10 m of that cable — reflection after reflection, converging on 11.25 V.

Work each problem on paper first. Under it, Hints opens a ladder one rung at a time — each rung appears only once the one before it is open, and the last gives the first line of the working — and Worked solution has the whole thing: the idea, the working, then a check. Read the solution after your attempt, including when you got it right. F folds everything back; D switches dark and light.

Thirteen problems, about four and a half hours. P1, P2 and P3 are the checkpoint's. Conventions as in the deck: a line along \(z\); \(v\) the voltage of the upper (or inner) conductor over the other, \(i\) the current along \(+z\) in the upper (or inner) conductor; a load is marked by the passive convention, its current entering at \(+\); forward and backward waves \(v_\pm\), with \(i = (v_+ - v_-)/Z_0\); \(\Gamma = v_-/v_+\) at a resistive end; a source of EMF \(\mathcal E\) and internal resistance \(r_i\). Phasors are RMS with \(e^{+i\omega t}\).

Constants: \(\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-7}\) H/m, \(\varepsilon_0 = 8.854\times10^{-12}\) F/m, so \(c = 2.998\times10^8\) m/s and \(\eta_0 = \sqrt{\mu_0/\varepsilon_0} = 376.7\ \Omega\). Polyethylene is taken as \(\varepsilon_r = 2.25\) (an assumption, as in unit 02). The 50 Ω cable: \(b/a = 3.49\), \(Z_0 = 49.96\ \Omega\), \(u = 1.999\times10^8\) m/s. All other device values are stated in each problem and are assumptions for the problem.

The line from Maxwell's equations

P1 \(Z_0\) of coaxial lines routine

exercises: \(Z_0 = (\eta_0/2\pi\sqrt{\varepsilon_r})\ln(b/a)\), the wave speed, inverting a logarithm — the checkpoint's number

  1. A coaxial cable is filled with polyethylene and has \(b/a = 3.49\). Find \(L'\), \(C'\), \(Z_0\) and the wave speed \(u\).
  2. What \(b/a\) gives exactly \(Z_0 = 50\ \Omega\)?
  3. Unit 01's cable is in vacuum with \(b/a = 4\). Find its \(Z_0\).
  4. An air-spaced cable (take \(\varepsilon_r = 1\)) is to have \(Z_0 = 75\ \Omega\). Find \(b/a\), and explain why the answer is what it is.
  5. How long does a step take to travel 10 m of the cable of part 1?
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Every part is a number from two formulas: \(L' = (\mu_0/2\pi)\ln(b/a)\), \(C' = 2\pi\varepsilon_r\varepsilon_0/\ln(b/a)\).

Rung 2

\(Z_0 = \sqrt{L'/C'}\) and \(u = 1/\sqrt{L'C'}\). For part 2, solve \(Z_0 = (\eta_0/2\pi\sqrt{\varepsilon_r})\ln(b/a)\) for \(\ln(b/a)\). For part 4, compare \(75/\sqrt1\) with \(50\sqrt{2.25}\).

Rung 3

First line: \(\ln3.49 = 1.2499\), so \(L' = 2\times10^{-7}\times1.2499 = 2.500\times10^{-7}\) H/m.

Worked solution

Idea. The cross-section fixes \(L'\) and \(C'\); their ratio is \(Z_0^2\) and their product is \(1/u^2\).

1. \(L' = 2\times10^{-7}\times1.24990 = 250.0\) nH/m. \(C' = 2\pi\times2.25\times8.854\times10^{-12}/1.24990 = 100.1\) pF/m. \(Z_0 = \sqrt{2.4998\times10^{-7}/1.00144\times10^{-10}} = \sqrt{2496.2} = 49.96\ \Omega\). \(u = 1/\sqrt{2.5034\times10^{-17}} = 1.999\times10^8\) m/s \(= c/1.5\).

2. \(\ln(b/a) = 2\pi\sqrt{2.25}\times50/376.73 = 1.25092\), so \(b/a = e^{1.25092} = 3.493\).

3. \(Z_0 = 376.73\times\ln4/2\pi = 376.73\times1.38629/6.28319 = 83.12\ \Omega\) — unit 01's \(R_*\).

4. \(\ln(b/a) = 2\pi\times75/376.73 = 1.25092\): \(b/a = 3.493\), the same ratio as part 2. Because \(Z_0\) depends on the insulation only through \(1/\sqrt{\varepsilon_r}\), and \(75/1 = 50\times1.5\): a 75 Ω air line and a 50 Ω polyethylene line share a cross-section.

5. \(T = 10/1.9986\times10^8 = 5.003\times10^{-8}\) s \(= 50.03\) ns.

Check. Units: \(\sqrt{\mathrm{H/F}} = \Omega\); \(1/\sqrt{\mathrm{H\,F\,m^{-2}}} = \mathrm{m/s}\) ✓. \(L'C' = 2.5034\times10^{-17} = 2.25\times\mu_0\varepsilon_0\) ✓. Limit: \(b\to a\) gives \(Z_0\to0\), two conductors touching ✓.

P2 Proof: the telegrapher's equations, and \(L'C' = \mu\varepsilon\) medium

exercises: Faraday's law and charge conservation over a length \(\mathrm dz\), the TEM assumption, uniqueness in two dimensions — the checkpoint's derivation

A coaxial cable has perfect conductors of radii \(a \lt b\) and is filled with a uniform non-magnetic insulator of permittivity \(\varepsilon\). Assume the fields between the conductors are transverse: \(E_z = B_z = 0\).

  1. Show that in each cross-section \(\vec E\) is the gradient of a potential, constant on each conductor, so that a voltage \(v(z, t)\) is defined.
  2. Using Faraday's law round a suitable loop, show \(\partial_zv = -L'\partial_ti\) with \(L' = (\mu_0/2\pi)\ln(b/a)\).
  3. Using charge conservation on a suitable surface, show \(\partial_zi = -C'\partial_tv\) with \(C' = 2\pi\varepsilon/\ln(b/a)\).
  4. For any two-conductor TEM line in a uniform insulator, prove \(L'C' = \mu_0\varepsilon\).
Hints · 4 rungs
Rung 1

Parts 1–3 are the deck's section 1. Part 4 is a statement about two two-dimensional field problems — one electric, one magnetic — on the same cross-section.

Rung 2

Part 1: the \(z\)-component of Faraday's law involves \(B_z\) only. Part 2: a rectangle in a plane through the axis, two sides radial, two along the conductors. Part 3: a closed surface enclosing the inner conductor between \(z\) and \(z + \mathrm dz\).

Rung 3

Part 4: consider \(\vec F = \hat z\times\vec E\). Show it has every property the magnetic field in the cross-section has, and use uniqueness to conclude \(\vec B = k\vec F\).

Rung 4

First line of part 2: \(\oint\vec E\cdot\mathrm d\vec l = v(z) - v(z + \mathrm dz)\), since \(\vec E\) has no component along a perfect conductor.

Worked solution

Idea. In each cross-section the fields are those of a static problem; Faraday's law and charge conservation then only need the flux and the charge per metre, which are \(L'i\) and \(C'v\).

1. Faraday: \((\nabla\times\vec E)_z = -\partial_tB_z = 0\), so in the plane \(\vec E\) is curl-free. Its circulation round any loop encircling the inner conductor equals that round a loop just inside the conductor (the two differ by the integral of \((\nabla\times\vec E)_z = 0\)), where \(\vec E = \vec 0\); so every circulation vanishes and \(\vec E = -\nabla_\perp\varphi\). At a perfect conductor the tangential field vanishes, so \(\varphi\) is constant on each. \(v = \varphi_{\text{inner}} - \varphi_{\text{outer}}\) is then path-independent.

2. Take the loop: radially out at \(z\), along the outer conductor to \(z + \mathrm dz\), radially in, back along the inner. Its circulation is \(v(z) - v(z + \mathrm dz)\). In each cross-section \(\vec B\) solves Ampère's law with the current \(i\) on the inner conductor (no displacement current through the plane, since \(E_z = 0\)): \(B_\theta = \mu_0i/2\pi r\). The flux through the loop is \(\mathrm dz\int_a^b\mu_0i/(2\pi r)\,\mathrm dr = L'i\,\mathrm dz\) in size. Draw the loop in the upper half with \(z\) to the right: traversed as described it runs clockwise, so its right-hand normal points into the page, while \(\vec B\) above a current to the right points out of it. The flux is therefore \(-L'i\,\mathrm dz\), and Faraday gives \(v(z) - v(z + \mathrm dz) = \partial_t(L'i\,\mathrm dz)\), i.e. \(\partial_zv = -L'\partial_ti\).

3. The closed surface round the inner conductor from \(z\) to \(z + \mathrm dz\) is crossed by conduction current only at its ends: \(i(z)\) in, \(i(z + \mathrm dz)\) out. The charge inside is the surface charge \(\lambda\,\mathrm dz\), and by Gauss's law in the cross-section, with \(E_r = v/(r\ln(b/a))\), \(\lambda = \varepsilon\cdot2\pi a\cdot E_r(a) = 2\pi\varepsilon v/\ln(b/a) = C'v\). Continuity: \(i(z) - i(z + \mathrm dz) = \partial_t(C'v\,\mathrm dz)\), so \(\partial_zi = -C'\partial_tv\).

4. Let \(\vec E\) be the cross-section's electrostatic field for voltage \(v\) and charge \(\lambda\) per metre, so \(C' = \lambda/v\). Put \(\vec F = \hat z\times\vec E = (-E_y, E_x)\). Then \(\nabla\cdot\vec F = -(\nabla\times\vec E)_z = 0\), \((\nabla\times\vec F)_z = \nabla\cdot\vec E = 0\), \(\vec F\) is tangent to the conductors (where \(\vec E\) is normal), and \(\oint\vec F\cdot\mathrm d\vec l = \oint\vec E\cdot\hat n\,\mathrm dl = \lambda/\varepsilon\) round conductor 1. The magnetic field in the cross-section is curl-free, divergence-free, tangent to the conductors and has \(\oint\vec B\cdot\mathrm d\vec l = \mu_0i\). With \(k = \mu_0\varepsilon i/\lambda\), \(\vec B - k\vec F\) has all four properties with zero circulation, so it is \(\nabla\chi\) with \(\chi\) harmonic and \(\partial_n\chi = 0\) on the conductors: constant, by MR 23's energy identity (Uniqueness, three ways, proof 2): \(\int|\nabla\chi|^2 = \oint\chi\,\partial_n\chi = 0\). So \(\vec B = k\vec F\). The flux per metre across a path from conductor 1 to conductor 2 is \(k\int\vec E\cdot\mathrm d\vec l = kv\) in size, so \(L' = kv/i = \mu_0\varepsilon v/\lambda = \mu_0\varepsilon/C'\). \(\square\)

Check. For the coax: \(L'C' = \frac{\mu_0}{2\pi}\ln\frac ba\cdot\frac{2\pi\varepsilon}{\ln(b/a)} = \mu_0\varepsilon\) ✓. Units of both equations: \(\mathrm{V/m}\) and \(\mathrm{A/m}\) on each side ✓. Eliminating \(i\) gives \(\partial_z^2v = L'C'\partial_t^2v\), a wave at \(1/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon}\) — light's speed in the insulator, as it must be for fields that are, locally, plane waves between the conductors.

Reflections and bounces

P3 Bounce diagrams routine

exercises: launched wave, both reflection coefficients, reading a bounce diagram — the checkpoint's diagram

A source of EMF 12 V, switched on at \(t = 0\), drives 10 m of the 50 Ω cable (\(T = 50.03\) ns).

  1. With \(r_i = 10\ \Omega\) and a 150 Ω load, draw the bounce diagram to \(t = 6T\) and list the load voltage after \(T, 3T, 5T, 7T, 9T\) and the source-end voltage after \(0, 2T, 4T\). What do both approach?
  2. With a matched source, \(r_i = 50\ \Omega\), and the far end open, find the load and source-end voltages for all \(t\).
  3. With \(r_i = 10\ \Omega\) and the far end shorted, find the current in the short after \(T, 3T, 5T, 7T\), and its limit.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Each part needs three numbers before any drawing: \(v_+ = \mathcal EZ_0/(r_i + Z_0)\), \(\Gamma_L\), \(\Gamma_s\).

Rung 2

At the load each arrival of a forward wave \(a\) adds \((1 + \Gamma_L)a\) to the voltage and \((1 - \Gamma_L)a/Z_0\) to the current. The next forward wave is \(\Gamma_s\Gamma_La\).

Rung 3

First line of part 1: \(v_+ = 12\times50/60 = 10\) V, \(\Gamma_L = 100/200 = 0.5\), \(\Gamma_s = -40/60 = -2/3\).

Worked solution

Idea. Waves bounce, each reflection scales by that end's \(\Gamma\), and the voltage at a point is the sum of what has passed it.

1. Forward waves \(10, -3.333, 1.111, -0.370, \dots\) (ratio \(\Gamma_L\Gamma_s = -1/3\)); backward waves half of each. Load: \(15\), \(15 - 5 = 10\), \(11.667\), \(11.111\), \(11.296\) V at \(T, 3T, 5T, 7T, 9T\). Source end: \(10\) V from 0, then \(10 + 5\times\tfrac13 = 11.667\) at \(2T\), \(11.667 - 1.667\times\tfrac13 = 11.111\) at \(4T\). Both approach \(12\times150/160 = 11.25\) V. The diagram is the deck's Worked: the checkpoint's bounce diagram: a zigzag between the two verticals with segments labelled \(10, 5, -3.33, -1.67, 1.11, 0.56\).

2. \(v_+ = 6\) V, \(\Gamma_L = +1\), \(\Gamma_s = 0\). The load sees \(0\) until \(T\), then \(6 + 6 = 12\) V for ever. The reflected 6 V reaches the source at \(2T\) and is absorbed: the source end is 6 V until \(2T\), then 12 V. Final state \(12\) V everywhere, no current ✓ (an open circuit draws none).

3. \(v_+ = 10\) V, \(\Gamma_L = -1\), \(\Gamma_s = -2/3\), ratio \(+2/3\). The short's voltage is always zero; each arrival adds \(2a/Z_0\) to its current: \(0.4\) A at \(T\), \(0.4 + 0.267 = 0.667\) at \(3T\), \(0.844\) at \(5T\), \(0.963\) at \(7T\). Limit \(\frac{2\times10}{50}\cdot\frac1{1 - 2/3} = 1.2\) A \(= 12/10\) ✓, the source shorted through its own resistance.

Check. In part 1, \(r_i \lt Z_0 \lt R_L\) gives reflections of opposite sign, hence the overshoot to 15 V and the oscillation; in part 3 both are negative, so the current rises monotonically. Every limit is the lumped circuit's.

P4 Proof: the limit is the lumped answer medium

exercises: summing the bounce series, the algebra of reflection coefficients, when a lossless system fails to settle

  1. For a source \((\mathcal E, r_i)\) switched onto a lossless line of \(Z_0\) ending in \(R_L\), prove that if \(r_i\) and \(R_L\) are not both in \(\{0, \infty\}\), the load voltage converges to \(\mathcal ER_L/(r_i + R_L)\).
  2. Prove the same for the source-end voltage.
  3. Take \(r_i = 0\), \(R_L = 0\). Show that the current grows by \(2\mathcal E/Z_0\) every round trip, and that this matches a lumped inductor \(L = L'l\) across an ideal source.
  4. Take \(r_i = 0\), \(R_L = \infty\). Describe the load voltage for all \(t\). Which circuit of unit 05 behaves like this?
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

The answer to parts 1 and 2 is a geometric series; parts 3 and 4 are what happens when its ratio has modulus 1.

Rung 2

Compute \(1 - \Gamma_L\Gamma_s\) over the common denominator \((R_L + Z_0)(r_i + Z_0)\); the cross terms cancel. For part 3, \(2T/(L'l) = 2/(L'u) = 2/Z_0\).

Rung 3

First line: after \(n\) arrivals, \(v_L = (1 + \Gamma_L)v_+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(\Gamma_L\Gamma_s)^k\).

Worked solution

Idea. Each round trip multiplies the waves by \(\Gamma_L\Gamma_s\); if that is smaller than 1 in size, the sum converges, and the algebra makes \(Z_0\) drop out.

1. \(|\Gamma| \le 1\) for any resistance, with equality only at \(0\) or \(\infty\); so under the hypothesis \(|\Gamma_L\Gamma_s| \lt 1\) and \(v_L\to(1 + \Gamma_L)v_+/(1 - \Gamma_L\Gamma_s)\). Now \((R_L + Z_0)(r_i + Z_0) - (R_L - Z_0)(r_i - Z_0) = 2Z_0(R_L + r_i)\), so \[v_L(\infty) = \frac{\mathcal EZ_0}{r_i + Z_0}\cdot\frac{2R_L}{R_L + Z_0}\cdot\frac{(R_L + Z_0)(r_i + Z_0)}{2Z_0(R_L + r_i)} = \frac{\mathcal ER_L}{r_i + R_L}.\] (For \(R_L = \infty\), \(\Gamma_L = 1\) and the same limit, \(\mathcal E\), follows by letting \(R_L\to\infty\).) \(\square\)

2. The source end starts at \(v_+\) and each return adds \((1 + \Gamma_s)\Gamma_La_k\) with \(a_k = (\Gamma_L\Gamma_s)^kv_+\). Its limit is \(v_+\big(1 + \Gamma_L(1 + \Gamma_s)/(1 - \Gamma_L\Gamma_s)\big) = v_+(1 + \Gamma_L)/(1 - \Gamma_L\Gamma_s)\), the same as the load's: in the steady state the lossless line has one voltage along its length. \(\square\)

3. \(v_+ = \mathcal E\), \(\Gamma_L = \Gamma_s = -1\), ratio \(+1\): every wave is \(\pm\mathcal E\), and each arrival at the short adds \(2\mathcal E/Z_0\) to its current, every \(2T\). Average rate \(\mathcal E/(Z_0T) = \mathcal E/(L'uT) = \mathcal E/(L'l)\), which is \(\mathrm di/\mathrm dt\) for an inductor \(L'l\) across \(\mathcal E\) ✓. The current grows without bound: no resistance anywhere, as in the lumped circuit.

4. \(v_+ = \mathcal E\), \(\Gamma_L = +1\), \(\Gamma_s = -1\), ratio \(-1\). Load: \(0\) until \(T\), then \(2\mathcal E\) until \(3T\), then \(2\mathcal E - 2\mathcal E = 0\) until \(5T\), and so on: a square wave between 0 and \(2\mathcal E\) with period \(4T\), for ever. Its average is \(\mathcal E\), the lumped answer, but it never settles: this is unit 05's lossless LC circuit switched onto a source, which rings at \(\pm\) its final value for ever; here the line's lowest mode has period \(4T\), a quarter-wavelength resonator.

Check. Part 1 with \(R_L = Z_0\): \(\Gamma_L = 0\) and the limit is reached at the first arrival, \(\mathcal EZ_0/(r_i + Z_0)\) ✓. Part 4's energy: nothing dissipates, so the energy drawn from the source while charging the line must be returned to it, which the sign changes do.

P5 Energy bookkeeping in a bounce medium

exercises: power from a bounce diagram, the energy stored on a line, energy balance

For the checkpoint's circuit (12 V, \(r_i = 10\ \Omega\), 10 m of the 50 Ω cable, 150 Ω, \(T = 50.03\) ns), up to \(t = 5T\):

  1. find the energy supplied by the EMF and the energy dissipated in \(r_i\);
  2. find the energy delivered to the load;
  3. find the energy stored on the line at \(t = 5T\), and check the balance.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Everything is piecewise constant: each answer is a sum of (power) × (duration) over the intervals of the bounce diagram.

Rung 2

The source current is \((\mathcal E - v_s)/r_i\) with \(v_s = 10\), \(11.667\), \(11.111\) V on \([0, 2T)\), \([2T, 4T)\), \([4T, 5T)\). At \(t = 5T\) the whole line carries the forward waves \(a_0 + a_1 + a_2\) and the backward waves \(b_0 + b_1\).

Rung 3

First line: on \([0, 2T)\) the source current is \((12 - 10)/10 = 0.2\) A, so the EMF supplies \(12\times0.2\times2T = 4.8T\) joules.

Worked solution

Idea. The EMF's energy goes three places: heat in \(r_i\), heat in the load, and fields on the line.

1. Source currents: \(0.2\) A on \([0, 2T)\), \(0.0333\) A on \([2T, 4T)\), \(0.0889\) A on \([4T, 5T)\). EMF: \(12(0.2\times2 + 0.0333\times2 + 0.0889)T = 6.667T\) = 333.6 nJ. In \(r_i\): \(10(0.04\times2 + 0.00111\times2 + 0.00790)T = 0.9012T\) = 45.1 nJ.

2. Load: \(15\) V on \([T, 3T)\), \(10\) V on \([3T, 5T)\): \((225 + 100)/150\times2T = 4.333T\) = 216.8 nJ.

3. At \(5T\): forward \(10 - 3.333 + 1.111 = 7.778\) V, backward \(5 - 1.667 = 3.333\) V, so \(v = 11.111\) V, \(i = (7.778 - 3.333)/50 = 0.0889\) A along the whole line. Stored: \(l(\tfrac12C'v^2 + \tfrac12L'i^2)\), and with \(lC' = T/Z_0\), \(lL' = TZ_0\) this is \(T(\tfrac12v^2/Z_0 + \tfrac12Z_0i^2) = T(1.2346 + 0.1975) = 1.4321T\) = 71.7 nJ. Balance: \(6.6667 - 0.9012 - 4.3333 = 1.4321\) ✓.

Check. Units: \(\mathrm{W\cdot s} = \mathrm J\) ✓. Limit: as \(t\to\infty\) the line holds \(\tfrac12C'l(11.25)^2 + \tfrac12L'l(0.075)^2 = T(1.2656 + 0.1406)\), finite, while the load's share grows without bound — the steady state, where every joule from the EMF goes to the two resistors.

P6 Finding a fault by its echo routine

exercises: time-domain reflectometry, the sign of a reflection, a junction with a shunt fault

A buried cable of the polyethylene type (\(u = 1.999\times10^8\) m/s, \(Z_0 = 50\ \Omega\)) is tested by sending a short pulse into one end from a matched instrument and watching for echoes. This is time-domain reflectometry.

  1. An echo returns 1.2 μs after the pulse. How far away is the fault?
  2. The echo has the same sign as the pulse. Is the fault more like an open or a short? And if the sign were opposite?
  3. Another cable shows an echo of half the pulse's size, inverted. Model the fault as a resistance \(R\) across the line at that point, with the cable continuing beyond it (and matched at its far end). Find \(R\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Part 1 is a distance; part 2 a sign of \(\Gamma\); part 3 a resistance from \(\Gamma\).

Rung 2

The echo travels there and back. For part 3, the pulse arriving at the fault sees \(R\) in parallel with the rest of the line, which looks like \(Z_0\).

Rung 3

First line of part 3: \(\Gamma = (Z_p - Z_0)/(Z_p + Z_0) = -\tfrac12\) gives \(Z_p = Z_0/3\).

Worked solution

Idea. The delay gives the distance, the sign and size of the echo give \(\Gamma\), and \(\Gamma\) gives the impedance the pulse met.

1. \(d = u\,t/2 = 1.999\times10^8\times1.2\times10^{-6}/2 = 119.9\) m.

2. Same sign: \(\Gamma \gt 0\), the fault's impedance exceeds \(Z_0\) — towards an open (a broken conductor). Opposite sign: \(\Gamma \lt 0\), towards a short (insulation broken down between the conductors).

3. \(Z_p = Z_0(1 + \Gamma)/(1 - \Gamma) = 50\times0.5/1.5 = 16.67\ \Omega\). With \(Z_p = R\parallel Z_0\): \(1/R = 1/16.67 - 1/50 = 0.04\) S, so \(R = 25\ \Omega\).

Check. Limits of part 3: \(R\to0\) gives \(\Gamma = -1\), a short; \(R\to\infty\) gives \(\Gamma = 0\), no fault ✓. Units ✓. The matched instrument absorbs the echo, so no second echo follows.

Sinusoids, loss and long lines

P7 Proof: Heaviside's condition medium

exercises: the propagation constant, choosing a square root, what "distortionless" means in the time domain

  1. Show that if \(R'/L' = G'/C'\), then \(\gamma = \alpha + i\beta\) has \(\alpha = \sqrt{R'G'}\) and \(\beta = \omega\sqrt{L'C'}\) for every \(\omega\), and \(Z_0 = \sqrt{L'/C'}\).
  2. Deduce that a signal \(v(0, t)\) sent into a long line matched at its far end arrives at \(z = l\) as \(e^{-\alpha l}\,v(0, t - l\sqrt{L'C'})\).
  3. Show that for a line with \(G' = 0\) and \(R' \gt 0\) the condition cannot be met, and that \(\alpha\) then depends on \(\omega\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Part 1 is a factorisation; part 2 is Fourier superposition (MR 20); part 3 is a limit.

Rung 2

Write \(R' + i\omega L' = L'(R'/L' + i\omega)\) and similarly for the other factor. For part 2, each frequency is multiplied by \(e^{-\gamma l}\).

Rung 3

First line: with \(k = R'/L' = G'/C'\), \(\gamma^2 = L'C'(k + i\omega)^2\).

Worked solution

Idea. Equal ratios make the product a perfect square, so its root is linear in \(\omega\).

1. \(\gamma^2 = (R' + i\omega L')(G' + i\omega C') = L'C'(k + i\omega)^2\). The root with positive real part is \(\gamma = \sqrt{L'C'}(k + i\omega)\) (since \(k \gt 0\)). So \(\alpha = k\sqrt{L'C'}\); with \(R' = kL'\), \(G' = kC'\), \(\sqrt{R'G'} = k\sqrt{L'C'}\) ✓. \(\beta = \omega\sqrt{L'C'}\). \(Z_0^2 = L'(k + i\omega)/(C'(k + i\omega)) = L'/C'\).

2. Write \(v(0, t)\) as a superposition of \(e^{i\omega t}\) (MR 20). On a matched line only the forward wave exists, and each component arrives multiplied by \(e^{-\gamma l} = e^{-\alpha l}e^{-i\omega l\sqrt{L'C'}}\): the same factor \(e^{-\alpha l}\) for all, and a phase that is a pure delay \(l\sqrt{L'C'}\). Summing, \(v(l, t) = e^{-\alpha l}v(0, t - l\sqrt{L'C'})\). \(\square\)

3. With \(G' = 0\), \(G'/C' = 0 \lt R'/L'\). Then \(\gamma = \sqrt{i\omega C'(R' + i\omega L')}\). At low frequency, \(\omega L' \ll R'\), \(\gamma\approx\sqrt{i\omega R'C'} = (1 + i)\sqrt{\omega R'C'/2}\), so \(\alpha\approx\sqrt{\omega R'C'/2}\), growing with \(\omega\); at high frequency \(\gamma\approx i\omega\sqrt{L'C'} + \tfrac12R'\sqrt{C'/L'}\), so \(\alpha\to\tfrac12R'\sqrt{C'/L'}\). Not constant: the line distorts.

Check. Units of \(\sqrt{R'G'}\): \(\sqrt{\Omega\,\mathrm m^{-1}\,\mathrm S\,\mathrm m^{-1}} = \mathrm m^{-1}\) ✓. Lossless limit \(k\to0\): \(\alpha\to0\) ✓.

P8 A telephone pair, with and without loading medium

exercises: computing \(\gamma\) as a complex number, attenuation and phase speed against frequency, what loading buys

A telephone pair has, per kilometre (assumed values): \(R' = 50\ \Omega\), \(L' = 0.6\) mH, \(C' = 50\) nF, \(G' = 1\ \mu\)S.

  1. Find \(\alpha\) (in nepers per km) and the phase speed \(\omega/\beta\) at 300 Hz and at 3 kHz. Over 50 km, by what factor is each frequency's amplitude reduced?
  2. Repeat with \(L'\) raised to 40 mH/km by loading coils.
  3. What \(L'\) would meet Heaviside's condition, and what would \(\alpha\) and the speed then be?

(Any tool may be used for the complex square roots; the solution works them by hand.)

Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Each answer is the real and imaginary part of \(\gamma = \sqrt{(R' + i\omega L')(G' + i\omega C')}\) in \(\mathrm{km^{-1}}\).

Rung 2

Write each factor in polar form, multiply moduli and add arguments, then halve the argument and take the root of the modulus.

Rung 3

First line at 300 Hz, unloaded: \(\omega L' = 1885\times0.0006 = 1.131\ \Omega\)/km against \(R' = 50\); \(\omega C' = 9.425\times10^{-5}\) S/km against \(G' = 10^{-6}\).

Worked solution

Idea. Unloaded, the series impedance is mostly resistance, so \(\gamma\) behaves like \(\sqrt{i\omega R'C'}\), which grows as \(\sqrt\omega\) in both parts: attenuation and speed both depend on frequency. Loading makes \(\omega L'\) dominant, and \(\gamma\) nearly \(i\omega\sqrt{L'C'}\) plus a constant.

1. At 300 Hz: \((50 + 1.131i)(10^{-6} + 9.425\times10^{-5}i)\) has modulus \(50.01\times9.425\times10^{-5} = 4.714\times10^{-3}\) and argument \(0.0226 + 1.5602 = 1.5828\) rad; \(\gamma = 0.06866\,e^{0.7914i} = 0.0483 + 0.0488i\) per km. So \(\alpha = 0.048\) Np/km, speed \(1885/0.0488 = 3.86\times10^4\) km/s. At 3 kHz: \(\gamma = 0.137 + 0.172i\), \(\alpha = 0.137\) Np/km, speed \(1.10\times10^5\) km/s. Over 50 km: \(e^{-2.41} = 0.090\) and \(e^{-6.87} = 0.0010\): the higher frequency arrives ninety times weaker relatively, and earlier.

2. \(L' = 40\) mH/km: at 300 Hz \(\gamma = 0.0271 + 0.0883i\), speed \(2.14\times10^4\) km/s; at 3 kHz \(\gamma = 0.0284 + 0.843i\), speed \(2.23\times10^4\) km/s. Over 50 km: \(0.258\) and \(0.242\). Both nearly flat.

3. \(L' = R'C'/G' = 50\times5\times10^{-8}/10^{-6} = 2.5\) H/km. Then \(\alpha = \sqrt{R'G'} = \sqrt{5\times10^{-5}} = 0.00707\) Np/km and speed \(1/\sqrt{2.5\times5\times10^{-8}} = 2828\) km/s at every frequency.

Check. Part 2 already has the attenuation within 5% across the band though the condition is far from met: what matters for distortion is that \(\omega L' \gg R'\) over the band, which 40 mH/km achieves above a few hundred hertz (\(\omega L' = 75\ \Omega\)/km at 300 Hz). Part 3's huge \(L'\) shows that loading need not meet the exact condition to remove most of the distortion. Units: \(\gamma\) in \(\mathrm{km^{-1}}\) ✓.

A lossless overhead line, 400 km long, in air, at 50 Hz. For its geometry take unit 07's single-phase estimate: two conductors of equivalent radius \(r_{\text{eq}} = 0.2097\) m, \(D = 10\) m apart.

  1. Find \(\lambda\), \(\beta l\), and the ratio of open-end to sending-end voltage.
  2. Find \(C'\), \(L'\) and \(Z_0\).
  3. Find the sending-end current of the open line per kilovolt at the sending end, exactly and from the lumped estimate \(\omega C'l|V|\). How much current at 400 kV?
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Part 1 is \(1/\cos\beta l\); part 2 the two-wire formulas with \(\ln(D/r)\); part 3 \(\tan(\beta l)/Z_0\).

Rung 2

From \(V(z) = V_+e^{-i\beta z} + V_-e^{i\beta z}\) with \(I(l) = 0\): \(V(z) \propto \cos\beta(l - z)\) and \(I(0) = i\tan(\beta l)V(0)/Z_0\).

First line: \(\lambda = 2.998\times10^8/50 = 5996\) km, so \(\beta l = 2\pi\times400/5996\).

P10 The puzzle's model medium

exercises: the two-wire \(Z_0\), the first current, a bounce series in current, the lumped time constant

A 12 V battery (ideal) and a bulb (taken as \(24\ \Omega\)) stand 1 m apart; from each terminal a wire of radius 1 mm runs 300 000 km out and back, so the circuit is two two-wire lines, 1 m apart, each joined at its far end, in series in the near-end loop.

  1. Find \(Z_0\) of each line and the first current, and when it starts (in the model).
  2. Show that after \(n\) round trips the current is \(I_n = \frac{\mathcal E}{R}\big(1 - \frac{2Z_0}{R + 2Z_0}q^n\big)\) with \(q = \frac{2Z_0 - R}{2Z_0 + R}\).
  3. Find \(I_1\), \(I_{10}\), \(I_{100}\), and the time to reach 90% of 0.5 A.
  4. Show that on long time scales the lines behave as an inductance, and compare its \(L/R\) with the staircase.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Two lines in series with equal delays act as one line of \(2Z_0\). The bulb is the resistance the near end reflects from.

Rung 2

The far short reflects with \(-1\); the near end with \(\Gamma = (R - 2Z_0)/(R + 2Z_0) = -q\). Each forward wave is \(q\) times the last. The current at the near end after \(n\) returns is \((a_0 + \dots + a_n + a_0 + \dots + a_{n-1})/2Z_0\).

Rung 3

First line: \(Z_0 = (\eta_0/\pi)\operatorname{arccosh}500 = 119.9\times6.908 = 828.4\ \Omega\), so \(I_0 = 12/(1656.7 + 24)\).

Worked solution

Idea. For two seconds the battery sees two resistors \(Z_0\); then each return of the inverted reflections moves the current a fixed fraction of the way to \(\mathcal E/R\).

1. \(Z_0 = 828.4\ \Omega\); \(I_0 = 12/1680.7 = 7.14\) mA, from about \((1\ \mathrm m)/c = 3.3\) ns (with the model's caveats at that scale).

2. With \(Z = 2Z_0\), \(a_0 = \mathcal EZ/(R + Z)\) and \(a_{k+1} = qa_k\). Then \(I_n = \big(2\sum_{k\lt n}a_k + a_n\big)/Z = \frac{2a_0(1 - q^n)}{Z(1 - q)} + \frac{a_0q^n}{Z}\). With \(1 - q = 2R/(R + Z)\) and \(a_0/Z = \mathcal E/(R + Z)\): \(I_n = \frac{\mathcal E}{R}(1 - q^n) + \frac{\mathcal Eq^n}{R + Z} = \frac{\mathcal E}{R}\Big(1 - \frac{Z}{R + Z}q^n\Big)\). \(\square\)

3. \(q = 1632.7/1680.7 = 0.97144\); \(Z/(R + Z) = 0.98572\). \(I_1 = 0.5(1 - 0.98572\times0.97144) = 21.2\) mA; \(I_{10} = 131\) mA; \(I_{100} = 473\) mA. 90%: \(0.98572q^n \le 0.1\), \(n \ge \ln(0.10145)/\ln(0.97144) = 78.97\): 79 round trips of 2.0014 s, 158 s.

4. Each round trip \(2T\) multiplies the gap \(\mathcal E/R - I_n\) by \(q = (Z - R)/(Z + R)\). When \(R \ll Z\), \(\ln q = -2R/Z + O((R/Z)^3)\), so over many round trips the gap decays as \(e^{-t/\tau}\) with \(\tau = 2T/(-\ln q) \approx ZT/R\): exactly a lumped \(L/R\) with \(L = ZT\). And \(ZT\) is the lines' inductance: \(Z_0T = (L'u)(l/u) = L'l\) for each line, so \(L = 2L'l\), the loop's two 300 000 km lines. Here \(2\times3\times10^8\times2.763\times10^{-6} = 1658\) H; \(\tau = 1658/24 = 69.1\) s. Per round trip \(e^{-2.0014/69.08} = 0.97144 = q\) ✓.

Check. \(n\to\infty\): 0.5 A ✓. \(n = 0\): \(I_0 = \frac{\mathcal E}{R}\cdot\frac{R}{R + Z}\) ✓. Units: \(\Omega\cdot\mathrm s = \mathrm H\), and \(\mathrm H/\Omega = \mathrm s\) ✓. The staircase and the exponential agree to five figures in their ratio per 2 s, as they must once each step is a small fraction of the gap.

P11 A charged line discharging medium

exercises: a line with initial charge, superposing a discharge wave, energy delivered — the model behind the return stroke's picture

A lossless line of \(Z_0\), length \(l\) and speed \(u\), open at \(z = l\), is charged to \(V_0\) with no current. At \(t = 0\) the end \(z = 0\) is connected through a resistance \(R\) to the other conductor.

  1. Show that a wave runs from \(z = 0\) towards \(+z\), behind which the voltage is \(V_0R/(R + Z_0)\) and the current is \(-V_0/(R + Z_0)\) (flowing towards the end at \(z = 0\)).
  2. Take \(R = Z_0\). Describe the line for all \(t\), and show that the energy delivered to \(R\) equals the energy initially stored, \(\tfrac12C'lV_0^2\).
  3. Take \(R = 0\). What happens, and where is the energy?
  4. In what sense is this the return stroke's picture, and in what sense is it not?
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

The charged line is a steady state; add to it a single forward wave launched from \(z = 0\) that makes the end obey \(v = -Ri\).

Rung 2

At \(z = 0\), with the resistor marked by the passive convention looking into it from the line, \(v = -Ri\) (the line's \(i\) is counted along \(+z\), out of the resistor). With \(v = V_0 + w\), \(i = w/Z_0\), solve for \(w\).

Rung 3

First line: \(V_0 + w = -Rw/Z_0\), so \(w = -V_0Z_0/(R + Z_0)\).

Worked solution

Idea. A charged line is a superposition that the connection disturbs; the disturbance is a discharge front running away from it.

1. Behind the front \(v = V_0 + w = V_0R/(R + Z_0)\) and \(i = w/Z_0 = -V_0/(R + Z_0)\): negative, so the current flows towards \(z = 0\) and through \(R\).

2. \(R = Z_0\): \(w = -V_0/2\), the voltage behind the front halves and \(V_0/2Z_0\) flows into \(R\). The front reaches the open end at \(t = l/u\) and reflects with \(\Gamma = +1\): a backward \(-V_0/2\) brings the voltage to zero and the current to zero behind it. It reaches \(z = 0\) at \(t = 2l/u\), where \(\Gamma = 0\): absorbed. The line is empty. Energy into \(R\): \((V_0/2)^2/Z_0\times2l/u = V_0^2l/(2Z_0u) = \tfrac12C'lV_0^2\), since \(Z_0u = 1/C'\) ✓.

3. \(R = 0\): \(w = -V_0\), the voltage behind the front is zero and the current \(-V_0/Z_0\). At the open end the front reflects with \(+1\); behind the returning front \(v = -V_0\), \(i = 0\); at the short it reflects with \(-1\) and the cycle continues. The line rings for ever with period \(4l/u\), its energy passing between electric and magnetic and never leaving: nothing dissipates it.

4. Like the stroke: a channel that starts charged, connected to ground at one end, discharges as a front running away from the connection, with current flowing behind the front and field energy flowing into whatever dissipates it. Unlike it: the channel is a lossy, non-uniform line whose resistance dissipates along its length, not at one end; its speed is a third to a half of \(c\) (Rakov, 2007) and its current is not \(V_0/Z_0\) of any simple line.

Check. Part 2's units: \(\mathrm{V^2/\Omega\cdot s} = \mathrm J\) ✓. Part 1 limits: \(R\to\infty\) leaves the line undisturbed ✓.

P12 The lightning channel's energy medium

exercises: a line charge and its image, how a logarithm hides an uncertainty, where field energy sits

Model a leader channel as a vertical line charge \(Q\) spread uniformly over a height \(H\), on a sheath of radius \(r\), above conducting ground. Use the estimate \(V \approx \frac{Q/H}{2\pi\varepsilon_0}\ln\frac{2H}{r}\) and \(W \approx \tfrac12QV\).

  1. For \(Q = 5\) C and \(H = 5\) km, find \(W\) at \(r = 1\) m and \(r = 10\) m.
  2. How does \(W\) depend on \(Q\), and on \(r\)? By what factor does a tenfold uncertainty in \(r\) change \(W\)?
  3. In the long-line approximation, what fraction of the field energy lies within 100 m of the channel? Where was the energy before the stroke, and how does it reach the channel?
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Parts 1 and 2 are the formula; part 3 is the energy of a line charge's field between two radii.

Rung 2

\(W = \frac{Q^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0H}\ln\frac{2H}{r}\). The energy per metre between radii \(r\) and \(\rho\) is \(\frac{\lambda^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\ln\frac\rho r\).

Rung 3

First line: \(\lambda/2\pi\varepsilon_0 = 10^{-3}/(2\pi\times8.854\times10^{-12}) = 1.798\times10^7\) V.

Worked solution

Idea. The energy is quadratic in the charge and logarithmic in the radius: the uncertain radius hardly matters.

1. \(V = 1.798\times10^7\times\ln10^4 = 165.6\) MV and \(1.798\times10^7\times\ln10^3 = 124.2\) MV; \(W = 2.5V = 414\) MJ and \(310\) MJ.

2. \(W\propto Q^2\): doubling the charge quadruples it. \(W\propto\ln(2H/r)\): from \(r = 10\) m to 1 m the logarithm goes from 6.91 to 9.21, a factor 1.33.

3. Fraction within \(\rho\): \(\ln(\rho/r)/\ln(2H/r)\). For \(r = 1\) m, \(\rho = 100\) m: \(\ln100/\ln10^4 = 0.5\); for \(r = 10\) m: \(\ln10/\ln10^3 = 0.33\). Before the stroke the energy is in the air around the channel, spread over hundreds of metres to kilometres. It reaches the channel as a Poynting flux through its surface (MR 24's Poynting theorem: what the channel dissipates must cross its surface, since little was inside), as unit 01's resistor was fed through its side.

Check. Units: \(\mathrm{C^2/(F\,m^{-1}\cdot m)} = \mathrm{C^2/F} = \mathrm J\) ✓. A more careful average over a finite channel with its image gives 234–338 MJ for \(r = 10\) to 1 m — the same order, which is all the model claims. \(Q = 5\) C is of the size measured for first strokes (median 5.2 C, Berger et al., 1975); \(H\) and \(r\) are assumed.

Each answer is a complex conjugate, or a statement that something real is unchanged.

Rung 2

\(\operatorname{Re}\bar w = \operatorname{Re}w\). In MR 24's convention \(\partial_t\) acts as \(-i\omega\).

Rung 3
