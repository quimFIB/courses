Part D · unit 09 · problems

Matching on a line: standing waves, the quarter-wave transformer, and the Smith chart as a Möbius map

the slides · the curriculum

Checkpoint. You prove that the Smith chart's grid is circles, compute the standing-wave ratio of the unmatched 12.5 Ω load — 4 — and match it two ways: a 25 Ω quarter-wave section, and a single shunt stub located on the chart.

Work each problem on paper first. Under it, Hints opens a ladder one rung at a time — each rung appears only once the one before it is open, and the last gives the first line of the working — and Worked solution has the whole thing: the idea, the working, then a check. Read the solution after your attempt, including when you got it right. F folds everything back; D switches dark and light.

Twelve problems, about three and a half hours. P3, P5, P7 and P8 are the checkpoint's. Conventions as in the deck: RMS phasors with \(e^{+i\omega t}\); a lossless line throughout, with \(Z_0 = 50\ \Omega\) unless stated; \(d\) is the distance from the load toward the generator, \(\beta = 2\pi/\lambda\); \(\Gamma(d) = \Gamma_Le^{-2i\beta d}\); normalised impedance \(z = Z/Z_0\), admittance \(y = 1/z\); \(\Gamma = (z - 1)/(z + 1)\); the input-impedance formula \(Z_{\mathrm{in}} = Z_0(Z_L + iZ_0\tan\beta d)/(Z_0 + iZ_L\tan\beta d)\).

The running example: a load such as an antenna presenting 12.5 Ω (assumed) on unit 08's polyethylene cable, \(\varepsilon_r = 2.25\), \(u = 1.999\times10^8\) m/s, at an assumed 100 MHz, so \(\lambda = 1.999\) m. Its per-metre values (unit 08): \(L' = 250\) nH/m, \(C' = 100.1\) pF/m. A transmitter with 100 W available, assumed. \(\eta_0 = 376.7\ \Omega\). A coaxial line has \(Z_0 = (\eta_0/2\pi\sqrt{\varepsilon_r})\ln(b/a)\).

The line in the steady state

P1 Input impedance routine

exercises: the input-impedance formula, the shorted and open lines as reactances, Foster's theorem checked on a line, the short-line limit

  1. Find \(Z_{\mathrm{in}}\) for a 100 Ω load at \(d = \lambda/8\), and for a \(25 + 25i\ \Omega\) load at \(d = 0.1\lambda\).
  2. A shorted and an open stub of 50 Ω line, each \(0.1\lambda\) long, at 100 MHz. What reactance does each present, and what single inductor or capacitor has that reactance at 100 MHz?
  3. Show that the shorted line's reactance \(X(\omega)\) at fixed length rises with \(\omega\), with zeros and poles alternating. Unit 05 proved Foster's theorem for finite \(LC\) networks; why is this a check rather than an application?
  4. For \(\beta d \ll 1\), show \(Z_{\mathrm{in}} \approx Z_L + i\beta d\,(Z_0^2 - Z_L^2)/Z_0\), and identify the two terms as the series inductance \(L'd\) and the shunt capacitance \(C'd\) of the length \(d\). Evaluate for the 12.5 Ω load and \(d = 1\) cm at 100 MHz, and compare with the exact formula.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Parts 1 and 2 are complex numbers in ohms. Part 3 is a derivative and its sign. Part 4 is a first-order Taylor expansion in \(t = \tan\beta d \approx \beta d\).

Rung 2

Work in normalised form: \(z_{\mathrm{in}} = (z_L + it)/(1 + iz_Lt)\). At \(\lambda/8\), \(t = 1\); at \(0.1\lambda\), \(t = \tan36° = 0.7265\). A shorted line is \(iZ_0\tan\beta d\), an open one \(-iZ_0\cot\beta d\). For part 4, \(1/(1 + iz_Lt) \approx 1 - iz_Lt\).

Rung 3

First line of part 1: \(z_{\mathrm{in}} = (2 + i)/(1 + 2i) = (2 + i)(1 - 2i)/5\).

Worked solution

Idea. Normalise, put in the tangent, and divide complex numbers. A shorted or open line is a pure reactance because a lossless line with a lossless termination cannot absorb power.

1. \(z_L = 2\), \(t = 1\): \(z_{\mathrm{in}} = (2 + i)(1 - 2i)/5 = (4 - 3i)/5 = 0.8 - 0.6i\), so \(Z_{\mathrm{in}} = 40 - 30i\ \Omega\). Second: \(z_L = 0.5 + 0.5i\), \(t = 0.72654\): \(z_{\mathrm{in}} = (0.5 + 1.22654i)/(1 - 0.36327 + 0.36327i) = 1.4216 + 1.1153i\), so \(Z_{\mathrm{in}} = 71.08 + 55.76i\ \Omega\).

2. Shorted: \(X = 50\tan36° = 36.33\ \Omega\), an inductor \(L = X/\omega = 36.33/(2\pi\times10^8) = 57.8\) nH. Open: \(X = -50\cot36° = -68.82\ \Omega\), a capacitor \(C = 1/(\omega|X|) = 23.1\) pF. Each equals that element at 100 MHz only.

3. \(X = Z_0\tan(\omega d/u)\), so \(\mathrm dX/\mathrm d\omega = (Z_0d/u)\sec^2(\omega d/u) = L'd\sec^2\beta d > 0\) wherever finite. Zeros at \(\beta d = n\pi\), poles at \(\beta d = \pi/2 + n\pi\): they alternate. Unit 05's theorem was proved for rational reactances of finitely many elements; \(\tan\) has infinitely many poles, so the theorem's hypotheses fail and the conclusion has to be checked directly. (The deck's notes on worked example 1 also check unit 05's energy form of the slope, \(2(\bar W_L + \bar W_C)/|I|^2\), exactly.)

4. \(Z_{\mathrm{in}} = Z_0(Z_L + iZ_0t)/(Z_0 + iZ_Lt) \approx (Z_L + iZ_0t)(1 - iZ_Lt/Z_0) \approx Z_L + it(Z_0^2 - Z_L^2)/Z_0\), dropping \(t^2\). With \(t \approx \beta d\): \(i\beta dZ_0 = i\omega\sqrt{L'C'}\sqrt{L'/C'}\,d = i\omega L'd\), a series inductance; and \(-i\beta dZ_L^2/Z_0 = -i\omega C'dZ_L^2\), which is the first-order effect of a shunt capacitance across \(Z_L\): \(1/(1/Z_L + i\omega C'd) \approx Z_L - i\omega C'dZ_L^2\). Numbers: \(\beta = 2\pi/1.999 = 3.143\) rad/m, \(\beta d = 0.03143\); correction \(i\times0.03143\times(2500 - 156.25)/50 = 1.473i\ \Omega\). From \(L'\), \(C'\): \(\omega L'd = 1.571\ \Omega\) and \(\omega C'dZ_L^2 = 0.098\ \Omega\), net \(1.473\ \Omega\) ✓. Exact formula: \(12.512 + 1.474i\ \Omega\) — the real part's 0.012 Ω is second order.

Check. Units: every term in ohms ✓. Limits: \(t = 0\) returns \(Z_L\) ✓; part 1's 100 Ω load has \(|\Gamma| = 1/3\), and \(\Gamma\) of \(40 - 30i\) is \((-10 - 30i)/(90 - 30i)\), of size \(31.62/94.87 = 1/3\) ✓ — the line keeps \(|\Gamma|\).

P2 Proof: the power is the same at every point medium

exercises: complex power along a line, the telegrapher's equations in phasors, energy bookkeeping for reactive power

  1. From the phasor telegrapher's equations \(\mathrm dV/\mathrm dz = -i\omega L'I\), \(\mathrm dI/\mathrm dz = -i\omega C'V\), show that \(\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dz}(VI^*)\) is purely imaginary. Conclude that \(P = \operatorname{Re}(VI^*)\) is the same at every point of a lossless line.
  2. Evaluate \(P\) from \(V(d) = V_+(e^{i\beta d} + \Gamma_Le^{-i\beta d})\) and the matching \(I(d)\), and show \(P = (|V_+|^2/Z_0)(1 - |\Gamma_L|^2)\).
  3. Show that \(Q = \operatorname{Im}(VI^*)\) changes along the line at the rate \(\mathrm dQ/\mathrm dz = 2\omega(w_E - w_M)\), where \(w_E = \tfrac12C'|V|^2\) and \(w_M = \tfrac12L'|I|^2\) are the mean electric and magnetic energies per metre. Interpret with unit 06.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Part 1 is a product rule. Part 2 is the expansion of \((1 + \Gamma)(1 - \Gamma^*)\). Part 3 is part 1's derivative, read in terms of energy.

Rung 2

\(\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dz}(VI^*) = V'I^* + V(I')^*\), and \((I')^* = (-i\omega C'V)^* = i\omega C'V^*\).

Rung 3

First line of part 1: \(\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dz}(VI^*) = -i\omega L'|I|^2 + i\omega C'|V|^2\).

Worked solution

Idea. The line's elements are \(L'\,\mathrm dz\) and \(C'\,\mathrm dz\), which absorb only reactive power; so the real part of \(VI^*\) cannot change from one point to the next. The wave form of the answer then splits it into forward and reflected.

1. \(\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dz}(VI^*) = (-i\omega L'I)I^* + V(i\omega C'V^*) = i\omega(C'|V|^2 - L'|I|^2)\), purely imaginary. So \(\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dz}\operatorname{Re}(VI^*) = 0\) and \(P\) is constant. (\(P\) is the power flowing toward \(+z\), the load, with \(I\) the current toward the load.)

2. \(I(d) = (V_+/Z_0)(e^{i\beta d} - \Gamma_Le^{-i\beta d})\). With \(\Gamma = \Gamma_Le^{-2i\beta d}\), \(VI^* = (|V_+|^2/Z_0)(1 + \Gamma)(1 - \Gamma^*) = (|V_+|^2/Z_0)(1 - |\Gamma|^2 + \Gamma - \Gamma^*)\). \(\Gamma - \Gamma^* = 2i\operatorname{Im}\Gamma\) is imaginary and \(|\Gamma| = |\Gamma_L|\), so \(P = (|V_+|^2/Z_0)(1 - |\Gamma_L|^2)\): forward power \(|V_+|^2/Z_0\) minus reflected \(|\Gamma_L|^2|V_+|^2/Z_0\).

3. From part 1, \(\mathrm dQ/\mathrm dz = \omega(C'|V|^2 - L'|I|^2) = 2\omega(w_E - w_M)\). Between \(z\) and \(z + \mathrm dz\) the reactive power flowing toward the load drops by \(2\omega(w_M - w_E)\,\mathrm dz\) — what that slice absorbs. That is unit 06's statement that a network's reactive power is \(2\omega\) times its mean magnetic minus mean electric energy, applied to one slice.

Check. Units: \(\omega L'|I|^2\) is \(\mathrm{s^{-1}\,H\,m^{-1}\,A^2} = \mathrm{W/m}\) ✓, a rate of change of power along \(z\). Example: \(|V_+| = 70.71\) V, \(\Gamma_L = -0.6\): \(P = 100\times0.64 = 64\) W, and at the load \(|V|^2/R = 28.28^2/12.5 = 64.0\) W ✓. Matched line: \(|V| = Z_0|I|\) everywhere, so \(w_E = w_M\) (since \(C'Z_0^2 = L'\)) and \(Q\) is constant: a travelling wave carries no reactive power ✓.

P3 Standing-wave ratios, and a load read from its standing wave routine

exercises: \(\Gamma_L\) and the VSWR, the delivered fraction, the impedance at a minimum, running the input-impedance formula backwards

  1. On a 50 Ω line, find \(\Gamma_L\), the VSWR \(S\) and the fraction of the forward power delivered, for loads of 12.5 Ω, 100 Ω and \(50 + 50i\ \Omega\).
  2. For the 12.5 Ω load and 100 W forward, where along the line are the voltage maxima and minima, and what are \(|V|_{\max}\), \(|V|_{\min}\) and the impedance there?
  3. A probe on a 50 Ω line reads a standing-wave ratio of 3, and the first voltage minimum is \(0.1\lambda\) from the load. Find \(Z_L\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Each load gives a complex \(\Gamma_L\) and a real \(S \ge 1\). Part 3's answer is a complex impedance.

Rung 2

\(S = (1 + |\Gamma|)/(1 - |\Gamma|)\); delivered fraction \(1 - |\Gamma|^2\). At a voltage minimum the impedance is \(Z_0/S\). Moving toward the load by \(d_{\min}\) is the input-impedance formula with \(d \to -d_{\min}\).

Rung 3

First line of part 3: \(z_L = (z_{\min} - it)/(1 - iz_{\min}t)\) with \(z_{\min} = 1/3\) and \(t = \tan36° = 0.7265\).

Worked solution

Idea. \(|\Gamma|\) fixes everything about the depth of the standing wave; its phase fixes where the pattern sits.

1. 12.5 Ω: \(\Gamma_L = -37.5/62.5 = -0.6\), \(S = 1.6/0.4 = 4\), delivered \(1 - 0.36 = 64\%\). 100 Ω: \(\Gamma_L = 50/150 = 1/3\), \(S = (4/3)/(2/3) = 2\), delivered \(1 - 1/9 = 88.9\%\). \(50 + 50i\): \(\Gamma_L = 50i/(100 + 50i) = i/(2 + i) = (1 + 2i)/5 = 0.2 + 0.4i\), \(|\Gamma_L| = 1/\sqrt5 = 0.4472\), \(S = 1.4472/0.5528 = 2.618\), delivered \(1 - 0.2 = 80\%\).

2. \(|V_+| = \sqrt{100\times50} = 70.71\) V. \(\Gamma_L\) is negative real, so the load is at a minimum: \(|V|_{\min} = 0.4\times70.71 = 28.28\) V at \(d = 0, \lambda/2, \lambda, \ldots\) (0, 1.000 m, 1.999 m at 100 MHz), where \(Z = 50/4 = 12.5\ \Omega\). Maxima \(1.6\times70.71 = 113.1\) V at \(d = \lambda/4, 3\lambda/4, \ldots\) (0.500 m, 1.499 m), where \(Z = 50\times4 = 200\ \Omega\).

3. \(z_{\min} = 1/3\), \(t = 0.72654\). \(z_L = (0.3333 - 0.7265i)/(1 - 0.2422i) = 0.4811 - 0.6100i\), so \(Z_L = 24.05 - 30.50i\ \Omega\): a resistance with a capacitive reactance.

Check. Part 3: \(\Gamma_L = (z_L - 1)/(z_L + 1) = -0.1545 - 0.4755i\), of size 0.5, and \(S = 1.5/0.5 = 3\) ✓; its angle is \(-108°\), and a clockwise turn of \(2\beta d = 72°\) takes it to \(-180°\), a voltage minimum, at \(d = 72/720\,\lambda = 0.1\lambda\) ✓. Running the formula forward from \(Z_L\) over \(0.1\lambda\) returns \(z = 1/3\) exactly ✓. Part 1: the \(50 + 50i\) load's \(S = 2.618 = (3 + \sqrt5)/2\) — the square of the golden ratio, as it happens.

Möbius maps and the chart

P4 Proof: every Möbius map is built from three moves medium

exercises: Möbius transformations, their decomposition, inverses and compositions

  1. Prove that every Möbius map \(T(z) = (az + b)/(cz + d)\), \(ad - bc \ne 0\) (in this problem \(d\) is a coefficient, not a distance), is a composition of translations, dilation-rotations and at most one inversion \(z \mapsto 1/z\).
  2. Show that \(T\) is one-to-one on the extended plane and that its inverse is Möbius.
  3. Decompose \(z = (1 + \Gamma)/(1 - \Gamma)\), the inverse of the Smith map, into the three moves.
  4. Show that the composition of two Möbius maps is Möbius, and that it corresponds to the product of their coefficient matrices \(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Two cases, \(c = 0\) and \(c \ne 0\). In the second, polynomial division of \(az + b\) by \(cz + d\).

Rung 2

\(az + b = \frac ac(cz + d) + b - \frac{ad}{c}\). For one-to-one, compute \(T(z) - T(w)\). For the composition, substitute one fraction into the other and clear the inner denominator.

Rung 3

First line of part 1, case \(c \ne 0\): \(T(z) = \frac ac + \frac{bc - ad}{c}\cdot\frac1{cz + d}\).

Worked solution

Idea. Division by \(cz + d\) leaves a constant plus a multiple of \(1/(cz + d)\); every operation in that expression is one of the three moves.

1. If \(c = 0\), then \(d \ne 0\) and \(T(z) = (a/d)z + b/d\): a dilation-rotation by \(a/d \ne 0\) then a translation. If \(c \ne 0\), \(T(z) = \frac ac + \frac{bc - ad}{c}\cdot\frac{1}{cz + d}\), which is \(z \mapsto cz\) (dilation-rotation), \(\mapsto cz + d\) (translation), \(\mapsto 1/(cz + d)\) (inversion), \(\mapsto \frac{bc - ad}{c}(\cdot)\) (dilation-rotation, non-zero since \(ad - bc \ne 0\)), \(\mapsto \frac ac + (\cdot)\) (translation). One inversion. \(\square\)

2. \(T(z) - T(w) = \frac{(az + b)(cw + d) - (aw + b)(cz + d)}{(cz + d)(cw + d)} = \frac{(ad - bc)(z - w)}{(cz + d)(cw + d)}\), non-zero for \(z \ne w\). Solving \(\Gamma = T(z)\): \(\Gamma(cz + d) = az + b\), so \(z = (d\Gamma - b)/(-c\Gamma + a)\), Möbius with determinant \(da - bc \ne 0\). The points \(-d/c\) and \(\infty\) are exchanged with \(\infty\) and \(a/c\) by the slide's rules, so \(T\) is a bijection of the extended plane.

3. \(z = (\Gamma + 1)/(-\Gamma + 1)\): \(a = 1\), \(b = 1\), \(c = -1\), \(d = 1\), and part 1's formula gives \(z = \frac{a}{c} + \frac{bc - ad}{c}\cdot\frac1{c\Gamma + d} = -1 + \frac{2}{1 - \Gamma}\). So \(\Gamma \mapsto -\Gamma\) (rotation by \(\pi\)), \(\mapsto 1 - \Gamma\) (translation), \(\mapsto 1/(1 - \Gamma)\) (inversion), \(\mapsto 2/(1 - \Gamma)\) (dilation), \(\mapsto -1 + 2/(1 - \Gamma)\) (translation).

4. \(T_1(T_2(z)) = \frac{a_1\frac{a_2z + b_2}{c_2z + d_2} + b_1}{c_1\frac{a_2z + b_2}{c_2z + d_2} + d_1} = \frac{(a_1a_2 + b_1c_2)z + (a_1b_2 + b_1d_2)}{(c_1a_2 + d_1c_2)z + (c_1b_2 + d_1d_2)}\), whose coefficients are the entries of the product \(M_1M_2\); its determinant is \(\det M_1\det M_2 \ne 0\).

Check. Part 3: \(\Gamma = 0 \mapsto -1 + 2 = 1\) ✓ (the match), \(\Gamma = -1 \mapsto -1 + 1 = 0\) ✓ (a short). Part 4: composing \(\Gamma\) with its inverse gives \(\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix}\), the identity map ✓ (a matrix and its non-zero multiples give the same map).

P5 Proof: the Smith chart's grid is circles medium

exercises: generalised circles, the circle-preserving theorem in full, the constant-resistance and constant-reactance circles — the checkpoint's proof

  1. Show that the circles and lines of the plane are exactly the sets \(A|z|^2 + B\bar z + \bar Bz + C = 0\) with \(A, C\) real and \(|B|^2 > AC\).
  2. Prove that a Möbius map sends generalised circles to generalised circles.
  3. Find the images under \(\Gamma = (z - 1)/(z + 1)\) of the lines \(\operatorname{Re}z = r\) (\(r \ge 0\)) and \(\operatorname{Im}z = x\) (\(x \ne 0\)): centres and radii.
  4. Check: the \(r = 0.25\) circle passes through \(\Gamma = -0.6\); the \(x = 1\) circle passes through \(\Gamma = i\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Part 1 is completing a square. Part 2 reduces, by P4, to three cases, of which only one is not obvious. Part 3 follows a line through the four steps of \(\Gamma = 1 - 2/(z + 1)\).

Rung 2

For \(w = 1/z\), substitute \(z = 1/w\) and multiply by \(|w|^2\). For part 3, \(\operatorname{Re}z = r\) becomes \(\operatorname{Re}w = r + 1\) after the translation; a vertical line \(\operatorname{Re}w = a\) inverts to the circle through 0 and \(1/a\) with its centre on the real axis.

Rung 3

First line of part 2's hard case: \(A/|w|^2 + B/\bar w + \bar B/w + C = 0\), times \(|w|^2\): \(A + Bw + \bar B\bar w + C|w|^2 = 0\).

Worked solution

Idea. One equation covers circles and lines; the inversion swaps its two real coefficients, and the similarities keep its shape. So every Möbius map keeps the family, and the chart's grid lines, being images of lines, are circles.

1. If \(A \ne 0\): \(|z|^2 + (B/A)\bar z + (\bar B/A)z + C/A = 0\) is \(|z + B/A|^2 = (|B|^2 - AC)/A^2\), the circle of centre \(-B/A\) and radius \(\sqrt{|B|^2 - AC}/|A|\), real and positive exactly when \(|B|^2 > AC\). Conversely \(|z - z_0|^2 = \rho^2\) expands to \(|z|^2 - z_0\bar z - \bar z_0z + |z_0|^2 - \rho^2 = 0\), of the form with \(A = 1\), \(B = -z_0\), \(C = |z_0|^2 - \rho^2\), and \(|B|^2 - AC = \rho^2 > 0\). If \(A = 0\): \(2\operatorname{Re}(\bar Bz) = -C\) with \(B \ne 0\) (the condition \(|B|^2 > 0\)), which in \(z = x + iy\) is a linear equation \(2(\beta_1x + \beta_2y) + C = 0\), \(B = \beta_1 + i\beta_2\): any line. \(\square\)

2. By P4, check three moves. A translation \(z = w - b_0\) gives \(A|w|^2 + (B - Ab_0)\bar w + \overline{(B - Ab_0)}w + C' = 0\) with \(C'\) real: same form, and it moves a circle rigidly, so the image is a circle (or line) of the same size. A dilation-rotation \(z = w/k\) gives, after multiplying by \(|k|^2\), \(A|w|^2 + kB\bar w + \overline{kB}w + |k|^2C = 0\), with \(|kB|^2 - A|k|^2C = |k|^2(|B|^2 - AC) > 0\). The inversion: substituting \(z = 1/w\) and multiplying by \(|w|^2\), \(C|w|^2 + \bar B\bar w + Bw + A = 0\), the same form with \(A\) and \(C\) swapped and \(B\) conjugated, and \(|\bar B|^2 - CA > 0\). The points 0 and \(\infty\) correspond: 0 is on the set iff \(C = 0\), and then the image has \(A' = 0\) and is a line, through \(\infty\). \(\square\)

3. \(\operatorname{Re}z = r\): translate, \(\operatorname{Re}w = r + 1\) (\(A = 0\), \(B = \tfrac12\), \(C = -(r + 1)\)); invert, \(A' = -(r + 1)\), \(B' = \tfrac12\), \(C' = 0\): \(|u|^2 - \operatorname{Re}u/(r + 1) = 0\), the circle of centre and radius \(1/2(r + 1)\); multiply by \(-2\): centre \(-1/(r + 1)\), radius \(1/(r + 1)\); add 1: centre \(r/(1 + r)\), radius \(1/(1 + r)\). \(\operatorname{Im}z = x\): translation leaves \(\operatorname{Im}w = x\); inversion gives the circle through 0 and \(1/(ix) = -i/x\), centred on the imaginary axis: centre \(-i/2x\), radius \(1/2|x|\); \(\times(-2)\): centre \(i/x\), radius \(1/|x|\); \(+1\): centre \(1 + i/x\), radius \(1/|x|\).

4. \(r = 0.25\): centre 0.2, radius 0.8; \(|-0.6 - 0.2| = 0.8\) ✓. \(x = 1\): centre \(1 + i\), radius 1; \(|i - 1 - i| = 1\) ✓, and indeed \(\Gamma(i) = (i - 1)/(i + 1) = i\).

Check. Every \(r\)-circle passes through \(\Gamma = 1\): \(r/(1 + r) + 1/(1 + r) = 1\) ✓, as it must, since every line passes through \(z = \infty\). \(r = 0\) gives the unit circle ✓; \(r \to \infty\) shrinks to the point 1 ✓. Dimensionless throughout ✓.

P6 Proof: the disc is the passive half-plane routine

exercises: the Smith map on the right half-plane, unit 05's positive-real impedances, reflection from an active load

  1. Prove that \(\operatorname{Re}z \ge 0\) if and only if \(|\Gamma| \le 1\), with equality on the imaginary axis.
  2. Explain, using the power formula of P2, why this is the same statement as "a passive load absorbs power".
  3. A device presenting a negative resistance of \(-25\ \Omega\) terminates a 50 Ω line. Find \(\Gamma\), where it lies relative to the chart, and what happens to the power.
  4. Where does the whole left half-plane \(\operatorname{Re}z < 0\) go, and which point of the \(z\)-plane goes to \(\Gamma = \infty\)?
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Part 1 compares two distances; part 3 is a number bigger than 1 in size; part 4 is a region and a point.

Rung 2

\(|\Gamma| \le 1\) is \(|z - 1| \le |z + 1|\). Expand both squared moduli.

Rung 3

First line of part 1: \(|z + 1|^2 - |z - 1|^2 = (z + 1)(\bar z + 1) - (z - 1)(\bar z - 1) = 2(z + \bar z) = 4\operatorname{Re}z\).

Worked solution

Idea. \(|\Gamma| \le 1\) says \(z\) is at least as near to \(+1\) as to \(-1\): the right half-plane.

1. \(|z + 1|^2 - |z - 1|^2 = 4\operatorname{Re}z\), so \(|z - 1| \le |z + 1| \iff \operatorname{Re}z \ge 0\), with equality iff \(\operatorname{Re}z = 0\). Since \(|\Gamma| = |z - 1|/|z + 1|\) (for \(z \ne -1\)), this is the claim. \(\square\)

2. The power absorbed by the load is \((|V_+|^2/Z_0)(1 - |\Gamma_L|^2)\); it is \(\ge 0\) iff \(|\Gamma_L| \le 1\). And it is also \(\operatorname{Re}Z_L|I|^2\), which is \(\ge 0\) iff \(\operatorname{Re}Z_L \ge 0\). The two characterisations of "absorbs" must agree, and part 1 is that agreement. Unit 05 proved every impedance of positive \(R\), \(L\), \(C\) positive-real, hence \(\operatorname{Re}Z(i\omega) \ge 0\): every such load sits in the closed disc.

3. \(\Gamma = (-25 - 50)/(-25 + 50) = -75/25 = -3\). Outside the chart, on the negative real axis, three radii from the centre. The reflected power is \(|\Gamma|^2 = 9\) times the forward power: the device returns nine times what it receives, the excess of eight times the incident power supplied by whatever powers it. A negative resistance is a source, not a load.

4. By part 1, \(\operatorname{Re}z < 0\) maps to \(|\Gamma| > 1\), the outside of the disc, and \(z = -1\) maps to \(\infty\) (the zero of \(z + 1\)). A load of \(-Z_0\) would be a source that emits a wave with no incident wave at all.

Check. \(z = 0.25\): \(|\Gamma| = 0.6 < 1\) ✓. \(z = i\): \(|i - 1| = |i + 1| = \sqrt2\), \(|\Gamma| = 1\) ✓. \(z = -0.5\) (part 3's \(-25\ \Omega\)): \(|-1.5|/|0.5| = 3\) ✓.

exercises: frequency dependence of a match, the quarter-wave transformer's \(|\Gamma(f)|\) in closed form, comparing matches over a band

  4. For the first stub match of P8, write \(\Gamma(f)\) as an explicit formula and evaluate \(|\Gamma|\) at \(0.9f_0\). Compare.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Part 1 is algebra in which \(Z_q^2 = Z_0Z_L\) cancels terms. Parts 2 and 3 are an inequality on \(\tan^2\theta\). Part 4 is a number.

Rung 2

Write \(Z_{\mathrm{in}} - Z_0\) and \(Z_{\mathrm{in}} + Z_0\) over the common denominator \(Z_q + iZ_Lt\). Then use \((Z_L + Z_0)^2 = (Z_L - Z_0)^2 + 4Z_0Z_L\).

Rung 3

First line of part 1: \(Z_{\mathrm{in}} - Z_0 = \frac{Z_qZ_L + iZ_q^2t - Z_0Z_q - iZ_0Z_Lt}{Z_q + iZ_Lt} = \frac{Z_q(Z_L - Z_0)}{Z_q + iZ_Lt}\).

Worked solution

Idea. Away from \(f_0\) the section is not a quarter wavelength, and the cancellation of P7 and the deck's Why it works is incomplete. For the quarter-wave section the leftover reflection has a closed form.

3. \(|\Gamma|^2 = (Z_L - Z_0)^2/[(Z_L - Z_0)^2 + 4Z_0Z_L\sec^2\theta]\). The deck quotes, from evaluating these formulas on a fine grid of \(f\), 0.948–1.037 (8.9%) for this stub and 0.965–1.037 (7.3%) for the other; part 4's 0.322 at \(0.9f_0\) is outside the first band, as 0.9 < 0.948 says it must be ✓.

Rung 1

Each part is a few powers in watts. At one frequency the line and load are one impedance \(Z_{\mathrm{in}}\) at the source's terminals.

Rung 2

\(|V_{\mathrm{th}}| = \sqrt{4\times50\times100} = 141.4\) V. \(Z_{\mathrm{in}}\) is 12.5 Ω at \(\lambda/2\), 200 Ω at \(\lambda/4\), \(23.53 + 44.12i\ \Omega\) at \(\lambda/8\). Then \(I = V_{\mathrm{th}}/(R_s + Z_{\mathrm{in}})\).

Idea. With a matched source there are only two waves, and the load's share is fixed by \(\Gamma_L\). With an unmatched source the returning wave is partly sent out again, and how it adds to the original depends on the round-trip phase, hence on the length.

1. Forward 100 W; delivered \(100(1 - 0.36) = 64\) W; reflected 36 W. The reflected wave reaches the source, which has \(\Gamma_s = 0\) and sends none of it back: the transmitter absorbs it. How a real output stage disposes of it is not something the model says.

2. \(\lambda/2\): \(Z_{\mathrm{in}} = 12.5\ \Omega\), \(|I| = 2.263\) A, source resistor \(2.263^2\times50 = 256\) W, load \(2.263^2\times12.5 = 64\) W, EMF supplies \(141.4\times2.263 = 320\) W \(= 256 + 64\) ✓. \(\lambda/4\): \(Z_{\mathrm{in}} = 200\ \Omega\), \(|I| = 141.4/250 = 0.566\) A, source resistor 16 W, load (all of \(Z_{\mathrm{in}}\)'s power, the line being lossless) \(0.566^2\times200 = 64\) W, EMF \(141.4\times0.566 = 80\) W ✓. P12 Proof: the admittance chart is a half-turn routine

  1. Prove that \(\Gamma(1/z) = -\Gamma(z)\), and deduce that the admittance grid is the impedance grid turned through \(180°\).
  2. Find \(\Gamma\) for \(z = 0.5 + 0.5i\) and for its admittance, and check that they are opposite.
  3. Show that the admittance chart's \(g = 1\) circle is \(|\Gamma + \tfrac12| = \tfrac12\), and that it passes through the centre.
  4. A quarter wavelength of line is also a half-turn. What does a quarter-wave line do to an admittance chart reading?
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Part 1 is one line of algebra. Part 3 rotates P5's \(r = 1\) circle.

Rung 2

\(\Gamma(1/z) = (1/z - 1)/(1/z + 1)\); multiply top and bottom by \(z\). The point carrying \(y\) on the admittance grid is where \(z\)'s \(\Gamma\) is.

Rung 3

First line: \(\Gamma(1/z) = (1 - z)/(1 + z) = -(z - 1)/(z + 1)\).

Worked solution

Idea. Inversion is the half-turn \(\Gamma \mapsto -\Gamma\); so reading admittances instead of impedances is turning the labels, not the points.

1. \(\Gamma(1/z) = (1 - z)/(1 + z) = -\Gamma(z)\). A load with impedance \(z\) sits at \(\Gamma(z)\). Its admittance \(y = 1/z\) is the impedance label found at \(\Gamma(y) = -\Gamma(z)\) on the impedance chart. So to label the point \(\Gamma(z)\) with \(y\), take the impedance grid and turn it by \(180°\) about the centre. \(\square\)

2. \(\Gamma(0.5 + 0.5i) = (-0.5 + 0.5i)/(1.5 + 0.5i) = -0.2 + 0.4i\). \(y = 1/(0.5 + 0.5i) = 1 - i\), \(\Gamma(1 - i) = -i/(2 - i) = 0.2 - 0.4i\) ✓.

3. P5: the impedance chart's \(r = 1\) circle is \(|\Gamma - \tfrac12| = \tfrac12\); turned by \(180°\) (\(\Gamma \to -\Gamma\)) it is \(|\Gamma + \tfrac12| = \tfrac12\). \(\Gamma = 0\) is on it: \(|\tfrac12| = \tfrac12\) ✓ — the match has \(g = 1\), as it must.

4. A quarter wavelength of \(Z_0\) line turns \(\Gamma\) by \(\pi\), so it sends \(z \mapsto 1/z\): the impedance at its input equals, in normalised value, the admittance at its output. Read on the admittance chart, the point after the turn shows \(y_{\mathrm{in}} = z_L\).

Check. \(z = 0.25\): \(\Gamma = -0.6\); \(y = 4\): \(\Gamma(4) = 3/5 = 0.6\) ✓, opposite. Dimensionless ✓.
