The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: one copper, three sizes. Copper, \(\rho = 1.68\times10^{-8}\
\Omega\,\mathrm m\) (CRC), has skin depth 9.23 mm at 50 Hz and 0.206 mm at
100 kHz. Unit 00's kettle cable (2.5 mm², radius 0.892 mm) at 50 Hz has
\(R_{\mathrm{ac}}/R_{\mathrm{dc}} = 1 + 1.8\times10^{-6}\). A copper bar 100 mm
across at 50 Hz: \(R_{\mathrm{dc}} = 2.14\), \(R_{\mathrm{ac}} = 6.37\
\mu\Omega/\mathrm m\); a tube of the same outside diameter with a 14.5 mm wall,
\(5.91\ \mu\Omega/\mathrm m\) with half the copper. A charging coil's 2 mm
conductor at 150 kHz (assumed): ratio 3.23 solid, 1.0002 per strand as 400
strands of 0.1 mm.

* The running example

- skin effect :: The concentration of an alternating current near the surface
  of a conductor, because the changing magnetic field inside the conductor
  induces an electric field that opposes the current in the middle and aids it
  near the surface. Its length scale is the skin depth. /Slide: One copper,
  three sizes./

* The field diffuses into the metal

- eddy currents :: Currents driven inside a conductor by Faraday's law, round a
  changing magnetic flux, and turned into current by Ohm's law. Unit 07 met them
  in transformer steel; in a wire carrying alternating current the conductor's
  own field drives them. /Slide: The half unit 00 left out./
- good conductor :: A conductor in which, at the frequency of interest, the
  displacement current is negligible against the conduction current:
  \(\omega\varepsilon_0/\sigma \ll 1\). Copper: \(4.7\times10^{-17}\) at 50 Hz,
  \(2.2\times10^{-9}\) at 2.4 GHz. /Slide: Inside a metal, the displacement
  current is nothing./
- diffusion equation :: Inside a uniform good conductor, \(\partial\vec
  B/\partial t = D\nabla^2\vec B\) with \(D = 1/\mu\sigma\), and the same for
  \(\vec E\) and \(\vec J\): the heat equation of MR 18, obtained by curling
  Ampère's law without displacement current and using Faraday's law and
  \(\nabla\cdot\vec B = 0\). /Slide: The diffusion equation./
- magnetic diffusivity :: The coefficient \(D = 1/\mu\sigma\) of the diffusion
  equation, in \(\mathrm{m^2/s}\). Copper: \(1.34\times10^{-2}\
  \mathrm{m^2/s}\). In a time \(t\) a field soaks a distance of order
  \(\sqrt{Dt}\) into the metal.
- parabolic :: MR 18's type for a second-order equation whose principal part
  is degenerate in one direction, as the heat equation's is in time.
  Parabolic equations spread a disturbance everywhere at once; the diffusion
  equation is parabolic because dropping the displacement current dropped
  retardation. /Slide: Parabolic: what the approximation threw away./

* Into a flat face: the skin depth

- skin depth :: \(\delta = \sqrt{2/\omega\mu\sigma} = \sqrt{\rho/\pi f\mu}\): the
  depth over which an alternating field and its current fall by a factor
  \(e\) inside a good conductor, while their phase falls behind by one radian.
  Copper: 9.23 mm at 50 Hz, 0.206 mm at 100 kHz. /Slide: A field at a flat
  face./
- surface resistance :: \(R_s = 1/\sigma\delta = \rho/\delta\), in ohms per
  square: the resistance of a conductor many skin depths thick to an
  alternating current along its face. Exactly that of the same current spread
  uniformly through a layer \(\delta\) thick (the surface-layer theorem).
  /Slide: The surface-layer theorem./
- surface impedance :: \(Z_s = E_{\parallel}/H_{\parallel}\) at the face of a
  conductor; for a thick good conductor \(Z_s = (1+i)R_s\). Its real part is the
  power entering per unit area per \(|H|^2\); its imaginary part is
  \(2\omega\) times the mean magnetic energy stored in the skin, per unit
  area and per \(|H|^2\). /Slide: Energy check: the
  Poynting flux into the skin./

* A round wire

- internal impedance :: For a long round wire, \(Z_i = E_z(a)/I\) per metre:
  the axial field at the surface over the current. Its product with
  \(|I|^2\) is the complex power entering the wire through its surface. For
  radius \(a\), \(Z_i = (k\rho/2\pi a)\,J_0(ka)/J_1(ka)\) with \(k =
  (1-i)/\delta\). /Slide: Internal impedance: the surface field over the
  current./
- AC resistance :: The real part of the internal impedance, \(R_{\mathrm{ac}}\).
  For \(a \ll \delta\), \(R_{\mathrm{dc}}\big(1 + (a/\delta)^4/48\big)\); for \(a
  \gg \delta\), \(R_{\mathrm{dc}}\big(a/2\delta + \tfrac14\big)\), rising as
  \(\sqrt f\).
- proximity effect :: The redistribution of current in a conductor by the
  magnetic field of neighbouring conductors' currents: with opposite currents
  the current crowds onto the facing sides, with equal currents onto the far
  sides; either way the AC resistance rises. Cited from the CDA's /Copper for
  Busbars/ and Sullivan (1999). /Slide: Neighbours: the proximity effect./

* The two numbers, read as hardware

- busbar :: A rigid conductor — bar, rod, tube or strip — carrying the current
  of a substation or switchboard between its pieces of equipment. At 50 Hz a
  copper tube with a wall near \(\pi\delta/2\) beats a solid bar of the same
  outside diameter. /Slide: A 100 mm copper bar at 50 Hz./
- aluminium conductor steel reinforced (ACSR) :: A stranded overhead-line
  conductor with zinc-coated steel wires at its centre and aluminium wires in
  concentric layers round them (EN 50182). The aluminium carries the current,
  the steel the tension. /Slide: Overhead lines: aluminium round a steel core./
- Litz wire :: A conductor of many individually insulated strands, each thinner
  than the skin depth, twisted or woven together (Sullivan 1999). Used for
  coils at high frequency, such as unit 15's charging coils. /Slide: Twisted,
  so every strand takes every place./
- transposition :: Moving each strand of a bundle through every position along
  its length, so that every strand links the same flux on average and carries
  the same share of the current.
