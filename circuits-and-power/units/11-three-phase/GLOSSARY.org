The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: unit 06's workshop on a 230/400 V four-wire supply from unit
07's street transformer, through 50 m of four-core 35 mm² copper (24.0 mΩ per
conductor). Assumed loads: balanced motors of 20 kW at power factor 0.7
(41.41 A per line), a 9.2 kW kiln on a (40.0 A) and 2.3 kW of lighting on b
(10.0 A). Neutral current 36.06 A, found as \(3I_0\) and as \(I_a + I_b + I_c\).
The street transformer: delta on the 20 kV side, star on the 400 V side
(assumed), turns ratio 86.6 per limb, line currents 11.55 A and 577.4 A.

* Balanced three-phase sets

- balanced three-phase set :: Three phasors of equal magnitude a third of a
  turn apart: \(V_a = V\), \(V_b = \alpha^2V\), \(V_c = \alpha V\). Their sum
  is zero, at every instant. /Slide: Three sinusoids, a third of a cycle apart./
- phase sequence :: The order in which the three phases reach their peaks. This
  course fixes a, b, c, with b lagging a by \(120^\circ\); the other order is
  the negative sequence.
- operator α :: \(\alpha = e^{i2\pi/3}\), with \(\alpha^3 = 1\) and \(1 +
  \alpha + \alpha^2 = 0\). Engineers write it \(a\); this course writes
  \(\alpha\) to keep it apart from phase a.
- star connection (Y) :: Three sources or loads each joined at one end to a
  common star point. /Slide: Star: four wires, two voltages./
- neutral :: The conductor from the star point; in a European low-voltage
  supply it is earthed at the transformer.
- lines :: The three conductors other than the neutral, a, b and c.
- phase voltage :: In a star, the voltage from a line to the neutral: 230 V.
- line voltage :: The voltage between two lines: 400 V nominal,
  \(\sqrt3\times230 = 398.4\) V when the phase voltage is taken as 230 V.
- four-wire system :: Three lines and a neutral, offering both the phase and
  the line voltage: 230/400 V (IEC 60038, Table 1).
- delta connection (Δ) :: Three sources or loads on the sides of a triangle,
  each between two lines; no star point. /Slide: Delta: phase currents and line
  currents./
- phase current :: In a delta, the current in one element, \(I_{ab}\).
- line current :: The current in a line conductor. In a balanced delta,
  \(I_a = \sqrt3e^{-i\pi/6}I_{ab}\).
- per-phase equivalent :: A balanced star–star circuit solved as one phase,
  line to neutral; the two star points are at one potential and the neutral
  carries nothing. /Slide: The per-phase equivalent./

* Constant power

- constant power :: For a balanced three-phase load the total instantaneous
  power is \(3VI\cos\varphi\) at every instant: the three 100 Hz pulsations
  cancel. /Slide: Balanced three-phase power does not pulse./
- reactive power :: Unit 06's \(Q = VI\sin\varphi\). In a balanced
  three-phase load each phase keeps its \(Q/3\), but the stored energy passes
  from phase to phase and its total is constant. /Slide: Where the reactive
  power went./

* Fewer conductors

- 75% figure :: At equal power, length, loss and voltage between conductors, a
  three-wire three-phase line needs three quarters of the conductor of a
  two-wire single-phase line. /Slide: Three wires, 75% of the copper./

* A field that rotates

- rotating field :: Three coils with axes \(120^\circ\) apart, fed with a
  balanced set of currents, make at their centre the field \(\tfrac32\hat
  Be^{i\omega t}\): constant magnitude, turning at \(\omega\). Swapping two
  supply wires reverses it. /Slide: Three coils, one rotating field./

* Star, delta, and the street transformer

- star–delta transformation :: Unit 04's \(Y_{ab} = Y_aY_b/(Y_a + Y_b +
  Y_c)\), valid with complex admittances; for a balanced load \(Z_\Delta =
  3Z_Y\). /Slide: Star–delta with complex weights./
- limb :: One leg of a three-phase transformer's core, carrying one phase's
  two windings. /Slide: The street transformer's three limbs./

* Unbalance: symmetrical components

- symmetrical components :: The three sequence parts of any three phasors:
  \(I_0 = \tfrac13(I_a + I_b + I_c)\), \(I_1 = \tfrac13(I_a + \alpha I_b +
  \alpha^2I_c)\), \(I_2 = \tfrac13(I_a + \alpha^2I_b + \alpha I_c)\). Named
  after Fortescue (1918). /Slide: Three phasors are a vector in ℂ³./
- zero sequence :: Three equal phasors, \(I_0(1, 1, 1)\). The only part that
  returns through the neutral: \(I_N = 3I_0\).
- positive sequence :: A balanced a-b-c set, \(I_1(1, \alpha^2, \alpha)\). A
  balanced load draws nothing else.
- negative sequence :: A balanced set in the order a-c-b, \(I_2(1, \alpha,
  \alpha^2)\). Its field rotates backwards.
- discrete Fourier transform :: Here on \(\mathbb Z/3\): the matrix \(F_{jk} =
  \alpha^{-jk}\), with \(F^{-1} = \bar F/3\), taking sequence components to
  phase quantities.
- circulant matrix :: A matrix unchanged by relabelling a→b→c→a, \(c_0\mathbb 1
  + c_1P + c_2P^2\) with \(P\) the cyclic shift; the columns of \(F\) are its
  eigenvectors. /Slide: Symmetric networks are diagonal in the sequence basis./
- sequence impedances :: The eigenvalues \(Z_0, Z_1, Z_2\) of a circulant
  impedance matrix; for three lines with self impedance \(Z_s\) and mutual
  \(Z_m\), \(Z_0 = Z_s + 2Z_m\) and \(Z_1 = Z_2 = Z_s - Z_m\).
- sequence networks :: The three uncoupled single-phase networks, one per
  sequence, into which a symmetric network with an unbalanced load splits.
  /Slide: Sequence networks: three problems, uncoupled./

* The broken neutral

- feeder :: A distribution line from the street transformer along a street:
