The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: a pump motor, a three-phase induction motor with four poles,
star-connected to 400 V (230.9 V per phase), 50 Hz. Per phase, assumed:
\(R_1 = 0.5\ \Omega\), \(X_1 = 1.2\ \Omega\), \(R_2' = 0.45\ \Omega\),
\(X_2' = 1.2\ \Omega\), \(X_m = 30\ \Omega\) — "a pump motor of about 9 kW",
not any product's data. At slip 0.03 (1455 rpm): 57.7 N·m, 8.80 kW, 16.4 A at
power factor 0.834. Maximum torque 164.1 N·m at slip 0.187 (1219 rpm);
starting torque 66.1 N·m at 91.2 A.

* The running example

- stator :: The fixed part of an electric machine: a ring of laminated iron
  with the windings in slots round its inner surface. In the pump motor it
  carries three windings fed from the three phases. /Slide: A pump, and the
  field that turns it./
- rotor :: The turning part of a machine, inside the stator and separated
  from it by a narrow air gap; it drives the load through a shaft.

* Torque from co-energy

- lossless coupling :: A model of the part of a machine that only stores
  magnetic energy and passes energy to a moving part: a coil whose flux
  linkage \(\Lambda\) depends on its current \(i\) and on a position
  \(\theta\). Its energy balance is \(i\,\mathrm d\Lambda = \mathrm dW +
  T\,\mathrm d\theta\); resistance, iron loss and friction are drawn outside
  it. /Slide: A lossless coupling: one electrical port, one shaft./
- co-energy :: \(W'(i, \theta) = i\Lambda - W = \int_0^i\Lambda\,\mathrm
  di'\), the Legendre transform of the stored energy in its first variable.
  The torque is \(T = \partial W'/\partial\theta\) at constant current, equal
  to \(-\partial W/\partial\theta\) at constant flux linkage. For a linear
  coupling \(W' = \tfrac12L(\theta)i^2\), so \(T = \tfrac12i^2\,\mathrm
  dL/\mathrm d\theta\). /Slide: Co-energy, and torque at constant current./
- reluctance torque :: The torque on an unmagnetised iron rotor in a coil,
  from the dependence of the coil's inductance on the rotor's angle: for
  \(L = L_0 + L_2\cos2\theta\), \(T = -i^2L_2\sin2\theta\), always towards
  greater inductance. /Slide: An iron rotor in a coil: reluctance torque./
- aligned position :: The angle at which a reluctance rotor lies along the
  coil's axis and the inductance is greatest; the stable equilibrium at
  constant current.
- synchronous speed :: The one speed at which a machine's rotor turns in step
  with its supply: \(n_s = 120f/p\) rpm for \(p\) poles. A reluctance motor
  has a non-zero mean torque only there, so it cannot start by itself.
  /Slide: A reluctance motor runs only at synchronous speed./

* Back-EMF, and the one constant

- DC machine :: A machine with a fixed field and a turning armature whose
  connections are switched by a commutator, so that it runs from and makes
  direct current. /Slide: The commutator: a switch that keeps the torque
  one-signed./
- armature :: The winding in which a machine's working current flows and its
  EMF is induced; in a DC machine, the turning coils on the rotor.
- commutator :: A ring cut into segments on a DC machine's shaft, each joined
  to a coil end, that reverses each coil's connection to the terminals every
  half-turn, so that \(\partial\Lambda/\partial\theta\) keeps one sign.
- brushes :: The fixed sliding contacts that connect the commutator's
  segments to the terminals.
- back-EMF :: The voltage a turning machine generates, opposing the current
  that drives it as a motor: \(e = k\omega\) for a DC machine. It is why a
  motor's current falls as it speeds up. /Slide: e = kω and T = ki: one
  constant./
- machine constant :: \(k = \partial\Lambda/\partial\theta\) for an ideal DC
  machine, both the EMF per unit speed and the torque per unit current, in
  V·s/rad = N·m/A. That the two are one constant is energy conservation.

* The synchronous machine

- poles :: The north and south regions of a machine's air-gap field; they
  come in pairs, and a \(p\)-pole winding repeats its pattern \(p/2\) times
  round the bore, so its field turns at \(\omega/(p/2)\). /Slide: Unit 11's
  field, with more poles./
- synchronous machine :: A machine whose rotor carries a steady field (a
  field winding or magnets) and turns at exactly the speed of the stator's
  rotating field; as a generator, the machine this unit locks to the grid's
  frequency. /Slide: A rotor that locks to
  the field./
- field winding :: The rotor winding of a synchronous machine, fed with
  direct current \(i_f\), that makes the rotor's field; its current sets the
  EMF \(E\).
- synchronous reactance :: \(X_s = \omega(L_{aa} - M_{ab})\): the stator's
  own rotating field, seen by one phase as a reactance in series with the
  rotor's EMF. /Slide: An EMF behind a reactance./
- generator convention :: Marking a port so that its reference current
  leaves the + terminal, so that \(\mathrm{Re}(VI^*) > 0\) means power
  delivered. The opposite of the passive convention; used in this unit for
  the synchronous generator only.
- load angle :: \(\delta\), the angle of the EMF \(E\) ahead of the terminal
  voltage \(V\); physically, how far the rotor's field is ahead of where it
  runs at no load. Positive for a generator. /Slide: The phasor diagram, and
  the angle δ./
- power–angle characteristic :: \(P = 3EV\sin\delta/X_s\) for a round-rotor
  synchronous machine, with \(Q = 3(EV\cos\delta - V^2)/X_s\); greatest at
  \(\delta = 90°\). Unit 13's \(P(\delta)\). /Slide: The power–angle curve./
- over-excited :: A synchronous machine whose field current is high enough
  that it supplies reactive power (\(Q > 0\)), like a capacitor bank. /Slide:
  The field current sets the reactive power./
- under-excited :: A synchronous machine whose field current is low enough
  that it absorbs reactive power (\(Q < 0\)).
- turbo-generator :: A synchronous generator driven by a steam turbine, a
  fast machine, so it needs few poles; with two poles on 50 Hz it turns at
  3000 rpm. /Slide: A fast generator and a slow one, on one clock./
- hydro generator :: A synchronous generator driven by a water turbine, a
  slow machine, so it needs many poles to make the same 50 Hz.

* The induction motor

- induction motor :: A machine whose rotor carries no supplied current: the
  stator's rotating field induces currents in it, which requires the rotor to
  turn slower than the field. /Slide: A cage in the rotating field must
  slip./
- squirrel cage :: An induction motor's rotor winding: bars in slots, shorted
  together by a ring at each end.
- slip :: \(s = (n_s - n)/n_s\), the fraction by which the rotor falls behind
  the field; the rotor's currents alternate at \(sf\). About 3% for the pump
  motor at full load.
- referred :: Of a rotor quantity: replaced by its equivalent seen from the
  stator through the turns ratio, as in unit 07 — voltages times \(n\),
  currents over \(n\), impedances times \(n^2\) — and marked with a prime.
  /Slide: The rotor as a transformer secondary at frequency sf./
- air-gap power :: \(P_{\mathrm{ag}} = 3|I_2'|^2R_2'/s\), the power crossing
  from stator to rotor; a fraction \(s\) of it heats the rotor and \(1 - s\)
  leaves by the shaft, and the torque is \(P_{\mathrm{ag}}/\omega_s\).
  /Slide: Air-gap power: s to the rotor's copper, 1 − s to the shaft./
- torque–speed curve :: An induction motor's torque against its speed (or
  slip), from the Thévenin form \(T = 3V_{\mathrm{th}}^2(R_2'/s)/
  (\omega_s[(R_{\mathrm{th}} + R_2'/s)^2 + (X_{\mathrm{th}} + X_2')^2])\).
  /Slide: The torque–slip curve./
- starting torque :: An induction motor's torque at standstill, \(s = 1\):
  66.1 N·m for the pump motor, at 91.2 A.
- pull-out torque :: An induction motor's maximum torque, reached where
  \(R_2'/s = |Z_{\mathrm{th}} + iX_2'|\); a load above it stalls the motor.
  It does not depend on \(R_2'\). 164.1 N·m at slip 0.187 for the pump motor.
- stable operating point :: A speed at which motor and load torques balance
  and \(\mathrm d(T - T_{\mathrm{load}})/\mathrm d\omega < 0\), so a small
  disturbance dies away; for an induction motor, on the low-slip side of the
  maximum. /Slide: Which side of the maximum is stable./
