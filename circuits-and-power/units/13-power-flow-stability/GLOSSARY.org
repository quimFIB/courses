The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: Iberia and the rest of Europe as a textbook two-area model,
every number assumed. Iberia is one machine of inertia constant \(H = 4\) s on
50 GW, exporting 2000 MW over one equivalent tie of \(40\ \Omega\) per phase at
400 kV to Continental Europe, taken as an infinite bus: \(P_{\max} = 4000\) MW,
\(\delta_0 = 30°\), 535.9 Mvar supplied to the tie by each end. Its swing
against Europe has a period of 3.8 s; a fault that cuts the transfer to zero
may last 1.05 s, to the critical clearing angle 79.56°. For frequency,
Continental Europe is one machine of 300 GW with \(H = 5\) s, and losing the
regulation's 3000 MW reference incident gives a 0.75 Hz dip in a
simple model whose reserve arrives along an assumed ramp.

* The running example

- infinite bus :: A bus whose voltage and frequency nothing connected to it can
  change: the model of a system so much larger than what it is joined to that
  its own response can be neglected. Here, Continental Europe as seen from
  Iberia. /Slide: Two areas, one tie, and three questions./

* Two buses and a reactance

- per-unit system, per unit, pu :: Every quantity divided by a base of the same
  kind: one base power \(S_b\) for the whole system and a base voltage \(V_b\)
  per voltage level, with \(I_b = S_b/\sqrt3V_b\) and \(Z_b = V_b^2/S_b\). With
  bases in an ideal transformer's ratio, the transformer is a plain wire in
  per unit. /Slide: Per unit, and a transformer that disappears./
- base impedance :: \(Z_b = V_b^2/S_b\), the impedance against which a
  per-unit impedance is measured. A nameplate's percentage impedance, such as
  the street transformer's 4%, is a per-unit impedance on the device's own
  rating.
- decoupling :: To first order about the unloaded state, real power depends
  only on the phase difference between buses, \(P \approx V_1V_2\delta/X\), and
  reactive power only on the magnitude difference, \(Q_1 \approx
  V_1(V_1 - V_2)/X\). /Slide: Real power follows angle; reactive power follows
  voltage./
- power-angle curve :: \(P(\delta) = P_{\max}\sin\delta\), the real power two
  buses exchange through a lossless reactance as a function of the angle
  between them. /Slide: The static limit, and the two crossings./
- static stability limit :: The top of the power-angle curve,
  \(P_{\max} = V_1V_2/X\) at \(\delta = 90°\): no operating point exists for a
  larger transfer.
- nose curve :: The receiving voltage against delivered power for a load fed
  through a reactance from a stiff source. It has two branches meeting at a
  maximum power, \(\mathcal E^2/2X\) for a unity power factor load; the upper
  branch is the operating one. /Slide: The nose: where voltage collapses./
- voltage stability :: The study of whether loads' voltages can be held as
  demand rises, whose limiting case is the nose of the nose curve (voltage
  collapse). Named only; Kundur's chapter "Voltage Stability".

* Many buses

- bus :: A node of a power network at which generators, loads and branches
  connect; its voltage is measured to the neutral, in the per-phase
  equivalent. /Slide: The bus admittance matrix is unit 04's Laplacian./
- bus admittance matrix :: \(Y_{\text{bus}} = A\operatorname{diag}(y_e)A^{\mathsf
  T}\), with \(I = Y_{\text{bus}}\bar V\) for injected currents and bus voltages:
  unit 04's weighted Laplacian with complex weights. For lossless branches
  without shunts \(Y_{\text{bus}} = -iB\), \(B\) a real Laplacian with weights
  \(1/X_e\).
- Kron reduction :: Unit 04's elimination of nodes with no injection by a Schur
  complement, applied to a grid's buses: the result is an equivalent network
  on the kept buses. /Slide: Kron reduction: a grid seen from a few buses./
- power-flow equations :: \(S_k = \bar V_k\sum_jY_{kj}^*\bar V_j^*\) at every bus
  \(k\): the conservation of complex power, bus by bus. For lossless lines each
  bus's \(P_k\) and \(Q_k\) is a sum of two-bus transfers, one per line.
  /Slide: The power-flow equations: complex power, bus by bus./
- slack bus :: The one bus whose voltage magnitude and angle are given
  (\(\delta = 0\), the reference) and whose \(P\) and \(Q\) are found: it takes up
  the balance. /Slide: Slack, PV and PQ: counting knowns against unknowns./
- PV bus :: A generator bus, at which real power \(P\) and voltage magnitude
  \(V\) are given; its angle and reactive power are found.
- PQ bus :: A load bus, at which \(P\) and \(Q\) are given; its voltage
  magnitude and angle are found.
- DC power flow :: The linearisation \(P = B\delta\) of the power-flow
  equations for flat voltages, small angles and lossless lines: unit 04's
  Kirchhoff problem with angles for potentials. Nothing to do with direct
  current. /Slide: The DC power flow is unit 04's Kirchhoff problem./

* Newton–Raphson

- mismatch :: The computed minus the specified bus powers, \(F(x)\), as a
  function of the unknown angles and voltage magnitudes; the power flow is
  solved when it is zero. /Slide: At a flat start, the Jacobian is $B$./
- flat start :: The initial guess every \(V = 1\) pu and every \(\delta = 0\).
  For lossless lines, the first Newton step from it gives the DC power flow's
  angles.
- Jacobian :: The matrix of partial derivatives of the mismatch with respect to
  the unknowns, which Newton's method inverts at each step. At a flat start it
  is block-diagonal, with blocks taken from \(B\).

* The swing equation

- synchronous machine :: A machine whose rotor carries a steady field and turns
  locked to the grid's frequency, inducing an EMF \(\mathcal E\) in the stator;
  modelled as \(\mathcal E\) behind a reactance \(X\), delivering
  \(\mathcal EV\sin\delta/X\) to a bus of voltage \(V\) (stated, cited from
  Fitzgerald, Kingsley and Umans, §5.4; derived in branch 12). /Slide: The
  synchronous machine, stated./
- rotor angle :: \(\delta\), the electrical angle by which a machine's EMF, and
  so its rotor, leads a reference turning at synchronous speed.
- inertia constant :: \(H = \tfrac12J\omega_{sm}^2/S\), the rotor's kinetic
  energy at synchronous speed divided by the machine's rating, in seconds.
  /Slide: Newton for the rotor: the swing equation./
- swing equation :: \((2H/\omega_s)\ddot\delta = P_m - P_e\) in per unit:
  Newton's law for the rotor, written in power and measured against the
  synchronously rotating reference. With \(P_e = P_{\max}\sin\delta\) it is MR
  02's pendulum with a constant torque.
- synchronism :: Running locked to the grid's frequency, the rotor angle
  staying bounded. A machine that loses it slips poles: its angle grows by
  \(2\pi\) again and again. /Slide: The tilted washboard, and the first
  integral./
- separatrix :: The orbit through the saddle of the swing equation's phase
  portrait, bounding the closed orbits round the stable equilibrium; a state
  inside it returns, a state outside slips poles. /Slide: The phase portrait:
  closed swings, and the separatrix./
- inter-area mode :: An oscillation of one group of machines against another
  across the network between them. In the two-area model its frequency is
  \(\sqrt{\omega_sP_{\max}\cos\delta_0/2H}/2\pi = 0.263\) Hz. /Slide: Small
  swings: Iberia against Europe, every 3.8 s./

* Equal areas

- equal-area criterion :: A machine faulted at \(\delta_0\) and cleared at
  \(\delta_c\) stays in step iff the accelerating area
  \(\int_{\delta_0}^{\delta_c}P_m\,\mathrm d\delta\) does not exceed the decelerating
  area \(\int_{\delta_c}^{\pi-\delta_0}(P_{\max}\sin\delta - P_m)\,\mathrm d\delta\): the
  swing equation's first integral, read off the power-angle curve. /Slide: A
  fault, and the equal-area criterion./
- critical clearing angle :: The clearing angle at which the two areas are
  equal: \(\cos\delta_c = (P_m/P_{\max})(\pi - 2\delta_0) + \cos(\pi - \delta_0)\),
  79.56° for \(\delta_0 = 30°\) and a fault that cuts the transfer to zero.
- critical clearing time :: The time the faulted rotor takes to reach the
  critical clearing angle; with zero transfer during the fault,
  \(t_c = \sqrt{4H(\delta_c - \delta_0)/\omega_sP_m}\). /Slide: Worked: 79.56°, and
  how long a fault may last./

* Frequency

- load damping :: The fall of load with frequency, \(D\,\Delta f\), mostly from
  motors, which helps to stop a frequency dip. /Slide: Every machine in step:
  one swing equation for a grid./
- reference incident :: The largest sudden power imbalance a synchronous area
  must be able to contain: 3000 MW in either direction for Continental Europe
  (Regulation (EU) 2017/1485, Art. 153). /Slide: The rule Europe is run to:
  the reference incident./
- frequency containment reserve, FCR :: Power held back and released
  automatically as frequency deviates; for
  Continental Europe fully activated within 30 s and at ±200 mHz (Regulation
  (EU) 2017/1485, Annex V).
- rate of change of frequency :: \(\mathrm d\Delta f/\mathrm dt\); just after
  losing \(\Delta P\) it is \(-f_0\Delta P/2HS\), 0.050 Hz/s for 3000 MW in 300 GW
  with \(H = 5\) s. /Slide: Worked: losing 3000 MW in 300 GW./
- droop :: A reserve's response proportional to the frequency deficit, up to
  its full amount. Sized for 3000 MW at 200 mHz, it settles the frequency
  0.2 Hz low. /Slide: Where it settles: droop, and the 200 mHz./
- frequency restoration reserve :: The slower reserve that brings the
  frequency back to nominal after droop has stopped its fall, within 15
  minutes for Continental Europe (Regulation (EU) 2017/1485, Annex III).
