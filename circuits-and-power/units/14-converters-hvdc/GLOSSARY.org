The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: INELFE, the France–Spain link between Baixas and Santa
Llogaia — 64.5 km underground, two 1000 MW links at \(\pm320\) kV DC on four
cables (Red Eléctrica de España, 2015; Prysmian Group, 2015). As an AC cable
(an assumed 400 kV cable of \(0.208\ \mu\)F/km rated 1500 A) it would spend
973 A, 65% of its rating, on charging current; the critical length is 99.4 km.
At the other end of the scale, a phone charger in a car, modelled as a buck
converter from 12 V to 5 V at 2 A (\(D = 0.4167\), \(\Delta I = 0.583\) A,
\(\Delta V = 6.6\) mV).

* The ideal switch

- ideal switch :: A two-terminal element with two states chosen from outside:
  on, \(v = 0\) whatever the current; off, \(i = 0\) whatever the voltage. Its
  power \(vi\) is zero at every instant, so it converts without loss.
  /Slide: The ideal switch: either v = 0 or i = 0./
- duty cycle :: The fraction \(D\) of each switching period \(T\) for which a
  switch is on. The switch node's average is then \(DV_{\text{in}}\).
  /Slide: Chop, then filter./
- switching loss :: The energy a real switch dissipates while changing state,
  when its voltage and current are both non-zero: \(\tfrac12VIt_c\) per
  transition in a linear model with crossover time \(t_c\), so \(VIt_cf\) at
  frequency \(f\). /Slide: Switching loss: \(\tfrac12 VIt_c\) per transition./

* The buck converter

- buck converter :: A switch from \(V_{\text{in}}\) to a node, a diode from
  ground to that node, and an \(LC\) filter to the load. In periodic steady
  state with continuous conduction its average output is \(DV_{\text{in}}\).
  /Slide: A switch, a diode, and unit 05's LC pair./
- freewheeling diode :: The diode that carries an inductor's current while the
  switch is off, so that the current is never interrupted; it turns on by
  itself.
- periodic steady state :: The state of a switched circuit in which every
  state variable — inductor currents, capacitor voltages — returns to its value
  after each period. /Slide: Volt-second balance: V_o = DV_in./
- volt-second balance :: In periodic steady state the average voltage across
  an inductor is zero, since \(\int_0^Tv_L\,\mathrm dt = L[i_L(T) - i_L(0)] =
  0\). Applied to the buck converter it gives \(\langle v_o\rangle =
  DV_{\text{in}}\).
- small-ripple approximation :: Treating a filtered quantity, such as the
  output voltage, as constant within one switching period; justified when its
  ripple is a small fraction of its value (0.13% for the charger).
- capacitor charge balance :: In periodic steady state the average current
  into a capacitor is zero; for the buck converter it makes the inductor's mean
  current equal the load current.
- continuous conduction :: Operation in which the inductor current never
  reaches zero, so the diode conducts for the whole off-interval. For the buck
  converter it holds while \(I_o > \Delta I/2\).
  /Slide: Continuous conduction, and two relatives./
- boost converter :: A rearrangement of switch, diode and inductor whose
  averaged output is \(V_{\text{in}}/(1 - D)\), above the input.
- buck–boost converter :: A rearrangement whose averaged output is
  \(-DV_{\text{in}}/(1 - D)\): either smaller or larger than the input, and
  reversed in sign.

* Making AC: PWM and inverters

- half-bridge :: Two switches in series across a DC source, exactly one on at a
  time, so that their midpoint sits at \(\pm V_{\text{dc}}/2\) relative to the
  midpoint of the source. One leg of an inverter.
  /Slide: The half-bridge: a leg that switches between two rails./
- pulse-width modulation (PWM) :: Varying a switch's duty cycle slowly so that
  the local average of the switched waveform follows a chosen signal, such as a
  sinusoid.
- modulation index :: The amplitude \(m\) of the reference, as a fraction of
  the carrier's range; the fundamental of the output is \(m\,V_{\text{dc}}/2\).
- inverter :: A circuit that makes AC from DC by switching.
- quasi-square wave :: A waveform of \(\pm V\) with notches of half-width
  \(\alpha\) at its zero crossings; its Fourier coefficients are
  \(b_n = (4V/n\pi)\cos n\alpha\) for odd \(n\).
  /Slide: One pulse per half-cycle: the quasi-square wave./
- carrier :: The fast triangular wave against which a PWM reference is
  compared; the switch changes state where the two cross.
  /Slide: Sine–triangle PWM: switching at the crossings./
- triplen harmonics :: Harmonics of order 3, 6, 9, …; identical in all three
  legs of an inverter whose legs are delayed copies of one waveform, so they
  cancel in every line-to-line voltage.
  /Slide: Three legs: the three-phase inverter./
- voltage-source converter :: A converter that makes, on its AC side, a
  voltage whose magnitude and phase its controller chooses; behind a reactance
  it sets real power with the phase and reactive power with the magnitude.
  INELFE's converters are of this kind (REE).
  /Slide: INELFE's converters: a voltage that chooses its phase./

* Currents that are not sinusoids

- distortion factor :: \(I_1/I\), the RMS of a current's fundamental over its
  total RMS: 0.900 for a square wave.
  /Slide: Only the fundamental carries power./
- displacement factor :: \(\cos\varphi_1\), the cosine of the phase between
  the voltage and the current's fundamental. With a sinusoidal voltage the
  power factor is the product of the two factors.

* Why the grid goes DC

- XLPE :: Cross-linked polyethylene, the insulation of the cables in this
  unit; its relative permittivity is taken as 2.4.
  /Slide: A cable is a long capacitor./
- charging current :: The current a line or cable draws on AC to charge its own
  capacitance each cycle, \(\omega C'V\) per unit length in the lumped
  estimate, a quarter-cycle ahead of the voltage.
  /Slide: The charging current uses up the rating./
- critical length :: The length at which an AC cable's charging current alone
  reaches its thermal rating, fed from one end: \(l_c = I_{\text{rated}}/\omega
  C'V\), 99.4 km for the assumed 400 kV cable.
  /Slide: Worked: the critical length, and INELFE as an AC cable./
- shunt reactor :: An inductor connected from a line to earth to absorb the
  reactive current of its capacitance. Equal reactors at both ends of a cable
  double its critical length.
  /Slide: Is the lumped estimate good here, and what compensation buys./
- converter station :: The installation at each end of a DC link that converts
  between AC and DC; INELFE's each have more than 5,400 power modules (REE).
  /Slide: What direct current removes, and what it costs./

* Generators without inertia

- grid-following :: Control of an inverter that measures the grid's phase and
  injects a set current in step with it. It contributes no inertia unless told
  to. /Slide: Grid-following, and synthetic inertia./
- phase-locked loop :: A feedback scheme that adjusts an internal oscillator
  until its phase matches a measured waveform's, reporting that phase and its
  frequency.
- synthetic inertia :: A term \(-K\,\mathrm df/\mathrm dt\) added to an
  inverter's power; \(K = 2HS/f_0\) mimics a machine of inertia \(H\) on rating
  \(S\): 200 MW per Hz/s for 5 s on 1 GW.
- grid-forming :: Control of an inverter as a voltage source behind a
  reactance whose angle obeys a swing equation chosen in software, so that its
  power responds at once to the grid's angle.
  /Slide: Grid-forming: a voltage with an angle of its own./
- virtual synchronous machine :: A grid-forming controller that integrates
  unit 13's swing equation with a chosen inertia \(H_v\) and damping \(D_v\).
