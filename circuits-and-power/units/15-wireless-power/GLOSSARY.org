The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: a phone on a charging pad, modelled as two identical coaxial
coils of \(N = 10\) turns concentrated at radius \(a = 20\) mm (winding radius
0.5 mm), \(L = 9.47\ \mu\)H, \(Q = 100\), driven at 140 kHz and tuned with
\(C = 136.5\) nF — all assumed. At a 5 mm gap, \(k = 0.399\), \(U = 39.9\) and
\(\eta_{\max} = 95.1\%\) at a load of \(3.32\ \Omega\); 5 W crosses the gap as a
Poynting flux concentrated in a ring over the windings. At 80 mm, \(U = 0.55\)
and \(\eta_{\max} = 6.6\%\).

* A loosely coupled two-port

- inductive power transfer :: Power passed from one coil to another through
  their mutual inductance alone, with no conductor between them: a source on
  one port of unit 07's coupled pair, a load on the other. /Slide: A charging
  pad, from nothing./
- Qi :: The Wireless Power Consortium's standard for charging phones by
  inductive power transfer. Its /Introduction/ (v1.3, 2021) gives an operating
  frequency typically 87–205 kHz, power profiles up to 5 W and up to 15 W, a
  50 mm transmitter coil and a 40 mm receiver coil, and a coupling coefficient
  typically 0.5 or below.
- ferrite :: A magnetic material that the WPC names as the external shield of
  Qi coils, with which coupling is highest. Named in this unit, not modelled:
  the model coils sit in air.

* Untuned: a coil that mostly sloshes

- tuned :: A coil with a capacitor in series, \(C = 1/\omega^2L\), so that the
  loop's reactance is zero at the drive frequency. Tuning the secondary raises
  the efficiency (it removes \(Q_2^2\) from the untuned formula); tuning the
  primary removes the reactive power the driver carries and leaves the
  efficiency unchanged. /Slide: What tuning buys./

* Tuned: MR 07's two coupled oscillators

- normal mode, mode :: A motion of the two tuned loops, lossless and free, in
  which both charges oscillate at one frequency with a fixed ratio: MR 07's
  generalised eigenvalue problem \(Kv = \omega^2\mathcal Lv\) with the
  inductance matrix as mass matrix. For identical coils, currents together at
  \(\omega_0/\sqrt{1 + k}\) and opposed at \(\omega_0/\sqrt{1 - k}\). /Slide:
  The inductance matrix is the mass matrix./
- figure of merit :: \(U = k\sqrt{Q_1Q_2} = \omega M/\sqrt{R_1R_2}\),
  dimensionless: the rate at which energy crosses between the coils against
  the rate at which each loses it. The tuned pair's best efficiency depends on
  it alone. /Slide: The figure of merit U./
- reflected resistance :: At resonance, the resistance the driver sees added
  to coil 1's own: \(\omega^2M^2/(R_2 + R_L) = U^2R_1R_2/(R_2 + R_L)\). Unit
  07's reflected impedance, made real by tuning. /Slide: At resonance: a real
  input, currents in quadrature./
- quadrature :: Two sinusoids a quarter-cycle (90°) apart. At resonance the
  secondary current lags the primary by exactly 90°, the phase at which the
  power across, \(\omega M|I_1||I_2|\sin\theta\), is largest for given current
  magnitudes.

* Where the energy crosses

- mid-plane :: The plane halfway between the two coils. Closed by a
  hemisphere at infinity on the phone's side, it encloses all of circuit 2,
  so everything the phone absorbs must cross it as a Poynting flux. /Slide: A
  closed surface between the coils./
- self term :: In the mean Poynting vector of the two coils' superposed
  fields, a product of one coil's \(\vec E\) with its own \(\vec H^*\). On the
  mid-plane each vanishes point by point: one coil's own field only stores
  energy and returns it.
- cross term :: The products \(\vec E_1\times\vec H_2^* + \vec E_2\times\vec
  H_1^*\) of one coil's field with the other's. Integrated over the mid-plane
  its real part is \(\omega M\operatorname{Im}(I_1I_2^*)\): it carries all the
  energy that crosses.

* The efficiency, and the one number

- maximum-efficiency load :: For the tuned pair, \(R_L = R_2\sqrt{1 + U^2}\),
  where \(\eta_{\max} = U^2/(1 + \sqrt{1 + U^2})^2\). Not the conjugate match,
  \(R_2(1 + U^2)\), which gives the most power from a given drive at
  \(\eta = U^2/2(2 + U^2) < \tfrac12\). /Slide: The maximum-efficiency
  theorem./
- coupled-mode theory :: A description of coupled resonators by their
  amplitudes, a coupling rate \(\kappa\) and decay rates \(\Gamma\), used by
  Kurs et al. (2007). Its dictionary to this unit: \(\kappa = \omega k/2\),
  \(\Gamma = \omega/2Q\), and \(\kappa/\sqrt{\Gamma_S\Gamma_D} = U\). /Slide:
  Kurs's optimum is the same statement./
- strong coupling :: Kurs et al.'s name for \(\kappa^2/(\Gamma_S\Gamma_D) >
  1\), that is \(U > 1\), where \(\eta_{\max}\) exceeds 17%.

* How k falls with distance

- dipole–dipole coupling :: The coupling of two loops far apart compared with
  their size, each seen by the other as a magnetic dipole: \(M \approx
  \mu_0\pi N^2a^4/2d^3\), so \(k \propto d^{-3}\). /Slide: Far apart: the
  leading term vanishes, and M falls as 1/d³./
- scale invariance :: Scaling every length of the coils and the gap by the
  same factor scales \(M\) and \(L\) alike and leaves \(k\) unchanged: \(k\)
  depends only on shape. \(Q\) does not scale this way. /Slide: Scale, and why
  metres need very high Q./
- Z-class, Z-classes :: SAE J2954's grouping of electric-vehicle wireless
  charging by the gap between the ground assembly and the vehicle assembly,
  which is set by the car's ground clearance. No gap values are quoted in this
  unit.
- self-resonant coil :: A coil that resonates with its own distributed
  capacitance, with no separate capacitor, as Kurs et al.'s copper helices did
  at 9.90 MHz. /Slide: Kurs's coils by U, and the near field./
- near field :: The region \(d \ll \lambda/2\pi\) round a source, where the
  quasi-static field dominates and coupling falls as \(d^{-3}\): 341 m at
  140 kHz, 4.82 m at 9.9 MHz. Beyond it the field radiates (branch 16).

* Hardware: Litz wire, and a scrap of foil

- skin depth :: \(\delta = \sqrt{2/\omega\mu\sigma}\), the depth to which
  alternating current penetrates a good conductor: 0.174 mm in copper and
  0.219 mm in aluminium at 140 kHz. Stated in unit 07, proved in branch 10.
  /Slide: Litz wire: keeping R, and so U./
- Litz wire :: Wire made of many thin insulated strands, each thinner than the
  skin depth, twisted so that each takes every position in the bundle and the
  strands share the current equally. Keeps a coil's AC resistance near its DC
  value, and so keeps \(Q\) and \(U\).
- proximity effect :: Extra AC loss in a conductor caused by the fields of
  neighbouring conductors — other strands, other turns. Named in this unit,
  not computed.
- foreign object :: A piece of metal on a charging pad that is not part of the
  receiver — a coin, a key, a scrap of foil — in which the pad's field drives
  eddy currents. A thin disc loses \(\sigma\omega^2\hat B^2\pi tR^4/16\) while
  that formula holds. /Slide: A scrap of foil: eddy currents in circles./
- reaction field :: The field the eddy currents in a conductor make, opposing
  the applied one. At a thin disc's centre it is \(\hat B\,tR/2\delta^2\), so
  the thin-disc loss formula needs \(tR \ll 2\delta^2\). /Slide: When the
  formula holds: tR ≪ 2δ²./
- foreign object detection, FOD :: A transmitter's means of noticing a
  foreign object before it heats. The WPC requires that foreign objects be
  detected, notes that a
  transmitter cannot by itself tell foreign objects from friendly metals, and
  does not define a single method. A scrap changes the pad coil's \(Q\), its
  resonance, and the power unaccounted for. /Slide: How the pad can notice./
- friendly metal :: The WPC's term, set against foreign objects: "metallic
  parts that are used in the construction of Power Transmitter and Power
  Receiver Products", usually shielded from the field. A transmitter cannot by
  itself tell a foreign object from a friendly metal that is insufficiently
  shielded.
