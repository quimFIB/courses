Wireless power: two coupled resonators, and the one number that decides the efficiency

the slides · the curriculum

Checkpoint. You derive \(\eta_{\max} = U^2/(1 + \sqrt{1 + U^2})^2\) for two coupled resonators and evaluate it: 17% at \(U = 1\), 82% at \(U = 10\).

Work each problem on paper first. Under it, Hints opens a ladder one rung at a time — each rung appears only once the one before it is open, and the last gives the first line of the working — and Worked solution has the whole thing: the idea, the working, then a check. Read the solution after your attempt, including when you got it right. F folds everything back; D switches dark and light.

Twelve problems, about four hours. P3 is the checkpoint's; P3, P4, P5 and P8 are proofs. Conventions as in the deck: RMS phasors with \(e^{+i\omega t}\); both coils marked by the passive convention, both reference currents entering the dotted terminals, so \(M \gt 0\) for coaxial coils wound the same way; complex power \(S = VI^*\); \(U = k\sqrt{Q_1Q_2} = \omega M/\sqrt{R_1R_2}\); the load ratio \(a = R_L/R_2\); \(\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-7}\) H/m.

The running example (all assumed): two identical coaxial coils, \(N = 10\) turns concentrated at radius \(a = 20\) mm, winding radius 0.5 mm, \(L = 9.471\ \mu\)H, \(Q = 100\), \(f = 140\) kHz (\(\omega = 8.7965\times10^5\) s⁻¹), \(\omega L = 8.331\ \Omega\), \(R = 0.08331\ \Omega\) per coil, tuning capacitors \(C = 136.5\) nF. Mutual inductance at gap \(d\) (from the deck's section 7): 5.006 μH at 3 mm, 3.776 at 5 mm, 2.225 at 10 mm, 0.988 at 20 mm, 0.2837 at 40 mm, 0.0520 at 80 mm, 0.00737 at 160 mm. Copper \(\rho = 1.68\times10^{-8}\ \Omega\) m, aluminium \(2.65\times10^{-8}\ \Omega\) m (CRC); aluminium's density \(2.70\times10^3\) kg/m³ (assumed, the usual handbook value) and \(c_p = 897\) J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ (NIST-JANAF, as in the deck).

The tuned pair, and the power across

P1 The tuned pair routine

exercises: unit 07's coupled-coil equations with tuning capacitors, the reflected resistance, quadrature, a number

Two coils \(L_1\), \(L_2\) with winding resistances \(R_1\), \(R_2\) and mutual inductance \(M\) each have a series capacitor resonating them at \(\omega_0\). A source \(V_s\) drives loop 1; loop 2 is closed through a resistor \(R_L\).

  1. Write both loops' voltage laws at \(\omega_0\). Solve for \(I_2/I_1\) and for \(Z_{\text{in}} = V_s/I_1\).
  2. Show that \(Z_{\text{in}}\) is real and that \(I_2\) lags \(I_1\) by exactly 90°, whatever \(R_L\).
  3. For the running example at 5 mm with \(R_L = 3.322\ \Omega\), find \(U\), \(|I_2/I_1|\), the reflected resistance and \(Z_{\text{in}}\).
  4. Repeat part 3 at 20 mm with \(R_L = 0.8732\ \Omega\). What do you notice about \(Z_{\text{in}}\) in both cases?
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Two linear equations in two phasors; then numbers. The capacitors are there to remove two terms.

Rung 2

Each loop's impedance is \(R_k + i\omega L_k + 1/(i\omega C_k)\) plus whatever else is in it; at \(\omega_0\) the reactances cancel. The coupling term in loop \(k\) is \(i\omega M\) times the other loop's current.

Rung 3

First line: \(0 = i\omega MI_1 + (R_2 + R_L)I_2\), so \(I_2 = -i\omega MI_1/(R_2 + R_L)\); put it in \(V_s = R_1I_1 + i\omega MI_2\).

Worked solution

Idea. At resonance each loop is purely resistive except for the coupling, so the coupling term is the only imaginary number left, and it carries a factor \(i\).

1. Loop 1: \(V_s = R_1I_1 + i\omega MI_2\). Loop 2: \(0 = i\omega MI_1 + (R_2 + R_L)I_2\). So

\[ \frac{I_2}{I_1} = \frac{-i\omega M}{R_2 + R_L},\qquad Z_{\text{in}} = R_1 + i\omega M\frac{I_2}{I_1} = R_1 + \frac{\omega^2M^2}{R_2 + R_L}. \]

2. \(\omega^2M^2/(R_2 + R_L)\) is a positive real number, so \(Z_{\text{in}}\) is real. \(I_2/I_1\) is a positive number times \(-i = e^{-i\pi/2}\): \(I_2\) lags by 90° for every \(R_L \gt 0\).

3. At 5 mm, \(\omega M = 8.7965\times10^5\times3.776\times10^{-6} = 3.3213\ \Omega\) and \(U = \omega M/R = 39.87\). With \(R_2 + R_L = 3.4056\ \Omega\): \(|I_2/I_1| = 3.3213/3.4056 = 0.975\); reflected resistance \(3.3213^2/3.4056 = 3.239\ \Omega\); \(Z_{\text{in}} = 0.0833 + 3.239 = 3.322\ \Omega\).

4. At 20 mm, \(\omega M = 0.8692\ \Omega\), \(U = 10.43\); \(R_2 + R_L = 0.9565\ \Omega\): \(|I_2/I_1| = 0.909\); reflected resistance \(0.7556/0.9565 = 0.790\ \Omega\); \(Z_{\text{in}} = 0.873\ \Omega\). In both cases \(Z_{\text{in}} = R_L\): the two loads chosen are \(R_2\sqrt{1 + U^2}\) (3.322 and 0.873 Ω), and P4 proves that at that load the input resistance is \(R_1\sqrt{1 + U^2}\), equal to it for identical coils.

Check. Units: \(\omega^2M^2/R\) is \(\Omega^2/\Omega\) ✓. Limits: \(R_L \to \infty\) gives \(I_2 \to 0\) and \(Z_{\text{in}} \to R_1\), coil 1 alone ✓; \(M \to 0\) the same ✓. Energy: \(Z_{\text{in}}|I_1|^2 = R_1|I_1|^2 + (R_2 + R_L)|I_2|^2\), since \(\omega^2M^2|I_1|^2/(R_2 + R_L) = (R_2 + R_L)|I_2|^2\) by part 1 ✓.

P2 Power across the gap routine

exercises: mean power from phasors and from waveforms, the phase that maximises it, when the flow reverses

Loop 2 contains coil 2 and any passive load, total impedance \(Z_2\), and is driven only through \(M\) by \(I_1\).

  1. From loop 2's voltage law, show that the mean power absorbed in loop 2 is \(\omega M|I_1||I_2|\sin\theta\), \(\theta = \arg I_1 - \arg I_2\).
  2. Derive the same mean from the waveforms: \(i_1 = \sqrt2|I_1|\cos\omega t\), \(i_2 = \sqrt2|I_2|\cos(\omega t - \theta)\), with coil 1 inducing \(-M\,\mathrm di_1/\mathrm dt\) round loop 2.
  3. For fixed \(|I_1|\) and \(|I_2|\), which \(\theta\) maximises the power, and why is fixing the magnitudes the fair comparison?
  4. Show that at \(\theta = 90°\) the instantaneous power \(p(t)\) never reverses. For the untuned pair at 20 mm, \(\theta = 134.56°\): for what fraction of each cycle does \(p\) run backwards?
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Part 1 is one line of algebra with a conjugate; part 2 a product-to-sum identity; part 4 an inequality on a sine.

Rung 2

Multiply \(0 = i\omega MI_1 + Z_2I_2\) by \(I_2^*\) and take real parts; the real part of a passive impedance times \(|I|^2\) is the power it absorbs (unit 06). For part 2, \(2\sin A\cos B = \sin(A + B) + \sin(A - B)\).

Rung 3

First line of part 4: \(p(t) = \omega M|I_1||I_2|\big(\sin(2\omega t - \theta) + \sin\theta\big)\), which is negative exactly when \(\sin(2\omega t - \theta) \lt -\sin\theta\).

Worked solution

Idea. The only thing that reaches loop 2 is the voltage coil 1 induces in it; the power is that voltage times loop 2's current, averaged.

1. \(Z_2|I_2|^2 = -i\omega MI_1I_2^*\). The real part of the left is the power absorbed in loop 2. On the right, \(\operatorname{Re}(-iz) = \operatorname{Im}z\), and \(I_1I_2^* = |I_1||I_2|e^{i\theta}\), so the power is \(\omega M|I_1||I_2|\sin\theta\).

2. \(-M\,\mathrm di_1/\mathrm dt = \sqrt2\omega M|I_1|\sin\omega t\), and

\[ p = 2\omega M|I_1||I_2|\sin\omega t\cos(\omega t - \theta) = \omega M|I_1||I_2|\big(\sin(2\omega t - \theta) + \sin\theta\big), \]

whose mean is \(\omega M|I_1||I_2|\sin\theta\) ✓.

3. \(\sin\theta \le 1\), with equality only at \(\theta = 90°\). The magnitudes fix the copper losses \(R_1|I_1|^2\) and \(R_2|I_2|^2\) — the price of carrying the power — so the right question is how much power a given price buys, and the answer is: the most at quadrature. Resonance puts the currents there (P1).

4. At \(\theta = 90°\), \(\cos(\omega t - 90°) = \sin\omega t\) and \(p = 2\omega M|I_1||I_2|\sin^2\omega t \ge 0\). At \(\theta = 134.56°\), \(\sin\theta = 0.7121\), and \(p \lt 0\) when \(\sin x \lt -0.7121\) with \(x = 2\omega t - \theta\), which holds on an interval of \(x\) of length \(\pi - 2\arcsin(0.7121) = 180° - 90.88° = 89.12°\) per \(360°\) of \(x\). The fraction is \(89.12/360 = 0.248\): about a quarter of each cycle. In general it is \((\theta - 90°)/180°\) for \(90° \le \theta \le 180°\).

Check. Units: \(\mathrm{s^{-1}\,H\,A^2} = \Omega\,\mathrm A^2 = \mathrm W\) ✓. Limits: \(\theta = 180°\) gives zero mean and a reversal half the time — pure slosh ✓; \(\theta = 90°\) gives zero reversal ✓. Compare unit 06's \(\varphi/\pi\) for a reactive load.

The one number

P3 Proof: \(\eta_{\max}\) and the optimal load medium

exercises: the checkpoint — deriving the efficiency, AM–GM, inverting for U, numbers

The tuned pair of P1, with \(U = \omega M/\sqrt{R_1R_2}\) and \(a = R_L/R_2\). Efficiency \(\eta\) is the power into \(R_L\) over the power the source delivers.

  1. Show that \(\eta(a) = \dfrac{aU^2}{(1 + a)(1 + a + U^2)}\).
  2. Prove that \(\eta\) is largest at \(a = \sqrt{1 + U^2}\), i.e. \(R_L = R_2\sqrt{1 + U^2}\), and that there \(\eta_{\max} = U^2/(1 + \sqrt{1 + U^2})^2\).
  3. Evaluate \(\eta_{\max}\) at \(U = 1\) and \(U = 10\).
  4. Invert: find \(U\) as a function of \(\eta_{\max}\), and the \(U\) needed for 40, 50, 80 and 90%.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Part 1 is a product of two fractions; part 2 a minimisation with no calculus needed; parts 3 and 4 numbers and a square root.

Rung 2

The input power divides in loop 1 between \(R_1\) and the reflected resistance, and what crosses divides in loop 2 between \(R_2\) and \(R_L\). For part 2, minimise \(1/\eta\); write \(s = \sqrt{1 + U^2}\).

Rung 3

First line of part 2: \((1 + a)(1 + a + U^2) = a^2 + (2 + U^2)a + s^2\), so \(1/\eta = (a + s^2/a + 2)/U^2 + 1\).

Worked solution

Idea. Two divisions of power, each a ratio of resistances; then AM–GM on a sum of a number and its reciprocal.

1. From P1, \(R_{\text{refl}} = \omega^2M^2/(R_2 + R_L) = U^2R_1R_2/(R_2(1 + a)) = U^2R_1/(1 + a)\). Loop 1 passes the share \(R_{\text{refl}}/(R_1 + R_{\text{refl}}) = U^2/(1 + a + U^2)\) across; loop 2 gives the share \(R_L/(R_2 + R_L) = a/(1 + a)\) to the load. The product is \(\eta(a)\).

2. With \(s^2 = 1 + U^2\), \((1 + a)(1 + a + U^2) = a^2 + 2a + 1 + U^2 + U^2a = a^2 + (2 + U^2)a + s^2\). Dividing by \(aU^2\):

\[ \frac1\eta = \frac{a + s^2/a + 2}{U^2} + 1 . \]

For \(a \gt 0\), \(a + s^2/a - 2s = (\sqrt a - s/\sqrt a)^2 \ge 0\), with equality iff \(a = s\). So

\[ \frac1\eta \ge \frac{2s + 2 + U^2}{U^2} = \frac{(1 + s)^2}{U^2}, \]

using \(U^2 = s^2 - 1\). Hence \(\eta \le U^2/(1 + s)^2\) for every load, with equality only at \(a = s\). Since \(U^2 = (s - 1)(s + 1)\), also \(\eta_{\max} = (s - 1)/(s + 1)\). \(\square\)

3. \(U = 1\): \(s = \sqrt2\), \(\eta_{\max} = (\sqrt2 - 1)/(\sqrt2 + 1) = (\sqrt2 - 1)^2 = 3 - 2\sqrt2 = 0.1716\), 17%. \(U = 10\): \(s = \sqrt{101} = 10.0499\), \(\eta_{\max} = 9.0499/11.0499 = 0.8190\), 82%.

4. From \(\eta = (s - 1)/(s + 1)\), \(s = (1 + \eta)/(1 - \eta)\) and \(U^2 = s^2 - 1 = \dfrac{(1 + \eta)^2 - (1 - \eta)^2}{(1 - \eta)^2} = \dfrac{4\eta}{(1 - \eta)^2}\), so

\[ U = \frac{2\sqrt\eta}{1 - \eta}: \quad 2.108,\ 2\sqrt2 = 2.828,\ 8.944,\ 18.97 \]

for 40, 50, 80 and 90%.

Check. Dimensionless throughout ✓. Limits: \(U \to 0\), \(s \approx 1 + U^2/2\), \(\eta \approx U^2/4\) ✓ (0.0025 at \(U = 0.1\), against the exact 0.00249); \(U \to \infty\), \(\eta \approx 1 - 2/U\) ✓. A variant: \(U = 3\), \(s = \sqrt{10}\), \(\eta = 2.1623/4.1623 = 0.519\). The optimum is not the conjugate match \(R_2(1 + U^2)\): P6 finds that there \(\eta \lt \tfrac12\).

P4 Proof: matched at both ends medium

exercises: the input resistance at the optimum, unequal coils, reciprocity

Let the two tuned coils differ: \(R_1 \ne R_2\), \(L_1 \ne L_2\), \(Q_1 \ne Q_2\), with \(U = \omega M/\sqrt{R_1R_2}\) and \(s = \sqrt{1 + U^2}\).

  1. Show that at the maximum-efficiency load \(R_L = sR_2\) the input resistance is \(R_{\text{in}} = sR_1\): seen from either end, the link looks like \(\sqrt{1 + U^2}\) times that end's coil resistance.
  2. Now drive coil 2 and load coil 1. Show that the best efficiency is the same \(U^2/(1 + s)^2\), reached at \(R_L = sR_1\). Which property of the coupled pair makes this true?
  3. Show that \(U = k\sqrt{Q_1Q_2}\) for unequal coils, and explain why \(U = kQ\) is right only when \(Q_1 = Q_2\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Three short identities. Nothing in P3's derivation assumed the coils were equal.

Rung 2

\(R_{\text{in}} = R_1 + \omega^2M^2/(R_2 + R_L)\) with \(\omega^2M^2 = U^2R_1R_2\). For part 2, swap the labels 1 and 2 in P3's derivation and see what changes; then ask what \(M\) is when the roles swap.

Rung 3

First line of part 1: \(R_{\text{in}} = R_1\Big(1 + \dfrac{U^2R_2}{R_2(1 + s)}\Big) = R_1\Big(1 + \dfrac{U^2}{1 + s}\Big)\), and \(U^2 = (s - 1)(s + 1)\).

Worked solution

Idea. \(U\) is symmetric in the two coils, and the efficiency depends on nothing else but the load ratio.

1. \(R_{\text{in}} = R_1 + U^2R_1R_2/(R_2 + sR_2) = R_1\big(1 + U^2/(1 + s)\big) = R_1(1 + s - 1) = sR_1\), using \(U^2/(1 + s) = s - 1\). So \(R_{\text{in}}/R_1 = R_L/R_2 = s\). \(\square\)

2. Driving coil 2 and loading coil 1, P3's derivation goes through with 1 and 2 swapped: the efficiency is \(a'U'^2/((1 + a')(1 + a' + U'^2))\) with \(a' = R_L/R_1\) and \(U' = \omega M_{21}/\sqrt{R_2R_1}\). Because the coupled pair's matrix is symmetric — \(M_{12} = M_{21}\), Neumann's formula, unit 07's two-port — \(U' = U\). So the best efficiency is the same \(U^2/(1 + s)^2\), at \(R_L = sR_1\). \(\square\)

3. \(k\sqrt{Q_1Q_2} = \dfrac{M}{\sqrt{L_1L_2}}\sqrt{\dfrac{\omega L_1}{R_1}\dfrac{\omega L_2}{R_2}} = \dfrac{\omega M}{\sqrt{R_1R_2}}\). It involves the geometric mean of the two \(Q\)'s; \(kQ\) is that mean only if \(Q_1 = Q_2\). A phone coil with \(Q = 50\) on a pad coil with \(Q = 200\) has the same \(U\) as two coils of \(Q = 100\); a careless \(kQ\) with either value is off by a factor of 2.

Check. With identical coils at 5 mm: \(R_{\text{in}} = R_L = 0.08331\times39.879 = 3.322\ \Omega\) ✓ (P1). Units: \(sR\) is ohms ✓. Limit \(U \to 0\): \(s \to 1\), \(R_{\text{in}} \to R_1\), the coupling reflects nothing ✓.

P5 Proof: what tuning buys hard

exercises: the untuned efficiency, AM–GM against a reactance, what each capacitor does, reactive power

Remove both capacitors. Loop 2 is \(Z_2 = R_2 + R_L + iX_2\) with \(X_2 = \omega L_2\); the source drives coil 1 directly.

  1. Show that \(\eta = \dfrac{\omega^2M^2R_L}{R_1|Z_2|^2 + \omega^2M^2(R_2 + R_L)}\).
  2. Prove that the best \(R_L\) is \(R_L^* = \sqrt{R_2^2 + X_2^2 + \omega^2M^2R_2/R_1}\) and that there \(\eta^* = U^2/\big(U^2 + 2 + 2\sqrt{1 + U^2 + Q_2^2}\big)\), \(Q_2 = X_2/R_2\).
  3. Show that a series capacitor in loop 2 tuned to \(\omega\) turns this into P3's \(\eta_{\max}\), and that a capacitor in loop 1 changes \(\eta\) not at all. What does the loop 1 capacitor change?
  4. Evaluate \(\eta^*\) and the drive's \(Q/P\) for the running example at 5, 20 and 40 mm.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

The same structure as P3 — a ratio, then AM–GM on \(x + C/x\) — with a reactance that a resistor cannot cancel.

Rung 2

\(|I_2|^2 = \omega^2M^2|I_1|^2/|Z_2|^2\), and the driver's real power is \(\operatorname{Re}Z_{\text{in}}|I_1|^2\) with \(\operatorname{Re}Z_{\text{in}} = R_1 + \omega^2M^2(R_2 + R_L)/|Z_2|^2\). In part 2 divide by \(R_1R_2\) at the end and recognise \(U^2\).

Rung 3

First line of part 2, with \(x = R_L\), \(W = \omega^2M^2\): \(1/\eta = \big(R_1x + C/x + 2R_1R_2 + W\big)/W\), \(C = R_1(R_2^2 + X_2^2) + WR_2\).

Worked solution

Idea. Without tuning, coil 2's own reactance sits in its loop, limiting its current; the best a resistor can do is match a magnitude. A capacitor in loop 2 removes that reactance; one in loop 1 touches only the reactive power.

1. \(I_2 = -i\omega MI_1/Z_2\), so \(R_L|I_2|^2 = \omega^2M^2R_L|I_1|^2/|Z_2|^2\). \(Z_{\text{in}} = R_1 + i\omega L_1 + \omega^2M^2/Z_2\), whose real part is \(R_1 + \omega^2M^2(R_2 + R_L)/|Z_2|^2\). Divide and clear \(|Z_2|^2\).

2. \(|Z_2|^2 = (R_2 + x)^2 + X_2^2\). The denominator over \(Wx\) is

\[ \frac1\eta = \frac{R_1x^2 + (2R_1R_2 + W)x + R_1(R_2^2 + X_2^2) + WR_2}{Wx} = \frac{R_1x + C/x + 2R_1R_2 + W}{W}. \]

By AM–GM \(R_1x + C/x \ge 2\sqrt{R_1C}\), equality iff \(x = \sqrt{C/R_1} = \sqrt{R_2^2 + X_2^2 + WR_2/R_1}\). Then \(1/\eta^* = (2\sqrt{R_1C} + 2R_1R_2 + W)/W\). Dividing top and bottom by \(R_1R_2\): \(W/(R_1R_2) = U^2\) and \(\sqrt{R_1C}/(R_1R_2) = \sqrt{R_1^2(R_2^2 + X_2^2) + WR_1R_2}/(R_1R_2) = \sqrt{1 + Q_2^2 + U^2}\). So \(\eta^* = U^2/(U^2 + 2 + 2\sqrt{1 + U^2 + Q_2^2})\). \(\square\)

3. A capacitor in loop 2 replaces \(X_2\) by \(\omega L_2 - 1/(\omega C_2) = 0\): \(Q_2 = 0\) in the formula, and \(\eta^* = U^2/(U^2 + 2 + 2s) = U^2/(1 + s)^2\), P3's result, at \(R_L^* = \sqrt{R_2^2 + U^2R_2^2} = sR_2\). A capacitor in loop 1 changes only \(\operatorname{Im}Z_{\text{in}}\); part 1's \(\eta\) contains \(R_1\), \(M\) and \(Z_2\) and nothing of loop 1's reactance. So \(\eta\) is unchanged. What changes is the reactive power: tuned, \(Z_{\text{in}}\) is real (if loop 2 is tuned too) and the driver carries none. \(\square\)

4. \(Q_2 = 100\). At 5 mm, \(U = 39.87\): \(\sqrt{1 + 1589.4 + 10\,000} = 107.66\), \(\eta^* = 1589.4/1806.7 = 0.880\); \(R_L^* = 8.97\ \Omega\); \(Z_{\text{in}} = 0.743 + 7.724i\ \Omega\), \(Q/P = 10.4\). At 20 mm, \(U = 10.43\): \(\eta^* = 108.86/311.96 = 0.349\); \(Z_{\text{in}} = 0.1287 + 8.2863i\), \(Q/P = 64.4\). At 40 mm, \(U = 2.996\): \(\eta^* = 8.974/(8.974 + 2 + 2\times100.05) = 0.043\); \(Z_{\text{in}} = 0.0870 + 8.327i\), \(Q/P = 95.7\). Tuned, P3 gives 0.951, 0.826, 0.519 with \(Q/P = 0\).

Check. Units: every term under the root is \(\Omega^2\) ✓. Limit \(X_2 \to 0\) gives P3 ✓. Limit \(Q_2 \gg U\): \(\eta^* \approx U^2/(U^2 + 2Q_2)\), which for equal coils is \(k^2Q/(k^2Q + 2)\) — one factor of \(Q\) where the tuned pair has two; at 20 mm, 0.353 against the exact 0.349 ✓.

P6 Maximum power against maximum efficiency medium

exercises: Thévenin at the secondary port, conjugate matching, energy balance, a number

The tuned pair, driven by an ideal voltage source \(V_s\).

  1. Find the Thévenin equivalent seen by \(R_L\): show \(R_{\text{th}} = R_2(1 + U^2)\) and \(|V_{\text{th}}| = \omega M|V_s|/R_1\).
  2. By unit 06's theorem the most power from a given \(V_s\) goes to \(R_L = R_{\text{th}}\). Show that there \(\eta = U^2/\big(2(2 + U^2)\big)\), below \(\tfrac12\) for every \(U\). Why is unit 04's "50% at the match" not the real link's efficiency?
  3. For the running example at 5 mm, deliver 5 W at each design point. Find \(R_L\), \(|I_1|\), \(|I_2|\), \(V_s\) and both coils' losses, and check the energy balance of each.
  4. With the efficient point's \(V_s\), what power would the conjugate-matched load draw?
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

A Thévenin source (open-circuit voltage, and impedance with the source shorted), then P3's \(\eta(a)\) at a particular \(a\), then a table.

Rung 2

Shorting \(V_s\) leaves loop 1 as \(R_1\) at resonance; seen from loop 2 through the coupling it is a reflected resistance \(\omega^2M^2/R_1\). For part 3 work backwards from \(|I_2| = \sqrt{5/R_L}\).

Rung 3

First line of part 2: \(a = 1 + U^2 = s^2\), so \(\eta = s^2U^2/\big((1 + s^2)(1 + s^2 + U^2)\big)\) and \(1 + s^2 + U^2 = 2s^2\).

Worked solution

Idea. The Thévenin resistance stands for the losses of two coils, so the matched load splits the drive between the load and those losses — and the losses land mostly in coil 1.

1. Open circuit: \(I_2 = 0\), \(I_1 = V_s/R_1\), and the voltage across the open terminals is what coil 1 induces, \(-i\omega MI_1\): \(|V_{\text{th}}| = \omega M|V_s|/R_1\). With \(V_s\) shorted, loop 2 sees \(R_2\) plus loop 1's \(R_1\) reflected, \(\omega^2M^2/R_1 = U^2R_2\): \(R_{\text{th}} = R_2(1 + U^2)\).

2. With \(a = s^2\), \(\eta = s^2U^2/(2s^2(1 + s^2)) = U^2/(2(2 + U^2))\) \(\lt \tfrac12\). Unit 04's ½ is the share of the equivalent's EMF power that reaches the load; but the real network's losses are \(R_1|I_1|^2 + R_2|I_2|^2\), not \(R_{\text{th}}|I_2|^2\), and the real driver supplies \(V_s\), not \(V_{\text{th}}\). The two agree only in the limit \(U \to \infty\).

2. \(K = \sigma t\omega\hat Br/2\); the centre field is \(\int_0^R\mu_0K\,\mathrm dr/(2r) = \mu_0\sigma t\omega\hat BR/4 = \hat B\,tR/(2\delta^2)\). It must be small against \(\hat B\). In aluminium at 140 kHz, \(\sigma = 3.774\times10^7\) S/m, \(\delta = 0.219\) mm, \(2\delta^2 = 9.59\times10^{-8}\) m². Small scrap: \(5\times10^{-8}/9.59\times10^{-8} = 0.52\); large: \(2\times10^{-7}/9.59\times10^{-8} = 2.09\), outside the range.

A coin is much thicker than \(\delta\): the field is largely kept out, its current flows in a surface layer, and its loss is less than the thin-disc formula would say — the falling side of the deck's \(P(t)\) curve.
