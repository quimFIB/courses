# Unit 01 — Determinism, and the one hypothesis it rests on

- part: Part A — the equation of motion, and the calculus it forces (data-trunk="a")
- part thesis: Newton's second law is a differential equation, and this part is one long answer to 'which equation, and how do you solve it?'. The mathematics arrives in five waves, each pulled in by a problem that stalls without it: dimensional analysis and quadrature for one-dimensional motion, qualitative theory for the equations with no formula, multivariable calculus for motion in space, line and flux integrals for work and gravity, and linear ODE theory plus series solutions for oscillators and orbits. It ends with the Kepler problem solved completely — shape and timetable — and the inclination of a real satellite predicted to within a degree.

## Physics
What 'Newton's laws determine the future' means as a mathematical claim. Norton's dome: a mass on a dome shaped so it can spontaneously start moving after an arbitrary delay, with no force applied and no hypothesis violated. Finite-time blow-up: an inverse-cube attractor reaching the origin in finite time, and what that does to global determinism.

## Mathematical tools
The phase-space reformulation: any m x'' = F(x, x', t) is a first-order system y' = f(y,t) on R^{2n}. Picard-Lindelof proved via the Banach fixed-point theorem on C^0 with a weighted sup norm, with the Lipschitz condition doing all the work. Gronwall's inequality and continuous dependence on initial data. Peano: existence without uniqueness. Maximal intervals of existence and blow-up.

## The worked example the slides are built around
Norton's dome in full: the surface h(r) = -(2/3g) r^{3/2} gives r'' = sqrt(r); exhibit the one-parameter family r(t) = (t-T)^4/144 for t >= T together with r = 0, verify every member solves the equation with identical initial data, and locate precisely which hypothesis fails — Lipschitz continuity at r = 0, since d/dr sqrt(r) diverges there.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Verifying the hypotheses where you care and breaking them where you do not. "Prove the pendulum is deterministic": verify local Lipschitz on bounded sets and conclude global existence. "Blow-up": show x' = x^2 escapes in finite time and m x'' = -k/x^3 reaches the origin in finite time, then decide whether either contradicts Picard-Lindelof.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You state and prove Picard-Lindelof, and exhibit Norton's dome's one-parameter solution family together with the exact hypothesis that fails — without appealing to physical intuition at any step.
