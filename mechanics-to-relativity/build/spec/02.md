# Unit 02 — Phase portraits: what you know when there is no formula

- part thesis: Newton's second law is a differential equation, and this part is one long answer to 'which equation, and how do you solve it?'. The mathematics arrives in five waves, each pulled in by a problem that stalls without it: dimensional analysis and quadrature for one-dimensional motion, qualitative theory for the equations with no formula, multivariable calculus for motion in space, line and flux integrals for work and gravity, and linear ODE theory plus series solutions for oscillators and orbits. It ends with the Kepler problem solved completely — shape and timetable — and the inclination of a real satellite predicted to within a degree.

## Physics
The honest position, stated early because everything later depends on accepting it: almost no equation of motion has a closed-form solution, and that is not a gap in the literature. What you get instead is the qualitative picture — how many equilibria, of what type, with which basins, and whether anything periodic exists. The pendulum's separatrix on the cylinder. Damped systems as gradient-like flows; conservative systems as flows preserving a function, which is the first hint of the symplectic structure of unit 14.

## Mathematical tools
Autonomous systems as vector fields; flows and the one-parameter group property. Equilibria, linearisation, and classification of planar linear systems by trace and determinant. Hartman-Grobman stated, with the sharp fact that it says nothing about centres. Lyapunov's direct method, with LaSalle's invariance principle for the degenerate case. Limit cycles; Poincare-Bendixson, stated with its essential reliance on the plane. The index of a vector field around a closed curve. Bifurcations — saddle-node, transcritical, pitchfork, Hopf — with normal forms.

## The worked example the slides are built around
The damped pendulum theta'' + gamma theta' + sin theta = 0. Draw the full cylinder phase portrait; classify the alternating saddles and spirals; construct V = (1/2) theta'^2 + (1 - cos theta) and use LaSalle to prove asymptotic stability of the down position — a proof linearisation alone cannot give. Then set gamma = 0 and watch the spiral become a centre, which is exactly the case Hartman-Grobman excludes.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Qualitative analysis where no formula exists, and the traps in linearisation. "When linearisation lies": for x' = -y - x^3, y' = x - y^3 show the linearisation predicts a centre while the origin is asymptotically stable, and give the Lyapunov function that proves it. "Van der Pol has a limit cycle": construct an explicit trapping region and apply Poincare-Bendixson to prove existence without computing the cycle.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
Given a planar nonlinear system you locate and classify every equilibrium, decide whether linearisation is conclusive at each, construct a Lyapunov function where it is not, and either prove or rule out a periodic orbit using Poincare-Bendixson together with the index theorem.
