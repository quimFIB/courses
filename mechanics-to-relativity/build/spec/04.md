# Unit 04 — Work, and the arrival of vector calculus

- directory: `units/04-work-vector-calculus/`
- part: Part A — the equation of motion, and the calculus it forces (data-trunk="a")
- part thesis: Newton's second law is a differential equation, and this part is one long answer to 'which equation, and how do you solve it?'. The mathematics arrives in five waves, each pulled in by a problem that stalls without it: dimensional analysis and quadrature for one-dimensional motion, qualitative theory for the equations with no formula, multivariable calculus for motion in space, line and flux integrals for work and gravity, and linear ODE theory plus series solutions for oscillators and orbits. It ends with the Kepler problem solved completely — shape and timetable — and the inclination of a real satellite predicted to within a degree.

## Physics
Work as a line integral; the work-energy theorem. Conservative forces and potential energy; energy conservation as a first integral in three dimensions. Why friction and magnetism both fall outside this frame, for opposite reasons. Potential landscapes; equilibrium and stability read off the Hessian.

## Mathematical tools
Line integrals of vector fields; the gradient and the fundamental theorem of line integrals — with the distinction planted quietly and once: the derivative is a covector and the gradient is what you get after using an inner product to convert it, which is index-raising in unit 26 and nothing else. Green's theorem in the plane and Stokes' theorem in R^3, proved for a rectangle with the patching argument sketched. The curl as the local obstruction to being a gradient; the Poincare lemma on a simply connected domain. Taylor in R^n, the Hessian and classification of nondegenerate critical points; the inverse and implicit function theorems by contraction mapping; Lagrange multipliers derived from the implicit function theorem rather than asserted.

## The worked example the slides are built around
Show the inverse-square field is conservative by constructing V = -GMm/r along a radial path, then confirm path-independence on a deliberately awkward path. Then the punctured-plane vortex F = (-y, x)/(x^2+y^2): compute curl F = 0 everywhere it is defined and the loop integral 2 pi around the origin, and state exactly what is being measured — the topology of the domain, not the field. The question is left open until unit 34.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Deciding conservativeness and producing either the potential or the offending loop. "Is it conservative?": five fields, three yes and two no, each requiring V explicitly or a closed loop with nonzero circulation. "Stability": for V(x,y) = x^3 - 3xy^2 classify every critical point by the Hessian and sketch the resulting flow.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
Given F on an open set U you decide whether it is conservative and defend the answer by producing V explicitly or exhibiting a loop with nonzero circulation — including at least one case where the answer depends on the shape of U rather than on F.
