# Unit 05 — Flux, Gauss, and gravity stops being a force between pairs

- part thesis: Newton's second law is a differential equation, and this part is one long answer to 'which equation, and how do you solve it?'. The mathematics arrives in five waves, each pulled in by a problem that stalls without it: dimensional analysis and quadrature for one-dimensional motion, qualitative theory for the equations with no formula, multivariable calculus for motion in space, line and flux integrals for work and gravity, and linear ODE theory plus series solutions for oscillators and orbits. It ends with the Kepler problem solved completely — shape and timetable — and the inclination of a real satellite predicted to within a degree.

## Physics
Newton's law of universal gravitation and the shell theorem, which cost Newton years. The gravitational field as an object in its own right; the field equation div g = -4 pi G rho, equivalently Laplacian Phi = 4 pi G rho. Tidal forces as the variation of the field across a body, and the tidal tensor d_i d_j Phi, trace-free in vacuum. Why the Moon raises two bulges and not one.

## Mathematical tools
Multiple integrals and Fubini; change of variables and the Jacobian; spherical and cylindrical coordinates, with the coordinate singularities identified. Surface integrals and flux. The divergence theorem, proved for a box with patching sketched. The Laplacian and harmonic functions; the mean-value property and the maximum principle. Solid angle, and why 1/r^2 is the unique power in three dimensions for which flux is radius-independent.

## The worked example the slides are built around
Prove the shell theorem twice: by brute integration over the sphere with the law of cosines, where the punchline hides in an algebraic cancellation, then by Gauss's flux law plus symmetry in three lines. Then compute Phi inside a uniform sphere and show a body dropped down a tunnel through the Earth executes simple harmonic motion with period 84.5 min, independent of where the tunnel's ends are.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Flux arguments where symmetry does the work, and the tidal tensor. "The trace-free tidal tensor": compute d_i d_j Phi for a point mass, show the trace vanishes and the eigenvalues are (-2, 1, 1) GM/r^3, and deduce the two tidal bulges from the eigenvectors — this object is the Newtonian limit of the Riemann tensor and unit 33 will say so. "Where Gauss fails": a non-symmetric distribution for which Gauss's law is true but useless, with a precise statement of what symmetry buys.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You prove the shell theorem by Gauss's law in under half a page, and compute the tidal tensor of a point mass, showing it is trace-free and reading its two bulges off its eigenvectors.
