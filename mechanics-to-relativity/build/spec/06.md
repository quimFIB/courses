# Unit 06 — The damped driven oscillator, which is most of linear physics

- directory: `units/06-driven-oscillator/`
- part: Part A — the equation of motion, and the calculus it forces (data-trunk="a")
- part thesis: Newton's second law is a differential equation, and this part is one long answer to 'which equation, and how do you solve it?'. The mathematics arrives in five waves, each pulled in by a problem that stalls without it: dimensional analysis and quadrature for one-dimensional motion, qualitative theory for the equations with no formula, multivariable calculus for motion in space, line and flux integrals for work and gravity, and linear ODE theory plus series solutions for oscillators and orbits. It ends with the Kepler problem solved completely — shape and timetable — and the inclination of a real satellite predicted to within a degree.

## Physics
Small oscillations about a stable equilibrium as the universal local model: Taylor-expand any V at a minimum and the leading term is (1/2) k x^2. Damping regimes and the quality factor Q. Sinusoidal driving; resonance, the phase lag sweeping through it, the half-power width. The impulse response, and the claim that a linear system's response to everything is its response to one thing. Causality as a constraint on the response function — stated here, cashed at unit 20.

## Mathematical tools
C and the complex exponential as computational infrastructure — algebra, not yet analysis. Linear ODEs with constant coefficients: characteristic polynomial, the solution space as a two-dimensional vector space (Picard-Lindelof from unit 01, not folklore), the Wronskian and Abel's identity, superposition, variation of parameters, resonance as the failure of the particular-solution ansatz. The Green's function of L = d^2/dt^2 + gamma d/dt + omega_0^2, built by matching a jump in the derivative across t = t': G(t) = exp(-gamma t/2) sin(omega_d t)/omega_d for t > 0 and 0 otherwise. Convolution and Duhamel's principle.

## The worked example the slides are built around
Solve x'' + gamma x' + omega_0^2 x = F_0 cos(omega t) completely: amplitude F_0/sqrt((omega_0^2-omega^2)^2 + gamma^2 omega^2), phase lag sweeping 0 to pi, peak at omega_0 sqrt(1 - gamma^2/2 omega_0^2), full width at half power gamma. Then build G(t) from its defining property and recover the identical steady state by convolution — the same answer twice by unrelated machinery.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Building and using Green's functions; regime classification. "Square-pulse drive": solve by convolution with G and separately by matching piecewise solutions at the pulse edges, and confirm they agree including at the corners. "Resonance is not at omega_0": show the amplitude response peaks at sqrt(omega_0^2 - 2 gamma^2) while the velocity response peaks exactly at omega_0, and say which a physicist means. "Critical damping is genuinely special": show gamma = 2 omega_0 gives the Jordan-block degeneracy t exp(-gamma t/2), and that it is the fastest return without overshoot.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You construct G(t - t') from its defining property, solve an arbitrary drive by convolution, verify against a directly computed piecewise solution, and can say why the solution space is two-dimensional by citing unit 01.
