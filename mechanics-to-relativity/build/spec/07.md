# Unit 07 — Many oscillators, and the spectral theorem earning its keep

- directory: `units/07-normal-modes/`
- part: Part A — the equation of motion, and the calculus it forces (data-trunk="a")
- part thesis: Newton's second law is a differential equation, and this part is one long answer to 'which equation, and how do you solve it?'. The mathematics arrives in five waves, each pulled in by a problem that stalls without it: dimensional analysis and quadrature for one-dimensional motion, qualitative theory for the equations with no formula, multivariable calculus for motion in space, line and flux integrals for work and gravity, and linear ODE theory plus series solutions for oscillators and orbits. It ends with the Kepler problem solved completely — shape and timetable — and the inclination of a real satellite predicted to within a degree.

## Physics
Two masses and three springs; beats as interference between two normal modes. Normal modes and normal coordinates: the change of basis in which a coupled system becomes n uncoupled oscillators. N masses in a line with fixed ends. Zero modes and the symmetries responsible for them. Why a system with n coordinates has exactly n modes.

## Mathematical tools
Linear systems x' = Ax; the matrix exponential; the general solution from eigendecomposition and the Jordan form for the degenerate case. The generalised eigenvalue problem K v = omega^2 M v with M symmetric positive definite: reduce by M^{1/2} to a symmetric problem, hence real omega^2 and M-orthogonal modes — the spectral theorem doing physical work, and the reason the naive 'diagonalise both' is false in general. Simultaneous diagonalisation of two quadratic forms when one is definite, with the counterexample when neither is. The Rayleigh quotient and the variational characterisation of the lowest frequency.

## The worked example the slides are built around
N equal masses and N+1 equal springs between fixed walls: write M and K, try v_j = sin(j k pi/(N+1)), verify, and obtain exactly omega_k = 2 sqrt(k/m) sin(k pi/2(N+1)). Plot omega_k against k for N = 20 and watch the curve bend away from the straight line. That bending is dispersion; the straight part is the sound wave unit 18 recovers in the continuum limit, and the leading correction -(ck)(ka)^2/24 is a rare chance to see a continuum theory's own error bar.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Modes of small systems and the M-inner product. "Beats": for two masses and three springs compute both modes and show displacing one mass alone makes energy slosh at the difference frequency. "Orthogonality by hand": for a chain with one heavy mass verify v_i^T M v_j = 0 numerically, show naive Euclidean orthogonality fails, and explain which inner product was right all along. "Rayleigh bounds a frequency": bound the lowest mode of a five-mass chain with a trial vector and compare with the exact answer.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
For any (M, K) with M symmetric positive definite you produce the normal frequencies and mode shapes, verify orthogonality in the M-inner product rather than the Euclidean one, identify every zero mode with the symmetry responsible, and can explain why complex frequencies cannot occur.
