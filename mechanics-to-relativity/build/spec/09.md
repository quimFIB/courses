# Unit 09 — Where is the planet at time t? Kepler's equation and the birth of Bessel functions

## Physics
The orbit's shape was easy; its timetable is not. The eccentric anomaly; Kepler's equation E - e sin E = M, transcendental and with no closed-form inverse — the problem that occupied celestial mechanics for two centuries and that every ephemeris still solves.

## Mathematical tools
Solution by series in a parameter. The Fourier-Bessel expansion E = M + 2 sum_n J_n(ne) sin(nM)/n, which is where Bessel functions were actually born. Bessel's equation x^2 y'' + x y' + (x^2 - n^2) y = 0; the Frobenius method at a regular singular point; the indicial equation, the two roots, and the logarithmic second solution when they differ by an integer. Recurrence relations, the generating function exp((x/2)(t - 1/t)), Bessel's integral representation, and the asymptotic sqrt(2/pi x) cos(x - n pi/2 - pi/4). Lagrange inversion as the general mechanism. The Laplace limit e = 0.6627434..., asserted here and proved in unit 20 — a debt deliberately incurred.

## The worked example the slides are built around
Derive the Bessel series for E(M) by computing the Fourier coefficients of E - M and recognising Bessel's integral representation. Then locate Mars (e = 0.0934) at a stated epoch to five significant figures using three terms, and cross-check against Newton's method applied directly to the transcendental equation, counting the iterations each route costs.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Frobenius practice and honest convergence. "Frobenius by hand": solve Bessel's equation of order zero by series, exhibit the logarithmic second solution, and observe that the method detects the obstruction on its own rather than being told about it. "Halley's comet": with e = 0.967, past the Laplace limit, show the series fails numerically and locate empirically where it begins to diverge.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You derive the Bessel-function solution of Kepler's equation from its Fourier coefficients and use it to place a planet to five digits — and you can state exactly where it diverges while owing unit 20 the reason.
