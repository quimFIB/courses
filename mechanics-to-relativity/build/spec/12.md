# Unit 12 — Lagrangian mechanics: coordinates stop mattering

- directory: `units/12-lagrangian/`
- part: Part B — the action, and coordinates ceasing to matter (data-trunk="b")
- part thesis: Nothing here says anything Newton did not, and that is the point: it is a change of language whose entire value is what it makes obvious. Extremising an integral gives the equations of motion; those equations are then invariant under any change of coordinates, which is the idea that ends the course as general covariance. Symmetry becomes conservation by one theorem. And the part quietly builds the two objects relativity needs — an action principle, and a real definition of a tensor, arriving with the inertia tensor because that is the first place nine numbers per basis will not do.

## Physics
Hamilton's principle with L = T - V. D'Alembert's principle and virtual work. Generalised coordinates; holonomic constraints eliminated by choosing coordinates rather than by computing constraint forces. The double pendulum. The bead on a rotating hoop and its bifurcation. Constraint forces recovered when wanted, via multipliers. Gauge freedom L -> L + df/dt. Velocity-dependent potentials, with the Lagrangian for a charge in an electromagnetic field written down now and justified in unit 24. Non-holonomic constraints, with an honest statement that their variational treatment is subtle and often done wrong.

## Mathematical tools
Proof that the Euler-Lagrange equations of L = T - V are equivalent to Newton's equations in Cartesian coordinates. Proof of the central fact: they are form-invariant under any invertible change of coordinates q -> Q(q,t) — the reason the formalism exists, and the first appearance of the idea that ends the course as general covariance. Generalised momenta and cyclic coordinates. Linearisation about equilibria, reconnecting with unit 07's generalised eigenvalue machinery unchanged. Configuration space as a manifold, informally here and precisely in unit 31.

## The worked example the slides are built around
The bead on a hoop rotating at Omega: derive L, read off V_eff, and find the pitchfork bifurcation at Omega^2 = g/R where the bottom of the hoop goes unstable and two equilibria appear at cos(theta) = g/R Omega^2 — three lines, against the page it takes from a force balance, and unit 02's normal form recognisable in it. Draw the phase portrait on each side. Then the double pendulum's L and its linearised normal modes.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Writing Lagrangians and exploiting invariance. "Same system, two coordinate systems": treat a spherical pendulum in (theta, phi) and again in Cartesian coordinates with a multiplier, and show the motions agree. "Gauge freedom is real": show L and L + df/dt give identical equations but different canonical momenta, and identify which physical quantity is therefore not gauge-invariant. "Atwood's machine with an elastic string": three degrees of freedom, and the point is that you never draw a free-body diagram or name a tension.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You write L for one system in two genuinely different coordinate systems, derive both sets of Euler-Lagrange equations, show they describe the same motion, and state the theorem that guaranteed this in advance.
