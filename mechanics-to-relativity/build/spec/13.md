# Unit 13 — Noether: every conservation law you have met is one theorem

- directory: `units/13-noether/`
- part: Part B — the action, and coordinates ceasing to matter (data-trunk="b")
- part thesis: Nothing here says anything Newton did not, and that is the point: it is a change of language whose entire value is what it makes obvious. Extremising an integral gives the equations of motion; those equations are then invariant under any change of coordinates, which is the idea that ends the course as general covariance. Symmetry becomes conservation by one theorem. And the part quietly builds the two objects relativity needs — an action principle, and a real definition of a tensor, arriving with the inertia tensor because that is the first place nine numbers per basis will not do.

## Physics
Momentum conservation is spatial homogeneity, angular momentum is isotropy, energy is invariance under time translation. The Galilean boost and its less familiar conserved charge. The Runge-Lenz vector of unit 08 finally explained as the charge of a symmetry that is not a symmetry of space at all — the hidden SO(4) of the Kepler problem. What the theorem does not say: the converse is false.

## Mathematical tools
One-parameter groups of transformations of configuration space and the induced variation of the action; the infinitesimal generator. Statement and complete proof of Noether's theorem for point mechanics — three lines, which this learner will appreciate seeing — including the quasi-invariant case where L changes by a total time derivative. Why the energy function h = sum q'_i dL/dq'_i - L is the conserved quantity, and equals T + V only when the constraints are time-independent: a distinction almost universally glossed over, and one this course needs later.

## The worked example the slides are built around
Prove Noether's theorem in full generality, then run it four times on the Kepler Lagrangian: time translation gives energy, rotation gives all three components of angular momentum, the Galilean boost q -> q + vt is quasi-invariant and gives G = pt - mq (the centre of mass moving uniformly, restated as a conservation law), and the SO(4) generator gives the Runge-Lenz vector.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Finding symmetries and their charges, including hidden ones and one careful counterexample. "Energy is not always T+V": unit 12's bead on the rotating hoop has a conserved h that is not the energy, and the motor driving the hoop does work — compute both and reconcile them. "Scaling symmetry of Kepler": show (t, r) -> (lambda^3 t, lambda^2 r) maps solutions to solutions and recognise Kepler's third law falling out of the resulting charge. "The converse fails": exhibit a conserved quantity arising from no point symmetry, and say what enlargement to phase space (unit 14) would rescue it.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
Given any Lagrangian and any one-parameter symmetry you write the conserved quantity and verify it is constant on-shell — including one quasi-invariant case where L itself is not invariant and the charge acquires the extra term.
