# Unit 15 — Hamilton-Jacobi: mechanics was optics all along, and free motion is already geodesic motion

- directory: `units/15-hamilton-jacobi/`
- part: Part B — the action, and coordinates ceasing to matter (data-trunk="b")
- part thesis: Nothing here says anything Newton did not, and that is the point: it is a change of language whose entire value is what it makes obvious. Extremising an integral gives the equations of motion; those equations are then invariant under any change of coordinates, which is the idea that ends the course as general covariance. Symmetry becomes conservation by one theorem. And the part quietly builds the two objects relativity needs — an action principle, and a real definition of a tensor, arriving with the inertia tensor because that is the first place nine numbers per basis will not do.

## Physics
Action as a function of its endpoints; solving a mechanical problem by solving one PDE and then differentiating. Action-angle variables: the frequencies of a bounded motion without ever solving for the motion. Adiabatic invariants via the slowly shortened pendulum. Then the two payoffs that make this the course's quiet centre. The optical-mechanical analogy — Fermat against Maupertuis, wavefronts against surfaces of constant S, rays against trajectories. And the Jacobi metric: a particle of energy E in a potential V moves on a *geodesic* of the conformally rescaled metric 2(E - V) delta_ij. Free motion is geodesic motion, in Newtonian physics, before any curvature exists. This is the branch that makes unit 32 feel inevitable rather than imposed, and the one I would fight hardest to keep.

## Mathematical tools
The Hamilton-Jacobi equation dS/dt + H(q, dS/dq, t) = 0, the course's first nonlinear first-order PDE. Complete integrals versus general solutions; Jacobi's theorem proved in the verification direction, which needs only the chain rule. Separability and Staeckel's conditions, stated. Action variables I_i as loop integrals of p dq over topological cycles, and the conjugate angles. The eikonal limit. Conformal rescaling of a metric.

## The worked example the slides are built around
Solve the Kepler problem by Hamilton-Jacobi separation in spherical coordinates: write S = -Et + L_z phi + Theta(theta) + R(r), separate, and read the orbit off dS/d(alpha) = constant without integrating an equation of motion. Compute the action variables, obtain H = -mk^2/2I^2 and hence the Kepler frequency by differentiation — and note that all three frequencies being degenerate is exactly why Kepler orbits close, which is Bertrand's theorem seen from above. Then write the Jacobi metric for the same problem and exhibit Kepler orbits as its geodesics.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Separation, invariants, and frequencies without trajectories. "Frequency without motion": for V = (1/2)k x^2 + lambda x^4 compute I(E) to first order in lambda, obtain the amplitude-dependent frequency shift, and check it against unit 21's Poincare-Lindstedt answer. "Adiabatic invariance": show I is conserved for a pendulum shortened slowly, compute the work done pulling the string through a hole, and confirm the two answers agree.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You solve a separable Hamilton-Jacobi problem, extract action-angle variables, produce the oscillation frequency of an anharmonic oscillator without ever integrating its equation of motion, and write the Jacobi metric for a given V and E.
