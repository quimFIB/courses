# Unit 16 — Rigid bodies, and what a tensor actually is

- directory: `units/16-rigid-bodies-tensors/`
- part: Part B — the action, and coordinates ceasing to matter (data-trunk="b")
- part thesis: Nothing here says anything Newton did not, and that is the point: it is a change of language whose entire value is what it makes obvious. Extremising an integral gives the equations of motion; those equations are then invariant under any change of coordinates, which is the idea that ends the course as general covariance. Symmetry becomes conservation by one theorem. And the part quietly builds the two objects relativity needs — an action principle, and a real definition of a tensor, arriving with the inertia tensor because that is the first place nine numbers per basis will not do.

## Physics
Rigid-body kinematics; the configuration space SO(3) x R^3. Angular momentum L = I omega, and the fact that L is generally not parallel to omega, which is the whole subject. Principal axes; the parallel-axis theorem; Euler's equations in the body frame; the free symmetric top and its two cones; the gyroscope; the intermediate-axis (tennis-racket) theorem. Euler angles and their coordinate singularities, which unit 31 will explain are a fact about SO(3) and not about carelessness.

## Mathematical tools
Multilinear maps as the definition of a tensor: a (0,2)-tensor is a bilinear map V x V -> R, and the transformation law is a consequence of a change of basis rather than a definition. (p,q)-tensors, tensor products, contraction, symmetric and antisymmetric parts. The dual space and why it is not canonically V — the covector-versus-gradient point planted in unit 04, now made structural, so that units 28 through 35 can raise and lower honestly. Index notation and the Einstein summation convention introduced properly. The spectral theorem producing principal axes. SO(3), so(3), and the exponential map.

## The worked example the slides are built around
Compute the inertia tensor of a uniform rectangular block about a corner, diagonalise it, verify the transformation law I' = R I R^T component by component under a 45-degree rotation, then linearise Euler's equations about each principal axis and prove the intermediate axis unstable with growth rate sqrt((I_2 - I_1)(I_3 - I_2)) omega / sqrt(I_1 I_3) — a prediction testable with a book and a rubber band, and checkable against the Dzhanibekov footage's flip period.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Tensor computations and rotational dynamics. "The transformation law is a theorem": compute I in two frames and verify I' = R I R^T, then exhibit a collection of nine numbers per basis that is not a tensor and say precisely what it fails. "The gyroscope": derive the steady precession rate of a heavy symmetric top and the nutation superposed on it, and identify which approximation the elementary torque-equals-dL/dt argument silently makes.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
For an explicitly given body you compute I in two frames, verify the transformation law component by component, predict which axis is unstable, and can explain in one sentence why nine numbers per basis is not enough to be a tensor.
