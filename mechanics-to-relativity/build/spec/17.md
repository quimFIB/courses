# Unit 17 — Integrability and chaos: determinism without predictability

- directory: `units/17-chaos/`
- part: Part B — the action, and coordinates ceasing to matter (data-trunk="b")
- part thesis: Nothing here says anything Newton did not, and that is the point: it is a change of language whose entire value is what it makes obvious. Extremising an integral gives the equations of motion; those equations are then invariant under any change of coordinates, which is the idea that ends the course as general covariance. Symmetry becomes conservation by one theorem. And the part quietly builds the two objects relativity needs — an action principle, and a real definition of a tensor, arriving with the inertia tensor because that is the first place nine numbers per basis will not do.

## Physics
The three-body problem and what Poincare found in it, including the error in the prize memoir. Sensitive dependence; the double pendulum. Integrable systems as the exception rather than the rule: Liouville-Arnold tori. KAM as the statement that most tori survive small perturbation, and that the ones that do not are exactly the resonant ones. Resonances and the Kirkwood gaps in the asteroid belt, an observable consequence with a number attached. What this implies for the stability of the solar system.

## Mathematical tools
Complete integrability; the Liouville-Arnold theorem with hypotheses and the geometry of its proof. Poincare sections and return maps; area-preserving maps; the standard map; Chirikov's overlap criterion. Lyapunov exponents defined via the linearised flow, with Oseledets cited. KAM stated with its Diophantine condition and an honest account of what 'small' means. Melnikov's method stated. And the contrast with unit 01: solutions are unique and depend continuously on data, which is entirely compatible with being useless for prediction, because continuous dependence is not uniform in t.

## The worked example the slides are built around
Henon-Heiles, computed. Integrate at four energies, plot the Poincare section at each, and watch the invariant curves dissolve into a chaotic sea as E passes 1/8 — the single most convincing picture in classical mechanics. Then compute the largest Lyapunov exponent in the chaotic region and confirm it is positive there and zero on a surviving torus.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Distinguishing integrable from chaotic with the tools rather than by eye. "Gronwall's revenge": use unit 01's continuous-dependence bound to show error grows at most like exp(Lt), show the rate is achieved, and conclude determinism and unpredictability are consistent rather than in tension. "The last torus": apply Chirikov's criterion to the standard map, then locate the critical kick strength numerically and compare with Greene's 0.9716. "Kirkwood": compute the semi-major axes of the 3:1 and 5:2 Jovian resonances against the observed gaps.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You compute a largest Lyapunov exponent for a given map, produce a Poincare section that constitutes evidence for chaos rather than an impression of it, and explain — quoting unit 01's theorem precisely — why a positive exponent does not contradict uniqueness.
