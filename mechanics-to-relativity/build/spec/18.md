# Unit 18 — From N masses to a field: the wave equation

- directory: `units/18-wave-equation/`
- part: Part C — the analytic toolkit, fields, and the crisis that ends Newtonian physics (data-trunk="c")
- part thesis: Take a chain of oscillators to the continuum and you get a partial differential equation, a field, and the need for four more pieces of mathematics: Fourier and Sturm-Liouville theory, the Fourier transform with the complex analysis that makes it usable, asymptotics for the problems with no exact answer, and tensor fields on space. Electromagnetism is here at full strength and on purpose: it is the only place vector calculus reaches its final form, it is the historical and logical road to relativity, and F_{mu nu} is the canonical tensor field that makes the Riemann tensor's index work familiar rather than alarming. The part ends by showing, with the chain rule and nothing else, that Maxwell's equations do not survive a Galilean boost — and by finding which transformation they do survive.

## Physics
The loaded string as N goes to infinity with total mass fixed; the wave equation with c = sqrt(T/mu), derived from a force balance on an element with the small-slope approximation stated and its error named. Travelling and standing waves; superposition; reflection at fixed and free ends, with and without inversion. Energy density and flux, and the local conservation law relating them. The string as a Lagrangian system with infinitely many degrees of freedom: the Lagrangian density and the Euler-Lagrange equation for a field, which is the first field theory in the course.

## Mathematical tools
The continuum limit as a limit of second-difference operators. Classification of second-order linear PDEs by the principal symbol — hyperbolic, parabolic, elliptic — reduction to canonical form, and what each class will accept as data. Characteristics; d'Alembert's formula derived by xi = x - ct, eta = x + ct; domain of dependence and range of influence; finite propagation speed as a theorem rather than an assumption. Hadamard well-posedness, with an explicit ill-posed problem to show the classification has teeth. Uniqueness by energy methods.

## The worked example the slides are built around
Pluck a string at a/L of its length: take the triangular initial profile with zero initial velocity, extend it oddly and periodically to handle the fixed ends, and follow d'Alembert's solution to draw the exact shape at t = L/4c, L/2c and 3L/4c — the flat-topped travelling kinks that high-speed photography of a real string actually shows.

## What the problem sheet exercises
d'Alembert with boundaries, and classification with consequences. "Reflection": solve for a pulse meeting a fixed end and a free end by images, and get both signs right. "Classify and break": classify u_xx + 2b u_xy + c u_yy = 0 by b^2 - ac, then work through Hadamard's example showing the Cauchy problem for Laplace's equation is ill-posed with data as small as you please. "Tricomi": classify u_yy + y u_xx = 0 and show it changes type across y = 0, which is what makes transonic flow hard.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You solve an initial-boundary-value problem for the finite string by images, classify a given second-order PDE and state what data it will accept, and write the Lagrangian density whose Euler-Lagrange equation is the wave equation.
