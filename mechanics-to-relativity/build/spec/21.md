# Unit 21 — Perturbation theory: the series that diverges and is still right

- directory: `units/21-asymptotics/`
- part: Part C — the analytic toolkit, fields, and the crisis that ends Newtonian physics (data-trunk="c")
- part thesis: Take a chain of oscillators to the continuum and you get a partial differential equation, a field, and the need for four more pieces of mathematics: Fourier and Sturm-Liouville theory, the Fourier transform with the complex analysis that makes it usable, asymptotics for the problems with no exact answer, and tensor fields on space. Electromagnetism is here at full strength and on purpose: it is the only place vector calculus reaches its final form, it is the historical and logical road to relativity, and F_{mu nu} is the canonical tensor field that makes the Riemann tensor's index work familiar rather than alarming. The part ends by showing, with the chain rule and nothing else, that Maxwell's equations do not survive a Galilean boost — and by finding which transformation they do survive.

## Physics
The uncomfortable truth under most quantitative physics: the exact answer is unavailable, the approximate one is a divergent series, and truncated in the right place it is accurate to ten digits. What an asymptotic expansion means, and why convergence is neither necessary nor sufficient for usefulness. Secular terms and why naive perturbation of an oscillator fails after one period. Boundary layers as the generic behaviour when a small parameter multiplies the highest derivative. Nondimensionalisation as the systematic way to find out which terms you are allowed to drop — unit 00's dimensional habit, now in its working form.

## Mathematical tools
Order notation, asymptotic sequences, Poincare's definition of an asymptotic expansion with the standard example sum n! x^n; optimal truncation and the exponentially small error; Borel summation named. Regular perturbation. Singular perturbation: matched asymptotic expansions, inner and outer solutions, the intermediate variable, Van Dyke's matching rule. Poincare-Lindstedt and multiple scales for removing secular terms. The WKB approximation, its validity condition and turning points, with the connection formulae stated — and the observation that this is unit 15's eikonal again. Integral asymptotics lives in unit 20 and is cited here, not repeated.

## The worked example the slides are built around
Two calculations, joined. First the Duffing oscillator x'' + x + epsilon x^3 = 0: show naive perturbation produces a secular term t sin t that destroys the approximation after t ~ 1/epsilon, then apply Poincare-Lindstedt to get omega = 1 + 3 epsilon A^2/8 + O(epsilon^2) and check it numerically. Then point the same machinery at its eventual target: set up u'' + u = GM/L^2 + epsilon u^2 and show that here the secular term is *not* to be removed — it is the perihelion precession, and it is real. The calculation is completed in unit 37, and this is the unit that makes it honest rather than a conjuring trick.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Getting numbers out of expansions and knowing when to stop. "Optimal truncation": for the Stieltjes series sum (-1)^n n! x^n at x = 0.1 find the smallest term, truncate there, compare with the exact integral value, and confirm the error is the size of the first omitted term. "A boundary layer": solve epsilon y'' + y' + y = 0 on [0,1] with y(0)=0, y(1)=1 by matched asymptotics, locate the layer, and compare the composite approximation with the exact solution at epsilon = 0.01. "Kolmogorov by dimensions alone": from the dissipation rate and the wavenumber derive E(k) proportional to epsilon^{2/3} k^{-5/3} and the Kolmogorov length, and check the exponent against published wind-tunnel spectra.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
Given a perturbed problem you nondimensionalise it, tell regular from singular, remove secular terms by Poincare-Lindstedt or multiple scales, construct and match a boundary layer, and for a divergent asymptotic series find the optimal truncation point and state the accuracy it achieves — and you can say, for the relativistic orbit equation, why the secular term must be kept.
