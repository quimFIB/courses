# Unit 24 — Maxwell: the displacement current, and light

- directory: `units/24-maxwell/`
- part: Part C — the analytic toolkit, fields, and the crisis that ends Newtonian physics (data-trunk="c")
- part thesis: Take a chain of oscillators to the continuum and you get a partial differential equation, a field, and the need for four more pieces of mathematics: Fourier and Sturm-Liouville theory, the Fourier transform with the complex analysis that makes it usable, asymptotics for the problems with no exact answer, and tensor fields on space. Electromagnetism is here at full strength and on purpose: it is the only place vector calculus reaches its final form, it is the historical and logical road to relativity, and F_{mu nu} is the canonical tensor field that makes the Riemann tensor's index work familiar rather than alarming. The part ends by showing, with the chain rule and nothing else, that Maxwell's equations do not survive a Galilean boost — and by finding which transformation they do survive.

## Physics
Faraday's law and induction. Maxwell's repair: Ampere's law is inconsistent with charge conservation for time-dependent currents, and demanding consistency forces the displacement current — a term added on purely logical grounds whose consequence is the existence of light. The complete system. Field energy and momentum; Poynting's theorem as local energy conservation; the Maxwell stress tensor, which is the first energy-momentum tensor in the course. Plane waves and polarisation. Potentials and gauge freedom; Lorenz and Coulomb gauges; retarded potentials; the Larmor formula and dipole radiation.

## Mathematical tools
The wave operator and its retarded Green's function in three dimensions, computed by unit 20's contour methods rather than asserted — the i-epsilon prescription is the place where causality enters the mathematics and it is made explicit. Kirchhoff's formula and the method of descent; the strong Huygens principle, so that sharp wavefronts exist in three dimensions and not in two. Gauge transformations as redundancy of description, with the gauge-invariant fields carrying the physical content and the residual freedom in Lorenz gauge made explicit.

## The worked example the slides are built around
Derive the displacement current: take the divergence of Ampere's law as Ampere left it, find it contradicts charge conservation, and show exactly one term repairs it. Then derive the vacuum wave equation and compute c = 1/sqrt(epsilon_0 mu_0) = 2.998e8 m/s from two bench-top constants, comparing with Fizeau's 1849 measurement of 3.13e8 — the moment light became an electromagnetic phenomenon — and estimate how good the measurements had to be for the identification to convince anyone in 1862.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Field computations, gauge, energy accounting and radiation. "Same fields, two gauges": compute the potentials of a point charge in Lorenz and in Coulomb gauge, note the Coulomb-gauge scalar potential is instantaneous, and show E and B are nevertheless identical and causal. "Huygens fails in two dimensions": obtain the 2D wave Green's function by descent from 3D and show it is supported inside the light cone, not on it, so a two-dimensional flash has a fading tail. "Larmor": derive the radiated power of an accelerated charge and show a classical orbiting electron collapses in 1.6e-11 s — a failure this course records and deliberately does not repair.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You derive the displacement current from charge conservation alone, obtain the vacuum wave equation and c from epsilon_0 and mu_0, compute the retarded Green's function by contour integration with a defensible account of why that prescription is the causal one, and show two different gauges produce identical E and B.
