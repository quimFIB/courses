# Unit 25 — The crisis: Maxwell's equations do not survive a Galilean boost

- directory: `units/25-the-crisis/`
- part: Part C — the analytic toolkit, fields, and the crisis that ends Newtonian physics (data-trunk="c")
- part thesis: Take a chain of oscillators to the continuum and you get a partial differential equation, a field, and the need for four more pieces of mathematics: Fourier and Sturm-Liouville theory, the Fourier transform with the complex analysis that makes it usable, asymptotics for the problems with no exact answer, and tensor fields on space. Electromagnetism is here at full strength and on purpose: it is the only place vector calculus reaches its final form, it is the historical and logical road to relativity, and F_{mu nu} is the canonical tensor field that makes the Riemann tensor's index work familiar rather than alarming. The part ends by showing, with the chain rule and nothing else, that Maxwell's equations do not survive a Galilean boost — and by finding which transformation they do survive.

## Physics
Galilean relativity, which unit 03 assumed without naming. The problem: Maxwell's equations contain c with no frame attached, and they change form under x -> x - vt. Three responses and their fates — an ether, with its absurd required properties; emission theories, killed by de Sitter's binary stars; and modifying Maxwell, killed by Maxwell continuing to be right. Michelson-Morley's null result; Kennedy-Thorndike and Ives-Stilwell and what each independently pins down. Stellar aberration and Fizeau's moving water, which no ether model ever explained together. Lorentz's contraction hypothesis and local time as a patch, and Poincare's realisation that the patches form a group.

## Mathematical tools
What covariance of a PDE system under a group action means precisely. Direct computation: apply the chain rule for a Galilean boost to the wave operator and exhibit the cross terms that appear; then ask which linear substitution kills them, and derive the Lorentz transformation as the answer — before any postulate has been stated. Symmetry groups of differential operators, with the conformal group of the wave equation mentioned as the larger answer.

## The worked example the slides are built around
Transform the one-dimensional wave equation under a Galilean boost, display the extra terms, and show no choice of v removes them. Then solve for the general linear map on (x,t) preserving the operator's form: two conditions, a one-parameter family, and the Lorentz transformation emerges with c fixed by the equation itself — Einstein's postulate arriving as a conclusion rather than an assumption. This is the unit that lets unit 26 say 'the invariant speed is c' rather than 'assume the invariant speed is c'.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Invariance computations and the experimental record. "Michelson-Morley quantitatively": compute the expected fringe shift for the 1887 apparatus under a 30 km/s ether wind (about 0.4 fringes), compare with the measured bound below 0.01, and state what Lorentz contraction alone would have predicted. "Which experiment pins which": show Michelson-Morley alone is consistent with several transformation laws, and identify exactly what Kennedy-Thorndike and Ives-Stilwell each add.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You show by direct computation that the wave equation is not Galilean-invariant and is Lorentz-invariant, and derive the boost by demanding form-invariance — without assuming the constancy of c as a postulate.
