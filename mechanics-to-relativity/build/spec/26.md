# Unit 26 — Minkowski space: relativity as the geometry of a quadratic form

- directory: `units/26-minkowski/`
- part: Part D — Minkowski: relativity as the geometry of a quadratic form, and fields on it (data-trunk="d")
- part thesis: Special relativity is presented as the structure it is: a group derived from homogeneity, isotropy and one invariant speed, acting on a vector space carrying an indefinite bilinear form. Every paradox dissolves into a statement about that form — the twin paradox is the reversed triangle inequality — and every dynamical result follows from the free action being proper time. Electromagnetism is then recognised as having been relativistic all along, field theory supplies an honest definition of the stress-energy tensor, and the part closes by taking the equivalence principle seriously and showing, from inside flat spacetime, exactly which feature of gravity Minkowski space cannot represent.

## Physics
Einstein's two postulates and the work each does. The relativity of simultaneity as the actual content, with time dilation and length contraction as corollaries rather than postulates. The invariant interval. The twin paradox. The pole and the barn. Velocity addition, relativistic Doppler, aberration and the headlight effect. Causal structure: which parts of 'before' survive and which do not, so that no superluminal signalling is a theorem. A note on provenance: unit 25 *earned* the invariant speed's value from Maxwell; a learner who has not done unit 25 takes it on credit here, and the course says so rather than pretending otherwise.

## Mathematical tools
Derive the Lorentz group from structural assumptions alone: homogeneity gives linearity, isotropy and the group axioms give a one-parameter family with a single invariant speed as the only free parameter, and unit 25's wave operator fixes it at c. Then the bilinear form eta of signature (-,+,+,+); O(1,3) and its four connected components; the proper orthochronous subgroup; boosts as hyperbolic rotations with rapidity as the additive parameter, so velocity addition is nothing but the addition formula for tanh. The Alexandrov-Zeeman theorem cited: preserving causal order alone already forces the Lorentz group up to scale. Minkowski diagrams with correctly scaled axes and calibration hyperbolae, which is the only way the diagrams do not lie. The reversed triangle inequality for timelike curves, proved.

## The worked example the slides are built around
The twin paradox three ways, all agreeing: by evaluating proper time tau = integral sqrt(-eta_{mu nu} dx^mu dx^nu) along each worldline; by counting Doppler-shifted signals each twin receives, which makes the asymmetry visible as the moment the shift flips and needs no appeal to acceleration at all; and on a Minkowski diagram, tracking the travelling twin's lines of simultaneity as they jump across the turnaround. Then the general point: because the signature is Lorentzian, timelike geodesics locally *maximise* proper time — the opposite of the Riemannian case, and the sign that makes unit 32's geodesic principle a maximum principle. Worth being uncomfortable about for ten minutes.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Boosts, rapidity, and causal structure taken seriously. "Rapidity is additive": prove velocity addition is tanh addition and compute the speed after ten successive 0.5c boosts. "The barn": resolve the pole-and-barn paradox by writing both door closings as events and transforming them, showing the disagreement concerns simultaneity and no contradiction exists. "No superluminal signalling": show that spacelike-separated events have their order reversed in some frame, hence a faster-than-light signal is a backwards-in-time signal in another frame, and show the argument fails for timelike separation.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You derive the Lorentz transformation from homogeneity, isotropy, the group axioms and one invariant speed, compute proper time along any specified worldline, and prove the twin paradox is the reversed triangle inequality for timelike curves in eta.
