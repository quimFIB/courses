# Unit 27 — Relativistic dynamics: four-vectors, and why colliders exist

- directory: `units/27-relativistic-dynamics/`
- part: Part D — Minkowski: relativity as the geometry of a quadratic form, and fields on it (data-trunk="d")
- part thesis: Special relativity is presented as the structure it is: a group derived from homogeneity, isotropy and one invariant speed, acting on a vector space carrying an indefinite bilinear form. Every paradox dissolves into a statement about that form — the twin paradox is the reversed triangle inequality — and every dynamical result follows from the free action being proper time. Electromagnetism is then recognised as having been relativistic all along, field theory supplies an honest definition of the stress-energy tensor, and the part closes by taking the equivalence principle seriously and showing, from inside flat spacetime, exactly which feature of gravity Minkowski space cannot represent.

## Physics
Four-velocity, four-acceleration, four-momentum. E = gamma m c^2, rest energy, and the case against 'relativistic mass'. Invariant mass as the norm of a system's total four-momentum, which is why a box of hot gas weighs more. Conservation of four-momentum in collisions; thresholds; Compton scattering; decay; the relativistic rocket. Massless particles and why they must travel at c. Why a rigid body is impossible in relativity — Born rigidity and the Ehrenfest disc — and hence why unit 16 has no relativistic successor. Hyperbolic motion at constant proper acceleration and its Rindler horizon, which costs nothing here and is unit 30's main tool.

## Mathematical tools
Four-vectors as elements of a vector space carrying a representation of the Lorentz group, with the invariant pairing giving minus m^2 c^2. Proper time as arc length in Lorentzian signature; the constraint u dot u = -c^2 and its consequence that acceleration is always orthogonal to velocity. The free-particle action S = -mc times the integral of d(tau), and its Euler-Lagrange equations by unit 11 yielding p = gamma m v — the first Lagrangian in the course that is not T - V and does not come from a metric on configuration space, which is the point of departure. Legendre-transforming it by unit 14 to H = sqrt(p^2c^2 + m^2c^4), noting how nearly degenerate the massless case is. Mandelstam invariants and the systematic technique of squaring conservation equations to work frame-free.

## The worked example the slides are built around
The antiproton threshold: p + p -> p + p + p + antiproton requires s at least (4 m_p c^2)^2. Evaluate s in the fixed-target frame to get a beam kinetic energy of about 5.6 GeV, and in the collider frame to get 2 x 0.94 GeV — the factor-of-three argument that justified building colliders, and the reason the Bevatron was designed at 6.2 GeV. Three lines, no frame transformation at all.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Invariant technique, thresholds, and one instructive wrong answer. "Compton": derive the wavelength shift from four-momentum conservation and the photon's null four-momentum in four lines. "Mass is not additive": for two photons of energy E travelling oppositely compute the pair's invariant mass 2E/c^2 and explain what happens as the angle closes. "Relativistic Kepler precesses": solve the orbit for a 1/r potential with relativistic kinematics, obtain a perihelion advance, and note it is roughly one sixth of Mercury's observed residual — the single most instructive wrong answer in physics, because it shows time dilation alone is not gravity. Unit 37 supplies the other five sixths.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You compute a reaction threshold by evaluating the invariant s in whichever frame is easiest and compare it with a published accelerator design energy, and derive p = gamma m v from the action -mc times proper time by the Euler-Lagrange equations of unit 11.
