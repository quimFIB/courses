# Unit 28 — Electromagnetism was relativistic all along: the field tensor

- directory: `units/28-field-tensor/`
- part: Part D — Minkowski: relativity as the geometry of a quadratic form, and fields on it (data-trunk="d")
- part thesis: Special relativity is presented as the structure it is: a group derived from homogeneity, isotropy and one invariant speed, acting on a vector space carrying an indefinite bilinear form. Every paradox dissolves into a statement about that form — the twin paradox is the reversed triangle inequality — and every dynamical result follows from the free action being proper time. Electromagnetism is then recognised as having been relativistic all along, field theory supplies an honest definition of the stress-energy tensor, and the part closes by taking the equivalence principle seriously and showing, from inside flat spacetime, exactly which feature of gravity Minkowski space cannot represent.

## Physics
E and B are not two fields but one field seen from a frame. The field tensor F^{mu nu} and the transformation rules; a purely magnetic field in one frame is partly electric in another. Purcell's argument: the magnetic force between a current and a moving charge is electrostatics plus length contraction, so magnetism is what relativity does to Coulomb's law at v/c around 1e-13, amplified by the number of charges in a wire. The covariant Maxwell equations, four collapsing to two. The Lorentz force as dp^mu/d(tau) = q F u. The field's stress-energy tensor, its symmetry, tracelessness, and vanishing divergence — the same statement as unit 22's continuum balance, now with energy included. Radiation reaction named, and the Abraham-Lorentz runaway solutions flagged honestly as unresolved in classical theory.

## Mathematical tools
Tensor fields on Minkowski space; raising and lowering with eta, the first place where unit 16's distinction between vectors and covectors is not optional. The antisymmetric (0,2)-tensor and its six components. The Levi-Civita symbol versus the Levi-Civita tensor; the dual tensor; the two invariants, one proportional to B^2 - E^2/c^2 and the other to E dot B. The four-current and continuity as a vanishing divergence. This is where abstract index notation becomes fluent, and that fluency is the actual prerequisite for units 32 through 37.

## The worked example the slides are built around
The wire and the test charge, in full: in the lab frame a neutral current-carrying wire exerts a magnetic force on a charge moving parallel to it; boost to the charge's rest frame, where the positive and negative line densities contract differently, the wire acquires a net charge, and the force is purely electrostatic. Show the two forces agree down to the last factor of gamma in the transverse-force transformation. Then verify that the vanishing divergence of T^{mu nu} reproduces exactly Poynting's theorem for the time component and the Maxwell stress balance for the spatial ones.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Index gymnastics and field transformations. "Which field can you transform away?": given E and B in one frame, use the two invariants to decide whether a frame exists in which the field is purely electric, purely magnetic, or neither, and construct the boost when it exists. "Larmor, covariantly": derive the Lienard generalisation of Larmor's formula from the invariant a dot a and check it reduces correctly in the non-relativistic limit.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
Given the field tensor and a boost you derive the E and B transformation rules and verify both invariants numerically, and you show by explicit computation that the divergence of the electromagnetic stress-energy tensor vanishes and reproduces the familiar conservation laws.
