# Unit 30 — The equivalence principle, and why flat spacetime cannot hold gravity

- directory: `units/30-equivalence-principle/`
- part: Part D — Minkowski: relativity as the geometry of a quadratic form, and fields on it (data-trunk="d")
- part thesis: Special relativity is presented as the structure it is: a group derived from homogeneity, isotropy and one invariant speed, acting on a vector space carrying an indefinite bilinear form. Every paradox dissolves into a statement about that form — the twin paradox is the reversed triangle inequality — and every dynamical result follows from the free action being proper time. Electromagnetism is then recognised as having been relativistic all along, field theory supplies an honest definition of the stress-energy tensor, and the part closes by taking the equivalence principle seriously and showing, from inside flat spacetime, exactly which feature of gravity Minkowski space cannot represent.

## Physics
Inertial and gravitational mass coincide to one part in 1e15, from Eotvos through Adelberger's torsion balances to MICROSCOPE. Einstein's elevator; the weak, Einstein and strong forms stated separately because they are genuinely different claims, with an experiment that distinguishes them. Consequences derived before any geometry exists: light bends, clocks low down run slow, light climbing loses frequency. Pound-Rebka. GPS as a working system that fails without both corrections. Then the argument that ends the part: tidal forces cannot be transformed away, so there is no global inertial frame, so gravity is not a force field on Minkowski space.

## Mathematical tools
Accelerated observers in Minkowski space; proper acceleration; the hyperbolic worldline from unit 27. Rindler coordinates and the metric with a position-dependent lapse — a non-constant metric on a *flat* spacetime, which is precisely the point that non-constant metric components are not yet gravity, and the standard inoculation against the beginner's belief that coordinate effects are physics. The Rindler horizon. Ehrenfest's rotating disc, whose spatial geometry already has circumference over diameter greater than pi, so a rotating frame in flat spacetime already forces non-Euclidean spatial geometry. Unit 05's tidal tensor restated as the object no coordinate change removes — the first curvature invariant, met before curvature is defined.

## The worked example the slides are built around
Derive the gravitational redshift from the equivalence principle alone: in a uniformly accelerating rocket a signal takes h/c to reach the ceiling, by which time the ceiling has gained speed gh/c, so the fractional shift is gh/c^2. Evaluate for Harvard's 22.5 m tower: 2.46e-15, against Pound and Rebka's measured (2.57 +/- 0.26)e-15. Then the GPS budget: +45.7 microseconds per day gravitational, -7.2 kinematic, net +38.5 — about 11 km of position error per day if ignored.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Equivalence-principle derivations and the Rindler wedge. "Rindler": derive the coordinates from the hyperbolic worldline, find the horizon, and show a signal from beyond it never arrives — a horizon with no gravity anywhere in sight. "Tides are the real thing": show a transformation to a freely falling frame kills the first derivatives of the metric but cannot kill the second, and identify the surviving object, thereby proving that 'gravity is not a force' and 'gravity is real' are both true. "Why not a tensor field on flat space": show a spin-2 field on Minkowski coupled to stress-energy must couple to its own stress-energy, and state what that self-consistency demands (Deser's argument, whose iteration produces general relativity).

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You derive the gravitational redshift from the equivalence principle with no geometry and match Pound-Rebka within its error bar, state the three equivalence principles with an experiment distinguishing them, and exhibit a non-constant metric on flat spacetime — thereby proving that non-constant metric components alone are not gravity.
