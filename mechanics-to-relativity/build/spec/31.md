# Unit 31 — Manifolds: doing all of it without coordinates

- directory: `units/31-manifolds/`
- part: Part E — curved space: the mathematics of 'there is no global inertial frame' (data-trunk="e")
- part thesis: The one place in the course where three consecutive units are mathematics first. That is not a lapse in the just-in-time principle but its consequence: unit 30 ends by proving tidal forces cannot be transformed away, and the only honest response is to build the machinery that measures exactly that. Manifolds so that coordinates stop being data, connections so that differentiating a vector field means something, curvature so that the thing no coordinate change removes has a name. The payoff is immediate and precise: geodesic deviation set beside the Newtonian tidal tensor of unit 05, term for term.

## Physics
Configuration space from unit 12 was a manifold all along — the double pendulum's is a torus and the rigid body's is SO(3) x R^3, which is why Euler angles have coordinate singularities rather than because anyone was careless. Spacetime as a manifold with no preferred coordinates, and an honest account of why general covariance, on its own, says considerably less than it sounds like.

## Mathematical tools
Smooth manifolds, charts, atlases, smooth maps, diffeomorphisms. Tangent vectors defined three ways — equivalence classes of curves, derivations of germs, coordinate n-tuples with a transformation law — with the isomorphisms constructed, because reconciling them is what makes the physics literature readable. The differential of a smooth map; the cotangent space; one-forms and the dual basis; df as the canonical example. Tensor fields as sections, with unit 16's transformation law recovered as nothing more than a change of basis, so that 'transforms like a tensor' becomes a theorem rather than a definition. Vector fields as derivations and as flows; the Lie bracket and its interpretation as the failure of flows to commute; the Lie derivative of tensors by pullback along the flow. Killing vector fields defined as those with L_X g = 0, planted here for unit 36. Submanifolds, embeddings, the Frobenius theorem. Cited, not proved: partitions of unity, Whitney, the hairy ball theorem.

## The worked example the slides are built around
The two-sphere with two stereographic charts: write one vector field in each, verify the components transform by the Jacobian on the overlap, and show no single chart covers the sphere. Write the round metric in both charts and check they agree. Compute the flow of the rotation field d/d(phi) and verify L_{d/d phi} g = 0 by direct computation, so it is a Killing field — the first instance of the object that will make Schwarzschild's conserved energy and angular momentum appear in unit 36. Then compute the Lie bracket of two rotation generators and recover so(3) from unit 16, now realised by flows rather than matrices.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Charts, transformations and brackets. "Components are not the tensor": give nine functions on the sphere in one chart, transform them, and decide whether they define a tensor field. "Lie derivative versus partial derivative": show by explicit computation that L_X T is a tensor while d_mu T^{nu rho} is not, and identify which terms in the Lie derivative are exactly the ones repairing the transformation law. "The torus is a configuration space": write the double pendulum's configuration space as T^2, produce an atlas, and express its kinetic energy as a metric in each chart.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You construct an atlas for a given manifold and verify the transition maps smooth, define a tensor field in one chart and transform it to another verifying agreement on the overlap, compute Lie brackets and Lie derivatives in coordinates, and determine whether a given vector field is Killing for a given metric.
