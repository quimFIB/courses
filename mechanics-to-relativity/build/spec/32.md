## Physics
Free fall is geodesic motion; the geodesic equation replaces Newton's second law. Parallel transport is what an ideal gyroscope does. The Christoffel symbols as the 'gravitational field' that can be made to vanish at a point but not in a neighbourhood — which is the equivalence principle stated as a fact about connections. The Newtonian limit, recovering d^2x/dt^2 = -grad Phi with g_00 = -(1 + 2 Phi/c^2) and the factor of two everyone gets wrong once. Minimal coupling and the comma-goes-to-semicolon rule, with the ordering ambiguity flagged honestly, and the pleasant observation that dF = 0 needs no modification because the exterior derivative never needed a connection. The structural point that geodesic motion is not an independent postulate: a vanishing divergence of T for a localised source already implies its worldline is a geodesic.

## Mathematical tools
The metric tensor and the geometry it defines: lengths, angles, volume form, raising and lowering. The problem that the partial derivative of a vector field is not tensorial. Affine connections and the covariant derivative on tensors of every type, with sign conventions fixed once; torsion; metric compatibility. The fundamental theorem of Riemannian geometry proved via the Koszul formula, so the Christoffel symbols are derived rather than posited — six lines, and the single formula the rest of the course computes with. Parallel transport along a curve as an ODE. Geodesics two ways — auto-parallel and stationary points of length or energy by unit 11 — with the equivalence proved and the affine-parameter subtlety handled rather than waved past. Normal coordinates and the local flatness theorem: at any point one can set g to eta with vanishing first derivatives but not vanishing second derivatives, which is exactly why curvature survives, and exactly the mathematical content of the equivalence principle.

## The worked example the slides are built around
Geodesics of the round sphere are great circles, derived twice — by the auto-parallel equation with computed Christoffel symbols, and by extremising arc length — and shown to agree. Then the practical trick used for the rest of the course: write the geodesic Lagrangian, take its Euler-Lagrange equations, and read the Christoffel symbols straight off, demonstrated on the Schwarzschild form before that solution has been derived. Then recover Newton: take g_00 = -(1 + 2 Phi/c^2), g_ij = delta_ij, expand the geodesic equation to lowest order in v/c and in Phi/c^2, and watch Newton's law appear with the factor of two fixed by requiring exactly that.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Connection computations and parallel transport. "Christoffels by Lagrangian": compute every Christoffel symbol of a given metric by the geodesic-Lagrangian trick, check against the coordinate formula, and count the arithmetic saved. "Transport around a loop": parallel-transport a vector around a spherical triangle and find the holonomy angle equals the enclosed area — unit 33 arriving a week early. "Flat in disguise": compute the connection for ds^2 = dr^2 + r^2 d(theta)^2 and confirm it is polar coordinates on a plane rather than curvature.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
For a given metric you compute every Christoffel symbol by the geodesic-Lagrangian trick and verify against the coordinate formula, prove the Levi-Civita connection is the unique torsion-free metric-compatible one, construct normal coordinates at a point and state exactly what can and cannot be made to vanish there, and recover Newton's law from the geodesic equation with the correct factor in g_00.
