## Physics
Geodesic deviation: two nearby freely falling particles accelerate relative to one another at a rate given by the Riemann tensor. Set this beside unit 05's Newtonian tidal equation and read off the identification of R^i_{0j0} with the second derivatives of the Newtonian potential — the single most important correspondence in the course, and the reason curvature is not an analogy for gravity but the same computation. Curvature as the thing no coordinate change removes, and therefore as the honest definition of a gravitational field. Why in three dimensions Ricci determines Riemann completely, and hence why there are no vacuum gravitational waves in 3D.

## Mathematical tools
The Riemann tensor defined by the commutator of covariant derivatives, with the check that it is tensorial despite being built from derivatives. Its symmetries and the component count n^2(n^2-1)/12 — 20 in four dimensions, 6 in three, 1 in two. The first and second Bianchi identities. Sectional and Gaussian curvature; Gauss's Theorema Egregium as the statement that curvature is intrinsic. The Ricci tensor, the scalar curvature, and the Weyl tensor as the trace-free part carrying vacuum tidal effects. The Einstein tensor, and the fact that its divergence vanishes identically as a consequence of the contracted second Bianchi identity rather than by assumption — highlighted, because it is what selects Einstein's equation in unit 35.

## The worked example the slides are built around
Compute the Riemann tensor of the two-sphere of radius a from its metric, confirm the single independent component gives Gaussian curvature 1/a^2, and verify that unit 32's spherical-triangle holonomy equals the integral of curvature over it. Then derive geodesic deviation from scratch, apply it to two great circles leaving the equator a small distance apart to get xi'' = -xi/a^2 (they converge and cross at the pole — that *is* tidal force), and show that in the weak-field limit it reproduces unit 05's tidal eigenvalues (-2, 1, 1)GM/r^3 term for term.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Curvature computations and the Newtonian correspondence. "One component in two dimensions": prove a 2-surface's Riemann tensor has exactly one independent component and compute it for the hyperbolic plane. "Counting components": prove the count n^2(n^2-1)/12 from the symmetries alone, evaluate at n = 2, 3, 4, and explain why Ricci determines Riemann in three dimensions. "Bianchi does the work": prove the Einstein tensor divergence-free from the contracted second Bianchi identity, and note this is the only reason unit 35's field equations can be consistent at all.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You compute the Riemann tensor of a 2-surface and verify it has exactly one independent component, prove the contracted Bianchi identity, and show the geodesic deviation equation reduces to unit 05's Newtonian tidal equation with the correct identification of components.
