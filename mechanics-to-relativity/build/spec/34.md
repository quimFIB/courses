# Unit 34 — Differential forms and Cartan: Stokes' theorem was always one theorem

- directory: `units/34-differential-forms/`
- part: Part E — curved space: the mathematics of 'there is no global inertial frame' (data-trunk="e")
- part thesis: The one place in the course where three consecutive units are mathematics first. That is not a lapse in the just-in-time principle but its consequence: unit 30 ends by proving tidal forces cannot be transformed away, and the only honest response is to build the machinery that measures exactly that. Manifolds so that coordinates stop being data, connections so that differentiating a vector field means something, curvature so that the thing no coordinate change removes has a name. The payoff is immediate and precise: geodesic deviation set beside the Newtonian tidal tensor of unit 05, term for term.

## Physics
Maxwell as dF = 0 and d(star F) proportional to the current — four equations becoming two, with the first an identity once F = dA, and gauge freedom becoming exactness. Charge conservation as the same identity applied to the other pair. Magnetic monopoles as a cohomological obstruction to writing F = dA globally. Unit 14's symplectic form as an actual object, with Liouville's theorem as the closedness of its top power, proved in one line by Cartan's magic formula next to the divergence proof. Unit 23's solenoid as a de Rham class.

## Mathematical tools
The exterior algebra; k-forms; the wedge product; the exterior derivative characterised axiomatically with d^2 = 0 from Clairaut; pullbacks and the naturality of d, which is why forms are worth the trouble. Orientation, volume forms, integration over manifolds. The generalised Stokes theorem, stated once and proved on the cube, with the gradient theorem of unit 04, Green's theorem, the curl theorem of unit 23 and the divergence theorem of unit 05 recovered as its low-dimensional cases and the full dictionary to grad, div and curl written out. Closed and exact; the Poincare lemma; de Rham cohomology, with H^1 of the punctured plane computed — closing the question unit 04 deliberately left open. The Hodge star and the metric's role in it, with the sign worked out in signature (-,+,+,+). Cartan's structure equations as a faster route to curvature, with one worked comparison against unit 33's coordinate computation.

## The worked example the slides are built around
Rewrite all four of Maxwell's equations in form language and expand them back into components, recovering the originals exactly including every factor and sign. Then compute the de Rham class of unit 04's vortex field, show it is closed but not exact, and identify its circulation 2 pi as the pairing of its class with the generator of first homology — the question asked thirty units earlier, answered.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Form manipulation, cohomology, and a speed comparison. "d squared in disguise": show the homogeneous Maxwell pair is the identity ddA = 0, and that charge conservation is the same identity applied to the other pair. "Compute a cohomology": find H^1 of the punctured plane and of the torus, and say which physical statement each dimension encodes. "Cartan versus Christoffel": recompute the curvature of one metric from unit 33 by moving frames and structure equations, and count the arithmetic saved.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You express Maxwell's four equations as dF = 0 and d(star F) = mu_0 star J, expand them back into components recovering the originals exactly, compute H^1 of the punctured plane — closing unit 04's open question — and reproduce one of unit 33's curvature computations by Cartan's structure equations.
