# Unit 35 — The Einstein field equations, twice

- part thesis: Two derivations of the field equations — vary the Einstein-Hilbert action, or observe with Lovelock that nothing else was ever available — then one exact solution and three numbers. ## Physics
The requirements listed before any equation is written: a tensor equation so no coordinates are preferred; second order in the metric so the initial-value problem is the familiar kind; sourced by everything carrying energy and momentum, because E = mc^2; automatically compatible with local conservation since the divergence of T vanishes; and reducing to Poisson's equation in the weak, slow, static limit. The stress-energy tensor made concrete for dust, a perfect fluid and — for those who have done unit 29 — the electromagnetic field, with unit 22's continuum momentum balance recognisable as its non-relativistic shadow. The equations with the cosmological term, whose history and current status as dark energy are stated without editorialising. The vacuum equations R_{mu nu} = 0, and the crucial observation that they are not trivial because the Weyl tensor survives. Coordinate freedom and the count 10 - 4 - 4 = 2. Why global energy conservation genuinely fails in general spacetimes rather than merely looking difficult. Choquet-Bruhat's well-posed initial-value formulation cited, since it is not obvious and took forty years.

## Mathematical tools
Route one, requiring unit 29: the Einstein-Hilbert action varied with respect to the inverse metric — the identity for the variation of the metric determinant, the Palatini identity, the total-derivative term that becomes the Gibbons-Hawking-York boundary contribution, and T defined as the variational derivative of the matter action, which is unit 29's definition, so the two units lock together. Route two, requiring only units 33 and 30: the contracted Bianchi identity hands you a divergence-free second-order symmetric tensor, and Lovelock's theorem says that in four dimensions the Einstein tensor plus a cosmological term is the only one — so the equation is forced rather than chosen, and the coupling is fixed by the Newtonian limit. A learner arriving without unit 29 gets route two, which is complete but does not explain where the right-hand side comes from; the unit says so plainly.

## The worked example the slides are built around
The Newtonian limit in complete detail: perturb the metric about eta, take the field static and the test particles slow, use unit 32's geodesic result g_00 = -(1 + 2 Phi/c^2), compute the relevant Ricci component to first order, and show the field equation becomes Poisson's equation — which is the only thing that fixes the coupling at 8 pi G/c^4. Then, for those on route one, vary the Einstein-Hilbert action from beginning to end with nothing skipped and watch the same constant appear. Two different routes to 8 pi G/c^4 is the kind of consistency check that makes a theory believable.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Variation, uniqueness, and limits. "Which tensors are allowed": show Einstein's first guess, Ricci proportional to T, forces T to be trace-free by the Bianchi identity and is therefore wrong, then find the fix — reconstructing 1915 in one problem. "Vary it" (needs 29): derive the field equations from the Einstein-Hilbert action keeping every boundary term visible, and identify exactly where the Gibbons-Hawking-York term is needed and why. "Two polarisations, counted twice": obtain the plus and cross polarisations from the linearised vacuum equation in transverse-traceless gauge, then count independently as 10 minus 4 gauge minus 4 constraints and confirm the answers agree.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You argue from the contracted Bianchi identity and Lovelock's theorem why the Einstein tensor is forced, fix the coupling constant at 8 pi G/c^4 from the Newtonian limit, and linearise the equations to obtain the wave equation with the correct count of propagating degrees of freedom — and, if you have unit 29, you obtain the same field equations independently by varying the Einstein-Hilbert action.
