## Physics
Static, spherically symmetric, vacuum. The Schwarzschild metric, derived rather than quoted. Birkhoff's theorem: spherical symmetry forces staticity, so a pulsating star radiates nothing and the interior of a spherical shell is exactly flat — the relativistic shell theorem, and the reason there is no monopole gravitational radiation. The Schwarzschild radius: 2.95 km for the Sun, 8.9 mm for the Earth. Proper versus coordinate radial distance and time, and what an infalling and a distant observer each report. The event horizon as a one-way null surface with nothing locally remarkable about it, and the finite proper time to the singularity.

## Mathematical tools
Solving the vacuum equations with the ansatz ds^2 = -A(r)dt^2 + B(r)dr^2 + r^2 d(Omega)^2 — the full computation, done once, slowly, with every nonzero Christoffel and Ricci component displayed, and the integration constant fixed by unit 35's Newtonian limit. Killing vectors as infinitesimal isometries, and the conserved quantities they produce along geodesics, giving E and L for free — which is unit 13's Noether theorem in geometric clothing, and worth saying out loud. Diagnosing singularities with invariants: the Kretschmann scalar 48G^2M^2/c^4r^6 is finite at the horizon and divergent only at the centre. Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates removing the horizon singularity; the Kruskal-Szekeres maximal extension and its four regions; conformal compactification and Penrose diagrams, constructed for Minkowski first so the technique is clear.

## The worked example the slides are built around
Derive the Schwarzschild solution in full from the ansatz. Then show the horizon is a coordinate artefact by transforming explicitly to ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates, while the centre survives every transformation because the Kretschmann scalar diverges as 1/r^6. Then build the Kruskal extension step by step — tortoise coordinate, null coordinates, exponential Kruskal coordinates — and draw the resulting diagram with all four regions.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Geodesics, horizons and coordinates. "How long to fall": compute the proper time for radial infall from rest to the centre and show it is finite while the Schwarzschild coordinate time to reach the horizon diverges, then reconcile the two accounts without hand-waving. "Birkhoff": prove spherical symmetry plus vacuum forces staticity, and state the two consequences — no monopole radiation, and a flat shell interior. "Tidal death": compute the tidal tensor at the horizon as a function of M and find the mass above which a human survives horizon crossing.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You derive Schwarzschild from the ansatz, obtain the geodesic constants of motion from its Killing vectors and name the unit-13 theorem they are an instance of, and demonstrate by an explicit coordinate transformation plus an invariant scalar that exactly one of its two apparent singularities is real.
