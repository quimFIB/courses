# Unit 37 — The classical tests: three numbers, and one of them is 43

## Physics
Geodesics in Schwarzschild. The effective potential with its new inverse-cube term and everything that follows: no stable circular orbit below six gravitational radii, a plunge with no Newtonian analogue, and precession of everything else. Mercury: 5600 arcsec per century observed, 5025 from the precession of the equinoxes, about 531 from the other planets by unit 10's perturbation machinery, and 43 left over that Le Verrier attributed to a planet nobody found. Light deflection: 1.75 arcsec at the solar limb, Eddington in 1919, and modern VLBI at 1.7510 +/- 0.0008. The Shapiro delay, measured by Cassini to one part in 1e5. Gravitational redshift, already settled in unit 30. The photon sphere at 3GM/c^2, which is the ring in the Event Horizon Telescope images. Modern tests named and dated: Gravity Probe B, GW150914, the S2 orbit around Sagittarius A*.

## Mathematical tools
Null and timelike geodesic equations from unit 36's Killing conserved quantities. The orbit equation u'' + u = GM/L^2 + 3GM u^2/c^2 — exactly unit 08's Binet equation with one extra term, which is the cleanest possible statement of what general relativity adds to Newton. Unit 21's Poincare-Lindstedt method in its intended use: the secular term is the whole answer, giving 6 pi GM/c^2 a(1-e^2) per orbit. The null case with an impact parameter giving 4GM/c^2 b, and the explicit comparison with the Newtonian-corpuscle calculation, which gives exactly half — so the factor of two is the discriminating prediction, and it comes from the spatial part of the metric.

## The worked example the slides are built around
Mercury end to end, with a number: from the metric to the orbit equation to the perturbative solution to the answer, using a = 5.79e10 m, e = 0.2056 and 415.2 orbits per century, arriving at 42.98 arcsec per century against the observed 42.98 +/- 0.04. Then light deflection both ways — 0.87 arcsec as a Newtonian corpuscle, 1.75 in general relativity — identifying precisely which metric component supplies the missing half.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Geodesic computations and tests against real data with real uncertainties. "The ISCO": find the innermost stable circular orbit at six gravitational radii from the effective potential and compute the binding energy there, 5.7 per cent of rest mass, which is the efficiency of a thin accretion disc, and compare with the observed radiative efficiency of accreting black holes. "Shapiro from Cassini": compute the round-trip radar delay for a signal grazing the Sun, evaluate for the 2002 superior conjunction geometry, and compare with the published measurement including its stated uncertainty.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
From scratch, with no formula looked up, you compute Mercury's perihelion advance to within 1 arcsec per century of 43 and the solar light deflection as 1.75 arcsec, show the latter is exactly twice the Newtonian value and name the metric component responsible — and you can say why unit 21 forbade you from removing the secular term.
