## Physics
Linearised general relativity. Gauge freedom as infinitesimal diffeomorphism — structurally the same redundancy as unit 24's electromagnetic gauge, and worth meeting twice for exactly that reason. Transverse-traceless gauge; two polarisations; the effect on a ring of free test masses. The quadrupole formula, and the structural reason there is no dipole radiation where electromagnetism has one: mass conservation kills the monopole and momentum conservation kills the dipole, so gravity's lowest radiating multipole is two orders up, which is why the waves are so weak. The Hulse-Taylor binary's orbital decay matching prediction to 0.2 per cent over forty years. LIGO's GW150914: strain 1e-21, three solar masses radiated in 0.2 s. The detector is a Michelson interferometer, so the same experiment that found no ether found gravitational waves.

## Mathematical tools
Perturbation to first order; the trace-reversed perturbation and its sourced wave equation; the retarded solution using exactly the Green's function unit 24 computed by unit 20's contour methods. The transverse-traceless projector and an honest degree-of-freedom count: ten components, minus four gauge, minus four residual, equals two. Multipole expansion of the source in the slow-motion limit. Unit 21's two-timing for the adiabatic inspiral. Why the energy carried by a wave is not a local tensor, the Isaacson average, and what that says about the phrase 'energy of the gravitational field'.

## The worked example the slides are built around
The inspiral chirp: compute the quadrupole moment of a circular binary, get P = 32 G^4 mu^2 M^3/5c^5 r^5, balance against the orbital energy to obtain dr/dt, integrate to the coalescence time, and derive f-dot proportional to f^{11/3} with the chirp mass as the only combination that appears. Evaluate for PSR B1913+16 and obtain an orbital period derivative of -2.40e-12 against the measured -2.423e-12.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Linearisation, gauge counting and estimation. "Count to two": start from ten components and reduce to two by fixing the gauge twice, explicit about the residual freedom at each stage. "Why nobody built a laboratory source": compute the power radiated by a steel bar spun as fast as its tensile strength allows and compare with LIGO's sensitivity. "LIGO's number": estimate the strain from GW150914's parameters, compare with 1e-21, and compute how far a 4 km arm actually moves — about 1e-18 m.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You derive the linearised wave equation, reduce ten components to two by explicit gauge fixing, exhibit both polarisations' effect on a ring of test masses, and reproduce the Hulse-Taylor orbital decay rate to within 5 per cent of the measured value.
