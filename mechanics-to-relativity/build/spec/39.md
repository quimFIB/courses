# Unit 39 — Cosmology

- directory: `units/39-cosmology/`
- part: Part F — Einstein: the field equations and what they predict (data-trunk="f")
- part thesis: Two derivations of the field equations — vary the Einstein-Hilbert action, or observe with Lovelock that nothing else was ever available — then one exact solution and three numbers. The endpoint is not the equation but the arcseconds: Mercury's 43, the factor of two in light deflection that separates Einstein from a Newtonian corpuscle, and the Shapiro delay. Three branches extend the reach — waves, cosmology, rotating holes — and the last names precisely where this course's methods stop.

## Physics
The cosmological principle as an assumption, with the evidence for it (CMB isotropy to 1e-5, galaxy surveys) stated separately from the assumption itself, and the honest note that it concerns large scales only. FLRW geometry and the three spatial curvatures. The Friedmann equations read as an energy equation with an effective potential — a direct callback to unit 08, and the cheapest honest way to understand what a cosmological constant does. Matter, radiation and Lambda eras and their scalings. Cosmological redshift as the stretching of wavelengths, and why it is not a Doppler shift. The distance ladder, the Hubble parameter, and the current H_0 tension stated as an open observational problem. Big Bang nucleosynthesis as the earliest quantitative success. The horizon and flatness problems, with inflation presented with its motivation and its unresolved status.

## Mathematical tools
Maximally symmetric spaces and the uniqueness of the FLRW form. Computing the Einstein tensor for FLRW — substantial, entirely mechanical, and a good test of whether unit 33's machinery is fluent. Comoving and proper coordinates; conformal time. The fluid equation obtained both from the vanishing divergence of unit 22's perfect-fluid stress tensor and as a consequence of the two Friedmann equations. Solving for single-component and mixed universes; the age integral; particle and event horizons as integrals along the light cone.

## The worked example the slides are built around
Derive both Friedmann equations from scratch — metric, Christoffels, Ricci, scalar curvature, then G = 8 pi G T with T = diag(-rho, p, p, p) — and read off the standard forms. Solve the three pure cases, then integrate the flat matter-plus-Lambda case with Planck's parameters (0.315, 0.685, H_0 = 67.4) to get 13.8 Gyr, and locate matter-Lambda equality at about z = 0.30, noting we live rather close to it.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Cosmological computations against real numbers. "The age of the universe": integrate the Friedmann equation with the Planck parameters, then repeat with H_0 = 73 to see how much of the H_0 tension the age is sensitive to. "The horizon problem, quantified": compute the comoving particle horizon at recombination, convert it to an angle on today's sky, find roughly one degree, and state precisely why the observed uniformity across 180 degrees is therefore a problem.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You derive the Friedmann equations from FLRW plus Einstein without consulting a reference, solve for the scale factor in the matter-, radiation- and Lambda-dominated eras, and compute the age of the universe for the Planck parameters to within 0.3 Gyr of 13.8.
