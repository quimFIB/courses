The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: the Sun and Jupiter as two point masses, with Lagrangian
\(L = \tfrac12 m_S|\dot{\vec r}_S|^2 + \tfrac12 m_J|\dot{\vec r}_J|^2 + Gm_Sm_J/|\vec r_S - \vec r_J|\).
Time translation gives the energy \(E = -1.618\times10^{35}\) J, rotations the
angular momentum \(|\vec L| = 1.926\times10^{43}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}\), the
Galilean boost the charge \(\vec G = \vec P t - M\vec R\), and a hidden
velocity-dependent symmetry the Runge–Lenz vector, \(|\vec A|/\mu k = e = 0.0489\).

* Symmetry, made precise

- homogeneity of time (time-translation invariance) :: The laws do not single
  out an origin of time: the Lagrangian of an isolated system has no explicit
  \(t\). Its Noether charge is the energy function \(h\). /Slide: Three laws, three unrelated proofs./
- homogeneity of space :: No point of space is special: the Lagrangian of an
  isolated system is unchanged when every body is shifted by the same vector.
  Its charge is the total momentum.
- isotropy :: No direction of space is special: the Lagrangian is unchanged when
  every body is rotated about the same axis. Its charge is the total angular
  momentum.
- one-parameter group :: A family of maps \(\Phi_s\) of configuration space,
  \(C^2\) in \((s,q,t)\), with \(\Phi_0 = \mathrm{id}\) and
  \(\Phi_s\circ\Phi_{s'} = \Phi_{s+s'}\). Unit 02's flow of a vector field, seen
  from the family's side. /Slide: One-parameter groups of transformations./
- infinitesimal generator :: \(\xi = \partial_s\Phi_s|_{s=0}\), the velocity
  with which the family starts to move a point. It determines the group, which
  is the flow of \(\xi\).
- lift to velocities :: The map a transformation of positions induces on
  velocities, by the chain rule: \(\dot q\mapsto D_q\Phi_s\,\dot q + \partial_t\Phi_s\). /Slide: Moving curves, and what the Lagrangian does./
- variation :: \(\delta q = \partial_s q_s|_{s=0} = \xi\), the first-order
  change of a curve under the family. Commutes with \(\mathrm d/\mathrm dt\).
- induced variation of \(L\) :: \(\delta L = \frac{\partial L}{\partial q}\cdot\xi + \frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}\cdot\dot\xi\),
  the first-order change of the Lagrangian along a moved curve. Its integral
  is the variation of the action.
- symmetry :: A family with \(L(q_s,\dot q_s,t) = L(q,\dot q,t)\) for every
  curve and every \(s\); infinitesimally, \(\delta L = 0\) for every curve. /Slide: Symmetry, and symmetry up to a total derivative./
- quasi-symmetry :: A family that changes \(L\) only by a total time
  derivative, \(L(q_s,\dot q_s,t) = L + \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\Lambda_s(q,t)\);
  infinitesimally \(\delta L = \dot F\). It still maps solutions to solutions.
  The Galilean boost is one. Also called a divergence symmetry.
- off-shell :: Holding for every curve, not only for solutions of the equations
  of motion (on-shell). A symmetry must hold off-shell: on-shell every \(\xi\)
  satisfies \(\delta L = \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}(p\cdot\xi)\) and the charge
  would be zero.

* The theorem

- Noether's theorem :: If \(\delta L = \dot F\) for every curve, with
  \(\xi\) and \(F\) functions of \((q,\dot q,t)\), then \(Q = p\cdot\xi - F\) is
  constant on every solution. Proof: \(\dot Q = \dot p\cdot\xi + p\cdot\dot\xi - \dot F = \delta L - \dot F = 0\). /Slide: Noether's theorem./
- Noether charge :: The conserved quantity \(Q = \sum_i\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q_i}\xi_i - F\)
  attached to a symmetry. Defined up to an additive constant, and unchanged by
  adding a total derivative to \(L\).
- conjugate variables :: A symmetry parameter and the charge it produces:
  time and energy, position and momentum, angle and angular momentum. Their
  product has the dimensions of action. /Slide: Checking a charge: dimensions./

* Energy is time translation

- energy function :: \(h = \sum_i\dot q_i\,\partial L/\partial\dot q_i - L\), the
  Noether charge of time translation, with \(\dot h = -\partial L/\partial t\)
  on-shell. Unit 11's Beltrami quantity, with the sign reversed. /Slide: Time translation as a variation./
- Euler's theorem on homogeneous functions :: If \(f(\lambda v) = \lambda^kf(v)\)
  for \(\lambda > 0\), then \(v\cdot\nabla f = kf\). It gives \(h = L_2 - L_0\),
  hence \(h = T_2 - T_0 + V\), which equals \(T + V\) only when the
  coordinates do not move. /Slide: When is h the energy?/
- Jacobi's integral :: \(h = E - \Omega L_z\), the energy function in
  coordinates rotating uniformly at \(\Omega\); conserved when the rotating-frame
  Lagrangian has no explicit time. The bead on the rotating hoop has it, and so
  does the restricted three-body problem. /Slide: Where the motor's work goes./

* Space: momentum, angular momentum, and the boost

- boost charge :: \(\vec G = \vec P t - M\vec R\), the charge of the Galilean
  boost \(\vec r_a\mapsto\vec r_a + st\hat n\), which changes \(L\) by
  \(\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}(sM\hat n\cdot\vec R + \frac12Ms^2t)\). Its
  conservation says the centre of mass moves uniformly. /Slide: The boost, where L is not invariant./
- radial-velocity method :: Detecting a planet by the periodic Doppler shift of
  its star as the star circles the common centre of mass. Jupiter moves the Sun
  at \(12.46\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), the Earth at \(0.089\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). /Slide: What the boost charge says about the Sun./
- Galilean group :: The ten-parameter group of maps
  \((t,\vec r)\mapsto(t + t_0,\ R\vec r + \vec vt + \vec d)\): time shift, space
  shifts, rotations, boosts. Its ten charges are \(E\), \(\vec P\), \(\vec L\),
  \(\vec G\). /Slide: Ten symmetries, ten charges./

* The hidden symmetry

- Runge–Lenz vector :: \(\vec A = \vec p\times\vec L - \mu k\hat r\) for the
  inverse-square law, pointing at perihelion with \(|\vec A| = \mu ke\). Unit 08
  proved it conserved; it is the charge of no transformation of configuration
  space, but of a velocity-dependent symmetry. /Slide: A conserved vector with no symmetry of space behind it./
- generalised symmetry :: A variation \(\delta q = \xi(q,\dot q,t)\) depending on
  the velocities, with \(\delta L = \dot F\) off-shell. Noether's proof applies
  unchanged. Time translation (\(\xi = \dot q\)) and the Runge–Lenz generator are
  examples. /Slide: Letting the transformation see the velocity./
- converse of Noether's theorem :: The claim that every conserved quantity is the
  charge of a symmetry. False for transformations of configuration space — the
  Runge–Lenz vector is a counterexample — and true for flows on phase space,
  unit 14. /Slide: The converse is false, and what rescues it./
