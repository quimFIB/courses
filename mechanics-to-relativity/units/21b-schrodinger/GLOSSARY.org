The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: the harmonic oscillator \(H = P^2/2m + \frac12 m\omega^2X^2\)
on \(L^2(\mathbb R)\), with length scale \(\ell = \sqrt{\hbar/m\omega}\) and
spectrum \(E_n = \hbar\omega(n + \frac12)\), found three times — by a power
series that must stop, by ladder operators, and by the Bohr–Sommerfeld rule —
and then compared, at \(n = 20\), with the classical density
\(1/\pi\sqrt{A^2 - x^2}\). A physical instance: the CO molecule, \(\hbar\omega =
0.269\) eV, \(\ell = 4.76\) pm.

* Which wave equation?

- Planck–Einstein relations :: \(E = \hbar\omega\) and \(\vec p = \hbar\vec
  k\), with \(\hbar = h/2\pi = 1.0546\times10^{-34}\) J s: light is exchanged in
  lumps of energy \(\hbar\omega\) and momentum \(\hbar k\). Evidence: the
  photoelectric effect and Compton scattering. /Slide: Quanta, and de Broglie
  turning them round./
- de Broglie relation :: A particle of momentum \(p\) is a wave of wavelength
  \(\lambda = h/p\) (1924). Confirmed by Davisson and Germer (1927): 54 eV
  electrons, \(\lambda = 0.167\) nm predicted, \(0.165\) nm measured.
- Schrödinger equation :: \(i\hbar\,\partial_t\psi = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\psi
  + V\psi\). The simplest linear equation, first order in time, solved by de
  Broglie's plane waves with \(E = p^2/2m\), whose short-wavelength limit is
  the Hamilton–Jacobi equation. A hypothesis whose warrant is experiment.
  /Slide: Choosing the equation./
- wavefunction :: The complex-valued solution \(\psi(\vec x, t)\) of the
  Schrödinger equation. Complex because a first-order-in-time equation with
  real coefficients would be the heat equation, which decays.
- quantum potential :: \(Q = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2A/A\) for \(\psi =
  Ae^{iS/\hbar}\): the order-\(\hbar^2\) term that the Schrödinger equation adds
  to \(V\) in the Hamilton–Jacobi equation for \(S\). At order \(\hbar^0\) the
  equation for \(S\) is Hamilton–Jacobi exactly; at order \(\hbar^1\) the
  equation for \(A^2\) is a conservation law. /Slide: Hamilton–Jacobi at
  leading order, a conservation law at the next./
- Born rule :: \(|\psi|^2\,\mathrm d^3x\) is the probability of finding the
  particle in \(\mathrm d^3x\), for normalised \(\psi\) (1926). A postulate,
  shown consistent by the continuity equation. /Slide: Probability, and the
  current that carries it./
- continuity equation :: \(\partial_t\rho + \nabla\cdot\vec j = 0\) with
  \(\rho = |\psi|^2\): holds for real \(V\), and with the divergence theorem
  conserves \(\int|\psi|^2\). In polar form it is the order-\(\hbar\) equation,
  unit 21's transport equation read as a conservation law.
- probability current :: \(\vec j = \frac\hbar m\mathrm{Im}(\bar\psi\nabla\psi)
  = A^2\nabla S/m\). For a plane wave, \(\vec j = \rho\vec p/m\): probability
  flows at the classical velocity.

* What the objects are

- Hilbert space :: For one particle, \(L^2(\mathbb R^3)\) with \(\langle\varphi,
  \psi\rangle = \int\bar\varphi\psi\): conjugate-linear in the first slot, the
  opposite convention to units 19 and 20. Metrically complete. /Slide: States:
  unit vectors in L^2, up to a phase./
- state :: A unit vector of the Hilbert space, with \(\psi\) and
  \(e^{i\alpha}\psi\) identified: a ray. Global phases are invisible; relative
  phases between superposed vectors are not.
- position operator :: \((X\psi)(x) = x\psi(x)\). Its expectation value is the
  mean of the Born density. /Slide: Observables: position, momentum, and where
  they come from./
- momentum operator :: \((P\psi)(x) = -i\hbar\psi'(x)\). Its expectation value
  is the mean of the momentum density \(|\tilde\psi(p/\hbar)|^2/2\pi\hbar\), by
  Plancherel, where \(\tilde\psi(k) = \int\psi e^{-ikx}\,\mathrm dx\).
- expectation value :: \(\langle A\rangle_\psi = \langle\psi, A\psi\rangle\), the
  mean of the outcomes of measuring \(A\) in the state \(\psi\).
- domain :: The dense subspace \(D(A)\) on which an operator \(A\) is defined.
  Unbounded operators cannot be defined everywhere (Hellinger–Toeplitz), and
  which domain is chosen is part of the operator. /Slide: Unbounded operators
  need domains./
- symmetric operator :: \(\langle\varphi, A\psi\rangle = \langle A\varphi,
  \psi\rangle\) for all \(\varphi, \psi\) in \(D(A)\). Equivalently \(A \subset
  A^*\). Not enough for a real spectrum or for a dynamics.
- adjoint :: \(A^*\): defined on the \(\varphi\) for which \(\psi\mapsto\langle
  \varphi, A\psi\rangle\) is bounded on \(D(A)\), by \(\langle\varphi,
  A\psi\rangle = \langle A^*\varphi, \psi\rangle\). Well defined because \(D(A)\)
  is dense.
- self-adjoint :: \(A = A^*\), domains included. In finite dimensions the same
  as symmetric; in infinite dimensions strictly stronger, and the condition
  observables and Hamiltonians must meet.
- self-adjoint extension :: A self-adjoint operator extending a symmetric one.
  For \(-i\,\mathrm d/\mathrm dx\) on \((0, L)\) they are the conditions
  \(\psi(L) = e^{i\theta}\psi(0)\), with spectra \(\hbar(2\pi n + \theta)/L\):
  different boundary conditions, different physics. /Slide: Momentum in a box:
  symmetric is not enough./
- deficiency indices :: \(n_\pm = \dim\ker(A^*\mp i)\) for a symmetric \(A\).
  Self-adjoint extensions exist iff \(n_+ = n_-\) (von Neumann, cited). For
  momentum: \((0,0)\) on \(\mathbb R\), \((1,1)\) on an interval, \((1,0)\) on a
  half-line, where no momentum observable exists. /Slide: Deficiency indices,
  named./
- unitary group :: A family \(U(t)\) of unitary operators with \(U(t + s) =
  U(t)U(s)\), \(U(0) = I\), and \(t\mapsto U(t)\psi\) continuous: a
  probability-conserving, deterministic time evolution. /Slide: Why
  self-adjoint: Stone's theorem./
- Stone's theorem :: Unitary groups correspond exactly to self-adjoint
  operators, \(U(t) = e^{-iHt/\hbar}\). Cited. It is why the Hamiltonian must be
  self-adjoint and not merely symmetric.
- spectrum :: \(\sigma(A)\): the \(\lambda\) for which \(A - \lambda\) has no
  bounded inverse defined on all of \(\mathcal H\). Real for self-adjoint
  \(A\); the set of possible outcomes of measuring \(A\). Position and
  momentum have spectrum \(\mathbb R\) and no eigenvectors in \(L^2\). /Slide:
  Spectrum, and what a measurement returns./
- canonical commutation relation :: \([X, P] = i\hbar\) on the Schwartz class,
  by one line of the product rule. /Slide: The commutator replaces the bracket./
- Dirac's rule :: Replace the Poisson bracket by a commutator over
  \(i\hbar\): \(\{f, g\}\mapsto[\hat f,\hat g]/i\hbar\) (1925). Exact for
  polynomials of degree at most two; a heuristic beyond.
- commutator :: \([A, B] = AB - BA\), on a common domain that both map into
  itself. The time derivative of an expectation value is
  \(\frac i\hbar\langle[H, A]\rangle\).
- Groenewold's theorem :: No linear quantisation map on polynomials in
  \((x, p)\) sends \(x, p\) to \(X, P\) irreducibly and every Poisson bracket to
  the commutator over \(i\hbar\) (1946, cited). The obstruction is at degree
  four, by a term \(\frac13\hbar^2\).
- Robertson's inequality :: \(\Delta A\,\Delta B\ge\frac12|\langle[A,B]\rangle|\)
  for symmetric \(A, B\) (1929), by Cauchy–Schwarz. /Slide: Robertson's
  inequality./
- Heisenberg uncertainty relation :: \(\Delta x\,\Delta p\ge\hbar/2\), with
  equality exactly for Gaussians. Unit 20's \(\Delta t\,\Delta\omega\ge\frac12\)
  applied to a wave whose wavenumber is \(p/\hbar\).
- zero-point energy :: The ground-state energy above the potential's minimum,
  \(\frac12\hbar\omega\) for the oscillator. Forced by the uncertainty relation:
  \(\langle H\rangle\ge\omega\Delta x\Delta p\ge\frac12\hbar\omega\) for every
  state. /Slide: The same theorem as unit 20's./

* Stationary states: the oscillator as a Sturm–Liouville problem

- time-independent Schrödinger equation :: \(-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\varphi'' +
  V\varphi = E\varphi\): a Sturm–Liouville problem with \(p = \hbar^2/2m\),
  \(q = V\), \(w = 1\), eigenvalue \(E\). /Slide: Separating time./
- stationary state :: A solution \(\varphi(x)e^{-iEt/\hbar}\); its density
  \(|\psi|^2\) does not change in time.
- energy quantisation :: The discreteness of the Sturm–Liouville spectrum of
  a confining potential. Nothing is quantised by hand: square-integrability
  or the walls act as boundary conditions.
- packet spreading :: A free Gaussian of initial spread \(\sigma_0\) has
  \(\sigma(t) = \sigma_0\sqrt{1 + (\hbar t/2m\sigma_0^2)^2}\): unit 20's spreading
  law with \(\omega'' = \hbar/m\). A \(10^{-15}\) kg grain at \(1\ \mu\)m doubles
  in about a year. /Slide: Free evolution, solved by Fourier./
- Hermite's equation :: \(h'' - 2\xi h' + (\varepsilon - 1)h = 0\), what
  \(h\) obeys in \(\varphi = he^{-\xi^2/2}\). Its power series grows like
  \(e^{\xi^2}\) unless it terminates, which happens iff \(\varepsilon = 2n + 1\).
  /Slide: Worked: the series, and why it must stop./
- Hermite functions :: \(\varphi_n = H_n(x/\ell)e^{-x^2/2\ell^2}/\sqrt{2^nn!
  \sqrt\pi\,\ell}\), with \(H_0 = 1\), \(H_1 = 2\xi\), \(H_2 = 4\xi^2 - 2\),
  \(H_3 = 8\xi^3 - 12\xi\). Orthonormal, \(n\) nodes each, complete in
  \(L^2(\mathbb R)\). /Slide: Hermite functions./

* The oscillator by algebra

- ladder operators :: \(a = (m\omega X + iP)/\sqrt{2m\hbar\omega}\) and its
  adjoint \(a^\dagger\), with \([a, a^\dagger] = 1\) and \(H = \hbar\omega(a^\dagger a
  + \frac12)\). \(a\) lowers an eigenvalue of \(a^\dagger a\) by one, \(a^\dagger\)
  raises it. The quantised complex amplitude of the classical oscillator.
  /Slide: Ladder operators./
- number operator :: \(N = a^\dagger a\ge0\). Its spectrum is \(\{0, 1, 2,
  \dots\}\), because lowering would otherwise reach a negative eigenvalue; the
  bottom rung solves \(a\varphi_0 = 0\).

* WKB revisited: quantisation and tunnelling

- Bohr–Sommerfeld rule :: \(\oint p\,\mathrm dq = 2\pi\hbar(n + \frac12)\) for a
  well with two smooth turning points: the two WKB waves connected from either
  end agree only then. Exact for the oscillator, asymptotic in \(n\) for other
  wells. /Slide: Bohr–Sommerfeld, with its ½./
- Maslov index :: \(\mu\) in \(\oint p\,\mathrm dq = 2\pi\hbar(n + \mu/4)\): the
  number of quarter-phases picked up at the turning points. \(\mu = 2\) for two
  smooth turning points, \(4\) for two hard walls, \(3\) for one of each.
- tunnelling :: Transmission through a region where \(V > E\). WKB gives
  \(T\approx\exp(-\frac2\hbar\int\sqrt{2m(V - E)}\,\mathrm dx)\), valid when the
  exponent is large and \(V\) is smooth near the turning points. /Slide:
  Tunnelling./
- Gamow factor :: The tunnelling exponent through a nucleus's Coulomb wall,
  \(2\Gamma\), with \(\Gamma\to\pi\eta = 2\pi Z\alpha c/v\) for a point nucleus
  (Gamow; Gurney and Condon, 1928). It makes \(^{238}\)U and \(^{212}\)Po,
  a factor two apart in energy, 24 orders of magnitude apart in half-life.
  /Slide: Alpha decay: the Gamow factor./

* Correspondence is asymptotics

- correspondence principle :: For highly excited states, local averages of
  \(|\varphi_n|^2\) converge to the classical density \(2/Tv\), weakly as
  \(n\to\infty\): WKB plus Riemann–Lebesgue. Pointwise the quantum density
  oscillates between 0 and twice the classical one. /Slide: n = 20, plotted./
