The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: still air at \(20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C\) and one atmosphere,
\(p_0 = 101\,325\) Pa, \(\rho_0 = 1.204\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}\), \(\gamma = 1.400\).
Its sound speed is \(\sqrt{\gamma p_0/\rho_0} = 343.2\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)
(Laplace, adiabatic), against Newton's isothermal \(\sqrt{p_0/\rho_0} = 290.1\
\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), which is \(15.5\%\) low.

* Stress: the force on a surface

- continuum :: A body described by fields — density, velocity, stress — defined at
  every point, with the molecules forgotten. Legitimate when the scales of interest
  are far larger than the mean free path (about \(0.07\ \mu\mathrm m\) in air).
  /Slide: One gas, one number, two closures./
- body force :: A force acting on the bulk of a region, \(\int_P \rho\,\vec b\,\mathrm
  dV\), with \(\vec b\) per unit mass; gravity is the example, \(\vec b = \vec g\).
  /Slide: The force on a piece of a body is not a vector./
- surface force :: The force exerted across the boundary of a region by the material
  outside it, \(\oint_{\partial P}\vec t\,\mathrm dA\). The continuum idealisation of
  short-range molecular forces.
- traction :: \(\vec t\), the surface force per unit area across a surface at a point,
  in pascals. A priori a function of the surface's orientation, not a single vector.
- Cauchy's postulate :: The hypothesis that the traction depends on a surface only
  through its outward unit normal: \(\vec t = \vec t(\vec x, t, \vec n)\), continuous in
  \((\vec x, \vec n)\) jointly. Noll (1959) derived it from the balance law.
- Cauchy's stress theorem :: Under the postulate and momentum balance, the traction is
  linear in the normal: \(\vec t(\vec n) = \sigma\vec n\), \(t_i = \sigma_{ij}n_j\), with
  \(\sigma_{ij} = \vec e_i\cdot\vec t(\vec e_j)\). Proved by the tetrahedron argument:
  surface forces scale as area, volume forces as length cubed, so on a shrinking
  tetrahedron the surface forces must balance, and balance on a tetrahedron is
  linearity. /Slide: Cauchy's theorem./
- stress tensor :: The bilinear form \(\sigma(\vec m, \vec n) = \vec m\cdot\vec
  t(\vec n) = m_i\sigma_{ij}n_j\): a (0,2)-tensor at each point, and a tensor field as
  the point varies. Its components transform as \(\sigma' = R^{\top}\sigma R\) under a
  rotation of basis — a theorem, from bilinearity. Physically, the flux of momentum.
  /Slide: Stress is a bilinear form./
- normal stress :: \(\sigma(\vec n, \vec n)\), the component of the traction along the
  normal: positive for a pull (tension), negative for a push (compression).
- shear stress :: The part of the traction tangent to the surface, \(\vec t -
  \sigma(\vec n,\vec n)\,\vec n\).
- principal stresses :: The eigenvalues of the symmetric stress tensor. On the planes
  normal to its eigenvectors the traction is purely normal.
- fluid :: A material that at rest sustains no shear stress on any plane; for small
  motions, an elastic material with shear modulus \(\mu = 0\).
  /Slide: Pressure is the isotropic part./
- pressure :: \(p = -\tfrac13\operatorname{tr}\sigma\), minus the mean normal stress.
  Basis-independent because the trace is.
- isotropic part :: \(-pI\), the part of the stress that looks the same in every
  rotated basis.
- deviatoric stress :: \(\tau = \sigma + pI\), the trace-free remainder of the stress.
- Pascal's law :: In a fluid at rest \(\sigma = -pI\): the traction on every plane is
  \(-p\vec n\). Proof: every vector is an eigenvector of \(\sigma\), so \(\sigma\) is
  scalar.

* Conservation laws in local form

- divergence of a tensor field :: \((\operatorname{div}T)_i = \partial_jT_{ij}\),
  taken on the second slot; basis-free, \(\vec a\cdot\operatorname{div}T =
  \nabla\cdot(T^{\top}\vec a)\) for constant \(\vec a\). It satisfies
  \(\oint_{\partial\Omega}T\vec n\,\mathrm dA = \int_\Omega\operatorname{div}T\,\mathrm
  dV\). /Slide: The divergence theorem for a tensor field./
- mass flux :: \(\rho\vec v\), the mass crossing unit area per unit time.
  /Slide: Mass: from a budget to a field equation./
- localisation lemma :: If \(f\) is continuous and its integral over every ball is
  zero, then \(f \equiv 0\). The step that turns an integral budget into a PDE.
- continuity equation :: \(\partial_t\rho + \nabla\cdot(\rho\vec v) = 0\): local
  conservation of mass.
- Eulerian description :: Fluid fields as functions of fixed points of space and time.
  /Slide: The material derivative./
- Lagrangian description :: Following each particle along its path \(\dot{\vec x} =
  \vec v(\vec x, t)\).
- material derivative :: \(\mathrm Df/\mathrm Dt = \partial_tf + \vec v\cdot\nabla
  f\), the rate of change of \(f\) seen by a moving particle; the fluid acceleration is
  \(\partial_t\vec v + (\vec v\cdot\nabla)\vec v\).
- incompressible :: Each particle keeps its density, \(\mathrm D\rho/\mathrm Dt = 0\);
  by continuity, equivalent to \(\nabla\cdot\vec v = 0\). Not the same as uniform
  density.
- flow map :: \(\varphi_t\), sending each particle's initial position to its position at
  time \(t\): \(\partial_t\varphi_t = \vec v(\varphi_t, t)\), \(\varphi_0 = \mathrm{id}\).
  /Slide: The Reynolds transport theorem./
- material region :: \(\Omega_t = \varphi_t(\Omega_0)\), a region made of the same
  particles at every time.
- Reynolds transport theorem :: \(\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\int_{\Omega_t}f =
  \int_{\Omega_t}(\mathrm Df/\mathrm Dt + f\nabla\cdot\vec v) = \int_{\Omega_t}\partial_tf +
  \oint_{\partial\Omega_t}f\vec v\cdot\vec n\). With continuity, \(\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm
  dt}\int\rho g = \int\rho\,\mathrm Dg/\mathrm Dt\). Not unit 03's transport theorem for
  rotating frames.
- Euler's expansion formula :: \(\partial_tJ = (\nabla\cdot\vec v)J\) for the Jacobian
  determinant \(J = \det D\varphi_t\) of the flow map: divergence is the rate of volume
  growth.
- Jacobi's formula :: \(\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\det F = \det F\,
  \operatorname{tr}(F^{-1}\dot F)\), from \(\det(I + hB) = 1 + h\operatorname{tr}B +
  O(h^2)\).
- Cauchy's equation of motion :: \(\rho\,\mathrm D\vec v/\mathrm Dt =
  \operatorname{div}\sigma + \rho\vec b\): local momentum balance, from the integral
  balance by the transport theorem, the tensor divergence theorem and localisation.
  /Slide: Momentum: Cauchy's equation of motion./
- momentum flux tensor :: \(\Pi_{ij} = \rho v_iv_j - \sigma_{ij}\), in the
  conservation form \(\partial_t(\rho v_i) + \partial_j\Pi_{ij} = \rho b_i\): momentum
  carried by moving matter plus momentum transmitted by stress.
- hydrostatic equilibrium :: \(\nabla p = \rho\vec g\) in a fluid at rest; in water,
  \(p = p_{\text{atm}} + \rho_wgd\), one atmosphere more every \(10.35\) m.
  /Slide: Archimedes, from the divergence theorem./
- Archimedes' principle :: A fluid at rest pushes on an immersed body with an upward
  force equal to the weight of the displaced fluid, whatever the body's shape — the
  tensor divergence theorem applied to \(-pI\).
- angular momentum balance :: The torque on every material region equals the rate of
  change of its angular momentum, written with the antisymmetric pairs \(x_ip_j -
  x_jp_i\) in place of cross products. /Slide: Angular momentum makes stress symmetric./
- symmetry of stress :: \(\sigma_{ij} = \sigma_{ji}\), proved from angular momentum
  balance when every torque is a moment of forces.
- body couple :: A torque per unit volume acting on the material directly, not as a
  moment of forces — as on a ferrofluid in a magnetic field. Its presence makes the
  antisymmetric part of the stress nonzero: \(\sigma_{ij} - \sigma_{ji} = m_{ij}\).

* Closing the equations

- constitutive law :: The material-specific relation giving the stress in terms of the
  motion; it supplies the six equations the conservation laws lack.
  /Slide: Ten unknowns, four equations./
- ideal fluid :: A fluid with \(\sigma = -pI\) even in motion: no viscosity.
- Euler's equations :: \(\partial_t\rho + \nabla\cdot(\rho\vec v) = 0\), \(\rho\,\mathrm
  D\vec v/\mathrm Dt = -\nabla p + \rho\vec b\): the ideal fluid (Euler, 1757).
- equation of state :: The material relation among pressure, density and temperature
  that closes Euler's equations.
- barotropic :: Of a fluid or a motion: the pressure is a function of the density
  alone, \(p = P(\rho)\).
- displacement field :: \(\vec u(\vec X) = \vec x - \vec X\), how far the particle
  from reference position \(\vec X\) has moved. /Slide: Strain./
- infinitesimal rotation :: The antisymmetric part \(\omega\) of \(D\vec u\); to first
  order it changes no lengths.
- strain tensor :: \(\varepsilon = \tfrac12(D\vec u + D\vec u^{\top})\), a symmetric
  bilinear form; \(\varepsilon(\vec a,\vec a)\) is the fractional stretch of a fibre along
  the unit vector \(\vec a\).
- dilatation :: \(\operatorname{tr}\varepsilon = \nabla\cdot\vec u\), the fractional
  change of volume.
- elasticity tensor :: The linear map \(C\) with \(\sigma = C(\varepsilon)\): a
  (0,4)-tensor, 36 constants in general, 21 if an elastic energy exists.
  /Slide: Isotropy leaves two constants./
- isotropic material :: One with \(C(Q\varepsilon Q^{\top}) = Q\,C(\varepsilon)\,Q^{\top}\)
  for every rotation \(Q\): its response does not depend on orientation.
- Lamé parameters :: The two constants \(\lambda, \mu\) to which isotropy reduces a
  linear elastic material: \(C(\varepsilon) = \lambda(\operatorname{tr}\varepsilon)I +
  2\mu\varepsilon\). A theorem, proved with half-turns and quarter-turns.
- Hooke's law :: \(\sigma = \lambda(\operatorname{tr}\varepsilon)I + 2\mu\varepsilon =
  K(\operatorname{tr}\varepsilon)I + 2\mu\varepsilon_{\text{dev}}\) for an isotropic
  linear elastic solid. /Slide: Hooke's law and its two moduli./
- bulk modulus :: \(K = \lambda + \tfrac23\mu\): pressure per fractional decrease in
  volume, \(p = -K\,\Delta V/V\). For a gas, \(K = \rho\,\mathrm dp/\mathrm d\rho\);
  adiabatically \(\gamma p\).
- shear modulus :: \(\mu\): shear stress per radian of shear. Zero for a fluid.
- strain energy density :: \(W = \tfrac12K(\operatorname{tr}\varepsilon)^2 +
  \mu|\varepsilon_{\text{dev}}|^2\), with \(\sigma = \partial W/\partial\varepsilon\).
  Positive definite iff \(K > 0\) and \(\mu > 0\), which forces \(\lambda + \mu > 0\).
- rate-of-strain tensor :: \(e = \tfrac12(D\vec v + D\vec v^{\top})\), the symmetric
  part of the velocity gradient. /Slide: Viscosity: the Navier–Stokes equations./
- Newtonian fluid :: One whose viscous stress is linear and isotropic in the
  rate of strain: \(\tau = 2\eta(e - \tfrac13(\operatorname{tr}e)I) +
  \zeta(\operatorname{tr}e)I\).
- viscosity :: \(\eta\), in Pa s: \(1.002\times10^{-3}\) for water and \(1.81\times10^{-5}\)
  for air at \(20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C\).
- bulk viscosity :: \(\zeta\), the resistance to the rate of volume change. Zero for a
  monatomic gas (Stokes' hypothesis).
- Navier–Stokes equations :: \(\rho\,\mathrm D\vec v/\mathrm Dt = -\nabla p +
  \eta\nabla^2\vec v + (\zeta + \tfrac13\eta)\nabla(\nabla\cdot\vec v) + \rho\vec b\);
  the viscous term \(\eta\nabla^2\vec v\) is momentum diffusing with diffusivity \(\nu =
  \eta/\rho\).

* Sound

- linearisation :: Writing each field as a rest value plus a small perturbation and
  dropping every product of perturbations. /Slide: Linearising about rest./
- wave equation :: Here \(\partial_t^2\rho' = c^2\nabla^2\rho'\) with \(c^2 = \mathrm
  dp/\mathrm d\rho\) at the rest density: unit 18's equation in three dimensions.
- longitudinal wave :: A wave whose displacement or velocity points along its direction
  of travel. Sound in a fluid is one: \(\vec v = (p'/\rho_0c)\,\vec n\).
- ideal gas law :: \(p = \rho RT/M\), with \(R = 8.314\ \mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}\) and
  \(M\) the molar mass. Empirical; cited. /Slide: The closure: how does pressure follow
  density?/
- isothermal :: At constant temperature. Newton's hypothesis for sound.
- Boyle's law :: \(p \propto \rho\) at fixed temperature (1662).
- adiabatic :: With no heat exchanged. For an ideal gas it gives \(p \propto
  \rho^\gamma\), and it is Laplace's (correct) hypothesis for sound.
- adiabatic index :: \(\gamma = c_p/c_V = 1 + R/c_V\); \(\tfrac75\) for a diatomic gas
  such as air, \(\tfrac53\) for a monatomic one.
- thermal diffusivity :: \(\kappa = k/\rho c_p\), in \(\mathrm{m^2s^{-1}}\): heat
  spreads a distance \(L\) in a time of order \(L^2/\kappa\). \(2.14\times10^{-5}\
  \mathrm{m^2s^{-1}}\) for air at \(20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C\). /Slide: Newton's error, and
  Laplace's repair./
- acoustic energy density :: \(e = \tfrac12\rho_0|\vec v|^2 + p'^2/2\rho_0c^2\), kinetic
  plus compressional energy per unit volume in linear acoustics.
  /Slide: Energy in a sound wave, and a preview of the stress–energy tensor./
- acoustic intensity :: \(\vec S = p'\vec v\), the energy flux of a sound wave, with
  \(\partial_te + \nabla\cdot\vec S = 0\).
- stress–energy tensor :: \(T^{\mu\nu}\), the symmetric \(4\times4\) object of units
  29 and 35 assembling energy density, energy flux and momentum flux;
  \(\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0\) is local conservation of energy and momentum. Flagged
  here, not used.
- perfect fluid :: The relativistic name for the ideal fluid; at rest its
  stress–energy tensor is \(\operatorname{diag}(\rho c^2, p, p, p)\), \(c\) the speed of
  light.
- acoustic impedance :: \(\rho_0c\), the ratio \(p'/|\vec v|\) in a plane sound wave:
  \(413.3\ \mathrm{Pa\,s\,m^{-1}}\) for air at \(20\,{}^\circ\mathrm C\).

* Waves in solids, and the Earth's liquid core

- Navier's equation :: \(\rho\,\partial_t^2\vec u = (\lambda + \mu)\nabla(\nabla\cdot\vec
  u) + \mu\nabla^2\vec u\), small motions of an isotropic elastic solid.
  /Slide: Navier's equation for an elastic solid./
- plane wave :: A solution \(\vec u = \vec a\,f(\vec n\cdot\vec x - ct)\): a fixed profile
  travelling along \(\vec n\) at speed \(c\) with polarisation \(\vec a\).
  /Slide: Plane waves: two speeds and no third./
- acoustic tensor :: \(A(\vec n) = (\lambda + \mu)\,\vec n\otimes\vec n + \mu I\), whose
  eigenvalues are \(\rho c^2\) for the plane waves travelling along \(\vec n\).
- P-wave :: The longitudinal (primary) elastic wave, \(c_L = \sqrt{(\lambda +
  2\mu)/\rho}\). The first to arrive.
- S-wave :: The transverse (secondary) elastic wave, \(c_T = \sqrt{\mu/\rho}\), with two
  polarisations. Always slower: \(c_L/c_T > \sqrt{4/3}\).
- transverse wave :: A wave whose displacement is perpendicular to its direction of
  travel. Needs a shear modulus, so no fluid carries one.
- S-wave shadow zone :: The region beyond about \(103^\circ\) of arc from an earthquake
  where no direct S-waves arrive, because they cannot cross the liquid outer core.
  /Slide: Why only solids carry shear waves./
- outer core :: The Earth's liquid iron layer, from \(2891\) km to \(5150\) km depth:
  \(c_T = 0\).
- core–mantle boundary :: The surface at \(2891\) km depth (radius \(3480\) km) where the
  solid mantle meets the liquid core.
