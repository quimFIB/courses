The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: a parallel-plate capacitor with circular plates of radius
\(a = 5.00\) cm and gap \(d = 1.00\) mm, charged by a steady current
\(I = 1.00\) A. Between the plates the displacement current density is
\(\varepsilon_0\partial_tE = I/\pi a^2 = 127.3\) A m\(^{-2}\) and the magnetic field
is \(B = \mu_0Is/2\pi a^2\), reaching \(4.00\ \mu\)T at the rim. Energy enters
through the rim at \(VI\); in Coulomb gauge its potentials are
\(\varphi = -E(t)z\), \(\vec A = -\tfrac12(\mu_0I/2\pi a^2)s^2\hat z\). The two
constants that fix its fields also fix \(c = 1/\sqrt{\varepsilon_0\mu_0} = 2.998\times10^8\) m s\(^{-1}\).

* Faraday: a changing magnetic field curls the electric one

- electromotive force :: \(\mathcal E = \oint_{\partial S}\vec E\cdot\mathrm d\vec r\), the work per
  unit charge done by the electric field once round a closed curve, in volts.
  Zero for a static field, which is a gradient. /Slide: Faraday's law./
- Faraday's law :: The measured statement that for every fixed surface \(S\),
  \(\oint_{\partial S}\vec E\cdot\mathrm d\vec r = -\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\int_S\vec B\cdot\mathrm d\vec A\);
  by Stokes' theorem and localisation it is equivalent to
  \(\nabla\times\vec E = -\partial_t\vec B\). The minus sign is Lenz's law.
- continuity equation :: \(\partial_t\rho + \nabla\cdot\vec J = 0\), the local form of
  charge conservation, from unit 23. Ampère's law as unit 23 left it is
  consistent with it only when \(\partial_t\rho = 0\).
  /Slide: Four equations, one of them suspect./

* Maxwell's repair

- displacement current :: The term \(\mu_0\varepsilon_0\partial_t\vec E\) added to Ampère's
  law, \(\nabla\times\vec B = \mu_0\vec J + \mu_0\varepsilon_0\partial_t\vec E\). Its divergence is
  \(\mu_0\partial_t\rho\), which is exactly what continuity requires; between the
  plates of a charging capacitor \(\varepsilon_0\partial_t\vec E\) carries the current
  across the gap. /Slide: Worked: the displacement current, from continuity alone./
- mirror symmetry (parity) :: Invariance of the laws under \(\vec x\mapsto-\vec x\).
  Under it \(\vec E\mapsto-\vec E\) and \(\vec B\mapsto+\vec B\), as the Lorentz force
  requires. Together with linearity, locality and vanishing in statics it makes
  the displacement current the only possible repair. /Slide: Exactly one term./

* The complete system: energy and momentum

- Maxwell's equations :: \(\nabla\cdot\vec E = \rho/\varepsilon_0\), \(\nabla\cdot\vec B = 0\),
  \(\nabla\times\vec E = -\partial_t\vec B\), \(\nabla\times\vec B = \mu_0\vec J + \mu_0\varepsilon_0\partial_t\vec E\).
  With the Lorentz force they are all of classical electromagnetism, and they
  imply charge conservation. /Slide: Maxwell's equations./
- evolution equations :: The two curl equations, which contain \(\partial_t\) and
  step \((\vec E,\vec B)\) forward in time.
- constraints :: The two divergence equations, which contain no time derivative
  and restrict the data at each instant. If they hold initially the evolution
  equations preserve them, given charge conservation.
- field energy density :: \(u = \frac{\varepsilon_0}{2}|\vec E|^2 + \frac{1}{2\mu_0}|\vec B|^2\), in
  J m\(^{-3}\). /Slide: Poynting's theorem: energy, locally./
- Poynting vector :: \(\vec S = \vec E\times\vec B/\mu_0\), the energy flux density of the
  field, in W m\(^{-2}\).
- Poynting's theorem :: \(\partial_tu + \nabla\cdot\vec S = -\vec J\cdot\vec E\): field
  energy is conserved locally except for the work the field does on charges.
  Only \(\nabla\cdot\vec S\) is fixed by it.
- Maxwell stress tensor :: At each point the symmetric bilinear form
  \(T(\vec u,\vec w) = \varepsilon_0[(\vec E\cdot\vec u)(\vec E\cdot\vec w) - \frac12|\vec E|^2\vec u\cdot\vec w] + \mu_0^{-1}[(\vec B\cdot\vec u)(\vec B\cdot\vec w) - \frac12|\vec B|^2\vec u\cdot\vec w]\).
  \(T(\hat n,\cdot)\) is the force per unit area transmitted across a surface with
  normal \(\hat n\); minus \(T\) is the flux of field momentum. Its components change
  by \(T' = R^\top TR\), a theorem from bilinearity.
  /Slide: Momentum, and the Maxwell stress tensor./
- tensor field :: A tensor attached to each point of a region, varying with the
  point: here a symmetric bilinear form on \(\mathbb R^3\) at every point.
- field momentum density :: \(\vec g = \varepsilon_0\vec E\times\vec B = \vec S/c^2\). With the
  stress tensor it satisfies \(\partial_t\vec g - \operatorname{div}T = -(\rho\vec E + \vec J\times\vec B)\).

* Light

- wave operator :: \(\Box = c^{-2}\partial_t^2 - \nabla^2\), with \(c = 1/\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}\).
  In vacuum every Cartesian component of \(\vec E\) and \(\vec B\) satisfies \(\Box f = 0\).
  /Slide: The vacuum wave equation./
- d'Alembertian :: Another name for the wave operator \(\Box\). Sign conventions
  differ between books.
- plane wave :: A field \(\operatorname{Re}(\vec E_0e^{i(\vec k\cdot\vec x - \omega t)})\) with constant complex
  amplitude. It solves Maxwell's equations in vacuum iff \(\omega = c|\vec k|\),
  \(\vec k\cdot\vec E_0 = 0\) and \(\vec B_0 = \vec k\times\vec E_0/\omega\). /Slide: Plane waves./
- transverse :: Of a wave: with fields perpendicular to the direction of
  propagation. Light is transverse, \(\vec E\perp\vec B\perp\vec k\), and \(|\vec B| = |\vec E|/c\).
- polarisation :: For a wave along \(\hat z\), the complex line through the
  amplitude \(\vec E_0\) in the plane perpendicular to \(\hat z\). The tip of \(\vec E\)
  traces an ellipse; linear and circular are the degenerate and round cases.
  /Slide: Polarisation./

* Potentials and gauge

- potentials :: A scalar \(\varphi\) and a vector \(\vec A\) with \(\vec B = \nabla\times\vec A\) and
  \(\vec E = -\nabla\varphi - \partial_t\vec A\). On \(\mathbb R^3\) they exist iff the homogeneous
  pair of Maxwell's equations holds. /Slide: Potentials, and what they fail to determine./
- gauge transformation :: \((\varphi,\vec A)\mapsto(\varphi - \partial_t\chi,\ \vec A + \nabla\chi)\). Two pairs
  of potentials give the same fields iff they differ by one: the fields are the
  potentials modulo gauge.
- Lorenz gauge :: The condition \(\nabla\cdot\vec A + c^{-2}\partial_t\varphi = 0\), under which
  \(\Box\varphi = \rho/\varepsilon_0\) and \(\Box\vec A = \mu_0\vec J\). Named for Ludvig Lorenz.
  /Slide: The Lorenz gauge: two wave equations./
- residual gauge freedom :: The gauge transformations that preserve a gauge
  condition. In Lorenz gauge they are those with \(\Box\chi = 0\).
- Coulomb gauge :: The condition \(\nabla\cdot\vec A = 0\). In it \(\varphi\) is the
  instantaneous Coulomb potential of the charge present at each moment, while
  \(\vec E\) remains causal. /Slide: The Coulomb gauge: an instantaneous potential./

* The retarded Green's function

- fundamental solution :: A distribution \(G\) with \(\Box G = \delta^{(4)}\); then
  \(G * f\) solves \(\Box u = f\). Its transform is \(c^2/(c^2k^2 - \omega^2)\), an
  undamped oscillator of frequency \(ck\) for each wave vector.
  /Slide: Every mode is an oscillator./
- \(i0\) prescription :: The choice \(\omega\mapsto\omega + i0\) in inverting a transform with
  poles on the real axis: poles pushed below, contour passing above. It selects
  the solution that vanishes before the source acts.
- retarded Green's function :: \(G_\mathrm{ret}(\vec x,t) = \delta(t - r/c)/4\pi r\), the
  unique fundamental solution of \(\Box\) vanishing for \(t < 0\), supported on the
  sphere \(r = ct\). /Slide: Worked: the retarded Green's function by residues./
- advanced Green's function :: \(G_\mathrm{adv}(\vec x,t) = \delta(t + r/c)/4\pi r\), from
  passing below the poles: a wave converging on the source. It solves the same
  equation and is excluded by the condition that no radiation comes in from the
  infinite past. /Slide: Why this prescription is the causal one./
- retarded time :: \(t_\mathrm r = t - |\vec x - \vec y|/c\), the time at which a signal at
  speed \(c\) left \(\vec y\) to reach \(\vec x\) at time \(t\). /Slide: Retarded potentials./
- retarded potentials :: \(\varphi = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\int\rho(\vec y,t_\mathrm r)/|\vec x - \vec y|\) and
  \(\vec A = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\int\vec J(\vec y,t_\mathrm r)/|\vec x - \vec y|\). They satisfy the Lorenz
  condition because charge is conserved, and so solve Maxwell's equations.
- spherical mean :: \(M_r[h](\vec x)\), the average of \(h\) over the sphere of
  radius \(r\) about \(\vec x\). Darboux's identity says it satisfies
  \(\partial_r^2M + \frac2r\partial_rM = \nabla_x^2M\).
  /Slide: Kirchhoff's formula, and sharp wavefronts./
- Kirchhoff's formula :: The solution of \(\Box u = 0\) with data \(u = g\),
  \(u_t = h\) is \(\partial_t(t\,M_{ct}[g]) + t\,M_{ct}[h]\).
- strong Huygens principle :: In three dimensions the solution at \((\vec x,t)\)
  depends on the data only on the sphere \(|\vec y - \vec x| = ct\), so a localised
  disturbance passes with a sharp front and a sharp back.
- Hadamard's method of descent :: Obtaining the solution in fewer dimensions
  from one in more, by taking data independent of the extra coordinate. It gives
  \(G_2 = cH(ct - \varrho)/2\pi\sqrt{c^2t^2 - \varrho^2}\), supported inside the cone: in two
  dimensions a flash has a tail. /Slide: Descent: in two dimensions a flash has a tail./

* Radiation

- dipole approximation :: For sources of size \(d\) small compared with the
  distance and with \(c\) times their time scale,
  \(\vec A\approx\mu_0\dot{\vec p}(t - r/c)/4\pi r\), with \(\vec p\) the electric dipole moment.
  /Slide: Far from a small source./
- radiation field :: The part of the field falling off as \(1/r\):
  \(\vec B = \mu_0\ddot{\vec p}\times\hat r/4\pi cr\), \(\vec E = c\vec B\times\hat r\). It alone carries energy to
  infinity.
- Larmor formula :: \(P = q^2|\vec a|^2/6\pi\varepsilon_0c^3\), the power radiated by a slowly
  moving accelerated charge, distributed as \(\sin^2\theta\). Applied to a classical
  atom it predicts collapse in \(1.56\times10^{-11}\) s. /Slide: The Larmor formula./
