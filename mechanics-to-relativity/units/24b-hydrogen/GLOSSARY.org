The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: hydrogen, one electron bound to one proton by \(V = -k/r\)
with \(k = e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0 = 2.3071\times10^{-28}\) J m. Its ground state
three ways: the uncertainty estimate (\(a_0 = 0.5292\) Å, \(-13.61\) eV), the
exact \(1s\) state \(\psi = e^{-r/a}/\sqrt{\pi a^3}\) with \(\langle r\rangle = 3a/2\),
and a Gaussian trial function giving \(-\tfrac{8}{3\pi}\) Ry \(= -11.55\) eV, an
upper bound 15.1 per cent high. Its light: H-alpha, \(n = 3\to2\), at
\(656.470\) nm in vacuum and \(656.288\) nm in air with the reduced mass, against
the measured \(656.28\) nm; deuterium's line sits \(0.1786\) nm to the blue.

* The collapse, and why ħ stops it

- Bohr radius :: \(a_0 = \hbar^2/m_ek = \hbar/m_ec\alpha = 5.2918\times10^{-11}\) m,
  with \(k = e^2/4\pi\varepsilon_0\): the only length that can be built from
  \(m_e\), \(k\) and \(\hbar\). Without \(\hbar\) there is none, which is why a
  classical atom has no size. With the reduced mass it becomes
  \(a = \hbar^2/\mu k = 1.000545\,a_0\). /Slide: No length without ħ./
- Rydberg energy :: \(\mathrm{Ry} = m_ek^2/2\hbar^2 = \tfrac12\alpha^2m_ec^2 =
  13.6057\) eV, hydrogen's binding energy for an infinitely heavy nucleus;
  \(13.5983\) eV with the reduced mass.
- fine-structure constant :: \(\alpha = k/\hbar c = 1/137.036\), a pure number:
  the electron's speed on the first orbit in units of \(c\), so \(\alpha^2\)
  measures how relativistic hydrogen is.
- Hardy's inequality :: \(\int\lvert\nabla\psi\rvert^2 \ge \tfrac14\int\lvert\psi\rvert^2/r^2\)
  for smooth compactly supported \(\psi\) on \(\mathbb R^3\), proved by expanding
  \(\int\lvert\nabla\psi + \tfrac12(\vec x/r^2)\psi\rvert^2 \ge 0\) and the divergence
  theorem. With Cauchy–Schwarz it bounds hydrogen's energy below by
  \(-4\,\mathrm{Ry} = -54.4\) eV: the uncertainty principle's stabilisation of
  the atom, as a theorem. /Slide: Exact by luck in the constant, not in the scaling./

* Angular momentum, from its commutators alone

- orbital angular momentum :: The operators \(L_i = \varepsilon_{ijk}x_jp_k\) on
  \(L^2(\mathbb R^3)\), with \(p_k = -i\hbar\partial_k\). They satisfy
  \([L_i, L_j] = i\hbar\varepsilon_{ijk}L_k\), which follows from
  \([x_j,p_k] = i\hbar\delta_{jk}\) alone and is unit 14's Poisson bracket under
  Dirac's rule. /Slide: Angular momentum as an operator./
- rotation operator :: \((U(R)\psi)(\vec x) = \psi(R^{-1}\vec x)\) for
  \(R\in SO(3)\): unitary, a representation of the rotation group, and equal
  to \(\exp(-i\theta\,\hat n\cdot\vec L/\hbar)\) for rotation by \(\theta\) about
  \(\hat n\). The \(-iL_k/\hbar\) represent \(\mathfrak{so}(3)\).
  /Slide: \(\vec L\) generates rotations./
- ladder operators :: \(L_\pm = L_x\pm iL_y\), with \([L_z, L_\pm] = \pm\hbar L_\pm\):
  they raise or lower the eigenvalue of \(L_z\) by \(\hbar\) and leave that of
  \(L^2\) alone. \(L_\mp L_\pm = L^2 - L_z^2\mp\hbar L_z\). /Slide: Ladder operators./
- Casimir operator :: An operator built from the generators of a Lie algebra
  that commutes with all of them; for \(\mathfrak{so}(3)\), \(L^2\). Its
  eigenvalue labels a whole multiplet.
- multiplet :: The \(2l+1\) joint eigenvectors of \(L^2\) and \(L_z\) with
  eigenvalues \(\hbar^2l(l+1)\) and \(\hbar m\), \(m = -l,\dots,l\), joined by
  \(L_\pm\). That \(l\in\{0,\tfrac12,1,\dots\}\) and \(\lvert m\rvert\le l\) follows from
  \(\lVert L_\pm\psi\rVert^2 = \hbar^2(l(l+1) - m(m\pm1))\lVert\psi\rVert^2\ge0\).
  /Slide: The spectrum of \(L^2\) and \(L_z\), from the algebra alone./
- spin :: An internal angular momentum of the electron with quantum number \(\tfrac12\):
  allowed by the commutation relations, excluded for functions on space by
  single-valuedness (\(L_z = -i\hbar\partial_\phi\) forces \(m\in\mathbb Z\)). Named,
  not developed: the course excludes spinors.
  /Slide: Integers only: single-valuedness, and the half-integers that are spin./
- Stern–Gerlach experiment :: Stern and Gerlach, 1922: a beam of silver atoms
  in a non-uniform magnetic field splits into two, an even number \(2l+1\)
  that no integer \(l\) gives. The first sight of spin.
- spherical harmonics :: \(Y_l^m(\theta,\phi) = N_{lm}P_l^m(\cos\theta)e^{im\phi}\),
  unit 19's eigenfunctions of the angular Laplacian, here the joint
  eigenfunctions of \(L^2 = -\hbar^2\Delta_{S^2}\) and \(L_z = -i\hbar\partial_\phi\),
  built from unit 10's Legendre polynomials.
  /Slide: Spherical harmonics are the joint eigenfunctions./

* The radial equation, and where quantisation comes from

- reduced mass :: \(\mu = m_em_p/(m_e + m_p)\). Unit 08's change of variables,
  applied to operators, separates the two-body Hamiltonian into a free centre
  of mass and a one-body problem of mass \(\mu\). For hydrogen
  \(\mu = m_e/1.000544617\). /Slide: Two bodies become one: the reduced mass./
- radial equation :: \(-\frac{\hbar^2}{2\mu}u'' + \big[\frac{\hbar^2l(l+1)}{2\mu r^2} - \frac kr\big]u = Eu\)
  for \(\psi = (u/r)Y_l^m\), with \(\int_0^\infty\lvert u\rvert^2 < \infty\): a
  one-dimensional Schrödinger equation on the half-line.
  /Slide: Separating hydrogen: the radial equation./
- effective potential :: \(V(r) + \hbar^2l(l+1)/2\mu r^2\): unit 08's
  \(V + L^2/2\mu r^2\) with the classical \(L^2\) replaced by its eigenvalue.
- centrifugal barrier :: The repulsive term \(\hbar^2l(l+1)/2\mu r^2\) of the
  effective potential, which keeps states with \(l > 0\) away from the nucleus.
- Laguerre polynomials :: The polynomial factors \(f\) of the bound radial
  functions, \(u = \rho^{l+1}e^{-\rho/n}f(\rho)\); up to normalisation
  \(f = L^{(2l+1)}_{n-l-1}(2\rho/n)\), the associated Laguerre polynomials. Named, not needed.
  /Slide: The series, and why it must stop./
- principal quantum number :: \(n = N + l + 1\), where \(N\) is the degree at
  which the radial series stops. The bound energies are
  \(E_n = -\mu k^2/2\hbar^2n^2 = -13.6\ \mathrm{eV}/n^2\), and depend on \(n\) alone.
  /Slide: The bound spectrum./
- radial nodes :: The \(N = n - l - 1\) zeros of the radial function in
  \(0 < r < \infty\): two for \(3s\), one for \(3p\), none for \(3d\).
- spectroscopic notation :: The letters \(s, p, d, f\) for \(l = 0, 1, 2, 3\),
  from the old names sharp, principal, diffuse, fundamental; a state is
  written \(n\) then the letter, as in \(1s\), \(2p\).
- virial theorem :: For a bound eigenstate of a \(1/r\) potential,
  \(2\langle T\rangle = -\langle V\rangle\), so \(E = -\langle T\rangle = \tfrac12\langle V\rangle\).
  For the \(1s\) state: \(\langle T\rangle = 13.6\) eV, \(\langle V\rangle = -27.2\) eV.
  Proved by scaling the wavefunction in the variational principle (problem P8).
  /Slide: Worked: the \(1s\) state exactly./

* The variational principle

- variational principle (Rayleigh–Ritz) :: For self-adjoint \(H\) with lowest
  eigenvalue \(E_0\) and a complete eigenbasis,
  \(\langle\psi,H\psi\rangle/\langle\psi,\psi\rangle\ge E_0\) for every nonzero \(\psi\) in
  the domain, with equality only for ground states; an error of order
  \(\varepsilon\) in \(\psi\) gives one of order \(\varepsilon^2\) in the energy.
  For \(H\) merely bounded below, the infimum is the bottom of the spectrum
  (cited, the min–max principle). Unit 19's Rayleigh quotient, promoted.
  /Slide: Every trial function is an upper bound./
- trial function :: A \(\psi\), usually from a family with a few parameters,
  whose quotient \(\langle\psi,H\psi\rangle/\lVert\psi\rVert^2\) is minimised over the
  family. For hydrogen a Gaussian \(e^{-\beta r^2}\) gives at best
  \(-\tfrac8{3\pi}\) Ry \(= -11.55\) eV.

* The degeneracy rotation cannot explain

- accidental degeneracy :: Equality of energies not explained by the obvious
  symmetry. In hydrogen, states of the same \(n\) and different \(l\) share an
  energy, which rotations do not explain; the level holds
  \(\sum_{l<n}(2l+1) = n^2\) states. /Slide: \(n^2\) states on every level./
- quantum Runge–Lenz vector :: \(\vec A = \tfrac12(\vec p\times\vec L - \vec L\times\vec p) - \mu k\,\vec x/r\),
  unit 08's conserved vector symmetrised so that it is a symmetric operator.
  It commutes with \(H\), transforms as a vector under \(\vec L\), and satisfies
  \([A_i,A_j] = -2i\hbar\mu H\varepsilon_{ijk}L_k\) and
  \(A^2 = \mu^2k^2 + 2\mu H(L^2 + \hbar^2)\) (Pauli, 1926).
  /Slide: The quantum Runge–Lenz vector./
- \(\mathfrak{so}(4)\) :: The Lie algebra of rotations of \(\mathbb R^4\), from
  unit 14. On a bound level of energy \(E\), \(\vec L\) and \(\vec M = \vec A/\sqrt{-2\mu E}\)
  satisfy its relations; \(\vec J^\pm = \tfrac12(\vec L\pm\vec M)\) split it into two
  commuting angular-momentum algebras with equal Casimirs \(\hbar^2j(j+1)\),
  which forces \(E = -\mu k^2/2\hbar^2(2j+1)^2\) and \(n^2 = (2j+1)^2\) states.
  /Slide: \(\mathfrak{so}(4)\) on each level, and \(n^2\) out of it./

* The light it emits

- Bohr frequency condition :: A transition between levels emits a single
  photon carrying the energy difference, \(\hbar\omega = E_{n_2} - E_{n_1}\).
  /Slide: The Rydberg formula, and three series./
- Rydberg formula :: \(1/\lambda = R_\mu(1/n_1^2 - 1/n_2^2)\) for the line
  \(n_2\to n_1\), with \(R_\mu = (\mu/m_e)R_\infty\).
- Rydberg constant :: \(R_\infty = \mathrm{Ry}/hc = 1.0973731568\times10^7\ \mathrm m^{-1}\);
  for hydrogen \(R_\mathrm H = R_\infty/(1 + m_e/m_p) = 1.0967758\times10^7\ \mathrm m^{-1}\).
- Lyman series :: The lines ending on \(n_1 = 1\): ultraviolet, from
  \(121.57\) nm to the limit \(91.18\) nm.
- Balmer series :: The lines ending on \(n_1 = 2\): from H-alpha at
  \(656.47\) nm (vacuum) to the limit \(364.71\) nm; the four that Balmer fitted
  in 1885 are visible.
- Paschen series :: The lines ending on \(n_1 = 3\): infrared, from
  \(1875.6\) nm to the limit \(820.59\) nm.
- isotope shift :: The change of a line's wavelength with the nuclear mass,
  through \(\mu\) alone: relative size \(m_e/m_p - m_e/m_d\approx2.72\times10^{-4}\)
  between hydrogen and deuterium, \(0.1786\) nm for H-alpha. Urey, Brickwedde and
  Murphy found deuterium by it in 1932. /Slide: How deuterium was found./
- fine structure :: The splitting and shift of hydrogen's levels at relative
  order \(\alpha^2 = 5.3\times10^{-5}\), from relativistic kinematics and spin–orbit
  coupling; \(0.365\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}\) for \(n = 2\). Where unit 27's relativity
  returns; cited, not derived. /Slide: Where this stops./
- stability of matter :: That the ground-state energy of \(N\) electrons and
  nuclei is bounded below by \(-CN\), so that bulk matter does not collapse or
  release energy when lumps are combined. Needs the exclusion principle
  (Dyson and Lenard 1967; Lieb and Thirring 1975, cited).
- exclusion principle :: No two electrons occupy the same state. Not used in
  this course; named as what turns the stability of one atom into the
  stability of bulk matter.
