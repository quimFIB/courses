The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: the wave operator \(P = \partial_t^2 - c^2\partial_x^2\), which
every component of a vacuum Maxwell field obeys, pushed through a change of
frame. Under Galileo's \(x' = x - vt,\ t' = t\) it becomes
\(\partial_{t'}^2 - 2v\,\partial_{x'}\partial_{t'} - (c^2 - v^2)\partial_{x'}^2\), with
a cross term for every \(v \ne 0\); under the Lorentz boost
\(x' = \gamma(x - vt),\ t' = \gamma(t - vx/c^2)\) it is unchanged.

* Galilean relativity, named at last

- Galilean boost :: The change of frame \(t' = t,\ \vec x' = \vec x - \vec vt\):
  the frame whose origin moves at constant velocity \(\vec v\) without
  rotating. /Slide: The Galilean group, and why Newton cannot see it./
- Galilean group :: The ten-parameter group of maps
  \((t, \vec x) \mapsto (t + \tau,\ R\vec x - \vec vt + \vec a)\) with
  \(R \in SO(3)\): time and space translations, rotations and boosts.
  Velocities add under composition.
- principle of relativity :: The laws of physics take the same form in every
  inertial frame. For a field theory: the equations are covariant under the
  group relating inertial frames. Newton's mechanics satisfies it for the
  Galilean group; Maxwell's equations do not.
- group action on fields :: Given an action \(y \mapsto \varphi_g(y)\) of a
  group on spacetime and a homomorphism \(\rho : G \to GL(V)\), the rule
  \((g\cdot u)(y) = \rho(g)\,u(\varphi_g^{-1}(y))\). The inverse makes it an
  action rather than an anti-action; \(\rho\) says how the components mix.
  /Slide: What "the same law" means for a field./
- covariance (covariant) :: A linear system \(Pu = 0\) is \(G\)-covariant if
  \(g\cdot u\) solves it whenever \(u\) does, for every \(g \in G\): a statement
  about solution sets.
- invariant operator (invariant up to a factor) :: \(P(g\cdot u) =
  \lambda(g)\,g\cdot(Pu)\) with \(\lambda(g) \ne 0\): a statement about the
  operator. It implies covariance; for the wave operator the converse holds too.
- symmetry group :: The set \(\mathrm{Sym}(P)\) of group elements carrying the
  solution set of \(Pu = 0\) onto itself. It is always a subgroup, which is why
  "which changes of frame preserve Maxwell's equations?" has a group for its
  answer. /Slide: Symmetries of an equation form a group./

* The wave operator under a Galilean boost

- Lorentz force :: Unit 23's \(q(\vec E + \vec u\times\vec B)\) on a charge
  with velocity \(\vec u\). Demanding that it be the same force in two Galilean
  frames fixes how the fields would have to transform.
  /Slide: How \(\vec E\) and \(\vec B\) would have to transform./
- Galilean field transformation :: \(\vec B' = \vec B,\ \vec E' = \vec E +
  \vec v\times\vec B\) at the same event: the only rule compatible with a
  frame-independent Lorentz force under Galilean mechanics. It is a
  representation of the boosts on \(\mathbb R^6\).
- magnetic limit (Galilean electromagnetism) :: Maxwell's equations with the
  displacement current dropped. Faraday's law and \(\nabla\cdot\vec B = 0\) are
  exactly Galilean-covariant under the Galilean field transformation, and the
  rest can be made so; the resulting theory is consistent but has no
  electromagnetic waves. Cited: Le Bellac and Lévy-Leblond, 1973.
  /Slide: What survives, and the term that breaks./

* Three ways out, and the experiments that closed them

- ether (luminiferous ether) :: The hypothetical medium in whose rest frame
  alone Maxwell's equations hold, as the string's wave equation holds only in
  the string's rest frame. Light's transverse polarisation made it an elastic
  solid of absurd stiffness with no measurable drag.
  /Slide: The three responses./
- ether frame :: The rest frame of the ether: the one frame in which light
  would travel at \(c\) in every direction.
- stellar aberration :: The yearly displacement of every star towards the
  Earth's direction of motion, by up to \(\arctan(v/c) = 20.49''\). Found by
  Bradley in 1728; it needs light arriving through a medium not dragged by the
  Earth. /Slide: Aberration and Fizeau: the ether must stand still, and not quite./
- Fresnel drag coefficient :: The fraction \(f\) in the lab speed \(c/n + fw\)
  of light in a medium moving at \(w\). Fresnel's value \(f = 1 - 1/n^2\) (0.437
  for water) was confirmed by Fizeau (1851) and by Michelson and Morley (1886,
  \(0.434 \pm 0.02\)); relativity derives it from the composition law.
- Michelson interferometer :: Two perpendicular arms, light split between them
  and recombined; a change in the difference of round-trip times moves the
  fringes. With arms of 11 m an ether wind of 30 km s\(^{-1}\) would move them by
  0.40 fringe on rotating the apparatus.
  /Slide: Worked: Michelson–Morley, the expected shift./
- null result :: Michelson and Morley's 1887 finding: a fringe shift below 0.01,
  "probably less than the fortieth part" of the 0.40 expected; later
  repetitions at other seasons agreed.
- contraction hypothesis (FitzGerald–Lorentz contraction) :: FitzGerald 1889,
  Lorentz 1892: a body moving through the ether at \(v\) shortens along its
  motion by \(\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}\), which makes the two round-trip times equal
  at every speed. A patch, until section 5 derives it from the Lorentz boost.
  /Slide: The scoreboard: no ether fits all three./
- emission theory :: Ritz 1908: light leaves its source at \(c\) relative to the
  source, so its speed adds to the source's by Galileo's rule. Explains
  Michelson–Morley automatically.
  /Slide: Emission theories, and de Sitter's double stars./
- de Sitter's argument :: 1913: under an emission theory, light from a binary
  star emitted while approaching arrives earlier than light emitted while
  receding, by \(\approx 2Du/c^2\) — 244 days for \(u = 100\) km s\(^{-1}\) at
  1000 light-years — scrambling the orbit's appearance. Binary orbits look
  clean; X-ray binaries (Brecher 1977) bound any source dependence below
  \(2\times10^{-9}\).

* Which substitution kills the cross terms

- invariant speed :: A speed that every frame in a family assigns to the same
  motion. For the maps preserving the wave operator it is \(c\), the speed
  written in the operator: \(x = \pm ct\) if and only if \(x' = \pm ct'\).
  /Slide: Worked: solving the two conditions./
- transverse scale factor :: The factor \(s\) in \(y' = sy,\ z' = sz\) for a
  boost along \(x\). Form-invariance of the 3+1 wave operator forces
  \(A^2(1 - \beta^2) = s^2\), which gives \(|v| < c\) and leaves \(s(v)\) free.
  /Slide: Three dimensions: transverse lengths, and |v| < c./
- reciprocity :: The requirement that the inverse of the boost at \(v\) be the
  boost at \(-v\), since the old frame moves at \(-v\) in the new one. With
  isotropy it forces \(s = 1\): Poincaré's argument of 1905.
  /Slide: Fixing s: reciprocity and isotropy./
- Voigt's transformation :: Voigt 1887: \(x' = x - vt,\ t' = t - vx/c^2,\
  y' = y/\gamma,\ z' = z/\gamma\). It preserves the wave operator up to the
  factor \(1/\gamma^2\) — the family's member with \(s = 1/\gamma\) — but is not
  reciprocal, and predicts twice the Ives–Stilwell shift.
- Lorentz boost :: \(x' = \gamma(x - vt),\ t' = \gamma(t - vx/c^2),\ y' = y,\
  z' = z\), with \(|v| < c\). The unique reciprocal, isotropic, boost-shaped
  linear map preserving the wave operator; it preserves it exactly, and reduces
  to the Galilean boost as \(c \to \infty\). /Slide: The Lorentz boost./
- Lorentz factor :: \(\gamma = 1/\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}\). At 30 km s\(^{-1}\),
  \(\gamma - 1 = 5.0\times10^{-9}\).

* The patches form a group

- local time :: Lorentz 1895: the clock reading \(t - vx/c^2\) in a frame moving
  through the ether, which he regarded as a mathematical aid. It is exactly the
  term in the boost that kills the cross term.
  /Slide: Lorentz's patches, recognised./
- velocity composition law :: \(\Lambda(u)\Lambda(v) = \Lambda(w)\) with
  \(w = (u + v)/(1 + uv/c^2)\). Reduces to \(u + v\) as \(c \to \infty\), gives
  \(w = c\) when \(u = c\), and yields Fresnel's drag coefficient at first
  order. /Slide: Boosts compose to boosts./
- Lorentz group :: The group generated by the Lorentz boosts and the rotations
  (Poincaré's name, 1905); with translations added, the group of changes of
  inertial frame that preserves Maxwell's vacuum equations. Unit 26 treats it
  properly.
- conformal group :: The group of all maps whose transport of solutions
  preserves the wave equation, allowing a point-dependent factor. In 1+1 it is
  infinite-dimensional (any reparametrisation of each null coordinate); in 3+1
  it is fifteen-dimensional (cited: Bateman and Cunningham, 1909–10). Only the
  ten Poincaré maps among them are changes of inertial frame.
  /Slide: The full symmetry group of the wave operator./
- test theory :: A deliberately general ether theory, with lengths along and
  across the motion scaled by \(\ell_\parallel(v), \ell_\perp(v)\) and clock
  rates by \(\tau(v)\), in which each experiment becomes one equation for these
  functions (Robertson 1949; Mansouri and Sexl 1977).
  /Slide: Which experiment pins which./
- Kennedy–Thorndike experiment :: 1932: an interferometer with unequal arms,
  watched for months as the Earth's velocity changed. Its null result forces
  \(\tau\,\ell_\perp = 1/\gamma\), killing the contraction hypothesis without
  time dilation.
- Ives–Stilwell experiment :: 1938: the mean wavelength of light from fast
  hydrogen atoms seen forwards and backwards is shifted by
  \(\lambda_0(\gamma - 1) \approx \tfrac12\beta^2\lambda_0\), forcing
  \(\tau = 1/\gamma\) — the one experiment in the triple with a material clock,
  and the one that measures the transverse scale factor.
- time dilation :: A clock moving at \(v\) runs slow by the factor \(1/\gamma\)
  as timed in the frame it moves through. In the family allowed by the wave
  operator the rate is \(s/\gamma\); Ives–Stilwell measures it.
