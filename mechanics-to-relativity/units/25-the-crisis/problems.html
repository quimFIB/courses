The crisis: Maxwell's equations do not survive a Galilean boost

the slides · the curriculum

Checkpoint. You show by direct computation that the wave equation is not Galilean-invariant and is Lorentz-invariant, and derive the boost by demanding form-invariance — without assuming the constancy of \(c\) as a postulate.

Work each problem on paper first. Under it, Hints opens a ladder one rung at a time — each rung appears only once the one before it is open, and the last gives the first line of the working — and Worked solution has the whole thing: the idea, the working, then a check. Read the solution after your attempt, including when you got it right. F folds everything back; D switches dark and light.

Twelve problems, about four hours. Throughout, \(P = \partial_t^2 - c^2\partial_x^2\) (or \(\partial_t^2 - c^2\nabla^2\) in three dimensions), a boost has velocity \(v\) along \(+x\), \(\beta = v/c\) and \(\gamma = (1-\beta^2)^{-1/2}\). "Form-invariant" means invariant up to a nonzero constant factor, as on the slides.

Constants: \(c = 2.99792458\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\); the Earth's mean orbital speed \(29.78\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}\), rounded to \(30\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}\) where the problem says so; one light-year \(= 9.4607\times10^{15}\) m; H\(_\beta\) wavelength \(\lambda_0 = 486.13\) nm.

Covariance

P1 Newton, boosted routine

exercises: the Galilean group, invariance of Newton's laws, what "the same form" requires

  1. Two bodies attract by gravitation: \(m_1\ddot{\vec x}_1 = -Gm_1m_2(\vec x_1 - \vec x_2)/\lVert\vec x_1 - \vec x_2\rVert^3\) and the mirror equation for body 2. Show both equations keep exactly this form under a Galilean boost \(\vec x' = \vec x - \vec vt\), \(t' = t\).
  2. A ball falls through still air with drag: \(m\ddot{\vec x} = m\vec g - b\dot{\vec x}\). Write the equation in a frame moving at constant \(\vec v\). It changes form. Explain why this does not contradict the principle of relativity.
  3. Let \(g = (R_1, \vec v_1, \vec a_1, \tau_1)\) and \(h = (R_2, \vec v_2, \vec a_2, \tau_2)\) denote the Galilean maps \((t, \vec x)\mapsto(t + \tau, R\vec x - \vec vt + \vec a)\). Compute the parameters of \(g\circ h\), and show that the pure boosts (\(R = I\), \(\vec a = \vec 0\), \(\tau = 0\)) form an abelian subgroup isomorphic to \((\mathbb R^3, +)\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Parts 1 and 2 are each one substitution; the answer is an equation in primed variables, compared with the original. Part 3 is a composition of affine maps and a check of the group axioms on a subset.

Rung 2

Differentiate \(\vec x' = \vec x - \vec vt\) twice. Then ask which quantities in each force law are differences of positions or velocities and which are not.

Rung 3

First line of part 3: \(h\) sends \((t,\vec x)\) to \((t + \tau_2,\ R_2\vec x - \vec v_2t + \vec a_2)\); now apply \(g\) to that point, remembering that \(g\) sees the time \(t + \tau_2\), not \(t\).

Worked solution

Idea. A Galilean boost leaves accelerations and all differences of positions and velocities unchanged; a law built only from those is invariant, and a law containing an absolute velocity is secretly about a second body.

1. \(\dot{\vec x}_i' = \dot{\vec x}_i - \vec v\) and \(\ddot{\vec x}_i' = \ddot{\vec x}_i\). Also \(\vec x_1' - \vec x_2' = \vec x_1 - \vec x_2\), since the \(\vec vt\) cancels. So \(m_1\ddot{\vec x}_1' = -Gm_1m_2(\vec x_1' - \vec x_2')/\lVert\vec x_1' - \vec x_2'\rVert^3\): the same equation.

2. Substituting, \(m\ddot{\vec x}' = m\vec g - b(\dot{\vec x}' + \vec v)\): a new constant force \(-b\vec v\) appears. The drag is really \(-b(\dot{\vec x} - \vec w)\), where \(\vec w\) is the velocity of the air, and "still air" silently set \(\vec w = \vec 0\) in the first frame. In the new frame the air moves at \(\vec w' = -\vec v\) and the law reads \(m\ddot{\vec x}' = m\vec g - b(\dot{\vec x}' - \vec w')\) — the same form, once the air is admitted as a body. The principle of relativity is about the laws for the whole system, not about one body in a medium that picks out a frame.

3. \(g(h(t,\vec x)) = \big(t + \tau_2 + \tau_1,\ R_1(R_2\vec x - \vec v_2t + \vec a_2) - \vec v_1(t + \tau_2) + \vec a_1\big)\), so \[ g\circ h = \big(R_1R_2,\ \ R_1\vec v_2 + \vec v_1,\ \ R_1\vec a_2 - \vec v_1\tau_2 + \vec a_1,\ \ \tau_1 + \tau_2\big). \] For pure boosts this is \((I, \vec v_1 + \vec v_2, \vec 0, 0)\): closed, with identity \(\vec v = \vec 0\), inverse \(-\vec v\), and \(\vec v_1 + \vec v_2 = \vec v_2 + \vec v_1\). The map \(\vec v \mapsto (I, \vec v, \vec 0, 0)\) is therefore an isomorphism from \((\mathbb R^3, +)\).

Check. Dimensions in part 2: \(b\) is \(\mathrm{kg\,s^{-1}}\), so \(b\vec v\) is a force ✓. Special case in part 3: \(R_1 = R_2 = I\), \(\vec v_i = \vec 0\) gives translations composing by addition, \((\vec a_1 + \vec a_2, \tau_1 + \tau_2)\) ✓. Note the term \(-\vec v_1\tau_2\): a boost and a time translation do not commute, which is why the full group is not a direct product.

P2 The wave operator in three dimensions, and the crossing speed routine

exercises: the chain rule for a boost, plane waves, group velocity, the ether picture of Michelson–Morley

  1. Under \(\vec x' = \vec x - \vec vt\), \(t' = t\), with \(\vec v\) in any direction, show \[ \partial_t^2 - c^2\nabla^2 = \partial_{t'}^2 - 2\,\partial_{t'}(\vec v\cdot\nabla') + (\vec v\cdot\nabla')^2 - c^2\nabla'^2 . \]
  2. Find the dispersion relation \(\omega(\vec k)\) of plane waves \(e^{\mathrm i(\vec k\cdot\vec x' - \omega t')}\) of the new operator, and their group velocity \(\nabla_{\vec k}\,\omega\) (unit 20).
  3. A signal is to travel perpendicular to \(\vec v\) in the moving frame. Find its speed, and evaluate the fractional deficit \(1 - (\text{speed})/c\) for \(v = 30\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}\). Compare with the time \(t_\perp\) in the slides' Michelson–Morley example.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

An operator identity, then a function \(\omega(\vec k)\) with two branches, then a vector; the last part is a single speed and a pure number of order \(10^{-9}\).

Rung 2

The chain rule gives \(\nabla = \nabla'\) and \(\partial_t = \partial_{t'} - \vec v\cdot\nabla'\). For the dispersion relation, the new operator is \((\partial_{t'} - \vec v\cdot\nabla')^2 - c^2\nabla'^2\), so the symbol is a perfect square minus something.

Rung 3

First line of part 2: substituting, \(-(\omega + \vec v\cdot\vec k)^2 + c^2\lVert\vec k\rVert^2 = 0\). For part 3 you need the group velocity \(c\hat k - \vec v\) to be perpendicular to \(\vec v\).

Worked solution

Idea. A Galilean boost only changes \(\partial_t\), by \(-\vec v\cdot\nabla'\). Everything then follows from squaring, and the result is the ether picture: light moves at \(c\) relative to the old frame, so at \(c\hat k - \vec v\) relative to the new one.

1. With \(u(\vec x, t) = u'(\vec x - \vec vt, t)\), the chain rule gives \(\partial_{x_i}u = \partial_{x_i'}u'\) and \(\partial_tu = \partial_{t'}u' - \sum_iv_i\,\partial_{x_i'}u'\). So \(\nabla = \nabla'\) and \(\partial_t = \partial_{t'} - \vec v\cdot\nabla'\), constant coefficients, and \[ \partial_t^2 = \partial_{t'}^2 - 2\,\partial_{t'}(\vec v\cdot\nabla') + (\vec v\cdot\nabla')^2 \] (Clairaut lets \(\partial_{t'}\) and \(\vec v\cdot\nabla'\) commute). Subtract \(c^2\nabla'^2\).

2. On \(e^{\mathrm i(\vec k\cdot\vec x' - \omega t')}\): \(\partial_{t'}\to-\mathrm i\omega\), \(\nabla'\to\mathrm i\vec k\), so \(\partial_{t'} - \vec v\cdot\nabla' \to -\mathrm i(\omega + \vec v\cdot\vec k)\) and the operator gives \(-(\omega + \vec v\cdot\vec k)^2 + c^2\lVert\vec k\rVert^2\). Hence \[ \omega = \pm c\lVert\vec k\rVert - \vec v\cdot\vec k,\qquad \nabla_{\vec k}\,\omega = \pm c\,\hat k - \vec v . \] The phase speed in the direction \(\hat k\) is \(c - \vec v\cdot\hat k\); the energy moves at \(c\hat k - \vec v\).

3. We need \((c\hat k - \vec v)\cdot\vec v = 0\), i.e. \(c\,\hat k\cdot\vec v = v^2\). Then \(\lVert c\hat k - \vec v\rVert^2 = c^2 - 2c\,\hat k\cdot\vec v + v^2 = c^2 - v^2\), so the crossing speed is \(\sqrt{c^2 - v^2} = c/\gamma\): the wave front must be aimed slightly upwind. For \(v = 3.0\times10^4\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), \[ 1 - \sqrt{1 - \beta^2} = 5.007\times10^{-9}, \] about \(1.50\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) slower than \(c\). This is exactly the slides' \(t_\perp = (2L/c)\gamma\): a round trip of \(2L\) at speed \(c/\gamma\).

Check. Dimensions: \(\vec v\cdot\nabla'\) has the dimensions of \(\partial_{t'}\) ✓. Limit \(\vec v\to\vec 0\): \(\omega = \pm c\lVert\vec k\rVert\) ✓. Special case \(\hat k \parallel \vec v\): group velocity \(c - v\) downwind, matching the slides' worked example 1 ✓. And \(1 - \sqrt{1-\beta^2} \approx \tfrac12\beta^2 = 5.007\times10^{-9}\) ✓.

P3 Proof: the heat equation is Galilean, with a multiplier medium

exercises: covariance with a point-dependent multiplier, the principal symbol, a group action checked by hand

Let \(k \gt 0\). For a function \(w(x,t)\) and \(v\in\mathbb R\) define \[ (T_vw)(x,t) = e^{\alpha x + \beta t}\,w(x - vt,\ t). \]

  1. Find constants \(\alpha, \beta\) (depending on \(v, k\)) such that \(T_vw\) solves \(u_t = ku_{xx}\) whenever \(w\) does.
  2. Prove \(T_{v_2}T_{v_1} = T_{v_1+v_2}\), so \(v\mapsto T_v\) is an action of the boosts.
  3. Prove that no smooth nowhere-zero \(m(x,t)\) makes \(u(x,t) = m(x,t)\,w(x - vt, t)\) a solution of \(u_{tt} = c^2u_{xx}\) for every solution \(w\), unless \(v = 0\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Part 1 is two numbers; part 2 an identity between exponentials; part 3 a proof by contradiction that should use the second-order terms only.

Rung 2

Compute \(u_t - ku_{xx}\) for \(u = e^{\alpha x + \beta t}w(\xi, t)\), \(\xi = x - vt\). You will find \(w_t - kw_{\xi\xi}\) plus a multiple of \(w_\xi\) plus a multiple of \(w\); make both multiples vanish. For part 3, the multiplier's derivatives only produce terms with fewer derivatives of \(w\).

Rung 3

First line: \(u_t = e^{\alpha x+\beta t}\big(\beta w - v w_\xi + w_t\big)\) and \(u_{xx} = e^{\alpha x+\beta t}\big(\alpha^2w + 2\alpha w_\xi + w_{\xi\xi}\big)\).

Worked solution

Idea. A boost changes the heat equation only in a first-order term, which a multiplier can absorb. The wave equation's damage is second-order, in the symbol, where a multiplier cannot reach.

1. Using the derivatives in rung 3, \[ u_t - ku_{xx} = e^{\alpha x+\beta t}\Big[(w_t - kw_{\xi\xi}) - (v + 2k\alpha)\,w_\xi + (\beta - k\alpha^2)\,w\Big]. \] If \(w_t = kw_{\xi\xi}\), this vanishes for all such \(w\) exactly when \(\alpha = -v/2k\) and \(\beta = k\alpha^2 = v^2/4k\): \[ (T_vw)(x,t) = e^{-vx/2k + v^2t/4k}\,w(x - vt, t). \] (Necessity: \(w = 1\) and \(w = \xi\) are solutions, and force the two brackets to vanish.)

2. \((T_{v_2}T_{v_1}w)(x,t) = e^{-v_2x/2k + v_2^2t/4k}\,(T_{v_1}w)(x - v_2t, t) = e^{-v_2x/2k + v_2^2t/4k}\,e^{-v_1(x - v_2t)/2k + v_1^2t/4k}\,w(x - v_2t - v_1t, t)\). The exponent is \(-(v_1 + v_2)x/2k + (v_1^2 + 2v_1v_2 + v_2^2)t/4k = -(v_1+v_2)x/2k + (v_1+v_2)^2t/4k\), so the product is \(T_{v_1+v_2}w\). \(\square\)

3. Suppose \(u = m\,w(\xi,t)\) solves the wave equation for every solution \(w\) of \(w_{tt} = c^2w_{\xi\xi}\). Expanding with the product and chain rules, the terms with two derivatives of \(w\) are \(m\big(w_{tt} - 2v\,w_{\xi t} + v^2w_{\xi\xi}\big) - c^2m\,w_{\xi\xi}\); all others carry at most one derivative of \(w\), multiplied by derivatives of \(m\). Take \(w = e^{\lambda(\xi - ct)}\) with \(\lambda \gt 0\): then \(w_{tt} = c^2\lambda^2w\), \(w_{\xi t} = -c\lambda^2w\), \(w_{\xi\xi} = \lambda^2w\), and the second-derivative terms add up to \(m(2vc + v^2)\lambda^2w\), while the rest are \((\text{bounded near a point})\times\lambda w\) or \(\times w\). Dividing \(u_{tt} - c^2u_{xx} = 0\) by \(\lambda^2w\) and letting \(\lambda\to\infty\) gives \(m(2vc + v^2) = 0\). With \(w = e^{\lambda(\xi + ct)}\) the same argument gives \(m(v^2 - 2vc) = 0\). Since \(m \ne 0\), \(v^2 + 2vc = v^2 - 2vc = 0\), so \(v = 0\). \(\square\)

Check. Dimensions: \(k\) is \(\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}\), so \(vx/k\) and \(v^2t/k\) are pure numbers ✓. Limit \(v\to0\): \(T_0 = \) identity ✓. Test on a solution: \(w = e^{-kq^2t}\cos(qx)\) gives a \(T_vw\) that, by part 1, is a heat solution drifting at speed \(v\) while decaying — the heat pattern carried along by a uniformly moving medium, as it should be.

Another way

Part 3 is the slides' symbol argument: a multiplier changes only first- and zeroth-order terms, and the boosted symbol \(\tau^2 - 2v\tau\xi - (c^2 - v^2)\xi^2\) is not a multiple of \(\tau^2 - c^2\xi^2\) for \(v\ne0\). The plane-wave computation above is that argument unpacked: large \(\lambda\) isolates the symbol.

P4 Proof: actions, and why symmetries form a group medium

exercises: group actions on functions, the inverse in \(u\circ\varphi_g^{-1}\), the symmetry-group lemma

  1. Let \(\varphi_g(y) = 2y\) and \(\varphi_h(y) = y + 1\) on \(\mathbb R\). For \(u(y) = y\), compute \(u\circ\varphi_g\circ\varphi_h\) and \((u\circ\varphi_h)\circ\varphi_g\), and show the rule \(u\mapsto u\circ\varphi\) reverses the order of composition. Show \(u\mapsto u\circ\varphi^{-1}\) does not.
  2. For an action of a group \(H\) on functions and a linear operator \(P\) with solution set \(S\), prove that \(\{g\in H : g\cdot S = S\}\) is a subgroup.
  3. Show that \(\{g\in H : g\cdot S \subseteq S\}\) need not be a subgroup: take the order-zero operator \((Pu)(y) = \chi(y)\,u(y)\), with \(\chi\) the indicator function of \((0,1)\), and a dilation.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Part 1 is two explicit functions; parts 2 and 3 are a proof and a counterexample about sets of maps.

Rung 2

In part 1, "\(u\mapsto u\circ\varphi_g\)" applied first and then "\(u\mapsto u\circ\varphi_h\)" gives \(u\circ\varphi_g\circ\varphi_h\): the maps compose in the reverse order to the operations. For part 3, the solutions are the functions vanishing on \((0,1)\); a dilation stretches that interval.

Rung 3

First line of part 3: take \(\varphi_g(y) = 2y\) and \((g\cdot u)(y) = u(y/2)\). If \(u = 0\) on \((0,1)\) then \(g\cdot u = 0\) on \((0,2)\).

Worked solution

Idea. Precomposition is contravariant; the inverse repairs that. A set of maps preserving a set exactly is closed under inverses automatically; maps preserving it only one way are not.

1. \(\varphi_g\circ\varphi_h(y) = 2(y+1) = 2y + 2\) and \(\varphi_h\circ\varphi_g(y) = 2y + 1\), so \(u\circ\varphi_g\circ\varphi_h = 2y + 2\) and \(u\circ\varphi_h\circ\varphi_g = 2y + 1\). Define \(A_g u = u\circ\varphi_g\). Then \(A_h(A_gu) = (u\circ\varphi_g)\circ\varphi_h = u\circ(\varphi_g\circ\varphi_h) = u\circ\varphi_{gh}\) (with \(\varphi_{gh} = \varphi_g\circ\varphi_h\)), i.e. \(A_hA_g = A_{gh}\): order reversed. In the example, \(A_gA_h u = u\circ\varphi_h\circ\varphi_g = 2y + 1\) while \(A_{gh}u = 2y + 2\) — they differ. With \(B_gu = u\circ\varphi_g^{-1}\): \(B_gB_hu = u\circ\varphi_h^{-1}\circ\varphi_g^{-1} = u\circ(\varphi_g\circ\varphi_h)^{-1} = B_{gh}u\). \(\square\) In the example, \(\varphi_{gh}^{-1}(y) = y/2 - 1\), and \(B_gB_hu(y) = \varphi_h^{-1}(\varphi_g^{-1}(y)) = y/2 - 1\) ✓.

2. Let \(K = \{g : g\cdot S = S\}\). The identity is in \(K\). If \(g, h\in K\), then \((gh)\cdot S = g\cdot(h\cdot S) = g\cdot S = S\). And from \(g\cdot S = S\), applying \(g^{-1}\) to both sides gives \(S = g^{-1}\cdot S\). \(\square\)

3. Here \(S = \{u : u = 0 \text{ on } (0,1)\}\). With \((g\cdot u)(y) = u(y/2)\), a solution becomes a function vanishing on \((0,2) \supset (0,1)\), so \(g\cdot S\subseteq S\). But \(g^{-1}\cdot u(y) = u(2y)\) vanishes only on \((0,\tfrac12)\): for \(u\) equal to \(0\) on \((0,1]\) and to \(y - 1\) beyond, \(g^{-1}\cdot u\) is \(2y - 1 \ne 0\) at \(y = 0.75\). So \(g^{-1}\notin\{g : g\cdot S\subseteq S\}\), and that set is not a group. \(\square\)

Check. Part 3 is consistent with the slides' lemma: for a group \(G\) with \(G\cdot S\subseteq S\), equality is automatic; here \(\{g^n : n\ge0\}\) is only a semigroup. Special case: for the wave operator and linear maps the two notions agree, because \(S\) is preserved exactly by each symmetry found on the slides.

P5 Michelson–Morley quantitatively routine

exercises: round-trip times in the ether frame, second-order expansions, the contraction hypothesis at any angle

The 1887 apparatus: arms of effective length \(L = 11\) m, light of wavelength \(\lambda = 550\) nm, an ether wind of \(v = 30\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}\) (take \(\beta = 1.0\times10^{-4}\)).

  1. Compute the expected fringe shift when the apparatus is turned through \(90°\).
  2. The observed shift was below \(0.01\) fringe. Since the shift scales as \(v^2\), what ether-wind speed does that exclude?
  3. By how much would the Lorentz–FitzGerald contraction shorten an \(11\) m arm lying along the wind?
  4. Show that with the contraction, the round-trip time of an arm of rest length \(L\) at any angle \(\theta\) to the wind is \(2L\gamma/c\), so the hypothesis predicts exactly zero shift for every orientation and speed.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Three numbers — a fringe count of order \(0.1\), a speed of a few \(\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}\), a length of tens of nanometres — and an identity in \(\theta\).

Rung 2

For part 4 you need the round-trip time for an arm of length \(\ell\) at angle \(\varphi\) to the motion, in the ether frame: solve \(\lVert\ell\hat e + \vec vt_1\rVert = ct_1\) out and \(\lVert-\ell\hat e + \vec vt_2\rVert = ct_2\) back. Then work out \(\ell\) and \(\varphi\) for a contracted arm.

Rung 3

First line of part 4: \(t_1 + t_2 = \dfrac{2\ell\sqrt{c^2 - v^2\sin^2\varphi}}{c^2 - v^2}\); the contracted arm has components \((L\cos\theta/\gamma,\ L\sin\theta)\), so \(\ell\cos\varphi = L\cos\theta/\gamma\) and \(\ell\sin\varphi = L\sin\theta\).

Worked solution

Idea. The first-order terms cancel on a round trip, leaving a path difference \(L\beta^2\); rotation doubles it. The contraction makes the round trip exactly isotropic.

1. From the slides, \(c(t_\parallel - t_\perp) = 2L(\gamma^2 - \gamma) = L\beta^2 + O(\beta^4)\). Turning through \(90°\) swaps the arms, so the pattern moves by \[ \Delta N = \frac{2L\beta^2}{\lambda} = \frac{2\times11\times1.0\times10^{-8}}{5.5\times10^{-7}} = 0.40 . \]

2. \(\Delta N\propto v^2\), so \(\Delta N \lt 0.01\) requires \(v \lt 30\sqrt{0.01/0.40} = 4.7\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}\) — below the Earth's orbital speed by a factor of six, and the orbital velocity alone reverses every six months.

3. \(L(1 - 1/\gamma) = L\big(\tfrac12\beta^2 + O(\beta^4)\big) = 11\times5.0\times10^{-9} = 5.5\times10^{-8}\) m: 55 nm, a tenth of a wavelength. The round trip loses twice that, \(1.1\times10^{-7}\) m \(= L\beta^2\), which is exactly the path difference in part 1.

4. Outbound, the light must cover the arm plus the distance the far mirror moves: \(c^2t_1^2 = \lVert\ell\hat e + \vec vt_1\rVert^2 = \ell^2 + 2\ell vt_1\cos\varphi + v^2t_1^2\), so \((c^2 - v^2)t_1^2 - 2\ell v\cos\varphi\,t_1 - \ell^2 = 0\) and \(t_1 = \dfrac{\ell v\cos\varphi + \ell\sqrt{v^2\cos^2\varphi + c^2 - v^2}}{c^2 - v^2}\). Back, \(\cos\varphi \to -\cos\varphi\). Adding, \[ t_1 + t_2 = \frac{2\ell\sqrt{c^2 - v^2\sin^2\varphi}}{c^2 - v^2} = \frac{2\gamma^2}{c}\sqrt{\ell^2 - \beta^2\ell^2\sin^2\varphi}. \] For the contracted arm, \(\ell^2 = L^2\cos^2\theta/\gamma^2 + L^2\sin^2\theta\) and \(\ell^2\sin^2\varphi = L^2\sin^2\theta\), so \(\ell^2 - \beta^2\ell^2\sin^2\varphi = L^2\cos^2\theta/\gamma^2 + L^2\sin^2\theta(1 - \beta^2) = L^2/\gamma^2\). Hence \(t_1 + t_2 = (2\gamma^2/c)(L/\gamma) = 2L\gamma/c\), independent of \(\theta\). \(\square\)

Check. Dimensions: \(L\beta^2/\lambda\) is a number ✓. Special cases of part 4: \(\theta = 90°\) gives \(\ell = L\), \(\varphi = 90°\) and \(t = (2L/c)\gamma\), the slides' \(t_\perp\) ✓; \(\theta = 0\) gives \(\ell = L/\gamma\), \(t = (2L/\gamma c)\gamma^2\) ✓. With the exact \(c\) the shift in part 1 is \(0.4006\).

P6 Which experiment pins which medium

exercises: a test theory, round-trip bookkeeping, the Doppler shift of a moving source, eliminating candidate laws

Work in the frame where light moves at \(c\) in every direction. An apparatus moving at \(v\) through it has lengths along the motion scaled by \(\ell_\parallel(v)\), across by \(\ell_\perp(v)\), and its clocks tick at \(\tau(v)\) times the rate of clocks at rest, with \(\ell_\parallel(0) = \ell_\perp(0) = \tau(0) = 1\).

  1. Show that Michelson–Morley's null result forces \(\ell_\parallel/\ell_\perp = 1/\gamma\).
  2. Kennedy–Thorndike used arms of unequal length and looked, over months, for a change in the round-trip time as measured by the lab's clocks. Show a null result forces \(\tau\ell_\perp = 1/\gamma\).
  3. Ives–Stilwell viewed hydrogen atoms moving at \(\beta c\) forwards and backwards (the lab at rest in the isotropic frame). Show the mean of the two wavelengths is \(\lambda_0/\tau\).
  4. Three candidates: Lorentz (\(\ell_\parallel, \ell_\perp, \tau\) \(= 1/\gamma, 1, 1/\gamma\)); FitzGerald–Lorentz with absolute time (\(1/\gamma, 1, 1\)); Voigt (\(1, \gamma, 1/\gamma^2\)). Show all pass Michelson–Morley, say which fail Kennedy–Thorndike and Ives–Stilwell, and compute the mean shift each predicts for H\(_\beta\) at \(\beta = 0.005\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Three equations for three unknown functions, one derivation each; then a table of three candidates against three experiments, and three wavelength shifts measured in picometres.

Rung 2

Use the slides' round-trip times with the arm lengths scaled: along \(\frac{2L\ell_\parallel}{c}\gamma^2\), across \(\frac{2L\ell_\perp}{c}\gamma\). The lab's clocks multiply any ether-frame duration by \(\tau\). For part 3, an atom emitting \(\nu_0\) by its own clock emits \(\tau\nu_0\) per unit ether-frame time, and a source moving towards you at \(v\) compresses the wave to \((c - v)/(\text{emitted frequency})\).

Rung 3

Worked solution

Idea. Each experiment gives one equation; a light-only experiment can only see ratios that a common rescaling of lengths and times leaves alone, and a material clock is needed to see the rest.

2. Given part 1, an arm of rest length \(L_i\) in either direction takes \(\frac{2L_i\ell_\perp\gamma}{c}\) of ether time and \(\tau\ell_\perp\gamma\frac{2L_i}{c}\) of lab time.   • Lorentz: \(\lambda_0(\gamma - 1) = 0.00608\) nm \(= 6.08\) pm;
  • FitzGerald–Lorentz: \(0\);

Check. Dimensions: each condition is a ratio of pure numbers ✓. Limit \(v\to0\): all three conditions read \(1 = 1\) ✓. Note that the one-parameter family \(\ell_\parallel = 1/(s\gamma)\), \(\ell_\perp = 1/s\), \(\tau = s/\gamma\) passes MM and KT for every \(s\): light-only round trips are blind to a common dilation.

Rung 1

Rung 2

Rung 3

First line of part 1: \(p^2 - q^2 = 1\), \(pr - qm = 0\), \(r^2 - m^2 = -1\). The first gives \(|p|\ge1\), so the sign of \(p\) is locally constant along any path in the group.

Worked solution

Idea. The symbol is a quadratic form of signature \((1,1)\); the linear symmetries of \(P\) are the maps preserving that form up to a factor. In null coordinates the form is \(\xi\eta\) (up to sign), and the answer is visible: scale \(\xi\) and \(\eta\) separately, or swap them.

The functions \(\operatorname{sign}p\) and \(\det M\) are continuous with values in \(\{\pm1\}\), so they are constant on components: there are at least four. Exactly four: put \(v = -q/p\in(-1,1)\); then \(p^2(1 - v^2) = 1\), so \(p = \pm\gamma\), \(q = \mp\gamma v\). From \(pr = qm\), \(r = -vm\), and \(r^2 - m^2 = -1\) gives \(m = \pm\gamma\), \(r = \mp\gamma v\) (signs of \(m\) and \(r\) together). 2. Taking determinants of \(JAJ^{\mathsf T} = \lambda A\): \(-(\det J)^2 = -\lambda^2\), so \(\lambda\ne0\). 3. Check. Special case \(v = 0\): \(p = q = 1\) ✓. Composition: \(p(u)p(v) = \sqrt{\frac{(1+u)(1+v)}{(1-u)(1-v)}}\), and this equals \(p(w)\) for \(w = (u+v)/(1+uv)\), since \(\frac{1+w}{1-w} = \frac{1 + u + v + uv}{1 - u - v + uv}\) ✓ — the composition law in the form "Doppler factors multiply". Worked solution

Idea. Galilean mechanics fixes how the fields would have to transform; with that rule the curl-of-\(\vec E\) half of Maxwell survives and the displacement current does not, with a residual of first order in \(v/c\).

1. The particle's velocity is \(\vec u - \vec v\) in the new frame, and force is frame-independent in Galilean mechanics, so \(\vec E' + (\vec u - \vec v)\times\vec B' = \vec E + \vec u\times\vec B\) for all \(\vec u\). At \(\vec u = \vec v\): \(\vec E' = \vec E + \vec v\times\vec B\). The second identity, proved. So \(\vec a\times(\nabla\times\vec E) = \nabla(\vec a\cdot\vec E) - (\vec a\cdot\nabla)\vec E\). \(\square\) (Unit 24 proves the case \(\vec a = \vec E\); the constant-\(\vec a\) case is this one line.)

P12 Voigt's transformation is not a group medium

exercises: form-invariance up to a factor, reciprocity, closure under composition, the test-theory functions

Voigt (1887): \(V(v)\): \(x' = x - vt\), \(t' = t - vx/c^2\), \(y' = y/\gamma\), \(z' = z/\gamma\).

  1. Show \(V(v)\) carries \(\partial_t^2 - c^2\nabla^2\) to \(\gamma^{-2}(\partial_{t'}^2 - c^2\nabla'^2)\).
  2. Compute \(V(v)^{-1}\) and show it is not \(V(-v)\).
  3. Show \(V(u)V(v) = (1 + uv/c^2)\,V(w)\) with \(w = (u+v)/(1 + uv/c^2)\), so the Voigt maps are not closed under composition. Evaluate the factor for \(u = v = c/2\).
  4. Find \(\ell_\parallel\), \(\ell_\perp\), \(\tau\) for \(V(v)\), in the sense of P6.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

The answers are: a constant factor multiplying the operator; a map that is a Voigt-shaped map times a number; a number times a Voigt map, with that number evaluated at \(u = v = c/2\); and three functions of \(v\). Nothing here needs a fresh computation of the chain rule — the slides have already done every piece.

Rung 2

Compare \(V(v)\) coordinate by coordinate with the slides' family \(x' = s\gamma(x - vt)\), \(t' = s\gamma(t - vx/c^2)\), \(y' = sy\): Voigt is the member \(s = 1/\gamma\), that is, \(V(v) = \gamma^{-1}\Lambda(v)\) with \(\Lambda\) the Lorentz boost. Then use \(\Lambda(v)^{-1} = \Lambda(-v)\) and \(\Lambda(u)\Lambda(v) = \Lambda(w)\), \(\gamma_w = \gamma_u\gamma_v(1 + uv/c^2)\).

Rung 3

First line of part 3: \(V(u)V(v) = \gamma_u^{-1}\gamma_v^{-1}\Lambda(w) = \dfrac{\gamma_w}{\gamma_u\gamma_v}\cdot\gamma_w^{-1}\Lambda(w)\).

Worked solution

Idea. Voigt's map is a Lorentz boost followed by the dilation \(1/\gamma\). Dilations are symmetries of the wave operator, so Voigt passes that test; but a velocity-dependent dilation spoils reciprocity and closure.

1. \(\gamma^{-1}\Lambda(v)\) sends \(x\mapsto\gamma^{-1}\gamma(x - vt) = x - vt\), \(t\mapsto t - vx/c^2\), \(y\mapsto y/\gamma\): this is \(V(v)\). \(\Lambda(v)\) preserves the operator exactly, and a dilation \(y\mapsto sy\) (all four coordinates) gives \(\partial_y = s\,\partial_{y'}\), multiplying the operator by \(s^2\). With \(s = 1/\gamma\) the factor is \(\gamma^{-2}\). \(\square\)

2. \(V(v)^{-1} = \gamma\,\Lambda(v)^{-1} = \gamma\,\Lambda(-v)\): \(x = \gamma^2(x' + vt')\), \(t = \gamma^2(t' + vx'/c^2)\), \(y = \gamma y'\). But \(V(-v) = \gamma^{-1}\Lambda(-v)\). They differ by the factor \(\gamma^2\ne1\) for \(v\ne0\): each frame would find the other's transverse rulers shortened by \(1/\gamma\), and the two statements are incompatible with treating the frames alike.

3. \(V(u)V(v) = \gamma_u^{-1}\gamma_v^{-1}\Lambda(u)\Lambda(v) = \gamma_u^{-1}\gamma_v^{-1}\Lambda(w) = \dfrac{\gamma_w}{\gamma_u\gamma_v}\,V(w) = (1 + uv/c^2)\,V(w)\). This is not a Voigt map: the \(x'\)-row of \((1 + uv/c^2)V(w)\) is \((1 + uv/c^2)(x - wt)\), which fixes the velocity as \(w\), and then its coefficient of \(x\) is \(1 + uv/c^2\) rather than Voigt's \(1\). For \(u = v = c/2\): \(w = 0.8c\) and the factor is \(1.25\). \(\square\)

4. A rod along \(x'\) of rest length \(L\): at fixed \(t\), \(\Delta x' = \Delta x\), so \(\ell_\parallel = 1\). Across: \(\Delta y = \gamma\,\Delta y'\), so \(\ell_\perp = \gamma\). A clock at \(x' = 0\), i.e. \(x = vt\), reads \(t' = t(1 - \beta^2) = t/\gamma^2\), so \(\tau = 1/\gamma^2\). These are P6's Voigt values: MM and KT pass, Ives–Stilwell fails by a factor of two.

Check. Dimensions: all factors pure numbers ✓. Limit \(v\to0\): \(V(0) = I\) ✓, and \(1 + uv/c^2\to1\) as \(c\to\infty\), where Voigt's maps become Galileo's, which do form a group ✓. This is Poincaré's point: the changes of frame must form a group, and within the family \(s(v)\Lambda(v)\) the group property together with isotropy, \(s(-v) = s(v)\), leaves only \(s = 1\) — the slides' reciprocity argument.
