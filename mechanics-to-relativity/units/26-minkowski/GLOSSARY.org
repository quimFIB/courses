The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: the twins. One stays on Earth; the other flies at \(0.8c\) to
a star \(4\) light-years away and straight back. In years and light-years
(\(c = 1\)): \(\gamma = 5/3\), rapidity \(\ln 3\), Doppler factor \(3\). Earth
ages \(10\) years, the traveller \(6\), and three computations agree — proper
time, radio counts (\(1 + 9\) and \(3 + 3\)), and a \(6.4\)-year jump in the
traveller's simultaneity at the turnaround event \(T = (5, 4)\).

* The group, from structure alone

- event :: A point of spacetime: a place at an instant. Differences of events
  are vectors of \(V\). /Slide: Events, frames, and the question./
- inertial frame :: A coordinate system \((t, x, y, z)\) on the events in
  which every free particle moves on a straight line at constant speed. In the
  language of section 2: an origin together with a Lorentz orthonormal basis.
  /Slide: Events, frames, and the question./
- worldline :: The set of events a particle occupies; for a massive particle,
  a future-directed timelike curve. /Slide: Events, frames, and the question./
- principle of relativity :: No experiment distinguishes one inertial frame
  from another. Its first consequence: the coordinate changes between inertial
  frames form a group, and the change to a frame moving at \(\vec v\) depends
  on \(\vec v\) alone. /Slide: Events, frames, and the question./
- homogeneity :: No event is special: the \(S'\)-separation of two events
  depends only on their \(S\)-separation. With continuity it makes every change
  of inertial frame affine. /Slide: Events, frames, and the question./
- isotropy :: No spatial direction is special. It decouples the transverse
  coordinates from a boost and, with the group axioms, proves reciprocity:
  \(L(v)^{-1} = L(-v)\). /Slide: Events, frames, and the question./
- Lorentz transformation :: The boost \(t' = \gamma(t - vx/c^2)\),
  \(x' = \gamma(x - vt)\), \(y' = y\), \(z' = z\), with
  \(\gamma = (1 - v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}\): the case \(K = 1/c^2 > 0\) of the
  one-parameter family that homogeneity, isotropy and the group axioms allow.
  More generally, any element of the Lorentz group. /Slide: Only one constant is left free./
- invariant speed :: The speed \(c = K^{-1/2}\) that is the same in every
  inertial frame: \((c + v)/(1 + v/c) = c\). Experiment, or unit 25's wave
  operator, identifies it with the speed of light. /Slide: The invariant speed, and the work each postulate does./

* The form, and the group of the form

- interval :: \(s^2 = -(x^0)^2 + x^2 + y^2 + z^2\) with \(x^0 = ct\), the
  same in every inertial frame. /Slide: The interval is invariant./
- Minkowski space :: A 4-dimensional real vector space \(V\) with a symmetric
  bilinear form \(\eta\) of signature \((-,+,+,+)\); spacetime is an affine
  space over it. /Slide: Minkowski space, defined./
- signature :: The numbers of negative, positive and zero entries in any
  diagonal matrix of a symmetric bilinear form. Independent of the basis by
  Sylvester's law of inertia (cited). /Slide: Minkowski space, defined./
- orthonormal basis (Lorentz orthonormal basis) :: A basis with
  \(\eta(e_\mu, e_\nu) = \operatorname{diag}(-1, 1, 1, 1)\). Physically, the
  axes of an inertial frame. /Slide: Minkowski space, defined./
- Minkowski form :: The symmetric bilinear form \(\eta\) itself. Its matrix
  \(\eta_{\mu\nu}\) in a basis transforms as
  \(\eta' = \Lambda^{\mathsf T}\eta\Lambda\) — a theorem, from bilinearity, not
  a definition. /Slide: Minkowski space, defined./
- summation convention :: A repeated index, once up and once down, is summed
  over \(0, \dots, 3\): \(\eta(X, Y) = \eta_{\mu\nu}X^\mu Y^\nu\). Notation
  only. /Slide: Minkowski space, defined./
- timelike :: A vector with \(\eta(X, X) < 0\). Two events are timelike
  separated if some inertial observer passes through both.
  /Slide: Timelike, null, spacelike: the light cone./
- null :: A nonzero vector with \(\eta(X, X) = 0\): the direction of a light
  ray. /Slide: Timelike, null, spacelike: the light cone./
- spacelike :: A vector with \(\eta(X, X) > 0\). No signal at or below the
  speed of light connects spacelike-separated events.
  /Slide: Timelike, null, spacelike: the light cone./
- light cone :: The set of null vectors, or of events null-separated from a
  given one. It splits into a future and a past half.
  /Slide: Timelike, null, spacelike: the light cone./
- Lorentz group :: \(O(1,3) = \{\Lambda : \eta(\Lambda X, \Lambda Y) = \eta(X,
  Y)\}\), in an orthonormal basis \(\Lambda^{\mathsf T}\eta\Lambda = \eta\). It
  has four connected components, told apart by \(\det\Lambda = \pm1\) and the
  sign of \(\Lambda^0{}_0\). /Slide: The Lorentz group and its four pieces./
- proper orthochronous :: \(\det\Lambda = 1\) and \(\Lambda^0{}_0 \ge 1\): the
  identity component \(SO^+(1,3)\), generated by boosts and rotations, every
  element a boost times a rotation. /Slide: The Lorentz group and its four pieces./
- rapidity :: \(\varphi = \operatorname{artanh}(v/c)\), so that
  \(\gamma = \cosh\varphi\), \(\gamma\beta = \sinh\varphi\). Additive under
  composition of collinear boosts. /Slide: Boosts are hyperbolic rotations./
- velocity addition :: \(\beta_w = (\beta_u + \beta_v)/(1 + \beta_u\beta_v)\)
  for collinear velocities: the addition formula for \(\tanh\).
  /Slide: Boosts are hyperbolic rotations./
- Alexandrov–Zeeman theorem :: A bijection of \(\mathbb R^4\) preserving the
  causal order in both directions is a composition of an orthochronous Lorentz
  transformation, a translation and a dilation. No continuity or linearity is
  assumed. False
  in \(1+1\) dimensions. Cited: Zeeman, J. Math. Phys. 5 (1964) 490.
  /Slide: Causality alone forces the group./

* Simultaneity, and what follows from it

- relativity of simultaneity :: Events simultaneous in \(S\) and
  \(\Delta x\) apart have \(\Delta t' = -\gamma v\Delta x/c^2\) in \(S'\). The
  content of special relativity; dilation and contraction follow from it.
  /Slide: The relativity of simultaneity./
- Einstein synchronisation :: Radar time: an event reached by light sent at
  \(t_1\) and returned at \(t_2\) is assigned \(t_E = \tfrac12(t_1 + t_2)\).
  Radar-simultaneous events are those \(\eta\)-orthogonal to the observer's
  worldline. /Slide: The relativity of simultaneity./
- time dilation :: A clock moving at \(v\) has its ticks \(\gamma\) times
  further apart in coordinate time than in its own proper time.
  /Slide: Time dilation and length contraction, as corollaries./
- length contraction :: A rod of rest length \(L_0\) moving along its length
  has length \(L_0/\gamma\), length meaning the separation of its ends at one
  instant of the measuring frame. /Slide: Time dilation and length contraction, as corollaries./
- calibration hyperbola :: \(-(x^0)^2 + x^2 = \mp1\): the events at unit
  interval from the origin. It crosses every observer's time or space axis at
  that observer's unit tick. /Slide: Minkowski diagrams that do not lie./
- Minkowski diagram :: An \((x, ct)\) plot with light rays at \(45°\). A
  moving observer's axes lean towards the light line, and their ticks are found
  on the calibration hyperbolae, not with a ruler.
  /Slide: Minkowski diagrams that do not lie./
- Doppler factor :: For a source receding along the line of sight, received
  period over emitted proper period, \(\sqrt{(1+\beta)/(1-\beta)} = e^{\varphi}\).
  Doppler factors multiply as rapidities add. /Slide: Doppler: the rapidity, exponentiated./
- aberration :: The change of a light ray's direction between frames:
  \(\cos\theta = (\cos\theta' + \beta)/(1 + \beta\cos\theta')\). Stellar
  aberration is its first-order form, amplitude \(\beta \approx 20.5''\).
  /Slide: Aberration and the headlight effect./
- headlight effect :: Light emitted isotropically by a fast source is
  concentrated forward: half of it falls within \(\sin\theta < 1/\gamma\) of
  the direction of motion. /Slide: Aberration and the headlight effect./

* Causal structure

- causal future :: \(J^+(p)\), the events \(q\) with \(q - p\) future-directed
  timelike or null, or zero. Its order relative to \(p\) is the same in every
  orthochronous frame. /Slide: What survives of "before"./
- elsewhere :: The events outside \(J^+(p) \cup J^-(p)\), spacelike to \(p\):
  their time order relative to \(p\) is reversed in some inertial frame.
  /Slide: What survives of "before"./
- tachyonic antitelephone :: The two-signal construction showing that
  signals faster than \(c\), allowed in every frame, let a reply arrive before
  the question was sent. /Slide: No superluminal signalling./

* Proper time, and the twin paradox as a theorem

- proper time :: For a future-directed timelike curve,
  \(\tau = \frac1c\int\sqrt{-\eta_{\mu\nu}\dot X^\mu\dot X^\nu}\,\mathrm
  d\lambda = \int\sqrt{1 - |\dot{\vec x}|^2/c^2}\,\mathrm dt\). Invariant under
  reparametrisation and under Lorentz transformations.
  /Slide: Proper time: the length of a worldline./
- clock hypothesis :: The physical assumption that an ideal clock reads
  proper time along its worldline, whatever its acceleration. Tested with
  muons at about \(10^{18}\,g\). /Slide: Proper time: the length of a worldline./
- reversed Cauchy–Schwarz inequality :: For timelike \(X\) and any \(Y\):
  \(\eta(X, Y)^2 \ge \eta(X, X)\,\eta(Y, Y)\), with equality iff \(Y\) is a
  multiple of \(X\). /Slide: Cauchy–Schwarz and the triangle inequality, reversed./
- reversed triangle inequality :: For future-directed timelike \(X, Y\):
  \(\lVert X + Y\rVert \ge \lVert X\rVert + \lVert Y\rVert\) with
  \(\lVert X\rVert = \sqrt{-\eta(X, X)}\). The twin paradox, as a polygon.
  /Slide: Cauchy–Schwarz and the triangle inequality, reversed./
