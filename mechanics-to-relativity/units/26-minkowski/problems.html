Part D · unit 26 · problems

Minkowski space: relativity as the geometry of a quadratic form

the slides · the curriculum

Checkpoint. You derive the Lorentz transformation from homogeneity, isotropy, the group axioms and one invariant speed, compute proper time along any specified worldline, and prove the twin paradox is the reversed triangle inequality for timelike curves in \(\eta\).

Work each problem on paper first. Under it, Hints opens a ladder one rung at a time — each rung appears only once the one before it is open, and the last gives the first line of the working — and Worked solution has the whole thing: the idea, the working, then a check. Read the solution after your attempt, including when you got it right. F folds everything back; D switches dark and light.

Twelve problems, about five hours. Work them on paper first. Conventions as in the deck: signature \((-,+,+,+)\), \(x^0 = ct\), \(\beta = v/c\), \(\gamma = (1-\beta^2)^{-1/2}\), rapidity \(\varphi = \operatorname{artanh}\beta\), and \(\lVert X\rVert = \sqrt{-\eta(X,X)}\) for timelike \(X\). The boost along \(x\) is \(x^{0\prime} = \gamma(x^0 - \beta x)\), \(x' = \gamma(x - \beta x^0)\).

Constants: \(c = 299\,792\,458\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) (exact), muon rest lifetime \(\tau_\mu = 2.1969811\ \mu\mathrm s\), Earth's mean orbital speed \(29.78\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}\), \(g = 9.80665\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\), \(1\) yr \(= 365.25\) d. Where a problem says "years and light-years", \(c = 1\).

The group

P1 Rapidity is additive routine

exercises: boosts as hyperbolic rotations, the addition formulas for cosh and sinh, velocity addition

  1. Write the boost along \(x\) with velocity \(\beta\) as a matrix \(B(\varphi)\) acting on \((x^0, x)\), with \(\varphi = \operatorname{artanh}\beta\). Prove \(B(\varphi)B(\psi) = B(\varphi + \psi)\), and deduce the collinear velocity-addition formula.
  2. A rocket fires, reaching \(0.5c\) relative to the frame it started in. Then, from rest in its new frame, it does the same again — ten times in all, each boost \(0.5c\) relative to the frame of the previous stage. Find its final speed relative to the launch frame, exactly and to eight significant figures, and its final \(\gamma\).
  3. What would Galileo have said? Where does the Galilean answer come from in your formula?
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Part 1 is a matrix identity plus a quotient; part 2 is one number, which rapidity makes a one-line computation, and velocity addition applied ten times would make a page.

Rung 2

\(\gamma = \cosh\varphi\) and \(\gamma\beta = \sinh\varphi\), so the boost matrix has \(\cosh\varphi\) on the diagonal and \(-\sinh\varphi\) off it. The velocity is read off the product as (off-diagonal)/(diagonal). For part 2, rapidities add, so the final rapidity is \(10\operatorname{artanh}0.5\).

Rung 3

First line of part 2: \(\operatorname{artanh}0.5 = \tfrac12\ln\frac{1.5}{0.5} = \tfrac12\ln 3\), so the final rapidity is \(5\ln 3\) and \(e^{2\varphi} = 3^{10}\). Now use \(\tanh\varphi = (e^{2\varphi} - 1)/(e^{2\varphi} + 1)\).

Worked solution

Idea. Boosts along one line are hyperbolic rotations; the rapidity plays the angle, and angles add. Velocity addition is then nothing but the addition formula for \(\tanh\).

1. Since \(\cosh^2 - \sinh^2 = 1\), \(\cosh\varphi = (1 - \tanh^2\varphi)^{-1/2} = \gamma\) and \(\sinh\varphi = \gamma\beta\). So \[ B(\varphi) = \begin{pmatrix}\cosh\varphi & -\sinh\varphi\\ -\sinh\varphi & \cosh\varphi\end{pmatrix}. \] Multiplying, \[ B(\varphi)B(\psi) = \begin{pmatrix}\cosh\varphi\cosh\psi + \sinh\varphi\sinh\psi & -(\cosh\varphi\sinh\psi + \sinh\varphi\cosh\psi)\\ \cdot & \cdot\end{pmatrix}, \] with the second row the mirror image. From \(\cosh a = (e^a + e^{-a})/2\), \(\sinh a = (e^a - e^{-a})/2\), expanding gives \(\cosh\varphi\cosh\psi + \sinh\varphi\sinh\psi = \cosh(\varphi + \psi)\) and \(\sinh\varphi\cosh\psi + \cosh\varphi\sinh\psi = \sinh(\varphi + \psi)\). So \(B(\varphi)B(\psi) = B(\varphi + \psi)\). The composite velocity is \(\tanh(\varphi + \psi)\); dividing numerator and denominator of \(\sinh(\varphi+\psi)/\cosh(\varphi+\psi)\) by \(\cosh\varphi\cosh\psi\), \[ \beta_w = \frac{\tanh\varphi + \tanh\psi}{1 + \tanh\varphi\tanh\psi} = \frac{\beta_u + \beta_v}{1 + \beta_u\beta_v}. \]

2. Each stage has \(\varphi_1 = \operatorname{artanh}0.5 = \tfrac12\ln 3 = 0.549306\). Ten stages: \(\varphi = 5\ln 3 = 5.493061\), and \(e^{2\varphi} = 3^{10} = 59049\). So \[ \beta = \tanh\varphi = \frac{3^{10} - 1}{3^{10} + 1} = \frac{59048}{59050} = 0.99996613, \] short of \(1\) by \(3.39\times10^{-5}\). And \(\gamma = \cosh\varphi = \tfrac12(3^5 + 3^{-5}) = \tfrac12(243 + \tfrac1{243}) = 121.502\).

3. Galileo adds velocities: \(10\times0.5c = 5c\). In the formula, small rapidities have \(\tanh\varphi \approx \varphi\), so for small boosts the rapidities and the velocities both add. The first stage alone agrees with Galileo exactly; already after two, rapidity \(\ln 3\) gives \(0.8c\), not \(1.0c\).

Check. Dimensions: all quantities are pure numbers ✓. Two stages: \((3^2 - 1)/(3^2 + 1) = 0.8\), agreeing with \((0.5 + 0.5)/(1 + 0.25)\) ✓ — the deck's twins. \(\gamma\) and \(\beta\) consistent: \(1/\sqrt{1 - 0.99996613^2} = 121.50\) ✓.

Another way

By Doppler factors: a boost of rapidity \(\varphi\) multiplies received periods by \(e^{\varphi}\), and a relay of observers multiplies the factors. Each stage has factor \(\sqrt{1.5/0.5} = \sqrt3\); ten stages give \(3^5 = 243 = \sqrt{(1+\beta)/(1-\beta)}\), so \((1+\beta)/(1-\beta) = 3^{10}\), the same equation.

P2 The world with \(K \lt 0\) medium

exercises: the one-constant family of boosts, the role of the sign of \(K\), causal order on a single worldline

The deck showed that homogeneity, isotropy and the group axioms leave \(t' = \gamma(t - Kvx)\), \(x' = \gamma(x - vt)\), \(\gamma = (1 - Kv^2)^{-1/2}\), with \(K\) a universal constant. Suppose \(K = -1/k^2 \lt 0\), for some speed \(k\).

  1. Show that in the coordinates \((T, x) = (kt, x)\) each boost is a Euclidean rotation, and find its angle \(\theta(v)\).
  2. Compose two boosts with \(v = k\). What does the composite do to the worldline \(x = 0\) of a clock at rest in the original frame? What happens to the velocity-addition formula?
  3. Compose four. Explain why this world is excluded, naming the property of causes and effects it violates.
  4. Is there any speed that is the same in every frame of this world?
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

The answer to 1 is a \(2\times2\) rotation matrix with an angle depending on \(v/k\); after that, everything is "rotation angles add".

Rung 2

With \(K = -1/k^2\), \(\gamma = (1 + v^2/k^2)^{-1/2}\). Compare \(\gamma\) and \(\gamma v/k\) with \(\cos\theta\) and \(\sin\theta\) for \(\tan\theta = v/k\).

Rung 3

First line: \(T' = kt' = \gamma(kt + (v/k)x) = \cos\theta\,T + \sin\theta\,x\) and \(x' = \gamma(x - (v/k)T) = -\sin\theta\,T + \cos\theta\,x\).

Worked solution

Idea. A negative \(K\) makes the invariant form \(k^2t^2 + x^2\) positive definite, so the group is the rotation group of the plane — compact, with no invariant cone, and rotating far enough turns time around.

1. \(-Kv = v/k^2\), so \(t' = \gamma(t + vx/k^2)\) and \(kt' = \gamma(kt + (v/k)x)\). Put \(\tan\theta = v/k\), \(\theta\in(-\pi/2, \pi/2)\): then \(\cos\theta = (1 + v^2/k^2)^{-1/2} = \gamma\) and \(\sin\theta = \gamma v/k\). So \[ \begin{pmatrix}T'\\ x'\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta & \sin\theta\\ -\sin\theta & \cos\theta\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}T\\ x\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \theta(v) = \arctan(v/k), \] a rotation, which preserves \(T^2 + x^2 = k^2t^2 + x^2\).

2. Rotations compose by adding angles, and \(\theta(k) = \pi/4\). Two boosts at \(v = k\) give \(\theta = \pi/2\): \(T' = x\), \(x' = -T\). The worldline \(x = 0\) is sent to \(T' = 0\): every tick of the clock happens at the same instant \(t' = 0\) of the new frame, spread out along \(x'\). The clock's own history has become a line of simultaneity. The velocity-addition formula \(w = (u + v)/(1 - uv/k^2)\) has a zero denominator at \(u = v = k\): the composite "frame" moves at infinite speed. The velocities do not form a group; only the angles do.

3. Four boosts give \(\theta = \pi\): \(T' = -T\), \(x' = -x\). A clock at rest at the origin is seen, in an allowed inertial frame, with its ticks in reverse order. Its ticks are events on one worldline, each a cause of the next (the gear that moved moves the next gear), and the order of cause and effect along a single material worldline — timelike-related events — would be frame-dependent. No physics with causes survives that; so \(K \lt 0\) is excluded. In the Lorentz case \(K \gt 0\) section 4 of the deck proves exactly the opposite: timelike order is invariant.

4. No. Invariance of a speed \(u\) would need \((u + v)/(1 - uv/k^2) = u\) for all \(v\), i.e. \(v(1 + u^2/k^2) = 0\): impossible for \(v \ne 0\). A rotation has no invariant real direction (other than for angle \(0\) or \(\pi\)), so there is no light cone.

Check. Dimensions: \(v/k\) is a pure number, so \(\theta\) is an angle ✓. Limit \(k\to\infty\) (so \(K\to0^-\)): \(\theta \approx v/k \to 0\) and \(T' \approx T + (v/k)x\) gives \(t' = t + vx/k^2 \to t\), Galileo ✓.

P3 Proof: the four pieces of the Lorentz group hard

exercises: \(\Lambda^{\mathsf T}\eta\Lambda = \eta\), connectedness, the polar decomposition into boost and rotation

Let \(O(1,3) = \{\Lambda \in GL_4(\mathbb R) : \Lambda^{\mathsf T}\eta\Lambda = \eta\}\), \(\eta = \operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)\).

  1. Prove \(\det\Lambda = \pm1\) and \(|\Lambda^0{}_0| \ge 1\).
  2. Prove that \(\{\Lambda : \Lambda^0{}_0 \ge 1\}\) is a subgroup. (The obvious attempt — multiply and look at the \(00\) entry — needs an inequality; find it, or find a route that avoids it.)
  3. Prove every \(\Lambda\) with \(\det\Lambda = 1\), \(\Lambda^0{}_0 \ge 1\) is a boost times a rotation, and deduce that this set is path-connected.
  4. Conclude that \(O(1,3)\) has exactly four connected components.
Hints · 4 rungs
Rung 1

Four separate proofs. Parts 1 and 4 are short. Part 2 is about cones; part 3 is about what \(\Lambda\) does to \(e_0\).

Rung 2

Part 1: take determinants; then read off the \(00\) entry of \(\Lambda^{\mathsf T}\eta\Lambda = \eta\). Part 2: show \(\Lambda^0{}_0 \ge 1\) iff \(\Lambda\) maps the future timelike cone into itself; that property is obviously closed under composition.

Rung 3

Part 3: \(u = \Lambda e_0\) is a future unit timelike vector. Find a boost \(B\) with \(Be_0 = u\). What does \(B^{-1}\Lambda\) do to \(e_0\), and hence to \(e_0^\perp\)?

Rung 4

First line of part 2: \(\Lambda e_0\) has time component \(\Lambda^0{}_0\) and is timelike, since \(\eta(\Lambda e_0, \Lambda e_0) = \eta(e_0, e_0) = -1\). The future timelike cone is convex, hence connected…

Worked solution

Idea. Two continuous \(\{\pm1\}\)-valued invariants separate four pieces; the decomposition \(\Lambda = BR\) shows the identity piece is connected, and the others are translates of it.

1. \(\det(\Lambda^{\mathsf T}\eta\Lambda) = \det(\Lambda)^2\det\eta = \det\eta\), so \(\det(\Lambda)^2 = 1\). The \(00\) entry: \((\Lambda^{\mathsf T}\eta\Lambda)_{00} = \sum_{\mu}\eta_{\mu\mu}(\Lambda^\mu{}_0)^2 = -(\Lambda^0{}_0)^2 + \sum_{i}(\Lambda^i{}_0)^2 = -1\), so \((\Lambda^0{}_0)^2 = 1 + \sum_i(\Lambda^i{}_0)^2 \ge 1\).

2. Let \(C^+ = \{X : \eta(X,X) \lt 0,\ X^0 \gt 0\}\). It is convex: for \(X, Y \in C^+\) and \(s, t \ge 0\) not both zero, \(\eta(sX + tY, sX + tY) = s^2\eta(X,X) + 2st\,\eta(X,Y) + t^2\eta(Y,Y) \lt 0\) because \(\eta(X, Y) \lt 0\) (by \(\eta(X,Y) \le -X^0Y^0 + |\vec x||\vec y| \lt 0\)), and the time component is positive. So \(C^+\) is connected. \(\Lambda\) preserves \(\eta\), so maps \(C^+\) into the timelike vectors, which are the disjoint union of the open sets \(C^+\) and \(-C^+\). A connected image lies in one of them — the one containing \(\Lambda e_0\), whose time component is \(\Lambda^0{}_0\). So \(\Lambda^0{}_0 \ge 1 \iff \Lambda(C^+) \subseteq C^+\). Maps with \(\Lambda(C^+) \subseteq C^+\) are closed under composition; and for inverses, \(\Lambda^{-1} \in O(1,3)\) and if \(\Lambda^{-1}\) sent \(C^+\) into \(-C^+\) then \(\Lambda\) would send \(-C^+\) into \(C^+\), hence (by linearity) \(C^+\) into \(-C^+\), contradiction. So the set is a subgroup.

3. Let \(\Lambda \in SO^+(1,3)\) and \(u = \Lambda e_0 \in C^+\) with \(\eta(u,u) = -1\); write \(u = (\gamma, \gamma\vec\beta)\), \(|\vec\beta| \lt 1\), \(\gamma = (1 - |\vec\beta|^2)^{-1/2}\). Let \(B\) be the boost with velocity \(\vec\beta\): rotate \(\vec\beta\) to the \(x\)-axis, boost along \(x\), rotate back. Then \(Be_0 = u\). So \(R = B^{-1}\Lambda\) is in \(SO^+(1,3)\) and fixes \(e_0\). It preserves \(e_0^\perp = \operatorname{span}(e_1, e_2, e_3)\) (if \(\eta(e_0, Y) = 0\) then \(\eta(e_0, RY) = \eta(Re_0, RY) = \eta(e_0, Y) = 0\)), and there \(\eta\) is the Euclidean inner product, so \(R\) restricted to it is orthogonal; \(\det R = 1\) makes it a rotation. So \(\Lambda = BR\). Paths: a rotation has an axis (\(\det(R - I) = -\det(R - I)\) in odd dimension, so \(1\) is an eigenvalue) and turns the orthogonal plane by some \(\theta\); \(s\mapsto\) (the same axis, angle \(s\theta\)) joins \(I\) to \(R\). The boosts \(B_s\) with velocity \(s\vec\beta\) join \(I\) to \(B\). Then \(s\mapsto B_sR_s\) is a continuous path in \(SO^+(1,3)\) from \(I\) to \(\Lambda\).

4. \(\det\) and \(\operatorname{sign}\Lambda^0{}_0\) are continuous into \(\{\pm1\}\), so constant on each connected component: at least four components, one in each nonempty cell (\(I\), \(P\), \(T\), \(PT\) witness nonemptiness). Each cell is a single component: the cell of \(P\) is \(P\cdot SO^+(1,3)\) (if \(\det\Lambda = -1\) and \(\Lambda^0{}_0 \ge 1\) then \(P\Lambda \in SO^+\), since \(P\) does not change the \(00\) entry), the image of a path-connected set under the homeomorphism \(\Lambda\mapsto P\Lambda\); similarly for \(T\) and \(PT\). Exactly four.

Check. A special case: the boost at \(\beta = 0.8\) has \(\Lambda^0{}_0 = \gamma = 5/3 \ge 1\) and \(\det = \cosh^2 - \sinh^2 = 1\) ✓. \(T = \operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1,1)\) has \(\Lambda^0{}_0 = -1\), \(\det = -1\) ✓. Dimension count: \(O(1,3)\) is \(6\)-dimensional, three boost and three rotation parameters, matching \(\Lambda = BR\) ✓.

P4 Causal order in one space dimension medium

exercises: null coordinates, the causal order, what the Alexandrov–Zeeman theorem needs

In \(1+1\) dimensions, with \(c = 1\), write \(p \prec q\) if \(q - p\) is nonzero, future-directed and timelike or null. Use null coordinates \(u = t + x\), \(w = t - x\).

  1. Show \(p \prec q\) iff \(\Delta u \ge 0\) and \(\Delta w \ge 0\), not both zero.
  2. Show that \((u, w) \mapsto (u^3, w^3)\) is a bijection preserving \(\prec\) in both directions, and is not affine. So the Alexandrov–Zeeman theorem fails in \(1+1\) dimensions.
  3. Find all linear bijections of \(\mathbb R^2\) preserving \(\prec\) in both directions, and show they are exactly the orthochronous Lorentz boosts, the parity \(x\mapsto -x\), and their products with dilations.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

In null coordinates the future cone is a quadrant. Everything follows from that picture.

Rung 2

\(\eta(X, X) = -t^2 + x^2 = -uw\). For part 3, a linear bijection preserving the closed quadrant maps its two edge rays to its two edge rays.

Rung 3

First line of part 1: \(q - p\) is causal iff \(-\Delta u\,\Delta w \le 0\), i.e. \(\Delta u\), \(\Delta w\) have the same sign or one is zero; future-directed means \(\Delta t = \tfrac12(\Delta u + \Delta w) \gt 0\).

Worked solution

Idea. In two dimensions the cone has only two null directions, and nothing ties the scale along one to the scale along the other, so any pair of increasing reparametrisations preserves the order.

1. \(-t^2 + x^2 = -(t + x)(t - x) = -uw\). Causal: \(\Delta u\Delta w \ge 0\). Future: \(\Delta u + \Delta w \gt 0\). Together with \(q \ne p\): both \(\ge 0\), not both zero. Conversely both \(\ge 0\) and not both zero gives both conditions.

2. \(F(s) = s^3\) is a strictly increasing bijection of \(\mathbb R\), so \(\Delta u \ge 0 \iff \Delta(u^3) \ge 0\), with \(\Delta u = 0 \iff \Delta(u^3) = 0\); likewise for \(w\). By part 1 the map preserves \(\prec\) both ways. It is not affine: it sends the points \(u = 0, 1, 2\) on the line \(w = 0\) to \(u = 0, 1, 8\), not equally spaced. Back in \((t, x)\) it is a genuinely non-linear map of the plane preserving all causal relations.

3. A linear bijection \(A\) preserving \(\prec\) both ways maps the closed quadrant \(Q = \{u \ge 0, w \ge 0\}\) onto itself. The edge rays of \(Q\) are its extreme rays (not positive combinations of two independent vectors of \(Q\)), and a linear bijection of \(Q\) onto itself maps extreme rays to extreme rays. So either \(A(u, w) = (au, bw)\) or \(A(u, w) = (bw, au)\), with \(a, b \gt 0\). Write \(a = \lambda e^{\varphi}\), \(b = \lambda e^{-\varphi}\) with \(\lambda = \sqrt{ab}\). The map \((u, w)\mapsto(e^{\varphi}u, e^{-\varphi}w)\) is the boost of rapidity \(-\varphi\) (check: \(t + x \mapsto e^{\varphi}(t + x)\) is what \(\cosh\varphi\,t + \sinh\varphi\,x\), \(\sinh\varphi\,t + \cosh\varphi\,x\) do); the swap \(u \leftrightarrow w\) is \(x\mapsto -x\); and \(\lambda\) is a dilation. So the linear causal automorphisms are exactly orthochronous Lorentz transformations times dilations — Zeeman's conclusion, which in \(1+1\) holds for linear maps and fails without linearity.

Check. A special case: the boost at \(\beta = 0.8\) has \(e^{\varphi} = 3\), and acts as \((u, w)\mapsto(u/3, 3w)\) or its inverse; it preserves \(uw\), hence \(\eta\) ✓. The dilation by \(\lambda\) multiplies \(\eta\) by \(\lambda^2 \gt 0\), keeping every sign ✓.

Simultaneity and its corollaries

P5 Muons from the upper atmosphere routine

exercises: time dilation, length contraction, the same number from two frames

A muon is produced \(15.0\) km above sea level and travels straight down at \(0.995c\). Its rest lifetime is \(\tau_\mu = 2.1969811\ \mu\)s; the probability of surviving a proper time \(\tau\) is \(e^{-\tau/\tau_\mu}\).

  1. Compute the survival probability to sea level in the Earth frame, using time dilation.
  2. Compute it in the muon's frame, using length contraction. Show the two agree exactly, not just numerically.
  3. What would a Galilean physicist predict?
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Three numbers between \(0\) and \(1\); the first two must be equal, and the reason is one invariant.

Rung 2

Earth frame: the trip takes \(t = L/v\) and the muon's clock advances \(t/\gamma\). Muon frame: the atmosphere is \(L/\gamma\) thick and passes at \(v\).

Rung 3

First line: \(\gamma = (1 - 0.995^2)^{-1/2} = (0.009975)^{-1/2} = 10.0125\).

Worked solution

Idea. The muon's proper time from production to sea level is a frame-independent number; each frame computes it in its own way.

1. \(\gamma = 10.0125\). The trip: \(t = 15000/(0.995\times299\,792\,458) = 50.286\ \mu\)s. The muon's proper time is \(t/\gamma = 5.0223\ \mu\)s. Survival \(e^{-5.0223/2.19698} = e^{-2.2860} = 0.1017\). About one muon in ten reaches sea level. (Equivalently, the lab lifetime \(\gamma\tau_\mu = 21.997\ \mu\)s against \(50.286\ \mu\)s of flight.)

2. In the muon's frame the \(15.0\) km column is contracted to \(15000/10.0125 = 1498.1\) m, and passes at \(0.995c\) in \(1498.1/(0.995c) = 5.0223\ \mu\)s of the muon's own time: survival \(0.1017\). Exactly equal, because \((L/\gamma)/v = (L/v)/\gamma\): both are the proper time \(\tau = \sqrt{t^2 - L^2/c^2}\) between production and arrival, the invariant interval divided by \(c\). Indeed \(\sqrt{50.286^2 - 50.035^2} = 5.022\ \mu\)s, with \(L/c = 50.035\ \mu\)s.

3. No dilation: survival \(e^{-50.286/2.19698} = e^{-22.889} = 1.15\times10^{-10}\). Almost no muons would reach the ground; in fact they are the dominant charged component of cosmic rays at sea level.

Check. Dimensions: \(\tau/\tau_\mu\) is a ratio of times ✓. Limit \(v\ll c\): \(\gamma\to1\) and the two predictions merge ✓. Order of magnitude: \(\gamma\approx10\) makes the exponent \(10\) times smaller, \(22.9\to2.29\) ✓.

P6 The barn medium

exercises: events as the unit of description, transforming events, the relativity of simultaneity

A barn has rest length \(10\) m, a front door at \(x = 0\) and a back door at \(x = 10\) m. A pole of rest length \(20\) m moves in the \(+x\) direction with \(\gamma = 2\). In the barn frame, at \(t = 0\) the pole's rear passes the front door; the farmer shuts both doors at that instant and opens them again at once.

  1. Show that in the barn frame the pole is exactly inside when the doors shut.
  2. Write the two door closings as events \(A\) (front) and \(B\) (back), and transform both to the pole's frame. Which shuts first, and by how much?
  3. Show that in the pole's frame the pole's front is at the back door when \(B\) happens and its rear at the front door when \(A\) happens, and check the time between them by a separate argument in the pole's frame.
  4. Explain why there is no contradiction, in one sentence about the interval \(AB\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

The answer to 2 is a time in nanoseconds with a sign; the rest is bookkeeping of four worldlines (two doors, two pole ends) and two events.

Rung 2

\(\gamma = 2\) gives \(\beta = \sqrt3/2\). In the barn frame \(A = (0, 0)\) and \(B = (0, 10\ \mathrm m)\) as \((ct, x)\). Apply \(ct' = \gamma(ct - \beta x)\).

Rung 3

First line: \(ct'_B = 2\,(0 - \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\cdot10\ \mathrm m) = -10\sqrt3\ \mathrm m\), while \(ct'_A = 0\).

Worked solution

Idea. "The pole is inside the barn" means "both ends are inside at one instant" — a statement about simultaneity. The frames disagree about which events are simultaneous, and about nothing else.

1. In the barn frame the pole has length \(20/\gamma = 10\) m. At \(t = 0\) its rear is at \(x = 0\), so its front is at \(x = 10\) m: exactly spanning the barn.

2. \(\beta = \sqrt{1 - 1/4} = \sqrt3/2 = 0.8660\). \(A = (ct, x) = (0, 0) \mapsto (0, 0)\). \(B = (0, 10\ \mathrm m) \mapsto ct'_B = -\gamma\beta\cdot10\ \mathrm m = -17.32\ \mathrm m\), \(x'_B = \gamma\cdot10 = 20\) m. So in the pole's frame the back door shuts first, by \(17.32\ \mathrm m/c = 57.8\) ns.

3. Pole frame: the pole is at rest with rear at \(x' = 0\), front at \(x' = 20\) m (the rear is at the origin because the rear passes the front door at \(A\), the common origin). \(B\) is at \(x' = 20\) m: the front end ✓. \(A\) is at \(x' = 0\): the rear end ✓. Separate argument: in the pole frame the barn has length \(10/\gamma = 5\) m and moves at \(-\beta c\). At \(B\) the back door is at the pole's front, \(x' = 20\), so the front door is at \(x' = 15\) m. It must travel \(15\) m to reach the rear at \(x' = 0\), taking \(15\ \mathrm m/(0.8660c) = 17.32\ \mathrm m/c = 57.8\) ns ✓. So in the pole frame: the back door shuts and reopens just as the front arrives, the barn slides on, and \(57.8\) ns later the front door shuts and reopens just behind the rear. Never both shut, never the pole inside, never a collision.

4. \(A\) and \(B\) are spacelike separated (interval \((10\ \mathrm m)^2 \gt 0\)), so their time order is frame-dependent and "both shut at once" is not a frame-independent statement; every frame-independent fact — which pole end is at which door at each closing, and that nothing collides — is the same in both frames.

Check. Invariance: \(-(ct'_B)^2 + (x'_B)^2 = -300 + 400 = 100\ \mathrm m^2 = (10\ \mathrm m)^2\) ✓. Dimensions: metres over \(c\) is seconds, \(17.32/299\,792\,458 = 5.78\times10^{-8}\) s ✓. Limit \(\beta\to0\): the delay \(\gamma\beta\cdot10\ \mathrm m/c\to0\), and the paradox disappears along with the contraction ✓.

P7 Proof: radar simultaneity is orthogonality medium

exercises: Einstein synchronisation, null vectors, the bilinear form doing geometry

Units \(c = 1\). An inertial observer passes through the origin with unit future timelike direction \(u\), \(\eta(u, u) = -1\), and carries a clock reading \(\tau\) at the event \(\tau u\). To an event \(E\) the observer assigns radar time \(\tau_E = \tfrac12(\tau_1 + \tau_2)\) and radar distance \(d_E = \tfrac12(\tau_2 - \tau_1)\), where a light signal leaving at \(\tau_1\) reaches \(E\) and its reflection returns at \(\tau_2\).

  1. Prove \(\tau_E = -\eta(E, u)\) and \(d_E^2 = \eta(E, E) + \eta(E, u)^2\).
  2. Deduce that the events radar-simultaneous with the origin form the hyperplane \(u^\perp\), and that on it radar distance is \(\sqrt{\eta(E, E)}\).
  3. For \(u = (\gamma, \gamma\beta, 0, 0)\), write \(u^\perp\) as an equation in \((t, x)\) and identify it with the deck's lines of constant \(t'\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

\(\tau_1\) and \(\tau_2\) are the two roots of one quadratic equation; the statement asks for their sum and difference.

Rung 2

The signal from \(\tau u\) to \(E\) (or back) travels along a null vector: \(\eta(E - \tau u, E - \tau u) = 0\). Expand by bilinearity.

Rung 3

First line: \(0 = \eta(E,E) - 2\tau\,\eta(E,u) + \tau^2\eta(u,u) = \eta(E,E) - 2\tau\,\eta(E,u) - \tau^2\), i.e. \(\tau^2 + 2\eta(E,u)\,\tau - \eta(E,E) = 0\). Vieta.

Worked solution

Idea. Radar coordinates are the roots of the equation "\(E - \tau u\) is null", and the sum and product of the roots are read off the coefficients, which are values of \(\eta\).

1. Both \(\tau_1\) (outgoing: \(E - \tau_1u\) future null) and \(\tau_2\) (returning: \(\tau_2u - E\) future null) satisfy \(\eta(E - \tau u, E - \tau u) = 0\), which expands to \(\tau^2 + 2\eta(E,u)\tau - \eta(E,E) = 0\). For \(E\) not on the worldline this quadratic has two real roots (the discriminant \(4\eta(E,u)^2 + 4\eta(E,E) = 4d_E^2\) is positive, as follows), and they are \(\tau_1 \lt \tau_2\). Vieta: \(\tau_1 + \tau_2 = -2\eta(E,u)\), \(\tau_1\tau_2 = -\eta(E,E)\). So \(\tau_E = -\eta(E,u)\), and \(d_E^2 = \tfrac14\big((\tau_1 + \tau_2)^2 - 4\tau_1\tau_2\big) = \eta(E,u)^2 + \eta(E,E)\). Positivity: write \(E = aU + Z\) with \(U = u\), \(Z \perp u\); then \(\eta(E,u) = -a\) and \(\eta(E,E) = -a^2 + \eta(Z,Z)\), so \(d_E^2 = \eta(Z,Z) \ge 0\), positive unless \(Z = 0\), i.e. unless \(E\) is on the worldline (the deck's lemma: \(u^\perp\) is spacelike).

2. \(\tau_E = 0 \iff \eta(E, u) = 0 \iff E \in u^\perp\), a hyperplane since \(\eta\) is non-degenerate. On it, \(d_E^2 = \eta(E,E)\), so \(d_E = \sqrt{\eta(E,E)}\): the observer's radar distance to simultaneous events is the \(\eta\)-length, and \(u^\perp\) with \(\eta\) restricted is the observer's Euclidean space.

3. \(\eta(E, u) = -\gamma t + \gamma\beta x = 0 \iff t = \beta x\) (with \(c = 1\)): the \(x'\) axis, and the parallel hyperplanes \(\eta(E, u) = -\tau\) are \(\gamma(t - \beta x) = \tau\), i.e. \(t' = \tau\) — exactly the Lorentz transformation's lines of constant \(t'\).

Check. A special case: \(u = e_0\) (\(\beta = 0\)), \(E = (t, x)\): \(\tau_E = t\) and \(d_E^2 = -t^2 + x^2 + t^2 = x^2\) ✓. Dimensions (restoring \(c\)): \(\eta(E, u)\) with dimensionless \(u\) is a length, \(c\tau_E\) ✓. Number: for the deck's traveller on the outbound leg, \(u = (5/3, 4/3)\) and \(E = \) Earth at \(t = 1.8\): \(\tau_E = -(-\tfrac53\cdot1.8 + 0) = 3\), the traveller's clock at the turn ✓.

P8 Aberration, headlights and Doppler in one vector medium

exercises: boosting a null vector, aberration, the headlight effect, the oblique Doppler formula

A source moves at \(\beta\) along \(+x\) in the lab. In its rest frame it emits light of angular frequency \(\omega'\) at angle \(\theta'\) to the \(x\)-axis. Represent the ray by the null vector \(k' = (\omega'/c)(1, \cos\theta', \sin\theta', 0)\).

  1. Boost \(k'\) to the lab and read off the lab angle \(\theta\) and frequency \(\omega\).
  2. Show your \(\omega\) agrees with the deck's oblique Doppler formula \(\omega = \omega'/\big(\gamma(1 - \beta\cos\theta)\big)\).
  3. Headlight: for isotropic emission in the source frame, find the lab half-angle of the cone holding half the photons, and evaluate it at \(\gamma = 10\).
  4. Stellar aberration: an observer moving at Earth's orbital speed sees a star perpendicular to its motion displaced by what angle, in arcseconds?
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

One matrix times one vector, then ratios of components. Part 2 is an algebraic identity once part 1's angle formula is in hand.

Rung 2

The lab sees the source moving at \(+\beta\), so lab components are \(k^0 = \gamma(k'^0 + \beta k'^x)\), \(k^x = \gamma(k'^x + \beta k'^0)\). The frequency is \(c k^0\), and \(\cos\theta = k^x/k^0\). For part 4, the observer moves, not the source: apply the same formula with the roles of the frames exchanged, at \(\theta' = 90°\).

Rung 3

First line: \(k = (\omega'/c)\big(\gamma(1 + \beta\cos\theta'),\ \gamma(\cos\theta' + \beta),\ \sin\theta',\ 0\big)\).

Worked solution

Idea. A light ray, direction and frequency together, is one null vector. A boost acts on it linearly; aberration is its direction changing, Doppler its time component changing.

1. \(k = (\omega'/c)\big(\gamma(1 + \beta\cos\theta'),\ \gamma(\cos\theta' + \beta),\ \sin\theta',\ 0\big)\). So \[ \omega = \gamma(1 + \beta\cos\theta')\,\omega', \qquad \cos\theta = \frac{\cos\theta' + \beta}{1 + \beta\cos\theta'}, \qquad \sin\theta = \frac{\sin\theta'}{\gamma(1 + \beta\cos\theta')}. \] Null check: \(\gamma^2(\cos\theta' + \beta)^2 + \sin^2\theta' - \gamma^2(1 + \beta\cos\theta')^2 = \gamma^2(1 - \beta^2)(\cos^2\theta' - 1) + \sin^2\theta' = 0\) ✓.

2. \(1 - \beta\cos\theta = \dfrac{1 + \beta\cos\theta' - \beta\cos\theta' - \beta^2}{1 + \beta\cos\theta'} = \dfrac{1 - \beta^2}{1 + \beta\cos\theta'}\). Hence \(\gamma(1 - \beta\cos\theta)\cdot\gamma(1 + \beta\cos\theta') = \gamma^2(1 - \beta^2) = 1\), and \(\omega'/\big(\gamma(1 - \beta\cos\theta)\big) = \gamma(1 + \beta\cos\theta')\omega' = \omega\) ✓.

3. Half the photons have \(\theta' \lt 90°\). \(\theta' = 90°\) maps to \(\cos\theta = \beta\), \(\sin\theta = 1/\gamma\), and the map \(\theta'\mapsto\theta\) is increasing, so half the photons lie within \(\theta_{1/2} = \arcsin(1/\gamma)\). At \(\gamma = 10\): \(\arcsin 0.1 = 0.10017\) rad \(= 5.739°\).

4. In the Sun's frame the starlight arrives perpendicular to the Earth's velocity; in the Earth's frame it arrives tilted forward by \(\alpha\) with \(\sin\alpha = \beta\) to first order (from part 1's \(\cos\theta = \beta\) at \(\theta' = 90°\): \(\theta = 90° - \alpha\)). \(\beta = 29\,780/299\,792\,458 = 9.934\times10^{-5}\), so \(\alpha = 9.934\times10^{-5}\) rad \(= 20.49''\). The star traces a small ellipse of semi-major axis \(20.5''\) over the year; Bradley announced it in 1729.

Check. \(\beta = 0\): \(\theta = \theta'\), \(\omega = \omega'\) ✓. \(\theta' = 0\): \(\cos\theta = 1\), \(\omega = \gamma(1+\beta)\omega' = e^{\varphi}\omega'\), the collinear approaching Doppler factor ✓. \(\gamma\to\infty\): the cone closes to zero angle ✓.

Causal structure

P9 No superluminal signalling medium

exercises: the causal structure, reversing the order of spacelike events, the antitelephone

  1. Prove that if \(q - p\) is spacelike there is an inertial frame in which \(q\) precedes \(p\), and find the range of boost velocities (along \(\Delta\vec x\)) that do it.
  2. Prove that if \(q - p\) is future timelike, \(q\) is later than \(p\) in every frame obtained by a boost of speed below \(c\).
  3. Years and light-years. A signal leaves Earth at \(t = 0\) at speed \(2c\) and is received \(1\) light-year away. When is it received in the frame moving at \(0.8c\) along the signal's direction? For which boost speeds does it arrive before it left?
  4. Explain in words why one backwards-in-time signal is not yet a contradiction, and what is.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Everything reduces to the sign of \(\Delta t' = \gamma(\Delta t - v\Delta x/c^2)\) as \(v\) ranges over \((-c, c)\).

Rung 2

Orient \(x\) along \(\Delta\vec x\) so \(\Delta x \gt 0\). Spacelike means \(\Delta x \gt c|\Delta t|\), timelike means \(\Delta x \lt c\Delta t\). Compare \(c^2\Delta t/\Delta x\) with \(c\).

Rung 3

First line of part 1: \(\Delta t' \lt 0 \iff v \gt c^2\Delta t/\Delta x\), and \(|c^2\Delta t/\Delta x| \lt c\), so the interval \((c^2\Delta t/\Delta x,\ c)\) of admissible \(v\) is nonempty.

Worked solution

Idea. The boost changes \(\Delta t\) by an amount proportional to \(v\Delta x\). For spacelike separation that amount can exceed \(\Delta t\); for timelike separation it never can.

1. Rotate so \(\Delta\vec x = (\Delta x, 0, 0)\), \(\Delta x \gt 0\). With \(v_0 := c^2\Delta t/\Delta x\), spacelike gives \(|v_0| \lt c\). For a boost at \(v\): \(\Delta t' = \gamma(\Delta t - v\Delta x/c^2) = \gamma(\Delta x/c^2)(v_0 - v)\), negative exactly for \(v_0 \lt v \lt c\) — a nonempty range. (At \(v = v_0\) the events are simultaneous; below it, the order is kept.)

2. Timelike future: \(\Delta t \gt 0\) and \(|\Delta\vec x| \lt c\Delta t\). For any boost at \(|v| \lt c\) along any direction, the change is \(v\Delta x_\parallel/c^2\) with \(|v\Delta x_\parallel/c^2| \lt c\cdot c\Delta t/c^2 = \Delta t\), so \(\Delta t' = \gamma(\Delta t - v\Delta x_\parallel/c^2) \gt 0\).

3. \(\Delta t = 0.5\) yr, \(\Delta x = 1\) ly: \(v_0 = 0.5\). At \(v = 0.8\), \(\gamma = 5/3\): \(\Delta t' = \tfrac53(0.5 - 0.8) = -0.5\) yr. The signal arrives half a year before it left. It arrives before leaving for every boost with \(0.5c \lt v \lt c\).

4. A single signal whose reception precedes its emission in some frame is strange but contains no loop: the sender still sends before (in her frame) the receiver receives. A contradiction needs a round trip. By the principle of relativity, the receiver, moving at \(v\), may send a speed-\(2c\) signal backwards in his frame, and for \(v \gt 2u/(1 + u^2) = 0.8c\) (with \(u = 2c\)) it reaches the original sender before she sent the first signal — she could then decide not to send it. Relativity plus "no causal loops" forbids faster-than-light signals; relativity alone forbids only their being harmless.

Check. Invariance: \(-(0.5)^2 + 1^2 = 0.75\), and in the moving frame \(\Delta x' = \tfrac53(1 - 0.8\cdot0.5) = 1\), \(-(0.5)^2 + 1 = 0.75\) ✓. Limit: a signal at exactly \(c\) has \(v_0 = c\), and the range \((c, c)\) is empty — light signals keep their order ✓.

Proper time and the twins

P10 The twins by radio, again routine

exercises: proper time on piecewise-straight worldlines, the Doppler factor, simultaneity jumps

Years and light-years. A traveller flies at \(0.6c\) to a star \(12\) light-years away, turns at once, and returns at \(0.6c\). Each twin sends one radio pulse per year of their own clock.

  1. Compute both twins' elapsed proper times.
  2. Compute the Doppler factor and count the pulses each twin receives on each leg. When does each see the flip?
  3. Find where the traveller's two lines of simultaneity through the turn meet Earth's worldline, and the size of the jump.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Six numbers, and three consistency checks: the counts must reproduce the ages, and the simultaneity pieces must add up to Earth's age.

Rung 2

\(\gamma = 1.25\), rapidity \(\operatorname{artanh}0.6 = \tfrac12\ln 4 = \ln 2\), so the Doppler factor is \(2\). Earth sees the flip when the light from the turn arrives.

Rung 3

First line: each leg takes \(12/0.6 = 20\) Earth years, so the turn is the event \((t, x) = (20, 12)\).

Worked solution

Idea. The deck's three computations, with new numbers; if they disagree, an arithmetic slip has been made.

1. Earth: \(40\) yr. Traveller: each leg \(20\sqrt{1 - 0.36} = 20\times0.8 = 16\) yr, total \(32\) yr.

2. \(\sqrt{1.6/0.4} = 2\). Traveller receives: outbound, \(16\) yr at one pulse per \(2\) yr, \(8\) pulses; inbound, \(16\) yr at one per \(\tfrac12\) yr, \(32\) pulses; total \(40\) = Earth's age ✓. The flip is at the turn, traveller's year \(16\) of \(32\). Earth receives: light from the turn \((20, 12)\) arrives at \(t = 32\); for \(32\) yr pulses arrive every \(2\) yr, \(16\) pulses; in the last \(8\) yr every \(\tfrac12\) yr, \(16\) pulses; total \(32\) = the traveller's age ✓. Earth sees the flip at year \(32\) of \(40\).

3. Outbound simultaneity through \((20, 12)\): \(t - 20 = 0.6(x - 12)\), at \(x = 0\): \(t = 12.8\). Inbound: \(t = 20 + 7.2 = 27.2\). Jump: \(14.4\) yr. Pieces: \(12.8 + 14.4 + 12.8 = 40\) ✓, and \(12.8 = 16/\gamma\) ✓.

Check. The reversed triangle inequality: \(\lVert(20, 12)\rVert = \sqrt{400 - 144} = 16\), and \(16 + 16 = 32 \le 40 = \lVert(40, 0)\rVert\) ✓. The Earth events spacelike to the turn have \(|t - 20| \lt 12\), i.e. \(8 \lt t \lt 32\): the jump \([12.8, 27.2]\) lies inside, and \(8\) and \(32\) are the flip-related times (the last pulse the traveller gets before turning was sent at \(8\); Earth sees the turn at \(32\)) ✓. Limit \(\beta\to0\): all three give equal ages ✓.

P11 Proper time along three worldlines medium

exercises: evaluating \(\tau = \int\sqrt{1 - |\dot{\vec x}|^2/c^2}\,\mathrm dt\), substitutions, hyperbolic functions

Compute the proper time elapsed along each worldline, in the frame given.

  1. A probe whose speed rises linearly, \(v(t) = 0.8c\,t/T\), for \(0 \le t \le T\). Give \(\tau/T\) to three figures.
  2. A clock on the International Space Station, idealised as a circle at \(7.66\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}\) in an inertial frame (ignore gravity, which is unit 30's business). How much does it lose per day against a clock at rest, in microseconds?
  3. The worldline \(x(t) = \sqrt{\ell^2 + c^2t^2} - \ell\), for \(0 \le t \le t_1\). Show \(\tau = (\ell/c)\operatorname{arsinh}(ct_1/\ell)\). With \(\ell = c^2/g\) (\(g = 9.80665\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\)) and \(t_1 = 10\) yr, evaluate \(\tau\) and the distance covered.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Three definite integrals of \(\sqrt{1 - \beta(t)^2}\). One is trivial, one needs a trigonometric substitution, one a hyperbolic one.

Rung 2

Part 1: substitute \(\beta = 0.8t/T\). Part 2: constant speed, so \(\tau = t\sqrt{1 - \beta^2}\) and the loss is \(t(1 - \sqrt{1-\beta^2}) \approx t\beta^2/2\). Part 3: compute \(\dot x\) and simplify \(1 - \dot x^2/c^2\) first.

Rung 3

First line of part 3: \(\dot x = c^2t/\sqrt{\ell^2 + c^2t^2}\), so \(1 - \dot x^2/c^2 = \ell^2/(\ell^2 + c^2t^2)\).

Worked solution

Idea. Proper time is the Lorentzian arc length; parametrised by the frame's \(t\) it is an ordinary integral of \(\sqrt{1 - \beta^2}\), which is at most \(1\), so every answer must come out less than the coordinate time.

1. With \(\beta = 0.8t/T\), \(\mathrm dt = (T/0.8)\,\mathrm d\beta\): \[ \tau = \frac{T}{0.8}\int_0^{0.8}\sqrt{1 - \beta^2}\,\mathrm d\beta = \frac{T}{0.8}\cdot\tfrac12\Big[\beta\sqrt{1-\beta^2} + \arcsin\beta\Big]_0^{0.8} = \frac{T}{1.6}\,(0.48 + 0.92730) = 0.880\,T. \] (The antiderivative by \(\beta = \sin s\): \(\int\cos^2 s\,\mathrm ds = \tfrac12(s + \sin s\cos s)\).)

2. \(\beta = 7660/299\,792\,458 = 2.555\times10^{-5}\). Loss per day \(= 86\,400\,(1 - \sqrt{1 - \beta^2})\ \mathrm s \approx 86\,400\,\beta^2/2 = 2.820\times10^{-5}\) s \(= 28.2\ \mu\)s. (The exact and first-order values agree to five figures.)

3. \(\dot x = c^2t/\sqrt{\ell^2 + c^2t^2}\), \(1 - \dot x^2/c^2 = (\ell^2 + c^2t^2 - c^2t^2)/(\ell^2 + c^2t^2) = \ell^2/(\ell^2 + c^2t^2)\). So \[ \tau = \int_0^{t_1}\frac{\ell\,\mathrm dt}{\sqrt{\ell^2 + c^2t^2}} = \frac{\ell}{c}\operatorname{arsinh}\frac{ct_1}{\ell}, \] by \(ct = \ell\sinh s\), \(c\,\mathrm dt = \ell\cosh s\,\mathrm ds\), \(\sqrt{\ell^2 + c^2t^2} = \ell\cosh s\). Numbers: \(\ell = c^2/g = 9.1648\times10^{15}\) m \(= 0.96872\) ly, so \(\ell/c = 0.96872\) yr, and \(ct_1/\ell = 10/0.96872 = 10.323\). \(\tau = 0.96872\operatorname{arsinh}(10.323) = 2.935\) yr. Distance \(\sqrt{0.96872^2 + 10^2} - 0.96872 = 9.078\) ly. Ten years pass on Earth, under three on board. (This worldline has constant proper acceleration \(g\), as unit 27 shows; it is the rocket that would feel like standing on Earth.)

Check. Every \(\tau\) is below its coordinate time: \(0.880T \lt T\), \(2.935 \lt 10\) ✓. Limits: in part 1, \(0.8\to0\) gives \(\tau\to T\) ✓; in part 3, \(ct_1 \ll \ell\) gives \(\operatorname{arsinh}z \approx z\) and \(\tau \approx t_1\) ✓, while for \(ct_1 \gg \ell\), \(\tau \approx (\ell/c)\ln(2ct_1/\ell)\), only logarithmic growth ✓. Dimensions: \(\ell/c\) is a time ✓.

P12 Proof: the straight worldline is the longest hard

exercises: the reversed Cauchy–Schwarz and triangle inequalities, the maximum principle for proper time, the absence of a minimum

  1. Prove: if \(X\) is timelike, its orthogonal complement \(X^\perp\) is spacelike (every nonzero vector in it is spacelike).
  2. Prove the reversed Cauchy–Schwarz inequality \(\eta(X, Y)^2 \ge \eta(X, X)\eta(Y, Y)\) for timelike \(X\), with equality iff \(Y \parallel X\), and the reversed triangle inequality \(\lVert X + Y\rVert \ge \lVert X\rVert + \lVert Y\rVert\) for future timelike \(X, Y\).
  3. Prove that among all future-directed timelike piecewise-\(C^1\) curves from \(p\) to \(q\) (with \(q - p\) future timelike), the straight segment uniquely maximises proper time.
  4. Show that the infimum of proper time over such curves is \(0\) and is not attained.
Hints · 4 rungs
Rung 1

Four proofs, each a few lines, each using the one before. The only tools are bilinearity, a dimension count, and the monotonicity of the integral.

Rung 2

Part 1: a nonzero \(Z \perp X\) with \(\eta(Z,Z) \le 0\) makes \(\eta \le 0\) on a 2-plane; compare with the 3-plane where \(\eta\) is positive definite. Part 2: decompose \(Y = aX + Z\), \(Z \perp X\).

Rung 3

Part 3: choose the inertial frame in which \(p\) and \(q\) are at the same place, and parametrise by that frame's time. Part 4: go back and forth fast.

Rung 4

First line of part 2: with \(a = \eta(X,Y)/\eta(X,X)\) and \(Z = Y - aX\), \(\eta(X,Y)^2 - \eta(X,X)\eta(Y,Y) = -\eta(X,X)\,\eta(Z,Z)\).

Worked solution

Idea. Split any vector along and across a timelike \(X\); across is spacelike, and because \(\eta(X,X) \lt 0\) the spacelike part pushes the inequality the "wrong" way. For curves, go to the frame where the endpoints are at one place: there proper time is \(\int\sqrt{1 - |\dot{\vec x}|^2/c^2}\,\mathrm dt\) and the integrand is at most \(1\).

1. Suppose \(Z \ne 0\), \(\eta(X, Z) = 0\), \(\eta(Z, Z) \le 0\). \(Z\) is not a multiple of \(X\) (else \(\eta(X,Z) = a\eta(X,X) \ne 0\)), so \(W = \operatorname{span}(X, Z)\) is 2-dimensional, and \(\eta(aX + bZ, aX + bZ) = a^2\eta(X,X) + b^2\eta(Z,Z) \le 0\) on \(W\). The spatial span \(S = \operatorname{span}(e_1, e_2, e_3)\) has \(\dim(W \cap S) \ge 2 + 3 - 4 = 1\), so some nonzero \(w \in W \cap S\) has \(\eta(w,w) \gt 0\) — contradicting \(\eta \le 0\) on \(W\).

2. Let \(a = \eta(X,Y)/\eta(X,X)\), \(Z = Y - aX\); then \(\eta(X, Z) = 0\) and \(\eta(Y,Y) = a^2\eta(X,X) + \eta(Z,Z)\). So \[ \eta(X,Y)^2 - \eta(X,X)\eta(Y,Y) = a^2\eta(X,X)^2 - a^2\eta(X,X)^2 - \eta(X,X)\eta(Z,Z) = -\eta(X,X)\,\eta(Z,Z) \ge 0, \] since \(-\eta(X,X) \gt 0\) and, by part 1, \(\eta(Z,Z) \ge 0\) with equality iff \(Z = 0\), i.e. \(Y = aX\). For the triangle inequality: future timelike \(X, Y\) have \(\eta(X,Y) \le -X^0Y^0 + |\vec x||\vec y| \lt 0\), so \(-\eta(X,Y) = |\eta(X,Y)| \ge \lVert X\rVert\lVert Y\rVert\), and \[ \lVert X + Y\rVert^2 = \lVert X\rVert^2 + \lVert Y\rVert^2 - 2\eta(X,Y) \ge (\lVert X\rVert + \lVert Y\rVert)^2 . \] \(X + Y\) is future timelike (the cone is convex), so taking square roots is legitimate. Equality forces \(Y = aX\), \(a \gt 0\).

3. Translate \(p\) to \(0\). \(q = (c\Delta t, \Delta\vec x)\) with \(|\Delta\vec x| \lt c\Delta t\); rotate \(\Delta\vec x\) onto the \(x\)-axis and boost with \(\beta = |\Delta\vec x|/(c\Delta t)\): now \(q = (cT, \vec 0)\) with \(cT = \lVert q - p\rVert\). Proper time is invariant under these maps. A future timelike curve has \(\mathrm dt/\mathrm d\lambda \gt 0\), so \(t\) parametrises it on \([0, T]\) with \(\vec x(0) = \vec x(T) = \vec 0\), and \[ \tau = \int_0^T\sqrt{1 - |\dot{\vec x}(t)|^2/c^2}\,\mathrm dt \le \int_0^T 1\,\mathrm dt = T = \lVert q - p\rVert/c . \] If \(\tau = T\), the non-negative piecewise-continuous function \(1 - \sqrt{1 - |\dot{\vec x}|^2/c^2}\) has integral zero, so vanishes on each piece: \(\dot{\vec x} \equiv 0\), \(\vec x \equiv 0\), the straight segment. And the segment attains \(T\).

4. In the same frame, for \(0 \lt \beta \lt 1\) and \(n \ge 1\), the zig-zag moving at \(+\beta c\) for time \(T/2n\) then \(-\beta c\) for \(T/2n\), repeated \(n\) times, is future timelike, piecewise \(C^1\), from \(p\) to \(q\), with \(\tau = T\sqrt{1 - \beta^2}\). As \(\beta\to1\), \(\tau\to0\): the infimum is \(0\). It is not attained, because the integrand is strictly positive on a timelike curve, so \(\tau \gt 0\).

Check. On the deck's twins, \(X = (5, 4)\), \(Y = (5, -4)\): \(\eta(X,Y)^2 = 41^2 = 1681 \ge 81 = (-9)(-9)\) ✓, and \(\lVert X + Y\rVert = 10 \ge 3 + 3\) ✓. A number for part 4: at \(\beta = 0.99\) the zig-zag between the twins' meeting events has \(\tau = 10\sqrt{1 - 0.9801} = 1.41\) yr. Limit \(c\to\infty\): \(\tau\to T\) for every curve, absolute time ✓.
