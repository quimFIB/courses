The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: the antiproton threshold, \(p + p \to p + p + p + \bar p\).
The invariant \(s\) must reach \((4m_pc^2)^2 = (3.753\ \text{GeV})^2\). On a
fixed target \(s = 2m_p^2c^4 + 2Em_pc^2\), so the beam needs kinetic energy
\(6m_pc^2 = 5.630\) GeV; with two colliding beams \(s = 4E^2\), so each needs
only \(m_pc^2 = 0.938\) GeV. The Bevatron ran at about 6.2 GeV and found the
antiproton in 1955.

* Four-vectors

- representation :: Of a group \(G\) on a vector space \(W\): a homomorphism
  \(\rho: G \to GL(W)\). Each kind of relativistic quantity is an element of
  some representation of the Lorentz group. /Slide: A four-vector is an element of a representation./
- four-vector :: An element of Minkowski's vector space \(V\), which carries the
  defining representation of \(O(1,3)\). That its components transform as
  \(w'^\mu = \Lambda^\mu{}_\nu w^\nu\) is a theorem about change of basis, not the
  definition.
- Lorentz scalar :: An element of the trivial representation: a number every
  inertial frame agrees on, such as \(\eta(v,w)\) or a particle's mass.
- proper time :: The Lorentzian arc length of a worldline,
  \(\tau = \frac1c\int\sqrt{-\eta(\gamma',\gamma')}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\): what a clock
  carried along it reads. Independent of the parametrisation; \(\mathrm{d}\tau =
  \mathrm{d}t/\gamma\) in a frame. /Slide: Proper time is arc length./
- worldline :: A \(C^2\) curve in spacetime with timelike, future-pointing
  tangent: the history of a massive particle.
- four-velocity :: \(u = \mathrm{d}\gamma/\mathrm{d}\tau\), the tangent of a worldline
  parametrised by proper time. Always \(\eta(u,u) = -c^2\); in a frame
  \(u = \gamma(c,\vec v)\). /Slide: Four-velocity, and the constraint it obeys./
- four-acceleration :: \(a = \mathrm{d}u/\mathrm{d}\tau\). Always \(\eta\)-orthogonal to
  \(u\), hence spacelike or zero. /Slide: Acceleration is orthogonal to velocity./
- proper acceleration :: \(\alpha = \sqrt{\eta(a,a)}\), the magnitude of the
  four-acceleration: what an accelerometer carried by the particle reads.

* The action is proper time

- free-particle action :: \(S = -mc^2\int\mathrm{d}\tau\). The only functional of a
  worldline's tangent that is Lorentz-invariant and reparametrisation-invariant,
  up to the constant, which the Newtonian limit fixes. /Slide: The only free action there is./
- rest energy :: \(mc^2\), the energy of a particle at rest. Fixed, not
  conventional, because energy is a component of a four-vector.
  /Slide: Energy from Noether: E = \gamma mc^2./
- kinetic energy :: \(T = E - mc^2 = (\gamma - 1)mc^2\), tending to
  \(\tfrac12mv^2\) at low speed.
- four-momentum :: \(p = mu = (E/c,\ \vec p)\): energy and momentum as the
  components of one vector in \(V\). /Slide: Four-momentum, and the mass shell./
- mass shell :: The hyperboloid \(\eta(p,p) = -m^2c^2\) of allowed
  four-momenta; in a frame, \(E^2 = p^2c^2 + m^2c^4\). The mass is the
  Minkowski length of the momentum.
- relativistic Hamiltonian :: \(H = \sqrt{p^2c^2 + m^2c^4}\), the Legendre
  transform of \(-mc^2\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}\). Its velocity \(\partial H/\partial\vec p\)
  is below \(c\) for every \(\vec p\). /Slide: Hamilton: H = \sqrt{p^2c^2 + m^2c^4}./
- degenerate Lagrangian :: One whose velocity Hessian is singular, so the
  momenta obey constraints. The free particle with an arbitrary parameter is
  one: its canonical Hamiltonian vanishes and the mass shell is the constraint.
  /Slide: With any parameter, H \equiv 0 and the mass shell is a constraint./
- relativistic mass :: The discarded name \(\gamma m\) for \(E/c^2\). It fails as
  a mass because force and acceleration are related by \(\gamma m\) across the
  motion and \(\gamma^3m\) along it. This course's \(m\) is always the invariant.
  /Slide: The case against relativistic mass./
- massless particle :: A particle whose four-momentum is null, \(\eta(p,p) = 0\).
  It has \(E = |\vec p|c\), moves at \(c\), and has no rest frame.
  /Slide: Massless particles move at c./
- photon :: The quantum of light, a massless particle with \(E = hf\); the
  relation \(E = hf\) is cited from quantum physics.

* Systems, collisions, thresholds

- invariant mass :: Of a system: \(M = \frac1c\sqrt{-\eta(P,P)}\) with \(P\) the
  total four-momentum. At least the sum of the parts' masses, with equality only
  if all move together (for massive parts, at rest in one frame) — which is why a box of hot gas has more mass
  than its molecules. /Slide: Invariant mass is not additive./
- centre-of-momentum frame :: The inertial frame in which a system's total
  3-momentum vanishes; there \(Mc^2\) is the total energy.
- four-momentum conservation :: The total four-momentum of an isolated system is
  the same before and after a collision. Follows from 3-momentum conservation
  holding in every inertial frame. /Slide: Why four-momentum is conserved./
- Mandelstam invariants :: For \(a + b\to c + d\):
  \(s = -c^2\eta(p_a+p_b,p_a+p_b)\), \(t = -c^2\eta(p_a-p_c,p_a-p_c)\),
  \(u = -c^2\eta(p_a-p_d,p_a-p_d)\), with \(s + t + u = \sum m_i^2c^4\). \(\sqrt s\)
  is the energy in the centre-of-momentum frame. /Slide: Squaring, and the Mandelstam invariants./
- threshold :: The least initial \(s\) for which a reaction is kinematically
  possible: \((\sum m_f)^2c^4\), reached when the products are at rest together
  in the centre-of-momentum frame. /Slide: Thresholds./
- two-body decay :: \(M \to m_1 + m_2\). At rest, the products have a single,
  fixed momentum — a line, not a spectrum. /Slide: A decay: \pi^+\to\mu^+\nu_\mu./
- relativistic rocket :: A rocket with exhaust speed \(w\) in its own rest frame
  gains rapidity \(\theta = (w/c)\ln(M_i/M_f)\). Tsiolkovsky's equation with
  rapidity in place of speed. /Slide: The relativistic rocket: fuel buys rapidity./

* Acceleration, and why nothing is rigid

- hyperbolic motion :: Motion at constant proper acceleration \(\alpha\):
  rapidity grows as \(\alpha\tau/c\), and the worldline is the hyperbola
  \(x^2 - c^2t^2 = c^4/\alpha^2\). /Slide: Hyperbolic motion./
- Rindler horizon :: The null plane \(x = ct\) that a hyperbolic worldline
  approaches and never meets. Events on or beyond it can never send the
  accelerated observer a signal. /Slide: A year at one g, and a horizon behind you./
- Born rigidity :: The relativistic substitute for rigidity: the distance
  between neighbouring points of a body, measured in their instantaneous rest
  frame, stays constant. A Born-rigidly accelerated rod's back must accelerate
  harder than its front. /Slide: There are no rigid bodies./
- Ehrenfest paradox :: A disc cannot be spun up Born-rigidly: the rim would
  have to contract while the radius does not, which no circle in the plane
  allows. /Slide: The Ehrenfest disc, and unit 16's missing successor./
- Herglotz–Noether theorem :: A Born-rigid motion has three degrees of freedom,
  not six; in particular a body at rest cannot be set rotating Born-rigidly.
  Cited (Herglotz; F. Noether, 1910).
