The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: a \(14.4\) keV photon climbing the \(h = 22.5\) m tower of
Harvard's Jefferson Laboratory. The equivalence principle predicts a fractional
frequency loss \(gh/c^2 = 2.455\times10^{-15}\); Pound and Rebka measured
\((2.57\pm0.26)\times10^{-15}\) per one-way trip.

Conventions: signature \((-,+,+,+)\), \(x^0 = ct\), Greek indices \(0\) to
\(3\) and Latin \(1\) to \(3\), \(g = 9.80665\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\), and
\(\Phi\) the Newtonian potential with \(\vec g = -\nabla\Phi\), so \(\Phi\) is
negative near a mass.

* Two masses, and three principles

- inertial mass :: The constant \(m_{\mathrm i}\) in \(\vec F = m_{\mathrm
  i}\ddot{\vec x}\): a body's resistance to being accelerated by any force
  whatever. /Slide: Two masses Newton did not have to make equal./
- gravitational mass :: The charge \(m_{\mathrm g}\) in \(\vec F = -m_{\mathrm
  g}\nabla\Phi\), coupling a body to the gravitational field exactly as \(q\)
  couples to \(\vec E\). Newton's axioms do not relate it to \(m_{\mathrm i}\);
  free fall reads \(\ddot{\vec x} = -(m_{\mathrm g}/m_{\mathrm i})\nabla\Phi\).
- Eötvös parameter :: \(\eta_{AB} = 2(a_A - a_B)/(a_A + a_B)\), the fractional
  difference in \(m_{\mathrm g}/m_{\mathrm i}\) between two bodies in the same
  field. Dimensionless, antisymmetric, and independent of the source, so bounds
  from unlike experiments are comparable. Known to be below
  \(1.5\times10^{-15}\).
- torsion balance :: Two different materials on a horizontal beam hung from a
  fibre. A nonzero \(\eta\) gives a differential acceleration along the
  horizontal component of the source field, and so a torque; rotating the whole
  apparatus moves the signal to a known frequency. The instrument behind every
  bound from Eötvös (1922) to Eöt-Wash (2008). /Slide: How well it is known./
- weak equivalence principle (WEP) :: The worldline of a freely falling test
  body, given its initial position and velocity, is independent of its mass and
  composition. Equivalently \(\eta = 0\) for every pair. /Slide: Three
  principles, three different claims./
- Einstein equivalence principle (EEP) :: WEP, plus local Lorentz invariance
  and local position invariance. Strictly stronger than WEP: a scalar field
  multiplying the electromagnetic Lagrangian leaves free fall universal while
  making atomic energy levels depend on position.
- local Lorentz invariance :: In any local freely falling frame, the outcome of
  a non-gravitational experiment does not depend on the frame's velocity.
- local position invariance :: In any local freely falling frame, the outcome
  of a non-gravitational experiment does not depend on where or when it is
  performed. What a clock-comparison experiment tests.
- strong equivalence principle (SEP) :: EEP with "non-gravitational" deleted
  and "test body" weakened to admit bodies with appreciable gravitational
  self-energy. Every metric theory carrying an extra field violates it,
  Brans–Dicke included.
- Nordtvedt effect :: A violation of SEP would make the Earth and Moon, with
  different fractional self-energies, fall at different rates toward the Sun,
  polarising the lunar orbit along the Earth–Sun line. Lunar laser ranging
  gives \(\eta_{\mathrm N} = (4.4\pm4.5)\times10^{-4}\), consistent with zero.
  /Slide: An experiment for each./
- path structure :: The datum the WEP hands you: one preferred curve through
  each event in each direction, independent of the body travelling it. The
  mathematical object encoding such a family is an affine connection (unit 32),
  and its preferred curves are geodesics. /Slide: What the WEP says
  mathematically./

* Three consequences, before any geometry

- gravitational redshift :: A signal climbing from potential \(\Phi_1\) to
  \(\Phi_2\) is received with \(\Delta\nu/\nu = (\Phi_1 - \Phi_2)/c^2\); for a
  uniform field over height \(h\), \(-gh/c^2\). Derived from an accelerating
  box and a first-order Doppler shift, with no geometry. /Slide: The rocket
  argument./
- Mössbauer effect :: Recoil-free emission and absorption of a gamma ray by a
  nucleus bound in a crystal, the recoil being taken by the whole lattice. It
  makes the \(14.4\) keV line of \(^{57}\)Fe narrow enough
  (\(\Gamma/E = 3\times10^{-13}\)) that a shift of \(10^{-15}\) is measurable.
  /Slide: Worked example 1 — Pound and Rebka's tower./
- Pound–Rebka experiment :: The 1960 measurement of the gravitational redshift
  over \(22.5\) m at Harvard, reported as the source-up minus source-down
  difference \((5.13\pm0.51)\times10^{-15}\) against the predicted
  \(4.910\times10^{-15}\). Pound and Snider reached \(0.8\%\) in 1965.
- uniform-field metric :: \(g_{00} = -(1 + 2\Phi/c^2)\) to first order in
  \(\Phi/c^2\), read off the redshift by treating it as a permanent ratio of
  clock rates. The equivalence principle fixes only this component; \(g_{ij}\)
  is left undetermined, which is why the same argument gets light deflection
  wrong by a factor of two. /Slide: Clocks, and the first non-constant metric./
- light deflection :: A ray grazing a mass \(M\) at impact parameter \(b\) is
  bent. The equivalence principle alone gives \(2GM/c^2b\), which is
  \(0.876''\) at the Sun's limb; the true value is \(4GM/c^2b = 1.751''\), the
  extra half coming from the spatial metric. /Slide: Light bends — and by half
  the right amount./

* Rindler: a varying metric on a flat spacetime

- metric components :: \(g_{\alpha\beta}(y) = (\partial x^\mu/\partial
  y^\alpha)(\partial x^\nu/\partial y^\beta)\eta_{\mu\nu}\): the matrix of the
  bilinear form \(\eta\) in the coordinate basis, which varies from point to
  point. Not a new object — unit 16's change-of-basis theorem with a
  position-dependent change. /Slide: What a metric in arbitrary coordinates
  is./
- coordinate basis :: The frame \(e_{(\alpha)} = (\partial x^\mu/\partial
  y^\alpha)\hat e_\mu\), the columns of unit 03's Jacobian. Chosen so that a
  curve's velocity has components \(\mathrm dy^\alpha/\mathrm d\lambda\).
- Rindler coordinates :: \(ct = X\sinh(gT/c)\), \(x = X\cosh(gT/c)\) with \(X
  \gt 0\): unit 27's Born-rigid family of hyperbolae used as a chart, with
  \(X\) labelling the observer of proper acceleration \(c^2/X\) and \(T\) the
  proper time of the one at \(X_0 = c^2/g\). /Slide: The Rindler chart./
- Rindler metric :: \(\mathrm ds^2 = -(gX/c)^2\mathrm dT^2 + \mathrm dX^2 +
  \mathrm dy^2 + \mathrm dz^2\). Flat spacetime, written in a chart whose
  components depend on position — the unit's standing counterexample to
  "varying components means curvature".
- lapse function :: The factor \(N\) in \(\mathrm d\tau = N\,\mathrm dt\) for a
  static observer — the rate at which proper time runs per unit of coordinate
  time, and so dimensionless. It is \(\sqrt{-g_{00}}\) when the time coordinate
  is scaled as \(x^0 = ct\). For Rindler, written with \(T\) itself,
  \(\sqrt{-g_{TT}} = gX/c\) and \(N = gX/c^2 = X/X_0\); for the rotating frame
  \(N = \sqrt{1-\omega^2\rho^2/c^2}\).
- Rindler wedge :: The region \(x \gt |ct|\) of Minkowski space, which the
  Rindler chart covers bijectively and beyond which it does not reach. Three
  quarters of Minkowski space are outside it. /Slide: The wedge, and the
  horizon./
- Rindler horizon :: The null boundary \(x = |ct|\) of the wedge, where the
  lapse vanishes and the proper acceleration diverges. A causal boundary
  defined by the family of observers, not by the spacetime: every event on it
  is an ordinary event of flat Minkowski space.
- coordinate singularity :: A place where a chart degenerates —
  \(\det g \to 0\) or a component blows up — while the spacetime is perfectly
  regular. \(X = 0\) in Rindler, \(r = 0\) in plane polars, \(r = 2GM/c^2\) in
  the Schwarzschild chart of unit 36. Distinguished from a real singularity by
  invariants, not by components.
- static metric :: One whose components do not depend on the time coordinate
  (and which has no time–space cross terms). Two static observers then see a
  frequency ratio \(\nu_2/\nu_1 = \sqrt{-g_{00}(X_1)}/\sqrt{-g_{00}(X_2)}\),
  exactly, because the null curves joining them are time-translates of one
  another. /Slide: The redshift again, exactly./

* The rotating disc

- rotating metric :: Minkowski space in coordinates turning at \(\omega\):
  \(\mathrm ds^2 = -(1-\omega^2\rho^2/c^2)c^2\mathrm dt^2 +
  2\omega\rho^2\mathrm d\phi'\mathrm dt + \mathrm d\rho^2 + \rho^2\mathrm
  d\phi'^2 + \mathrm dz^2\). Non-constant lapse and a time–space cross term, on
  a flat spacetime. /Slide: Rotating coordinates, and their metric./
- light cylinder :: \(\rho = c/\omega\), where \(g_{tt}\) vanishes and beyond
  which no observer can co-rotate. \(66.6\) km for a \(716\) Hz pulsar, against
  a stellar radius of about \(12\) km.
- radar distance :: The distance a static observer assigns to a neighbour by
  bouncing light off them and halving \(c\) times the round-trip proper time.
  The only honest definition of spatial distance in a spacetime, since a metric
  on four-vectors does not by itself give one. /Slide: What "the distance
  between two rotating observers" means./
- spatial metric :: \(\gamma_{ij} = g_{ij} - g_{0i}g_{0j}/g_{00}\), the
  positive-definite form the radar construction produces. Equivalently the
  restriction of \(\eta\) to the orthogonal complement of the observer's
  four-velocity. Equals \(g_{ij}\) exactly when \(g_{0i} = 0\).
- Ehrenfest paradox :: A rigidly rotating disc whose riders measure
  circumference \(2\pi\gamma\rho\) and radius \(\rho\), so that \(C/2R = \pi\gamma
  \gt \pi\). Unit 27 met it as an obstruction to Born rigidity; here it is the
  statement that a family of observers in flat spacetime already has a
  non-Euclidean spatial geometry. /Slide: Circumference over diameter is
  \(\pi\gamma\)./
- Sagnac effect :: The failure of the rotating observers' simultaneity to close
  up around a loop: a time offset \(4\pi\omega\rho^2/(c^2-\omega^2\rho^2)\)
  between counter-propagating signals. Why the disc's "space" is a quotient
  rather than a slice — and why ring-laser gyroscopes work.

* What no coordinate change can remove

- freely falling frame :: Coordinates in which, at one chosen event,
  \(g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu}\) and every \(\partial_\alpha g_{\mu\nu}\)
  vanishes. Possible at a point, as a parameter count shows and unit 32 proves
  by constructing normal coordinates; impossible over a region wherever the
  tidal tensor is nonzero. /Slide: How much of a metric a freely falling frame
  can flatten./
- the counting argument :: At an event, \(g\) has \(10\) components, \(\partial
  g\) has \(40\) and \(\partial\partial g\) has \(100\), against \(16\), \(40\)
  and \(80\) free Taylor coefficients in a coordinate change. Hence \(6\)
  residual symmetries (the Lorentz group), all first derivatives removable, and
  \(100-80 = 20\) second-derivative combinations that survive — the number of
  components of unit 33's Riemann tensor, \(n^2(n^2-1)/12\) at \(n = 4\).
- tidal tensor :: Unit 05's \(T_{ij} = \partial_i\partial_j\Phi\), the symmetric
  bilinear form whose deviation equation \(\ddot\xi_i = -T_{ij}\xi_j\) a falling
  observer still measures. Invariant under every change to a freely falling
  frame, because such a change adds only a term linear in position. Trace-free
  in vacuum, so every stretch is paid for by a squeeze. /Slide: The Newtonian
  version, proved in three lines./
- global inertial frame :: A single chart covering a region in which every
  free-fall worldline is straight. It would exist if gravity were a force field
  on Minkowski space; it cannot exist where \(T_{ij} \ne 0\), since its
  existence forces \(\nabla\Phi\) to be a function of time alone. /Slide: There
  is no global inertial frame./
- gravitational self-energy :: \(U = -\frac1{8\pi G}\int|\nabla\Phi|^2\mathrm
  dV\), obtained from \(\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G\rho\) and the divergence theorem
  of unit 05. Negative, so field energy reduces the source; the fractional
  \(3GM/5Rc^2\) is \(4.17\times10^{-10}\) for the Earth and
  \(1.27\times10^{-6}\) for the Sun. /Slide: Why not just put a field on flat
  spacetime anyway?/
- Deser's argument :: That a spin-2 field on Minkowski space, once made to
  couple to its own stress-energy as \(E = mc^2\) and the strong equivalence
  principle demand, has for
  its self-consistent completion the Einstein–Hilbert action of \(g_{\mu\nu} =
  \eta_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}\) — in which nothing measures \(\eta\). Cited:
  S. Deser, /Gen. Rel. Grav./ *1*, 9 (1970).
