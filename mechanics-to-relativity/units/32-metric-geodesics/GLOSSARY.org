The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: the round sphere of radius \(R\) in the chart \((\theta,\varphi)\),
\(g = R^2(\mathrm{d}\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,\mathrm{d}\varphi^2)\). Volume element
\(R^2\sin\theta\), total area \(4\pi R^2\). Two nonzero Christoffel symbols,
\(\Gamma^\theta{}_{\varphi\varphi} = -\sin\theta\cos\theta\) and
\(\Gamma^\varphi{}_{\theta\varphi} = \cot\theta\). Test latitude
\(\theta_0 = \pi/3\): a vector parallel-transported once round it comes back
rotated by \(2\pi\cos\theta_0 = \pi\), and the cap it bounds has area
\(\pi R^2\). Geodesics are great circles; in normal coordinates at the pole
\(g_{ab} = \delta_{ab} - (\delta_{ab}|\mathbf y|^2 - y_ay_b)/(3R^2) + O(|\mathbf y|^4)\).

* What a metric measures

- pseudo-Riemannian metric :: A \((0,2)\)-tensor field \(g\) that is symmetric
  and nondegenerate at every point, with constant signature. Riemannian if
  positive definite, Lorentzian if of signature \((-,+,\dots,+)\).
  /Slide: The definition, with the positivity dropped./
- signature :: The numbers of negative and positive eigenvalues of \(g_{ij}\) at
  a point, well defined by Sylvester's law of inertia and constant on a
  connected manifold. It is the only part of a metric's algebra that is physics.
- musical isomorphism :: The map \(\flat: T_pM\to T_p^*M\),
  \(X\mapsto g(X,\cdot)\), and its inverse \(\sharp\). An isomorphism exactly
  because \(g\) is nondegenerate; in components it lowers and raises an index.
  /Slide: Raising and lowering is an isomorphism, not a notation./
- inverse metric :: \(g^{ij}\), the matrix inverse of \(g_{ij}\). Its entries are
  the components of a \((2,0)\)-tensor — a theorem, proved from
  \(\tilde g = S^{\mathsf T}gS\).
- arc length :: \(L[\gamma] = \int\sqrt{g_{ij}\dot x^i\dot x^j}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\)
  for a curve with \(g(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)\gt0\). Unchanged by
  reparametrisation, which is the root of the affine-parameter subtlety.
  /Slide: Length, proper time, and the volume element./
- proper time :: For a timelike curve in Lorentzian signature,
  \(\tau = c^{-1}\int\sqrt{-g_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\): the
  time an ideal clock carried along the curve records.
- volume element :: \(\sqrt{|\det g_{ij}|}\,\mathrm{d}^nx\), the same on every
  chart overlap because the Jacobians cancel. Needs no orientation, unlike the
  volume form of unit 34.

* A derivative that is a tensor

- affine connection :: A map \((X,Y)\mapsto\nabla_XY\) on vector fields,
  \(C^\infty\)-linear in \(X\), \(\mathbb R\)-linear in \(Y\), and Leibniz:
  \(\nabla_X(fY) = (Xf)Y + f\nabla_XY\). Tensorial in the direction slot, not in
  the differentiated one. /Slide: What a connection is./
- Christoffel symbols :: The components \(\Gamma^k{}_{ij}\) defined by
  \(\nabla_{\partial_i}\partial_j = \Gamma^k{}_{ij}\partial_k\) in a chart. Not a
  tensor: their transformation law has an inhomogeneous term built from second
  derivatives of the chart change.
- covariant derivative :: \(\nabla_iV^j = \partial_iV^j + \Gamma^j{}_{ik}V^k\), and
  on a general tensor one \(+\Gamma\) per upper index and one \(-\Gamma\) per lower
  index. The inhomogeneous part of \(\Gamma\) cancels that of \(\partial\).
- torsion :: \(T(X,Y) = \nabla_XY - \nabla_YX - [X,Y]\), a \((1,2)\)-tensor with
  components \(\Gamma^k{}_{ij} - \Gamma^k{}_{ji}\). Zero exactly when the
  Christoffel symbols are symmetric in their lower pair.
  /Slide: Torsion, and metric compatibility./
- metric compatible :: Of a connection: \(\nabla g = 0\), equivalently
  \(Xg(Y,Z) = g(\nabla_XY,Z) + g(Y,\nabla_XZ)\). Makes parallel transport an
  isometry and lets \(\nabla\) commute with raising and lowering.
- Levi-Civita connection :: The unique torsion-free metric-compatible connection
  of a pseudo-Riemannian manifold. Its components are
  \(\Gamma^k{}_{ij} = \tfrac12g^{kl}(\partial_ig_{jl}+\partial_jg_{il}-\partial_lg_{ij})\).
  /Slide: The fundamental theorem, in six lines./
- Koszul formula :: \(2g(\nabla_XY,Z) = Xg(Y,Z) + Yg(Z,X) - Zg(X,Y) +
  g([X,Y],Z) - g([X,Z],Y) - g([Y,Z],X)\). Derived from the two axioms, so it
  proves uniqueness; read as a definition, it proves existence.

* Parallel transport, and what a gyroscope does

- covariant derivative along a curve :: \(DV^k/\mathrm{d}\lambda =
  \mathrm{d}V^k/\mathrm{d}\lambda + \Gamma^k{}_{ij}\dot x^iV^j\) for a field \(V\)
  defined only along \(\gamma\). /Slide: Differentiating along a curve./
- parallel :: Of a field along a curve: \(DV/\mathrm{d}\lambda = 0\). A linear
  first-order ODE, so it has a unique solution for each initial vector.
- parallel transport :: The linear isomorphism
  \(T_{\gamma(\lambda_0)}M\to T_{\gamma(\lambda_1)}M\) sending an initial vector to
  its parallel continuation. An isometry for a metric-compatible connection.
- Fermi–Walker transport :: The transport law of a torque-free spin on an
  accelerated worldline, \(DS^\mu/\mathrm{d}\tau = c^{-2}(u^\mu a_\nu - a^\mu
  u_\nu)S^\nu\). Reduces to parallel transport on a geodesic.
  /Slide: What a gyroscope does, and why the path matters./
- holonomy :: The transport map \(P_\gamma\in GL(T_pM)\) round a loop based at
  \(p\), in general not the identity. Round the latitude \(\theta_0 = \pi/3\) of
  the sphere it is a rotation by \(\pi\).

* Geodesics, two ways

- geodesic :: A curve whose tangent is parallel along itself,
  \(\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma = 0\). On the sphere, the great circles.
  /Slide: Straightest: the curve that transports its own tangent./
- geodesic equation :: \(\ddot x^k + \Gamma^k{}_{ij}\dot x^i\dot x^j = 0\). A
  second-order ODE, so one geodesic through each point in each direction.
- auto-parallel :: Another name for a geodesic in the sense of
  \(\nabla_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma = 0\), stressing that it is defined by the
  connection alone, with no mention of length.
- affine parameter :: A parameter in which the geodesic equation holds. Unique
  up to \(s = a\lambda + b\); in any other parameter the equation acquires a term
  proportional to the tangent.
- energy functional :: \(E[\gamma] = \tfrac12\int g_{ij}\dot x^i\dot x^j\,
  \mathrm{d}\lambda\). Its Euler–Lagrange equation is the geodesic equation, and
  unlike length it picks out the affine parametrisation.
  /Slide: Shortest: the same curves, from unit 11./
- Clairaut's relation :: On a surface of revolution, \(r\sin\psi\) is constant
  along a geodesic. On the sphere it is the Beltrami constant of the length
  functional, or the momentum \(R^2\sin^2\theta\,\dot\varphi\) conjugate to the
  absent coordinate \(\varphi\).
  /Slide: Worked example 4 — great circles, twice, and one curve that is not./

* Normal coordinates, and the equivalence principle stated exactly

- exponential map :: \(\exp_p(v) = \gamma_v(1)\), where \(\gamma_v\) is the
  geodesic from \(p\) with initial velocity \(v\). Its differential at \(0\) is
  the identity, so it is a diffeomorphism near \(0\). /Slide: The exponential map./
- normal coordinates :: The chart \(\exp_p(y^ie_i)\mapsto(y^i)\) for an
  orthonormal basis \(e_i\) of \(T_pM\). Geodesics through \(p\) are straight
  lines through the origin.
- local flatness theorem :: At any point there are coordinates with
  \(g = \eta\), \(\partial g = 0\) and \(\Gamma = 0\) there; the second derivatives
  of \(g\) cannot in general be removed. The equivalence principle, stated as a
  fact about connections. /Slide: The local flatness theorem./

* Free fall is a straight line

- geodesic postulate :: A test body under no non-gravitational force follows a
  timelike geodesic, affinely parametrised by proper time; light follows a null
  geodesic. Replaces \(m\ddot{\vec x} = \vec F\), and turns out to follow from
  \(\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0\).
  /Slide: The geodesic postulate, and why it is the only candidate./
- minimal coupling :: Carry a special-relativistic law to curved spacetime by
  \(\eta\to g\), \(\partial\to\nabla\), \(\mathrm{d}^4x\to\sqrt{-g}\,\mathrm{d}^4x\).
  A choice, not a theorem: ambiguous in the order of derivatives and blind to
  curvature terms. /Slide: Minimal coupling, and the ambiguity nobody should hide./
- comma-goes-to-semicolon rule :: The same prescription in the notation
  \(T_{,\mu}\) for \(\partial_\mu T\) and \(T_{;\mu}\) for \(\nabla_\mu T\).
