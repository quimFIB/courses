The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Conventions throughout: signature \((-,+,+,+)\), \(x^0 = ct\),
\(R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} -
\partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma}
- \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma}\), \(R_{\mu\nu} =
R^\rho{}_{\mu\rho\nu}\), \(R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}\). With these a sphere has
positive curvature.

Running example: the round two-sphere \(S^2_a\) of radius \(a\), with
\(g = a^2(\mathrm{d}\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,\mathrm{d}\phi^2)\), its octant
triangle, and two of its meridians.

* The thing no chart removes

- Riemann curvature tensor :: The \((1,3)\)-tensor field measuring the failure
  of covariant derivatives to commute: \([\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]V^\rho =
  R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^\sigma\). Defined invariantly as the curvature
  operator; the component formula in \(\Gamma\) is a computation, not the
  definition. /Slide: What this unit is for./
- normal coordinates :: Unit 32's chart at \(p\) with \(g_{\mu\nu}(p) =
  \eta_{\mu\nu}\) and \(\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu}(p) = 0\), hence
  \(\Gamma(p) = 0\). The second derivatives of \(g\) cannot also be removed:
  \(100 - 80 = 20\) of them survive in four dimensions, and those twenty are
  the curvature. The standard device for proving tensor identities.
  /Slide: Where to look for it./
- curvature operator :: \(R(X,Y)Z = \nabla_X\nabla_YZ - \nabla_Y\nabla_XZ -
  \nabla_{[X,Y]}Z\). Manifestly coordinate-free; the bracket term is what makes
  it frame-independent, as a non-coordinate frame on the flat plane shows.
  /Slide: The curvature operator./
- Lie bracket :: Unit 31's \([X,Y]\), the failure of two flows to commute. It
  appears in the curvature operator solely to subtract the part of the
  non-commutation that is not curvature.
- tensoriality :: That \(R(X,Y)Z\) is \(C^\infty(M)\)-linear in each slot, so
  its value at \(p\) depends only on \(X_p, Y_p, Z_p\). Proved by watching the
  Leibniz terms cancel in the antisymmetrised pair. \(\nabla_XZ\) alone has no
  such property. /Slide: It is a tensor, and that is the surprise./
- Ricci identity :: \([\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]V^\rho = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^\sigma\)
  and \([\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]\omega_\rho = -R^\sigma{}_{\rho\mu\nu}\omega_\sigma\);
  one \(+R\) term per upper index, one \(-R\) per lower. On a scalar it gives
  zero, because the connection is torsion-free.
  /Slide: Components, and the Ricci identity./
- holonomy :: The linear map a vector undergoes on being parallel-transported
  once round a closed loop. For an infinitesimal coordinate parallelogram,
  \(\Delta V^\rho = -R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^\sigma\,\delta a^\mu\delta b^\nu\):
  curvature is holonomy per unit area. Metric compatibility makes it an
  isometry of the tangent space.
  /Slide: Curvature is the holonomy of a small loop./
- flatness theorem :: \(R = 0\) on a simply connected open set iff parallel
  transport there is path-independent, iff some chart has constant
  \(g_{\mu\nu}\). /Cited/: Lee, /Introduction to Riemannian Manifolds/ (2nd
  ed.), Thm 7.10. Simple connectivity is essential — the cone is the counterexample.
- angle excess :: For a geodesic triangle, the sum of its angles minus
  \(\pi\). Equal to \(\int K\,\mathrm{d}A\), and so equal to the holonomy angle.
  The octant triangle has excess \(\pi/2\); Gauss's survey triangle of area
  \(2929\ \mathrm{km^2}\) has \(14.9''\).
  /Slide: Worked example 2 — round the octant, by hand./
- Gauss–Bonnet theorem :: \(\int_M K\,\mathrm{d}A = 2\pi\chi(M)\) for a closed
  oriented surface, with a boundary version adding the geodesic curvature.
  /Cited/: do Carmo, /Curves and Surfaces/, §4-5. The holonomy theorem proved
  in the deck is its local half.
- Ambrose–Singer theorem :: The Lie algebra of the holonomy group is spanned by
  the curvature operators, transported to a base point. /Cited/: the
  \(n\)-dimensional statement of "holonomy is curvature".

* How many numbers is that?

- first Bianchi identity :: \(R_{\rho[\sigma\mu\nu]} = 0\), equivalently
  \(R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}+R_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma}+R_{\rho\nu\sigma\mu}=0\). Proved
  in three lines in normal coordinates; it needs torsion-freeness and nothing
  else. /Slide: Four symmetries./
- pair symmetry :: \(R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\). A
  /consequence/ of the other three symmetries, not an axiom — four copies of
  the first Bianchi identity, two added and two subtracted.
  /Slide: Pair symmetry is a consequence, not an axiom./
- algebraic curvature tensor :: A \((0,4)\)-tensor obeying S1–S4. Equivalently,
  a symmetric bilinear form on the space of index pairs whose totally
  antisymmetric part vanishes. /Slide: How many independent components./
- counting theorem :: An algebraic curvature tensor in \(n\) dimensions has
  \(n^2(n^2-1)/12\) independent components: \(\binom n2\)-by-\(\binom n2\)
  symmetric, minus \(\binom n4\) for the first Bianchi identity, which given
  the other symmetries says exactly that the totally antisymmetric part
  vanishes. One at
  \(n=2\), six at \(n=3\), twenty at \(n=4\), fifty at \(n=5\).
- second Bianchi identity :: \(\nabla_{[\lambda}R^\rho{}_{|\sigma|\mu\nu]} = 0\),
  equivalently \(\nabla_{[\lambda}R_{\rho\sigma]\mu\nu} = 0\): the derivative
  index cycles with an antisymmetric pair. The only differential constraint
  curvature obeys, true for every metric. Proved in normal coordinates from
  the symmetry of second partial derivatives.
  Contracted twice it gives \(\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0\).
  /Slide: The second Bianchi identity./

* Curvature belongs to the space

- sectional curvature :: For a plane \(\Pi\) spanned by \(X, Y\) in \(T_pM\),
  \(K(\Pi) = R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}X^\rho Y^\sigma X^\mu Y^\nu\) divided by the
  Gram determinant \(|X|^2|Y|^2 - \langle X,Y\rangle^2\). Independent of the
  basis chosen for \(\Pi\); the collection over all planes determines \(R\).
  Curvature is indexed by planes, never by directions.
  /Slide: Sectional curvature: one number per plane./
- constant curvature :: \(K(\Pi)\) the same for every plane at every point, so
  \(R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = K(g_{\rho\mu}g_{\sigma\nu}-g_{\rho\nu}g_{\sigma\mu})\)
  — the only tensor with S1–S4 built from \(g\) alone. Sphere, hyperbolic
  space, flat space; needed again for unit 39's cosmology.
- Gaussian curvature :: On a surface, the single sectional curvature
  \(K = R_{1212}/\det g\). The whole curvature of a 2-manifold, one function.
  \(1/a^2\) on \(S^2_a\), \(-1/a^2\) on the hyperbolic plane of radius \(a\),
  \(0\) on the cylinder and the cone.
  /Slide: In two dimensions there is only one plane./
- conformal factor :: The \(e^{2\varphi}\) in \(g = e^{2\varphi}(\mathrm{d}x^2 +
  \mathrm{d}y^2)\). Then \(K = -e^{-2\varphi}\Delta\varphi\) with \(\Delta\) the
  ordinary flat Laplacian — the fastest route to \(K\), with no Christoffel
  symbols at all.
- isothermal coordinates :: Coordinates in which a surface metric is
  conformally flat. Every smooth surface metric has them locally. /Cited/:
  Chern, /Proc. AMS/ 1955. A two-dimensional fact with
  no higher-dimensional analogue.
- intrinsic :: Computable from the metric alone, with no reference to any
  surrounding space. Gaussian curvature is intrinsic — that is Gauss's
  /Theorema Egregium/ — which is what licenses a course on spacetime, where
  there is no surrounding space at all. /Slide: Theorema Egregium./
- Theorema Egregium :: Gauss 1827: for a surface in \(\mathbb R^3\), the
  product of the principal curvatures equals the Gaussian curvature of the
  induced metric. /Cited/ (do Carmo §4-3); the corollary that an isometry
  preserves \(K\) is proved in the deck in one line. Consequence: no flat map
  of any region of the Earth.
- deficit angle :: For the cone \(\mathrm{d}r^2 + r^2\sin^2\alpha\,\mathrm{d}\phi^2\),
  the angle \(2\pi(1-\sin\alpha)\) missing from a circuit of the apex —
  \(\pi\) exactly for \(\alpha = 30^\circ\). Curvature zero everywhere off the
  apex, holonomy nonzero: all of it is concentrated in a delta function, and
  this is the point mass of three-dimensional gravity.

* Geodesic deviation

- deviation vector :: For a one-parameter family of geodesics
  \(\gamma(\tau,s)\), the field \(\xi = \partial\gamma/\partial s\) pointing to
  the neighbouring geodesic. Since \(\tau\) and \(s\) are coordinates,
  \([u,\xi] = 0\), and torsion-freeness gives \(\nabla_u\xi = \nabla_\xi u\).
  /Slide: A family of geodesics, and the field between them./
- geodesic deviation equation :: \(\mathrm D^2\xi^\rho/\mathrm{d}\tau^2 =
  -R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}u^\sigma\xi^\mu u^\nu\). Four one-line steps from the
  definition of \(R\). No force, no \(\Gamma\), no first derivative of the
  metric appears: the relative acceleration of two freely falling particles is
  curvature and nothing else. /Slide: The geodesic deviation equation./
- Jacobi field :: A solution of the geodesic deviation equation along a fixed
  geodesic. The solution space has dimension \(2n\) by unit 01's uniqueness
  theorem. Two points joined by a nontrivial Jacobi field vanishing at both are
  /conjugate/ — on \(S^2_a\), every pair of antipodes, which is why a great
  circle stops minimising once it passes the antipode of its starting point.
- weak-field metric :: \(g_{00} = -(1+2\Phi/c^2)\), \(g_{ij} = \delta_{ij}\),
  static and slow, from unit 32. The only surviving Christoffel symbol is
  \(\Gamma^i_{00} = \partial_i\Phi/c^2\), and the factor of two in \(g_{00}\) is
  fixed by demanding Newton's law, not chosen.
  /Slide: The Newtonian limit, set up./
- tidal tensor :: Unit 05's \(T_{ij} = \partial_i\partial_j\Phi\), the Hessian
  of the Newtonian potential; trace \(4\pi G\rho\), eigenvalues
  \((-2,1,1)GM/r^3\) for a point mass. This unit's result:
  \(R^i{}_{0j0} = \partial_i\partial_j\Phi/c^2\), so the tidal tensor /is/ six
  components of the Riemann tensor.
  /Slide: Worked example 5 — Riemann is the tidal tensor./
- electric part of the curvature :: \(E_{ij} = R_{i0j0}\) relative to an
  observer with four-velocity \(u\): symmetric, six components, and the only
  part with a Newtonian counterpart. The magnetic part \(R_{i0jk}\) has none,
  and is what makes frame dragging (unit 40) possible.
  /Slide: What the identification settles./

* The traces, and the one that is conserved

- Ricci tensor :: \(R_{\sigma\nu} = R^\rho{}_{\sigma\rho\nu}\), the only trace
  of Riemann up to sign, and symmetric by pair symmetry. A small ball of dust
  released at rest has \(\ddot V/V = -R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu\): the trace of the
  deviation equation, and in the Newtonian limit unit 05's
  \(\operatorname{tr} T = 4\pi G\rho\).
  /Slide: Ricci and the scalar curvature./
- scalar curvature :: \(R = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}\). Measures the deficit in the
  volume of a small geodesic ball: \(\mathrm{vol} = \omega_n
  r^n(1 - Rr^2/(6(n+2)) + O(r^4))\) — /cited/: Gray, /Tubes/, ch. 9.
  \(2/a^2\) on \(S^2_a\), and \(R = 2K\) on any surface.
- Weyl tensor :: The completely trace-free part of Riemann, \(n \ge 3\); the
  unique such combination with S1–S4. Component count
  \(n(n+1)(n+2)(n-3)/12\): zero at \(n=3\), ten at \(n=4\). Conformally
  invariant (/cited/: Wald, /General Relativity/, App. D). In vacuum \(C = R\), so the tides of empty
  space — the Moon's on the ocean, unit 38's waves — are Weyl curvature.
  /Slide: The Weyl tensor: what the traces leave behind./
- conformal invariance :: \(C^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\) is unchanged by
  \(g \to e^{2\omega}g\); and for \(n\ge4\), \(C = 0\) on an open set iff the
  metric is conformally flat there (Weyl–Schouten, /cited/).
- Einstein tensor :: \(G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12 Rg_{\mu\nu}\), the
  trace-reversed Ricci tensor. \(\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0\) /identically/, for
  every metric, as a consequence of the contracted second Bianchi identity —
  which is why unit 35's field equation can be consistent with
  \(\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0\) and why \(R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}\)
  cannot.
  /Slide: The Einstein tensor is divergence-free, and nobody chose that./
- contracted Bianchi identity :: \(2\nabla^\mu R_{\rho\mu} - \nabla_\rho R = 0\),
  got by contracting the second Bianchi identity twice. An identity, not a
  conservation law: it holds off shell, like \(\nabla\cdot\nabla\times = 0\) of
  unit 04.
- trace reversal :: \(\bar S_{\mu\nu} = S_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12 (g^{\alpha\beta}
  S_{\alpha\beta})\,g_{\mu\nu}\); in four dimensions it negates the trace and is
  its own inverse. \(G\) is the trace-reversed Ricci tensor, and unit 38's
  \(\bar h_{\mu\nu}\) is the trace-reversed metric perturbation.
- Kretschmann scalar :: \(R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}R^{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}\), a
  curvature invariant that does not vanish when Ricci does. Unit 36 uses it to
  decide which of Schwarzschild's two singularities is real.
- Ricci-flat :: \(R_{\mu\nu} = 0\). At \(n = 3\) this forces \(R = 0\) entirely,
  so vacuum is flat and there are no gravitational waves; at \(n = 4\) it
  leaves the ten Weyl components free, which is the whole of vacuum general
  relativity. /Slide: Three dimensions: Ricci is everything./
