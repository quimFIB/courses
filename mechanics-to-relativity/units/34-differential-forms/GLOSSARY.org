The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Conventions throughout: signature \((-,+,+,+)\), \(x^0 = ct\), SI units with
\(\mu_0\varepsilon_0c^2 = 1\); the wedge in the determinant convention, so
\((e^1\wedge\cdots\wedge e^n)(v_1,\dots,v_n) = \det[v^i_j]\) with no factorial;
\(\mathrm{vol} = c\,\mathrm{d}t\wedge\mathrm{d}x\wedge\mathrm{d}y\wedge\mathrm{d}z\)
on Minkowski space; \(\nabla e_b = \omega^a{}_b\otimes e_a\) for a frame.

Running example: one field and one hole. Maxwell's four equations as
\(\mathrm{d}F = 0\) and \(\mathrm{d}\star F = \mu_0\star J\), expanded back into
components; and unit 04's vortex \(\theta = (x\,\mathrm{d}y - y\,\mathrm{d}x)/(x^2+y^2)\)
on the punctured plane, closed, not exact, with period \(2\pi\), generating
\(H^1(\mathbb{R}^2\setminus\{0\}) \cong \mathbb{R}\).

* The exterior algebra

- k-form :: Short for differential \(k\)-form: a field of alternating
  \(k\)-linear maps on tangent vectors, the thing that is integrated over a
  \(k\)-dimensional patch. /Slide: Forms on a manifold./
- wedge product :: The product \(\alpha\wedge\beta\) of a \(k\)-covector and an
  \(l\)-covector, defined by a signed sum over \((k,l)\)-shuffles. Bilinear,
  associative, graded commutative: \(\alpha\wedge\beta = (-1)^{kl}\beta\wedge\alpha\),
  so one-forms anticommute. /Slide: The wedge product./
- de Rham cohomology :: \(H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\), the closed \(k\)-forms
  modulo the exact ones. It depends on the space alone, and measures how far
  "closed" falls short of "exact". /Slide: de Rham cohomology./
- Hodge star :: The linear map \(\star:\Lambda^k\to\Lambda^{n-k}\) fixed by a
  metric and an orientation through \(\alpha\wedge\star\beta =
  \langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\,\mathrm{vol}_g\). The only place a metric
  enters Maxwell's equations. /Slide: The Hodge star./
- structure equations :: Cartan's two equations: \(\mathrm{d}e^a =
  -\omega^a{}_b\wedge e^b\) fixes the connection one-forms from a coframe, and
  \(\Omega^a{}_b = \mathrm{d}\omega^a{}_b + \omega^a{}_c\wedge\omega^c{}_b\) gives
  the curvature. /Slide: Coframes and the first structure equation./
- k-covector :: A map \(V^k\to\mathbb{R}\), linear in each argument and changing
  sign under any transposition of two arguments: an antisymmetric
  \((0,k)\)-tensor in unit 16's sense. /Slide: \(\Lambda^k V^*\), defined./
- k-th exterior power :: \(\Lambda^kV^*\), the vector space of \(k\)-covectors on
  \(V\). Dimension \(\binom nk\), zero for \(k > n\); on spacetime
  \(1,4,6,4,1\). /Slide: \(\Lambda^k V^*\), defined./
- differential k-form :: A smooth section of \(\Lambda^kT^*M\); in a chart
  \(\sum_{i_1<\cdots<i_k}\omega_{i_1\cdots i_k}\,\mathrm{d}x^{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{i_k}\).
  The space of them is \(\Omega^k(M)\). /Slide: Forms on a manifold./
- field two-form :: \(F = B_x\,\mathrm{d}y\wedge\mathrm{d}z + B_y\,\mathrm{d}z\wedge\mathrm{d}x
  + B_z\,\mathrm{d}x\wedge\mathrm{d}y + (E_i\,\mathrm{d}x^i)\wedge\mathrm{d}t = \mathrm{d}A\),
  with \(A = A_i\,\mathrm{d}x^i - \varphi\,\mathrm{d}t\). One object for \(\vec E\)
  and \(\vec B\); both halves are in webers. /Slide: Worked example 1 — the field as a two-form./

* One derivative

- exterior derivative :: The unique \(\mathbb{R}\)-linear
  \(\mathrm{d}:\Omega^k\to\Omega^{k+1}\) that is the differential on functions,
  obeys the graded Leibniz rule and squares to zero. \(\mathrm{d}^2 = 0\) is
  Clairaut's theorem. Grad, curl and divergence are \(\mathrm{d}\) in degrees
  0, 1, 2. /Slide: The exterior derivative, axiomatically./
- pullback :: \((\phi^*\omega)_p(v_1,\dots) = \omega_{\phi(p)}(\mathrm{d}\phi_p v_1,\dots)\)
  for a smooth \(\phi:N\to M\). Defined along every smooth map, not only
  diffeomorphisms, and it commutes with \(\wedge\) and with \(\mathrm{d}\).
  /Slide: Pullback, and why forms are worth the trouble./
- gauge transformation :: \(A\mapsto A + \mathrm{d}\lambda\): adding an exact
  one-form to the potential, which leaves \(F = \mathrm{d}A\) unchanged because
  \(\mathrm{d}^2 = 0\). In components, unit 24's \(\vec A\mapsto\vec A + \nabla\lambda\),
  \(\varphi\mapsto\varphi - \partial_t\lambda\). /Slide: Gauge freedom is the freedom to add an exact form./
- closed :: A form with \(\mathrm{d}\omega = 0\). Every exact form is closed;
  the converse can fail. /Slide: Closed does not mean exact./

* One theorem

- orientation :: A class of nowhere-vanishing top forms, two being equivalent
  when they differ by a positive function. A connected orientable manifold has
  exactly two. /Slide: Orientation and the volume form./
- orientable :: Admitting an orientation. The Möbius band is not.
  /Slide: Orientation and the volume form./
- volume form :: Given a metric and an orientation, the top form equal to
  \(+1\) on positive orthonormal frames; in a chart
  \(\sqrt{|\det g|}\,\mathrm{d}x^1\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}x^n\).
  /Slide: Orientation and the volume form./
- induced orientation :: "Outward normal first": a frame of \(\partial M\) is
  positive when the outward normal followed by it is positive in \(M\). The
  one sign convention behind every classical integral theorem.
  /Slide: The generalised Stokes theorem./
- Stokes' theorem :: \(\int_M\mathrm{d}\omega = \int_{\partial M}\omega\) for a
  compactly supported \((n-1)\)-form on an oriented manifold with boundary.
  Proved on the cube; the gradient, Green, curl and divergence theorems are its
  low-dimensional cases. /Slide: The generalised Stokes theorem./

* Closed, exact, and the difference

- exact :: A form that is \(\mathrm{d}\) of something: \(\omega = \mathrm{d}\alpha\).
  /Slide: Closed does not mean exact./
- Poincaré lemma :: On a star-shaped open set of \(\mathbb{R}^n\), every closed
  \(k\)-form with \(k\ge1\) is exact. Proved for \(k = 1\) by integrating along
  rays. /Slide: Closed does not mean exact./
- homotopy :: Here a smooth map \(H:[0,1]^2\to U\) deforming one loop into
  another through loops. Integrals of a closed one-form do not change along it;
  hence closed implies exact on a simply connected set.
  /Slide: Homotopy invariance: unit 04's promise kept./
- Betti number :: \(b_k = \dim H^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\). \(b_0\) counts connected
  components; \(b_1 = 1\) for the punctured plane. /Slide: de Rham cohomology./
- periods :: The integrals \(\int_Z\omega\) of a closed form over closed
  oriented submanifolds \(Z\). They depend only on the class \([\omega]\); the
  vortex's period round the origin is \(2\pi\).
  /Slide: The pairing: a class against a cycle./

* The metric enters, once

- current one-form :: \(J = -c^2\rho\,\mathrm{d}t + J_i\,\mathrm{d}x^i\), unit 24's
  charge and current densities with the index lowered by \(\eta\). Its dual
  \(\star J\) is closed exactly when charge is conserved. /Slide: \(\star F\), computed./

* Cartan's moving frames

- coframe :: An orthonormal coframe: one-forms \(e^a\) with
  \(g = \eta_{ab}\,e^a\otimes e^b\). Its dual frame \(e_a\) is orthonormal.
  /Slide: Coframes and the first structure equation./
- connection one-forms :: \(\omega^a{}_b\), defined by \(\nabla e_b =
  \omega^a{}_b\otimes e_a\). For the Levi-Civita connection they are the unique
  solution of \(\mathrm{d}e^a = -\omega^a{}_b\wedge e^b\) with
  \(\omega_{ab} = -\omega_{ba}\). /Slide: Coframes and the first structure equation./
- curvature two-forms :: \(\Omega^a{}_b = \mathrm{d}\omega^a{}_b +
  \omega^a{}_c\wedge\omega^c{}_b = \tfrac12R^a{}_{bcd}\,e^c\wedge e^d\): unit 33's
  Riemann tensor, packaged as two-forms. /Slide: The second structure equation, and Bianchi for free./
- geodesic curvature :: For a unit-speed curve on a surface, \(\kappa_g =
  \langle\nabla_TT, N\rangle\): how fast it turns away from being a geodesic,
  measured inside the surface. In a frame, \(\kappa_g = \psi' - \omega^1{}_2(T)\).
  /Slide: Gauss–Bonnet, from Stokes and one connection form./
- interior product :: \(i_X\omega = \omega(X,\cdot,\dots,\cdot)\): feed a vector
  field into the first slot. It lowers degree by one.
  /Slide: Liouville's theorem, in one line./
- Cartan's magic formula :: \(\mathcal{L}_X = \mathrm{d}\,i_X + i_X\,\mathrm{d}\) on
  forms. With it, Liouville's theorem is \(\mathcal{L}_{X_H}\omega = -\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}H = 0\).
  /Slide: Liouville's theorem, in one line./
