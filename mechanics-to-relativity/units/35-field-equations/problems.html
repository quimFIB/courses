The Einstein field equations, twice

the slides · the curriculum

Checkpoint. You argue from the contracted Bianchi identity and Lovelock's theorem why the Einstein tensor is forced, fix the coupling constant at \(8\pi G/c^4\) from the Newtonian limit, and linearise the equations to obtain the wave equation with the correct count of propagating degrees of freedom — and, if you have unit 29, you obtain the same field equations independently by varying the Einstein-Hilbert action.

Work each problem on paper first. Under it, Hints opens a ladder one rung at a time — each rung appears only once the one before it is open, and the last gives the first line of the working — and Worked solution has the whole thing: the idea, the working, then a check. Read the solution after your attempt, including when you got it right. F folds everything back; D switches dark and light.

Thirteen problems, about six hours. P8 needs unit 29; every other problem needs only this unit's route (units 33 and 30 and what they rest on). A learner without unit 29 who can do P1, P2, P5, P6 and P12 has passed the first three clauses of the checkpoint.

Conventions, as in the deck: signature \((-,+,+,+)\), \(x^0 = ct\), Greek indices \(0\ldots3\), Latin \(1\ldots3\); \(R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma}\), \(R_{\sigma\nu} = R^\mu{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}\), \(G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12Rg_{\mu\nu}\), \(\Box = \eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\). The field equations are \(G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}\) with \(\kappa = 8\pi G/c^4\).

Constants: \(G = 6.674\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}s^{-2}}\), \(c = 2.998\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), \(GM_\odot = 1.32712\times10^{20}\ \mathrm{m^3s^{-2}}\), mean density of the Earth \(\bar\rho_\oplus = 5513\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}\), \(\Lambda = 1.09\times10^{-52}\ \mathrm{m^{-2}}\), 1 pc \(= 3.086\times10^{16}\) m.

Route two: the equation is forced

P1 Which tensors are allowed medium

exercises: the contracted Bianchi identity, traces, reconstructing November 1915

On 11 November 1915 Einstein proposed \(R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}\).

  1. Using only \(\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0\) and the identity \(\nabla^\mu R_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\nabla_\nu R\), show that the proposal forces \(T = g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}\) to be constant on every connected spacetime.
  2. Explain why this is fatal for a single star of dust surrounded by vacuum, and why it is not fatal for a universe filled only with electromagnetic radiation.
  3. Look for a repair of the form \(R_{\mu\nu} + \beta\,R\,g_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}\) with \(\beta\) a constant. Find the unique \(\beta\) for which no condition on \(T\) beyond \(\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0\) results.
  4. Show that the repaired equation and the original proposal have exactly the same vacuum solutions.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Part 1 is a statement about the gradient of a scalar: you want \(\nabla_\nu(\text{something}) = 0\). Two operations are available on the equation — take its divergence, and take its trace — and you need both.

Rung 2

The divergence of the left side is \(\tfrac12\nabla_\nu R\) and of the right side zero, so \(R\) is constant. The trace converts a statement about \(R\) into one about \(T\). For part 3, compute \(\nabla^\mu\) of \(R_{\mu\nu} + \beta Rg_{\mu\nu}\) using \(\nabla g = 0\), and ask when it vanishes for every metric.

Rung 3

First line of part 3: \(\nabla^\mu(R_{\mu\nu} + \beta Rg_{\mu\nu}) = \tfrac12\nabla_\nu R + \beta\nabla_\nu R = (\tfrac12 + \beta)\nabla_\nu R\). For part 4, trace the repaired equation with \(T_{\mu\nu} = 0\).

Worked solution

Idea. A tensor equation implies its own divergence and its own trace. The divergence of the left side is fixed by geometry; the divergence of the right side is fixed by physics. If they disagree, the equation constrains the matter, and that constraint is the test.

1. Apply \(\nabla^\mu\) to \(R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}\): \[ \tfrac12\nabla_\nu R = \kappa\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0 . \] So \(\partial_\nu R = 0\) (on a scalar \(\nabla = \partial\)), and on a connected manifold \(R\) is constant. Now trace the proposal with \(g^{\mu\nu}\): \(R = \kappa T\). Hence \(T = R/\kappa\) is constant too.

2. For dust, \(T = g_{\mu\nu}\rho u^\mu u^\nu = -\rho c^2\). Far from an isolated star there is vacuum and \(T = 0\); constancy then forces \(T = 0\) everywhere, so \(\rho = 0\) inside the star as well. The theory admits no isolated massive body: it is not merely restrictive but empty for the case Newton was built for. Electromagnetic radiation has \(T = 0\) identically (the deck's EM slide: \(g_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}_{\text{EM}} = 0\) since \(g_{\mu\nu}g^{\mu\nu} = 4\)), so the constraint is satisfied for free. That is exactly why Einstein, who in November 1915 briefly hoped all matter was electromagnetic in origin, could entertain the proposal for a week.

3. Using \(\nabla g = 0\), \[ \nabla^\mu(R_{\mu\nu} + \beta Rg_{\mu\nu}) = \tfrac12\nabla_\nu R + \beta\nabla_\nu R = (\tfrac12 + \beta)\nabla_\nu R . \] The field equation then implies \((\tfrac12+\beta)\nabla_\nu R = 0\). Tracing the equation gives \((1 + 4\beta)R = \kappa T\), so for \(\beta \ne -\tfrac14\) this is a condition \((\tfrac12+\beta)\nabla_\nu T = 0\) on the matter — the same disease — unless \(\beta = -\tfrac12\). (For \(\beta = -\tfrac14\) the trace gives \(T = 0\) directly, which is just as fatal.) So \(\beta = -\tfrac12\) uniquely, and the left side is \(G_{\mu\nu}\): the equation of 25 November.

4. With \(T_{\mu\nu} = 0\), the repaired equation traced gives \(R - 2R = 0\), so \(R = 0\), and then \(G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} = 0\). Conversely \(R_{\mu\nu} = 0\) gives \(R = 0\) and \(G_{\mu\nu} = 0\). Both are \(R_{\mu\nu} = 0\).

Check. Dimensions: \([R_{\mu\nu}] = [Rg_{\mu\nu}] = \mathrm{m^{-2}}\), so \(\beta\) is dimensionless, as a pure number fixed by an identity must be ✓. Special case: the vacuum agreement in part 4 is why Einstein's perihelion calculation of 18 November, made with the wrong equation, gave the right 43 arcseconds — the Sun's exterior is vacuum.

P2 Proof: Lovelock with linearity granted medium

exercises: what "identically" means, Christoffels and curvature of a surface of revolution, product metrics

Let \(A_{\mu\nu} = \alpha R_{\mu\nu} + \beta Rg_{\mu\nu} + \gamma g_{\mu\nu}\) with constants \(\alpha, \beta, \gamma\) — the general symmetric tensor linear in curvature (the deck's notes to the Lovelock slide say why).

  1. Show \(\nabla^\mu A_{\mu\nu} = (\tfrac12\alpha + \beta)\nabla_\nu R\).
  2. For the two-dimensional metric \(\mathrm dr^2 + f(r)^2\,\mathrm d\varphi^2\), compute the Christoffel symbols and show that the scalar curvature is \(R = -2f''/f\).
  3. On \(\mathbb R^2\times\{(r,\varphi): r\gt0\}\) take the Lorentzian metric \[ g = -c^2\mathrm dt^2 + \mathrm dz^2 + \mathrm dr^2 + (r + r^3)^2\,\mathrm d\varphi^2 . \] Show that its scalar curvature is not constant.
  4. Conclude: \(A_{\mu\nu}\) is divergence-free for every metric if and only if \(\beta = -\tfrac12\alpha\), and then \(A = \alpha G_{\mu\nu} + \gamma g_{\mu\nu}\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

The answer to part 4 is an "if and only if" about a universally quantified statement. "If" is part 1. "Only if" needs a single metric on which \(\nabla R \ne 0\) — that is what parts 2 and 3 build.

Rung 2

For part 2 the only nonzero Christoffels are \(\Gamma^r_{\varphi\varphi}\) and \(\Gamma^\varphi_{r\varphi} = \Gamma^\varphi_{\varphi r}\) — read them off the geodesic Lagrangian \(\tfrac12(\dot r^2 + f^2\dot\varphi^2)\) as unit 32 taught. For part 3, the metric is a product: the \((t,z)\) block is flat and constant, so no Christoffel symbol mixes the two factors.

Rung 3

\(\Gamma^r_{\varphi\varphi} = -ff'\), \(\Gamma^\varphi_{r\varphi} = f'/f\). First line of the curvature: \(R_{rr} = R^\varphi{}_{r\varphi r} = \partial_\varphi\Gamma^\varphi_{rr} - \partial_r\Gamma^\varphi_{\varphi r} + \Gamma^\varphi_{\varphi\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{rr} - \Gamma^\varphi_{r\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\varphi r}\).

Worked solution

Idea. "Divergence-free identically" means for every metric. A computation shows the divergence is a multiple of \(\nabla R\); one explicit metric with non-constant \(R\) then kills the multiple.

1. With \(\nabla g = 0\) and \(\nabla^\mu R_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\nabla_\nu R\): \(\nabla^\mu A_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\alpha\nabla_\nu R + \beta\nabla_\nu R + 0\).

2. The geodesic Lagrangian \(L = \tfrac12(\dot r^2 + f^2\dot\varphi^2)\) gives Euler-Lagrange equations \(\ddot r - ff'\dot\varphi^2 = 0\) and \(\ddot\varphi + 2(f'/f)\dot r\dot\varphi = 0\). Matching to \(\ddot x^\lambda + \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu = 0\): \(\Gamma^r_{\varphi\varphi} = -ff'\), \(\Gamma^\varphi_{r\varphi} = \Gamma^\varphi_{\varphi r} = f'/f\), all others zero. Nothing depends on \(\varphi\). Then \[ R_{rr} = -\partial_r\Big(\frac{f'}{f}\Big) - \Big(\frac{f'}{f}\Big)^2 = -\frac{ff'' - f'^2}{f^2} - \frac{f'^2}{f^2} = -\frac{f''}{f}, \] \[ R_{\varphi\varphi} = R^r{}_{\varphi r\varphi} = \partial_r\Gamma^r_{\varphi\varphi} - \Gamma^r_{\varphi\varphi}\Gamma^\varphi_{r\varphi} = -(f'^2 + ff'') + f'^2 = -ff'' . \] So \(R = g^{rr}R_{rr} + g^{\varphi\varphi}R_{\varphi\varphi} = -f''/f - f''/f = -2f''/f\).

3. The \((t,z)\) components of \(g\) are constant and do not depend on \(r,\varphi\), and the \((r,\varphi)\) components do not depend on \(t,z\); every Christoffel symbol with a mixed index therefore vanishes, the \((t,z)\) block contributes no curvature, and the scalar curvature is that of the surface. With \(f = r + r^3\), \(f'' = 6r\): \[ R = -\frac{12r}{r + r^3} = -\frac{12}{1 + r^2}, \qquad \partial_rR = \frac{24r}{(1+r^2)^2} \ne 0 \text{ for } r \gt 0 . \]

4. If \(\beta = -\tfrac12\alpha\), part 1 gives zero for every metric. If \(\beta \ne -\tfrac12\alpha\), the metric of part 3 has \(\nabla^\mu A_{\mu r} = (\tfrac12\alpha + \beta)\partial_rR \ne 0\). So divergence-freeness for every metric holds exactly when \(\beta = -\tfrac12\alpha\), and then \(A = \alpha(R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12Rg_{\mu\nu}) + \gamma g_{\mu\nu} = \alpha G_{\mu\nu} + \gamma g_{\mu\nu}\). \(\square\)

Check. Special case \(f = a\sin(r/a)\), the round sphere of radius \(a\): \(f''/f = -1/a^2\) and \(R = 2/a^2\), twice unit 33's Gaussian curvature \(1/a^2\) ✓. Limit \(f = r\), the flat plane in polar coordinates: \(R = 0\) ✓. Dimensions: \(f''/f\) is \(\mathrm{m^{-2}}\) ✓.

Another way

Part 3 does not need the product structure spelt out: \(R\) is a scalar, and in any chart adapted to a product of Riemannian and Lorentzian factors it is the sum of the factors' scalar curvatures. The flat factor contributes nothing.

P3 Dust, seen from a moving frame routine

exercises: tensors as bilinear maps, boosting components, the trace as an invariant

Dust of rest-frame density \(\rho\) moves with velocity \(v\) along \(x\) relative to an inertial observer \(O\) in Minkowski space.

  1. Write the dust's four-velocity in \(O\)'s coordinates and compute all components \(T^{\mu\nu}\) that are nonzero.
  2. Show the energy density \(O\) measures is \(\gamma^2\rho c^2\), and account for each factor of \(\gamma\) physically.
  3. Compute it from the definition \(\varepsilon = T(u_O,u_O)/c^2\) instead, without components in the dust frame, and check it agrees.
  4. Show \(T = g_{\mu\nu}T^{\mu\nu}\) is the same in both frames. For \(v = 0.6c\), give the ratio of the energy density \(O\) measures to the rest-frame one.
  5. Do the same for a perfect fluid and show \(O\) measures \(\gamma^2(\rho c^2 + p) - p\). Why does pressure enter the energy density a moving observer sees?
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Each part is a component or a scalar. \(T^{\mu\nu} = \rho u^\mu u^\nu\) needs only \(u^\mu\), and unit 27 wrote four-velocities as \(\gamma(c,\vec v)\).

Rung 2

For part 3 use \(T(u_O,u_O) = \rho\,(u\cdot u_O)^2\), which is a scalar and can be evaluated in whatever frame is convenient. For part 5 the extra term is \(p\,g(u_O,u_O)\).

Rung 3

First line: \(u^\mu = \gamma(c, v, 0, 0)\), so \(T^{00} = \rho\gamma^2c^2\), \(T^{01} = \rho\gamma^2cv\), \(T^{11} = \rho\gamma^2v^2\).

Worked solution

Idea. The tensor is one object; its components depend on the frame, its values on given vectors and its trace do not.

1. \(u^\mu = \gamma(c, v, 0, 0)\), \(\gamma = (1 - v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}\). Nonzero components: \(T^{00} = \gamma^2\rho c^2\), \(T^{01} = T^{10} = \gamma^2\rho cv\), \(T^{11} = \gamma^2\rho v^2\).

2. \(O\)'s orthonormal frame is the coordinate frame, so its energy density is \(T^{00} = \gamma^2\rho c^2\). One \(\gamma\): each particle's energy is \(\gamma mc^2\) (unit 27). The other: a box that holds \(N\) particles in the dust frame is Lorentz-contracted by \(\gamma\) in \(O\)'s frame, so the number density is \(\gamma n\).

3. \(u_O = (c,0,0,0)\), \(u\cdot u_O = \eta_{00}\gamma c\cdot c = -\gamma c^2\), so \(T(u_O,u_O)/c^2 = \rho(\gamma c^2)^2/c^2 = \gamma^2\rho c^2\) ✓. Evaluated in the dust frame instead, \(u = (c,\vec0)\) and \(u_O = \gamma(c,-\vec v)\): the same product \(-\gamma c^2\), as a scalar must give.

4. \(T = \rho\,u\cdot u = -\rho c^2\) in every frame; componentwise in \(O\)'s: \(-T^{00} + T^{11} = -\gamma^2\rho(c^2 - v^2) = -\rho c^2\) ✓. At \(v = 0.6c\), \(\gamma^2 = 1/(1 - 0.36) = 1.5625\): \(O\) measures \(1.5625\) times the rest-frame energy density.

5. \(T(u_O,u_O) = (\rho + p/c^2)(u\cdot u_O)^2 + p\,u_O\cdot u_O = (\rho + p/c^2)\gamma^2c^4 - pc^2\); divide by \(c^2\): \(\gamma^2(\rho c^2 + p) - p\). A moving fluid carries momentum, and pressure does work on the fluid's moving boundaries: the momentum flux \(T^{11}\) contains \(p\), and a boost mixes \(T^{11}\) into \(T^{00}\) with weight \(\gamma^2v^2/c^2\). Indeed \(\gamma^2(\rho c^2 + p) - p = \gamma^2\rho c^2 + \gamma^2(v^2/c^2)p\).

Check. \(v\to0\): \(\gamma\to1\), energy density \(\rho c^2\) ✓; \(p\to0\) in part 5 returns part 2 ✓. Dimensions: \(\gamma^2\rho c^2\) and \(p\) are both \(\mathrm{J\,m^{-3}}\) ✓.

P4 Trace reversal, and why four is special routine

exercises: linear maps on symmetric tensors, traces in \(n\) dimensions, what the field equations say in two

On an \(n\)-dimensional spacetime, for a symmetric \((0,2)\)-tensor \(S\) write \(\operatorname{tr}S = g^{\mu\nu}S_{\mu\nu}\) and define \(\tau(S) = S - \tfrac12(\operatorname{tr}S)\,g\).

  1. Compute \(\operatorname{tr}\tau(S)\). Show \(\tau\circ\tau = \mathrm{id}\) if and only if \(n = 4\).
  2. For general \(n \ge 3\), invert \(G_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}\) to express \(R_{\mu\nu}\) in terms of \(T\).
  3. In \(n = 2\) the Riemann tensor has one independent component (unit 33), and every metric satisfies \(R_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12Rg_{\mu\nu}\). Assume this and state what \(G_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}\) says in two dimensions.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

\(\tau\) is a linear map on the space of symmetric tensors at a point, and everything reduces to what it does to the trace. \(g^{\mu\nu}g_{\mu\nu} = n\).

Rung 2

\(\operatorname{tr}\tau(S) = (1 - n/2)\operatorname{tr}S\). Apply \(\tau\) again, and collect the coefficient of \((\operatorname{tr}S)\,g\). For part 2, trace the field equation first.

Rung 3

First line of part 2: \(g^{\mu\nu}G_{\mu\nu} = R - \tfrac n2R = \kappa T\), so \(R = -\dfrac{2\kappa T}{n-2}\).

Worked solution

Idea. On traces, \(\tau\) acts as multiplication by \(1 - n/2\); on trace-free tensors it is the identity. An involution must square to one on both pieces.

1. \(\operatorname{tr}\tau(S) = \operatorname{tr}S - \tfrac n2\operatorname{tr}S = (1-\tfrac n2)\operatorname{tr}S\). Then \[ \tau(\tau(S)) = \tau(S) - \tfrac12(1-\tfrac n2)(\operatorname{tr}S)g = S - \tfrac12\big(2 - \tfrac n2\big)(\operatorname{tr}S)\,g . \] This is \(S\) for every \(S\) iff \(2 - n/2 = 0\) iff \(n = 4\). (Take \(S = g\) to see the coefficient must vanish.) In four dimensions \(\operatorname{tr}\tau(S) = -\operatorname{tr}S\): the name "trace reversal" is literal.

2. Tracing, \(R(1 - n/2) = \kappa T\), so \(R = -2\kappa T/(n-2)\). Then \[ R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu} + \tfrac12Rg_{\mu\nu} = \kappa\Big(T_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{n-2}\,T\,g_{\mu\nu}\Big). \] At \(n = 4\) this is the deck's \(R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa(T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12Tg_{\mu\nu})\) ✓.

3. In two dimensions \(G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12Rg_{\mu\nu} = 0\) for every metric. The field equation becomes \(0 = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}\): it says nothing about the geometry and forbids all matter. Two-dimensional general relativity does not exist, and the formula of part 2 already warned of it with its \(1/(n-2)\).

Check. Dimensions: \(T/(n-2)\) has the dimensions of \(T\) ✓. Limit: in vacuum part 2 gives \(R_{\mu\nu} = 0\) in every dimension \(n \ge 3\) ✓.

P5 Where the cosmological constant wins medium

exercises: the weak-field limit with \(\Lambda\), the trace-reversed equation, a number

  1. Starting from \(G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}\), derive the trace-reversed form, and redo the deck's weak, static, slow computation to show \[ \nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G\rho - \Lambda c^2 . \]
  2. Find the spherically symmetric solution outside a point mass \(M\) that is regular away from the origin, and the radius \(r_\Lambda\) at which the outward \(\Lambda\) acceleration equals the inward Newtonian one.
  3. Evaluate \(r_\Lambda\) for the Sun, in parsecs, and compare with the nearest star (1.30 pc). Evaluate it for a mass of \(10^{12}M_\odot\), in megaparsecs.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

The answer to part 1 is a modified Poisson equation; the only new work is the trace, which now has a \(\Lambda\) in it. Part 2 is a radial ODE; part 3 is one cube root.

Rung 2

Trace: \(-R + 4\Lambda = \kappa T\). Substitute back for \(R\). The \(00\) component then has an extra \(\Lambda g_{00} = -\Lambda\). For part 2, \(\nabla^2(r^2) = 6\) in three dimensions (unit 05's Laplacian in spherical coordinates).

Rung 3

First line: \(R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa\big(T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12Tg_{\mu\nu}\big) + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}\), so \(R_{00} = \tfrac12\kappa\rho c^2 - \Lambda\).

Worked solution

Idea. \(\Lambda\) enters the Newtonian limit as a uniform negative mass density \(-\Lambda c^2/4\pi G\), and a uniform density's effect grows linearly with distance while a point mass's falls as \(1/r^2\). So they cross, and the crossing is far away.

1. Trace with \(g^{\mu\nu}\), using \(g^{\mu\nu}g_{\mu\nu} = 4\): \(R - 2R + 4\Lambda = \kappa T\), so \(R = 4\Lambda - \kappa T\). Then \[ R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu} + \tfrac12Rg_{\mu\nu} - \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \kappa\big(T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12Tg_{\mu\nu}\big) + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}. \] For static slow dust, as in the deck, \(T_{00} - \tfrac12Tg_{00} = \tfrac12\rho c^2\), and \(g_{00} \approx -1\): \(R_{00} = \tfrac12\kappa\rho c^2 - \Lambda\). Worked example 3 gave \(R_{00} = \nabla^2\Phi/c^2\), so with \(\kappa = 8\pi G/c^4\), \[ \nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G\rho - \Lambda c^2 . \] (\(\Lambda g_{00}\) is evaluated with \(g_{00} = -1\); the correction \(\Lambda h_{00}\) is a product of two small quantities and is dropped.)

2. Outside the mass, \(\nabla^2\Phi = -\Lambda c^2\). A particular solution is \(-\Lambda c^2r^2/6\) since \(\nabla^2 r^2 = 6\); the homogeneous radial solutions are \(a + b/r\), and matching to the point mass fixes \(b = -GM\): \[ \Phi = -\frac{GM}{r} - \frac{\Lambda c^2r^2}{6} . \] The radial acceleration is \(-\Phi'(r) = -GM/r^2 + \Lambda c^2r/3\). It vanishes at \[ r_\Lambda = \Big(\frac{3GM}{\Lambda c^2}\Big)^{1/3}. \]

3. Sun: \(3\times1.32712\times10^{20}/(1.09\times10^{-52}\times8.988\times10^{16}) = 4.06\times10^{55}\ \mathrm{m^3}\), cube root \(3.44\times10^{18}\) m \(= 111\) pc — about 85 times the distance to the nearest star, and far outside any orbit the Sun controls. For \(10^{12}M_\odot\), \(r_\Lambda\) scales as \(M^{1/3}\), up by \(10^4\): \(3.44\times10^{22}\) m \(= 1.11\) Mpc. That is the scale of the Local Group, where Andromeda sits at about 0.77 Mpc: \(\Lambda\) is invisible in the solar system and a real effect between galaxies.

Check. Dimensions: \([\Lambda c^2] = \mathrm{m^{-2}\,m^2s^{-2}} = \mathrm{s^{-2}}\), and \([4\pi G\rho] = \mathrm{m^3kg^{-1}s^{-2}\,kg\,m^{-3}} = \mathrm{s^{-2}}\) ✓; \([GM/\Lambda c^2] = \mathrm{m^3s^{-2}}/\mathrm{s^{-2}} = \mathrm{m^3}\) ✓. Limit \(\Lambda\to0\): \(r_\Lambda\to\infty\) and Poisson returns ✓.

P6 The coupling in \(n\) dimensions hard

exercises: the Newtonian limit from scratch, trace reversal in \(n\) dimensions, reading a theory off its limit

Work on an \(n\)-dimensional spacetime, \(n \ge 3\), with one time and \(n-1\) space dimensions. Suppose Newtonian gravity there is \(\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G_n\rho\) for some constant \(G_n\), and that unit 32's identification \(g_{00} = -(1 + 2\Phi/c^2)\) still holds.

  1. Check that worked example 3's result \(R_{00} = \nabla^2\Phi/c^2\) does not depend on \(n\).
  2. Using P4, find \(\kappa\) in terms of \(G_n\). Check \(n = 4\) and evaluate \(n = 5\).
  3. What happens at \(n = 3\)? Interpret it using unit 33's statement that in three dimensions Ricci determines Riemann.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

The answer is a formula \(\kappa(n)\) with a factor that misbehaves at one value of \(n\). Nothing in worked example 3 counted dimensions; the dimension enters only through the trace.

Rung 2

P4 gives \(R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa(T_{\mu\nu} - Tg_{\mu\nu}/(n-2))\). For static dust \(T = -\rho c^2\) in any dimension. Compute the \(00\) component and set it equal to \(\nabla^2\Phi/c^2\).

Rung 3

First line: \(T_{00} - \dfrac{Tg_{00}}{n-2} = \rho c^2 - \dfrac{\rho c^2}{n-2} = \dfrac{n-3}{n-2}\,\rho c^2\).

Worked solution

Idea. The geometric side of the Newtonian limit is dimension-blind; the source side carries a factor \((n-3)/(n-2)\) from the trace. That factor fixes the coupling for \(n \ge 4\) and destroys the Newtonian limit at \(n = 3\).

1. Worked example 3 used \(\Gamma^i_{00} = -\tfrac12\partial_ih_{00}\) and \(R_{00} = \partial_i\Gamma^i_{00}\) to first order with \(\partial_0 = 0\). Neither step used the range of \(i\): the sum \(\partial_i\partial_i\) is simply the Laplacian in \(n-1\) dimensions. So \(R_{00} = -\tfrac12\nabla^2h_{00} = \nabla^2\Phi/c^2\) for all \(n\).

2. With static dust, \(T_{00} = \rho c^2\), \(T = -\rho c^2\), \(g_{00} = -1\): \[ R_{00} = \kappa\,\frac{n-3}{n-2}\,\rho c^2 . \] Equate with \(\nabla^2\Phi/c^2\): \(\nabla^2\Phi = \kappa c^4\,\frac{n-3}{n-2}\,\rho\). This is Newton's \(4\pi G_n\rho\) iff \[ \kappa = \frac{4\pi G_n}{c^4}\cdot\frac{n-2}{n-3}. \] At \(n = 4\): \(\tfrac21\cdot4\pi G/c^4 = 8\pi G/c^4\) ✓. At \(n = 5\): \(\tfrac32\cdot4\pi G_5/c^4 = 6\pi G_5/c^4\).

Rung 1

The answer has three pieces: a bulk integrand that must vanish (the field equations), a boundary integrand that contains normal derivatives of \(\delta g\), and a number \(\beta\) proportional to \(1/\alpha\). Everything in part 1 is on the deck's "variation, assembled" slide; the new work is on the boundary.

Rung 2

On the boundary \(\delta g = 0\), so every derivative of \(\delta g\) along the boundary vanishes, and \(\nabla\delta g = \partial\delta g\) there. Rung 3

Worked solution

Idea. The bulk variation gives the field equations; what is left is a boundary integral of normal derivatives of \(\delta g\), which Dirichlet data do not control. The extrinsic curvature is the one boundary scalar whose variation contains exactly those normal derivatives, so a multiple of it cancels them.

1. As in worked example 4, \(\delta(\sqrt{-g}R) = \sqrt{-g}\,G_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu} + \sqrt{-g}\,\nabla_\lambda v^\lambda\) and \(\delta(\sqrt{-g}\mathcal L_{\text m}) = -\tfrac12\sqrt{-g}\,T_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}\) (unit 29's definition). With P10's identity \(\sqrt{-g}\nabla_\lambda v^\lambda = \partial_\lambda(\sqrt{-g}v^\lambda)\) and the divergence theorem, \[ c\,\delta S = \int_{\mathcal M}\Big[\frac{1}{2\alpha}G_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12T_{\mu\nu}\Big]\delta g^{\mu\nu}\sqrt{-g}\,\mathrm d^4x + \frac{1}{2\alpha}\oint_{\partial\mathcal M}v^\lambda n_\lambda\sqrt{|h|}\,\mathrm d^3x . \]

2. The deck's notes give \(v_\lambda = \nabla^\mu\delta g_{\lambda\mu} - \nabla_\lambda(g^{\mu\nu}\delta g_{\mu\nu})\), with \(\delta g_{\mu\nu}\) the variation of the lower-index metric. On the boundary \(\delta g = 0\), so the \(\Gamma\,\delta g\) terms in \(\nabla\delta g\) vanish and \[ n^\lambda v_\lambda = n^\lambda g^{\mu\alpha}\partial_\alpha\delta g_{\lambda\mu} - n^\lambda g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\lambda\delta g_{\mu\nu}. \] Write \(g^{\mu\alpha} = h^{\mu\alpha} + n^\mu n^\alpha\). In the first term, \(h^{\mu\alpha}\partial_\alpha\) differentiates along the boundary, where \(\delta g \equiv 0\), so it gives zero, leaving \(n^\lambda n^\mu n^\alpha\partial_\alpha\delta g_{\lambda\mu}\). 3. \(K = h^{\alpha\beta}(\partial_\alpha n_\beta - \Gamma^\gamma_{\alpha\beta}n_\gamma)\). Check. Rung 1

Rung 2

Worked solution

Idea. A Lagrangian with second derivatives leaves, after integration by parts, a boundary term in \(\delta y'\). Dirichlet data kill \(\delta y\) at the ends, not \(\delta y'\). Subtracting the right total derivative converts the action into a first-order one with the same bulk equation and a clean boundary. This is the Gibbons-Hawking-York term in one dimension.

1. \(\delta S = \int_0^1(\delta y\,y'' + y\,\delta y'')\,\mathrm dx\). Twice by parts, \(\int_0^1 y\,\delta y'' = [y\,\delta y']_0^1 - [y'\delta y]_0^1 + \int_0^1 y''\delta y\). 2. A stationary point needs \(\delta S = 0\) for all admissible \(\delta y\). Taking \(\delta y\) supported in the interior gives \(y'' = 0\) by unit 11's lemma. Then \(\delta S = b\,\delta y'(1) - a\,\delta y'(0)\), and \(\delta y = \epsilon\,x(1-x)\) has \(\delta y'(0) = \epsilon\), \(\delta y'(1) = -\epsilon\), giving \(\delta S = -\epsilon(a + b)\); \(\delta y = \epsilon\,x^2(1-x)\) has \(\delta y'(0) = 0\), \(\delta y'(1) = -\epsilon\), giving \(-\epsilon b\). Both vanish for all \(\epsilon\) only if \(a = b = 0\).

3. The Euler-Lagrange equation \(y'' = 0\) has a two-dimensional solution space, the lines. Prescribing \(y\) and \(y'\) at both ends is four conditions: generically no line satisfies them. Fixing more data is not a repair; it overdetermines, exactly as fixing both \(g\) and its normal derivative on \(\partial\mathcal M\) would.

4. Take \(B = [y\,y']_0^1\). Check. The two actions have the same stationary curves but different values there: the boundary term changes the value of the action and not the equations, which is exactly why it matters for black-hole thermodynamics and not for the field equations. Special case \(a = b = 0\): \(S\) and \(S - B\) agree on every admissible \(y\), since \(B = 0\) ✓.

Rung 1

Part 1 is Jacobi's formula from the deck's determinant lemma, applied to a derivative instead of a variation. Parts 2 and 3 are one application of the \(T\) formula, in which only the \(\nu = 0\) component is needed.

Rung 2

Worked solution

Idea. A divergence integrates to a boundary term only if it is an ordinary divergence. For a vector it always is; for a symmetric two-index tensor a \(\Gamma T\) term is left over, and in an expanding spacetime that term is the work done by pressure on the expansion — energy leaving with nowhere to go.

1. \(\Gamma^\mu_{\mu\lambda} = \tfrac12g^{\mu\sigma}(\partial_\mu g_{\sigma\lambda} + \partial_\lambda g_{\sigma\mu} - \partial_\sigma g_{\mu\lambda})\); the first and third terms are equal after relabelling \(\mu\leftrightarrow\sigma\) (\(g^{\mu\sigma}\) is symmetric) and cancel. So \(\Gamma^\mu_{\mu\lambda} = \tfrac12g^{\mu\sigma}\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\sigma} = \tfrac12\partial_\lambda\ln|g|\) by Jacobi's formula, which is \(\partial_\lambda\ln\sqrt{-g}\). 2. With \(x^0 = ct\), \(g_{00} = -1\), \(g_{ij} = a^2\delta_{ij}\), \(\partial_0g_{ij} = 2a\dot a\delta_{ij}/c\). 3. With \(p = \rho c^2/3\): \(\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}(\rho a^3) = -\rho a^2\dot a\). Then \(E = \int a^3\rho c^2\,\mathrm d^3x \propto a^3a^{-4} = 1/a\). For the CMB since emission, the energy in a comoving box has fallen by the factor 1091. No Killing field, no conserved energy (the deck's energy slide), and the loss is exactly the redshift of each photon.

Check. Dust, \(p = 0\): \(\rho a^3\) constant, the number of particles in a comoving box is conserved ✓. Static limit \(\dot a = 0\): all \(\Gamma\) vanish and \(E\) is conserved ✓. Dimensions: with \(a\) dimensionless, \([a\dot a/c] = \mathrm{s^{-1}}/(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}) = \mathrm{m^{-1}}\), an inverse length, as a Christoffel symbol in coordinates of length must be ✓.

Idea. Gauge conditions cut the ten components to six; the residual gauge moves four of those six and leaves two invariant combinations. The invariant two are the physical polarisations, and they match the count by constraints.

1. By the deck's plane-wave lemma, \(\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = -\eta^{\alpha\beta}k_\alpha k_\beta A_{\mu\nu}\cos(k\cdot x)\), zero for all \(x\) iff \(k\) is null or \(A = 0\). Also \(\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu} = -k^\mu A_{\mu\nu}\sin(k\cdot x)\), so \(k^\mu A_{\mu\nu} = 0\). With \(k^\mu = w(1,0,0,1)\): \(A_{0\nu} + A_{3\nu} = 0\), i.e. \(A_{3\nu} = -A_{0\nu}\) for \(\nu = 0,1,2,3\). This gives \(A_{30} = -A_{00}\), \(A_{31} = -A_{01}\), \(A_{32} = -A_{02}\), \(A_{33} = -A_{03} = A_{00}\). Free: \(A_{00}, A_{01}, A_{02}, A_{11}, A_{12}, A_{22}\) — six, which is \(10 - 4\).

2. The trace of \(\partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu\) is \(2\partial^\lambda\xi_\lambda\), so \(h\to h - 2\partial^\lambda\xi_\lambda\) and \(\bar h_{\mu\nu}\) changes by \(-(\partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu) + \eta_{\mu\nu}\partial^\lambda\xi_\lambda\). With \(\partial_\mu\xi_\nu = k_\mu B_\nu\cos(k\cdot x)\), the change in \(A\) is as stated. (It preserves the Lorenz condition since \(\Box\xi = 0\), by the deck's gauge computation.) With \(k_\mu = w(-1,0,0,1)\) and \(k^\lambda B_\lambda = w(B_0 + B_3)\):

  • \(\delta A_{00} = 2wB_0 - w(B_0 + B_3) = w(B_0 - B_3)\);
  • \(\delta A_{01} = wB_1\), \(\delta A_{02} = wB_2\);
  • \(\delta A_{11} = \delta A_{22} = w(B_0 + B_3)\);
  • \(\delta A_{12} = 0\).

Choose \(B_1 = -A_{01}/w\), \(B_2 = -A_{02}/w\), \(B_0 - B_3 = -A_{00}/w\) and \(B_0 + B_3 = -(A_{11} + A_{22})/2w\). Then \(A_{00} = A_{01} = A_{02} = 0\), hence all \(A_{3\nu} = 0\), and \(A_{11} + A_{22} = 0\). What is left is \(A_{11} = -A_{22} = h_+\), \(A_{12} = h_\times\): the deck's matrix. Its trace is zero, so \(\bar h = h\).

3. \(A_{12}\) and \(A_{11} - A_{22}\) are untouched by every \(B\). The other four directions are pure gauge. So: \(10\) components, \(-4\) from the Lorenz condition, \(-4\) from the residual gauge, \(= 2\); and independently, the deck's \(10 - 4 - 4\) from gauge and constraints. The two fours are different fours with the same count — Lorenz gauge is where the constraints went.

P13 Proof: linearised curvature is gauge invariant medium

exercises: linearising the Riemann tensor, infinitesimal diffeomorphisms, the tidal tensor as a Riemann component

Let \(g = \eta + h\) to first order.

  1. Show that \(R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = \tfrac12(\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma h_{\rho\nu} + \partial_\nu\partial_\rho h_{\mu\sigma} - \partial_\mu\partial_\rho h_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\partial_\sigma h_{\rho\mu})\).
  2. Prove it is unchanged by \(h_{\mu\nu}\to h_{\mu\nu} - \partial_\mu\xi_\nu - \partial_\nu\xi_\mu\).
  3. For a static field with \(h_{00} = -2\Phi/c^2\), show \(R_{0i0j} = \partial_i\partial_j\Phi/c^2\), and recover unit 05's tidal equation from geodesic deviation.
  4. For a transverse-traceless wave, show \(R_{0i0j} = -\tfrac12\partial_0^2h_{ij}\). Conclude that \(h_+\) and \(h_\times\) are not coordinate artefacts.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

To first order the \(\Gamma\Gamma\) terms drop and indices move with \(\eta\): the answer is a sum of four second derivatives of \(h\). Gauge invariance is then a matter of partial derivatives commuting.

Rung 2

Lower the first index: \(R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma_{\rho\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma_{\rho\mu\sigma}\) with \(\Gamma_{\rho\nu\sigma} = \tfrac12(\partial_\nu h_{\rho\sigma} + \partial_\sigma h_{\rho\nu} - \partial_\rho h_{\nu\sigma})\). For part 2, substitute \(h_{\rho\nu} = -\partial_\rho\xi_\nu - \partial_\nu\xi_\rho\) and expand into eight third derivatives of \(\xi\).

Rung 3

First line of part 1: \(\partial_\mu\Gamma_{\rho\nu\sigma} = \tfrac12(\partial_\mu\partial_\nu h_{\rho\sigma} + \partial_\mu\partial_\sigma h_{\rho\nu} - \partial_\mu\partial_\rho h_{\nu\sigma})\), and the first term cancels against one in \(\partial_\nu\Gamma_{\rho\mu\sigma}\).

Worked solution

Idea. An infinitesimal diffeomorphism changes \(h\) by a symmetrised gradient, and the linearised Riemann tensor is built so that symmetrised gradients drop out, just as \(F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu\) is blind to \(A\to A + \partial\chi\). What survives is physical, and in the Newtonian limit it is the tidal tensor.

1. To first order, \(\Gamma\Gamma\) is second order and \(R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = \eta_{\rho\lambda}R^\lambda{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu\Gamma_{\rho\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\Gamma_{\rho\mu\sigma}\). Then \(\partial_\mu\Gamma_{\rho\nu\sigma} = \tfrac12(\partial_\mu\partial_\nu h_{\rho\sigma} + \partial_\mu\partial_\sigma h_{\rho\nu} - \partial_\mu\partial_\rho h_{\nu\sigma})\) and \(\partial_\nu\Gamma_{\rho\mu\sigma} = \tfrac12(\partial_\nu\partial_\mu h_{\rho\sigma} + \partial_\nu\partial_\sigma h_{\rho\mu} - \partial_\nu\partial_\rho h_{\mu\sigma})\). The first terms cancel (Clairaut), leaving the stated formula.

2. Substitute \(h_{\alpha\beta} = -\partial_\alpha\xi_\beta - \partial_\beta\xi_\alpha\). Then \(2R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}\) is minus \[ \partial_\mu\partial_\sigma(\partial_\rho\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\rho) + \partial_\nu\partial_\rho(\partial_\mu\xi_\sigma + \partial_\sigma\xi_\mu) - \partial_\mu\partial_\rho(\partial_\nu\xi_\sigma + \partial_\sigma\xi_\nu) - \partial_\nu\partial_\sigma(\partial_\rho\xi_\mu + \partial_\mu\xi_\rho). \] Sort by which component of \(\xi\) appears. \(\xi_\nu\): \(\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma\partial_\rho\xi_\nu - \partial_\mu\partial_\rho\partial_\sigma\xi_\nu = 0\). \(\xi_\rho\): \(\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma\partial_\nu\xi_\rho - \partial_\nu\partial_\sigma\partial_\mu\xi_\rho = 0\). \(\xi_\sigma\): \(\partial_\nu\partial_\rho\partial_\mu\xi_\sigma - \partial_\mu\partial_\rho\partial_\nu\xi_\sigma = 0\). \(\xi_\mu\): \(\partial_\nu\partial_\rho\partial_\sigma\xi_\mu - \partial_\nu\partial_\sigma\partial_\rho\xi_\mu = 0\). All eight terms cancel in pairs, since partial derivatives commute. \(\square\)

3. Put \(\rho = 0, \sigma = i, \mu = 0, \nu = j\), static: \(R_{0i0j} = \tfrac12(\partial_0\partial_ih_{0j} + \partial_j\partial_0h_{0i} - \partial_0^2h_{ij} - \partial_j\partial_ih_{00}) = -\tfrac12\partial_i\partial_jh_{00} = \partial_i\partial_j\Phi/c^2\). Raising the first index with \(\eta^{ii} = 1\) and using pair symmetry, \(R^i{}_{0j0} = \partial_i\partial_j\Phi/c^2\), and geodesic deviation with \(u = ce_0\) gives \(\ddot\xi^i = -c^2R^i{}_{0j0}\xi^j = -(\partial_i\partial_j\Phi)\xi^j\): unit 05's tidal equation, term for term.

4. In transverse-traceless gauge \(h_{0\mu} = 0\), so the same component is \(R_{0i0j} = -\tfrac12\partial_0^2h_{ij}\). For a wave \(h_{ij} = A_{ij}\cos(k\cdot x)\) this is \(\tfrac12(\omega/c)^2A_{ij}\cos(k\cdot x)\), nonzero whenever \(h_+\) or \(h_\times\) is. By part 2 no coordinate change can remove it, so the polarisations are curvature, not coordinates.

Check. Dimensions: \([\partial\partial h] = \mathrm{m^{-2}}\) and \([\partial_i\partial_j\Phi/c^2] = \mathrm{m^{-2}}\) ✓. Special case: the point mass \(\Phi = -GM/r\) gives tidal eigenvalues \((-2, 1, 1)GM/r^3\) along the radial and two transverse directions, unit 33's result, and their sum \(\nabla^2\Phi = 0\) is \(R_{00} = 0\) in vacuum ✓.
