The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: a non-rotating black hole of ten solar masses,
\(GM = 1.3271\times10^{21}\ \mathrm{m^3\,s^{-2}}\). Its Schwarzschild radius is
\(r_s = 2GM/c^2 = 29.53\) km; at its horizon the Kretschmann scalar is
\(1.58\times10^{-17}\ \mathrm{m^{-4}}\) and the tidal stretch across two metres is
\(2.1\times10^{7}\,g\); a body falling from rest at infinity has \(65.7\ \mu\)s of
proper time between the horizon and \(r = 0\).

* The ansatz

- spherically symmetric :: A spacetime on which \(SO(3)\) acts by isometries
  with every orbit a round 2-sphere or a point. The geometry outside any
  non-rotating star. /Slide: Static and spherically symmetric, defined./
- areal radius :: The function \(r = \sqrt{\text{Area}/4\pi}\) of the orbit
  sphere through a point. Defined by the geometry, with no choice, which is why
  it can serve as a coordinate; it is not a distance from any centre.
- stationary :: Having a Killing vector field that is timelike (at least far
  away). The geometry does not change in time.
- static :: Stationary, with the timelike Killing field orthogonal to a family
  of hypersurfaces. Then \(g_{ti} = 0\) in adapted coordinates and time
  reversal is an isometry. A rotating star is stationary but not static.

* Solving the vacuum equations

- asymptotic flatness :: The requirement that the metric tend to Minkowski's
  far from the source, here \(A, B\to1\) as \(r\to\infty\). It fixes the
  constant in \(AB = \text{const}\) to 1, which is a choice of units for \(t\).
  /Slide: Solving: two lines and one constant./
- Schwarzschild metric :: \(\mathrm ds^2 = -(1 - r_s/r)c^2\mathrm dt^2 +
  (1 - r_s/r)^{-1}\mathrm dr^2 + r^2\mathrm d\Omega^2\): the unique round
  vacuum solution of the Einstein equations, derived from the ansatz with
  \(R_{\mu\nu} = 0\) and the Newtonian limit.
  /Slide: The constant, from the Newtonian limit./
- Schwarzschild radius :: \(r_s = 2GM/c^2\). \(2.953\) km for the Sun,
  \(8.870\) mm for the Earth, \(29.53\) km for the running example. Meaningful
  as a horizon only if it lies outside the matter.

* Birkhoff's theorem

- Birkhoff's theorem :: A spherically symmetric vacuum solution, where
  \(\nabla r\) is spacelike, is part of Schwarzschild: round and empty forces
  static. The proof is \(R_{tr} = \partial_tB/(rB) = 0\) and a rescaling of
  \(t\). /Slide: Birkhoff's theorem./
- monopole radiation :: A spherically symmetric gravitational wave. It does not
  exist: by Birkhoff, the exterior of a pulsating round star is unchanged.
  Gravitational radiation starts at the quadrupole.
  /Slide: Two consequences: silence, and a flat cavity./
- relativistic shell theorem :: The interior of an empty round cavity is flat
  spacetime, with clocks slowed relative to infinity by a constant factor.
  Newton's shell theorem with a redshift added.

* Killing fields and constants of motion

- Killing vector field :: A vector field \(K\) with \(\mathcal L_Kg = 0\)
  (unit 31), equivalently \(\nabla_\mu K_\nu + \nabla_\nu K_\mu = 0\). Its flow
  is by isometries; along any geodesic \(g(K,\dot x)\) is conserved, which is
  Noether's theorem for the geodesic Lagrangian. Schwarzschild has four.
  /Slide: The four Killing fields of Schwarzschild./
- specific energy :: \(E = -g(\partial_t,\dot x) = (1 - r_s/r)c^2\dot t\), the
  conserved quantity of the time-translation Killing field, per unit rest mass.
  Far away \(E\approx c^2 + \tfrac12v^2 - GM/r\).
  /Slide: Worked example 3 — E, L, and the radial equation./
- specific angular momentum :: \(L = g(\partial_\varphi,\dot x) =
  r^2\sin^2\theta\,\dot\varphi\), the conserved quantity of the rotation about
  the axis, per unit rest mass.

* Two singularities

- coordinate singularity :: A place where a chart's components misbehave but
  the geometry extends smoothly; some other chart covers it. Dismissed by
  exhibiting that chart. /Slide: Where the components misbehave./
- curvature singularity :: An end of spacetime, reached by a geodesic in finite
  affine parameter, along which a curvature scalar is unbounded. Convicted by
  exhibiting the scalar; no chart can remove it.
- Kretschmann scalar :: \(K = R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}R^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\).
  For Schwarzschild \(12r_s^2/r^6 = 48G^2M^2/(c^4r^6)\): finite at \(r_s\),
  divergent at \(r = 0\). /Slide: The Kretschmann scalar./
- tortoise coordinate :: \(r_* = r + r_s\ln|r/r_s - 1|\), defined by
  \(\mathrm dr_*/\mathrm dr = (1 - r_s/r)^{-1}\) so that radial light moves on
  \(ct \pm r_* = \text{const}\). The horizon is at \(r_* = -\infty\).
  /Slide: Worked example 4 — ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates./
- ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates :: \((v, r, \theta, \varphi)\) with
  \(v = ct + r_*\). The metric becomes \(-(1 - r_s/r)\mathrm dv^2 +
  2\,\mathrm dv\,\mathrm dr + r^2\mathrm d\Omega^2\), smooth with nonzero
  determinant at \(r = r_s\): the proof that the horizon is a coordinate
  singularity.
- null hypersurface :: A hypersurface whose normal covector is null; it is
  generated by light rays and can be crossed in one direction only. The
  surface \(r = r_s\) is one. /Slide: The horizon is a one-way null surface./
- event horizon :: The boundary of the region from which a future causal curve
  can escape to infinity — precisely, the boundary of the causal past of
  \(\mathscr I^+\). For Schwarzschild, \(r = r_s\). A global object, invisible
  to local measurement. /Slide: Nothing happens at the horizon, locally./

* Two observers

- static observer :: One at fixed \(r, \theta, \varphi\), moving along
  \(\partial_t\). Exists only for \(r \gt r_s\), needs proper acceleration
  \(GM/(r^2\sqrt{1 - r_s/r})\), and its clock runs at \(\sqrt{1 - r_s/r}\)
  times the rate at infinity.
  /Slide: Static observers: their rulers and their clocks./
- proper radial distance :: \(\int\mathrm dr/\sqrt{1 - r_s/r}\) at fixed
  \(t\), what a chain of static rulers measures. From \(r_s\) to \(2r_s\) it is
  \(2.296\,r_s\), \(67.79\) km for the running example.

* The whole spacetime

- conformal compactification :: An embedding of \((M, g)\) into a manifold
  with boundary carrying \(\omega^2g\), with the factor \(\omega\) positive
  inside and vanishing on the boundary. It keeps light cones and brings infinity to finite distance.
  /Slide: Conformal compactification, on Minkowski first./
- Penrose diagram :: The two-dimensional picture of a conformal
  compactification of a spherically symmetric spacetime: one point per round
  sphere, light at \(45^\circ\), infinity drawn as a boundary.
  /Slide: The Penrose diagram of Minkowski space./
- timelike infinity :: The points \(i^\pm\) of a Penrose diagram where
  timelike geodesics end and begin.
- null infinity :: The boundary segments \(\mathscr I^\pm\) ("scri") where
  light rays end and begin; the place where radiation is defined.
- spatial infinity :: The point \(i^0\) where spacelike slices end.
- Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates :: \(U = -e^{-u/2r_s}\), \(V = e^{v/2r_s}\),
  with \(u, v = ct \mp r_*\). The metric becomes
  \(-(4r_s^3/r)e^{-r/r_s}\mathrm dU\,\mathrm dV + r^2\mathrm d\Omega^2\) with
  \(UV = (1 - r/r_s)e^{r/r_s}\), smooth on all of \(UV \lt 1\).
  /Slide: Worked example 5 — Kruskal–Szekeres, step by step./
- maximal extension :: The Kruskal spacetime \(\{UV \lt 1\}\): every geodesic
  either runs for ever or ends at the curvature singularity, so no larger
  spacetime contains it.
- black hole region :: Region II, \(U, V \gt 0\): every future causal curve in
  it reaches \(r = 0\) within proper time \(\pi r_s/2c\).
  /Slide: Four regions, and which of them are real./
- white hole region :: Region IV, \(U, V \lt 0\): the time reverse of region
  II, which everything must leave. Absent from any hole formed by collapse.
- Einstein–Rosen bridge :: The throat of radius \(r_s\) joining the two
  exteriors I and III on the slice \(T = 0\). It pinches off too fast for any
  causal curve to cross it.
