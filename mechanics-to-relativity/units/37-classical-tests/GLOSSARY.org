The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: Mercury, with \(a = 5.7909\times10^{10}\) m, \(e = 0.20563\),
\(p = a(1-e^2) = 5.5460\times10^{10}\) m and \(415.20\) orbits per Julian
century, whose perihelion general relativity advances by \(0.10352''\) per orbit,
\(42.98''\) per century, against an observed \(42.98 \pm 0.04''\). Alongside it, a
ray of light grazing the Sun at \(b = R_\odot = 6.957\times10^{8}\) m, bent by
\(0.8756''\) as a Newtonian corpuscle and by \(1.7512''\) in general relativity.
Both obey the orbit equation \(u'' + u = GM/L^2 + 3r_gu^2\) (drop the first term
for light), with \(r_g = GM/c^2 = 1476.6\) m for the Sun.

* Geodesics of Schwarzschild

- gravitational radius :: \(r_g = GM/c^2\), the length that measures how strong
  a mass's field is; half the Schwarzschild radius \(r_s = 2r_g\) that unit 36
  used for the same metric, so unit 36's \(f = 1 - r_s/r\) is this unit's
  \(1 - 2r_g/r\). \(1476.6\) m for the Sun. Every effect in the unit is of
  relative size \(r_g/r\) where it happens.
  /Slide: One orbit, one ray, one extra term./
- specific energy :: \(E = -g(\partial_t, \dot x) = (1 - 2r_g/r)c^2\,\dot t\),
  conserved along a geodesic because \(\partial_t\) is a Killing vector. Unit 36's
  definition and sign, unchanged. Per unit rest mass, so the name holds only for
  a timelike geodesic with \(\tau\) as parameter; for a slow particle far away it
  is \(c^2\) plus Newton's energy per unit mass. For light the same
  \(-g(\partial_t, \dot x)\) is still conserved but is not specific anything: it
  scales with the affine parameter, and only \(b = cL/E\) is physical.
  /Slide: Two Killing vectors, two constants./
- specific angular momentum :: \(L = g(\partial_\varphi, \dot x) = r^2\sin^2\theta\,\dot\varphi\),
  conserved because \(\partial_\varphi\) is a Killing vector. Unit 08's
  \(r^2\dot\varphi\) per unit mass, with proper time as the clock; unit 21 wrote it
  \(h\). As with \(E\), "per unit mass" is a statement about massive particles;
  for light \(L = r^2\,\mathrm d\varphi/\mathrm d\lambda\) carries the affine
  parameter's scale. /Slide: Two Killing vectors, two constants./
- effective potential :: For Schwarzschild, \(V_{\rm eff} = -GM/r + L^2/2r^2 -
  GML^2/c^2r^3\), so that \(\tfrac12\dot r^2 + V_{\rm eff} =
  \tfrac12(E^2/c^2 - c^2)\) exactly, with \(\tau\) as parameter. Unit 08's
  potential plus one attractive inverse-cube term. /Slide: The effective potential./

* What the new term does close in

- innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO) :: \(r = 6r_g = 3r_s\), inside which no
  circular orbit is stable; there \(L^2 = 12r_g^2c^2\) and the binding energy is
  \(1 - \sqrt{8/9} = 5.72\%\) of the rest mass. /Slide: Six gravitational radii, and the plunge./
- plunge :: The fall into the hole of a particle whose angular momentum is
  below the ISCO value, or whose energy is above the top of the barrier. It has
  no Newtonian analogue: Newton's centrifugal barrier always wins at small \(r\).
  /Slide: Six gravitational radii, and the plunge./
- apsidal frequency :: The frequency \(\nu\), in \(\varphi\), of the radial
  oscillation of a nearly circular orbit: \(\nu = \sqrt{1 - 6r_g/r_c}\). The
  perihelion advances by \(2\pi(1/\nu - 1)\) per orbit, and \(\nu \to 0\) at the
  ISCO. /Slide: Nearly circular orbits precess, exactly./
- impact parameter :: \(b = cL/E\) for light: the perpendicular distance from
  the centre of the straight line the ray follows far away.
  /Slide: Light has a potential too: the photon sphere./
- photon sphere :: \(r = 3r_g\), where light can circle the hole on an unstable
  orbit. Rays from infinity with \(b \lt 3\sqrt3\,r_g\) are captured.
  /Slide: Light has a potential too: the photon sphere./
- shadow :: The dark disc of angular diameter \(2\cdot3\sqrt3\,r_g/D\) that the
  captured rays leave in the image of a black hole at distance \(D\):
  \(39.7\ \mu\)as for M87* with \(M = 6.5\times10^9M_\odot\), \(D = 16.8\) Mpc.
  /Slide: Light has a potential too: the photon sphere./

* Mercury: the 43

- general precession :: The slow rotation of the equinox, the zero of
  celestial longitude, caused by the Sun's and Moon's torque on the Earth's
  equatorial bulge: about \(5025''\) per century, a full circle in about 25 800
  years. It moves every perihelion longitude and is subtracted, not explained.
  /Slide: The ledger./
- anomalous perihelion advance :: What is left of Mercury's perihelion motion
  after the general precession and the other planets' pull are removed:
  \(42.6 \pm 0.9''\) per century in Clemence's 1947 accounting,
  \(42.98 \pm 0.04''\) today. Le Verrier's unexplained residual of 1859.
  /Slide: The ledger./
- perihelion advance :: \(\Delta\varpi = 6\pi GM/c^2a(1 - e^2)\) per orbit, from
  the orbit equation by Poincaré–Lindstedt: the radial frequency is
  \(\nu = 1 - 3r_g/p\). /Slide: Solving the orbit equation: Poincaré–Lindstedt./
- secular term :: In the naive expansion, the term \(\varepsilon e\varphi\sin\varphi\),
  which grows without bound. It is the first Taylor term of \(\cos((1 - \varepsilon)\varphi)\),
  so it is the precession written badly, and deleting it would predict no
  precession at all. /Slide: Why the secular term must not be removed./

* Light: the factor of two

- light deflection :: The total angle \(4GM/c^2b\) by which a ray of impact
  parameter \(b\) is bent: \(1.7512''\) at the Sun's limb.
  /Slide: Bending by general relativity./
- Newtonian corpuscle :: Light treated as a particle moving at \(c\) and
  attracted by \(-GM/r\). Its deflection is \(2GM/c^2b\), exactly half the true
  value, because it feels \(g_{tt}\) and not \(g_{rr}\). /Slide: Bending by Newton./
- PPN parameter γ :: The coefficient in \(g_{rr} = 1 + 2\gamma r_g/r\): how much
  spatial curvature a unit of mass produces. \(\gamma = 1\) in general
  relativity, \(0\) for Newtonian light; deflection and delay are proportional to
  \((1 + \gamma)/2\). /Slide: Which metric component supplies the other half./

* Clocks: the delay and the redshift

- Shapiro delay :: The extra round-trip time
  \((1 + \gamma)(2GM/c^3)\ln(4r_1r_2/b^2)\) of a signal passing a mass at
  impact parameter \(b\). About \(233\ \mu\)s for a radar echo from Venus
  grazing the Sun. /Slide: The Shapiro delay./
- superior conjunction :: The configuration in which a planet or spacecraft is
  on the far side of the Sun from the Earth, so a signal between them passes
  close to the Sun. /Slide: Worked example 4 — Shapiro's proposal: radar to Venus./
- gravitational redshift :: The exact ratio \(\nu_{\rm r}/\nu_{\rm e} =
  \sqrt{(1 - 2r_g/r_{\rm e})/(1 - 2r_g/r_{\rm r})}\) for static emitter and
  receiver, from the Killing energy of the photon. It involves only \(g_{tt}\)
  and so tests the equivalence principle, not the field equations.
  /Slide: The redshift, closed exactly./

* The tests, then and now

- geodetic precession :: The turning of a gyroscope's axis carried round a
  mass, from parallel transport in curved spacetime: \(6606\) milliarcseconds a
  year for Gravity Probe B, measured as \(6601.8 \pm 18.3\).
  /Slide: Modern tests, named and dated./
- frame dragging :: The additional gyroscope precession caused by the
  rotation of the central mass, a property of the Kerr metric (unit 40): \(39.2\)
  milliarcseconds a year predicted for Gravity Probe B, \(37.2 \pm 7.2\) measured.
  /Slide: Modern tests, named and dated./
