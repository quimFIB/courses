The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: a binary of masses \(m_1, m_2\) on a circular orbit of
separation \(a\), with \(M = m_1 + m_2\), \(\mu = m_1m_2/M\), orbital angular
frequency \(\Omega^2 = GM/a^3\) and wave frequency \(f = \Omega/\pi\). It
radiates \(P = 32G^4\mu^2M^3/5c^5a^5\), its separation falls as
\(\dot a = -64G^3\mu M^2/5c^5a^3\), and its frequency rises as
\(\dot f = \tfrac{96}{5}\pi^{8/3}(G\mathcal M/c^3)^{5/3}f^{11/3}\) with chirp mass
\(\mathcal M = \mu^{3/5}M^{2/5}\). Evaluated for PSR B1913+16 it gives
\(\dot P_b = -2.402\times10^{-12}\) against \(-2.423\times10^{-12}\) observed
(\(-2.398\times10^{-12}\) after the Galactic correction); for GW150914 it gives
a chirp from 35 Hz to coalescence in \(0.165\) s and a strain of order
\(10^{-21}\).

* Linearised gravity

- linearisation :: The derivative at \(\varepsilon = 0\) of a quantity \(F[g(\varepsilon)]\)
  along a smooth family of metrics with \(g(0) = \eta\). "First order in \(h\)"
  means exactly this derivative. /Slide: A metric near Minkowski's./
- metric perturbation :: \(h = \mathrm dg/\mathrm d\varepsilon\) at \(\varepsilon = 0\), a
  symmetric \((0,2)\)-tensor field on \(\mathbb R^4\); informally \(g = \eta + h\)
  with \(|h_{\mu\nu}|\ll1\). Indices on it are moved with \(\eta\).
- trace-reversed perturbation :: \(\bar h_{\mu\nu} = h_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}h\),
  with trace \(\bar h = -h\). In four dimensions trace reversal is an
  involution. In terms of \(\bar h\) the linearised Einstein tensor has four
  terms, three of them divergences. /Slide: The linearised field equations./
- linearised gauge transformation :: \(h_{\mu\nu}\to h_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\mu\xi_\nu +
  \partial_\nu\xi_\mu = h_{\mu\nu} + (\mathcal L_\xi\eta)_{\mu\nu}\): the first-order effect of
  pulling the metric back along the flow of a vector field \(\xi\). Two
  perturbations related by it describe one spacetime. The analogue of unit
  24's \(\vec A\to\vec A + \nabla\chi\). /Slide: Gauge: a diffeomorphism, infinitesimally./
- Lorenz gauge :: \(\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu} = 0\), reached by solving \(\Box\xi_\nu =
  -\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu}\) with unit 24's retarded solution. Named for Ludvig
  Lorenz, not Hendrik Lorentz. /Slide: Lorenz gauge, and the wave equation./
- linearised field equation :: In Lorenz gauge, \(\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = -16\pi
  GT_{\mu\nu}/c^4\) with \(\Box = \eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\); in unit 24's sign
  convention \(\Box_{24}\bar h_{\mu\nu} = +16\pi GT_{\mu\nu}/c^4\). Consistent only if
  \(\partial^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0\) in flat space.

* Two polarisations

- degree-of-freedom count :: For a plane wave with null \(k\ne0\): Lorenz-gauge
  amplitudes form a 6-dimensional space \(L\), pure-gauge ones a 4-dimensional
  subspace \(P\), and the physical amplitudes \(L/P\) a 2-dimensional space.
  \(10 - 4 - 4 = 2\). /Slide: Counting to two, honestly./
- residual gauge :: The gauge transformations that preserve Lorenz gauge: those
  with \(\Box\xi_\nu = 0\). Four more functions, spent in reaching TT gauge.
- transverse-traceless gauge (TT gauge) :: The representative of a Lorenz-gauge
  wave with \(h_{0\mu} = 0\) and zero trace; for a wave along \(n\) it also has
  \(h_{ij}n^j = 0\). Unique in each gauge class. In it \(\bar h = h\).
  /Slide: One representative per class: TT gauge./
- plus and cross polarisations :: The two gauge-invariant amplitudes of a wave
  along \(z\): \(h_+ = \tfrac12(h_{11} - h_{22})\) and \(h_\times = h_{12}\). One
  pattern, turned by \(45^\circ\).
- TT projector :: \(\Lambda(S) = PSP - \tfrac12P\,\mathrm{tr}(PS)\) with \(P = 1 - nn^{\mathsf T}\),
  a self-adjoint projector on symmetric \(3\times3\) matrices whose image, of
  dimension 2, is the transverse traceless ones. Extracts \(h^{\rm TT}\) from any
  Lorenz-gauge \(\bar h_{ij}\). /Slide: The transverse-traceless projector./
- strain :: The fractional change of proper separation between free masses,
  \(\Delta L/L_0 = \tfrac12h_{ij}n^in^j\); \(\tfrac12h_+\) along \(x\), \(-\tfrac12h_+\) along
  \(y\). Dimensionless; about \(10^{-21}\) for the strongest waves seen.
  /Slide: Free masses sit still, and distances change./
- helicity :: The integer \(s\) for which a rotation by \(\psi\) about the
  direction of travel rotates the polarisation amplitudes by \(s\psi\). Light has
  1; gravitational waves have 2, so the pattern repeats after a half turn.
  /Slide: Spin two./

* The quadrupole formula

- retarded Green's function :: \(G_{\rm ret} = \delta(t - r/c)/4\pi r\), the
  fundamental solution of unit 24's wave operator that vanishes before the
  source acts; found there by unit 20's contour integration with the poles below
  the axis. /Slide: The retarded solution./
- retarded time :: \(t - |\vec x - \vec y|/c\): the field at \(\vec x\) now reports
  the source at \(\vec y\) as it was when a signal at speed \(c\) left it.
- wave zone :: Distances large compared with both the source's size and the
  reduced wavelength \(c/\omega\), where only the \(1/r\) part of the field
  survives and carries energy. /Slide: Far away and slow./
- slow-motion approximation :: Source size small compared with \(c/\omega\),
  equivalently speeds small compared with \(c\); each term of the multipole
  series is then \(O(v/c)\) smaller than the one before.
- tensor virial theorem :: For a conserved \(T^{\mu\nu}\) of bounded support,
  \(\int T^{ij} = \tfrac12\,\mathrm d^2/\mathrm dt^2\int\rho\,y^iy^j\). Trades a source's
  stresses for its mass distribution. /Slide: Lemma: the stresses are the second moment./
- quadrupole formula :: \(h^{\rm TT}_{ij} = (2G/c^4r)\,\Lambda_{ij,kl}(n)\,\ddot Q_{kl}(t - r/c)\),
  with \(Q_{ij} = \int\rho(y^iy^j - \tfrac13\delta_{ij}|y|^2)\), one third of unit 10's
  quadrupole moment. Einstein 1918. /Slide: The quadrupole formula./
- gravitational stress :: \(t^{ij} = (4\pi G)^{-1}(\partial_i\Phi\,\partial_j\Phi -
  \tfrac12\delta_{ij}|\nabla\Phi|^2)\), whose divergence is \(\rho\,\partial_i\Phi\); with it a
  self-gravitating Newtonian source has a conserved total stress, and the virial
  lemma applies. /Slide: The binary breaks the hypothesis./

* Energy

- normal coordinates :: Unit 32's chart at a point \(p\) in which
  \(g_{\mu\nu}(p) = \eta_{\mu\nu}\) and \(\partial_\lambda g_{\mu\nu}(p) = 0\). The reason no
  tensor built from first derivatives of the metric can be a gravitational
  energy density. /Slide: No energy density at a point./
- Isaacson stress-energy tensor :: \(t_{\mu\nu} = (c^4/32\pi G)\langle\partial_\mu h^{\rm TT}_{ij}\,
  \partial_\nu h^{\rm TT}_{ij}\rangle\), averaged over several wavelengths; gauge
  invariant, and a source of background curvature. Energy flux
  \((c^3/16\pi G)\langle\dot h_+^2 + \dot h_\times^2\rangle\). Cited: Isaacson 1968.
  /Slide: The Isaacson average./
- quadrupole luminosity :: \(P = (G/5c^5)\langle\overset{...}{Q}_{ij}\overset{...}{Q}_{ij}\rangle\), the
  power radiated by a slow source. The gravitational Larmor formula.
  /Slide: The quadrupole luminosity./

* The chirp

- adiabatic inspiral :: The orbit treated as a Kepler circle whose radius drifts
  on a slow time; valid while the fraction of energy lost per cycle is small.
  Unit 21's multiple scales with radiation reaction as the perturbation.
  /Slide: A slow drift on a fast orbit: two times./
- solvability condition :: Unit 21's requirement that the next-order equation
  have no secular term. Here it is the energy balance \(\mathrm dE/\mathrm dt =
  -\langle P\rangle\).
- chirp mass :: \(\mathcal M = \mu^{3/5}M^{2/5} = (m_1m_2)^{3/5}/(m_1 + m_2)^{1/5}\), the
  only combination of the masses in \(\dot f\), in the time to coalescence and
  in the amplitude. \(1.2307\,M_\odot\) for PSR B1913+16, about \(30\,M_\odot\) for
  GW150914 as the detector sees it. /Slide: The chirp, and the one mass that shows./
- chirp :: The rising frequency and amplitude of an inspiral,
  \(f\propto(t_c - t)^{-3/8}\), \(h\propto(t_c - t)^{-1/4}\). /Slide: What the chirp looks like./

* Two binaries, measured

- ringdown :: The damped oscillation of the black hole formed at merger, the
  last part of a signal like GW150914's. Outside the methods of this unit.
  /Slide: GW150914./
