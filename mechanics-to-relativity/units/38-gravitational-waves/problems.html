Gravitational waves: ten components, two polarisations, one chirp

the slides · the curriculum

Checkpoint. From scratch, you derive the linearised wave equation \(\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu} = -16\pi GT_{\mu\nu}/c^4\) in Lorenz gauge, reduce ten components to two by explicit gauge fixing — naming the residual freedom at each stage — exhibit both polarisations' effect on a ring of free test masses, and reproduce the Hulse–Taylor orbital decay rate to within 5 per cent of the measured \(-2.423\times10^{-12}\).

Work each problem on paper first. Under it, Hints opens a ladder one rung at a time — each rung appears only once the one before it is open, and the last gives the first line of the working — and Worked solution has the whole thing: the idea, the working, then a check. Read the solution after your attempt, including when you got it right. F folds everything back; D switches dark and light.

Twelve problems, about six hours. Conventions as in the deck: signature \((-,+,+,+)\), \(x^0 = ct\), \(\Box = \eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu = -c^{-2}\partial_t^2 + \nabla^2\), indices on \(h\) moved with \(\eta\), gauge transformation \(h_{\mu\nu}\to h_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu\), and \(Q_{ij} = \int\rho\,(y^iy^j - \tfrac13\delta_{ij}|\vec y|^2)\,\mathrm d^3y\) — one third of unit 10's quadrupole moment.

Linearised gravity and its gauge

P1 Trace reversal, and why four dimensions routine

exercises: first-order expansion, index gymnastics with \(\eta\), a proof by direct computation

Work on \(\mathbb R^n\) with a Lorentzian \(\eta\) (so \(\eta^{\mu\nu}\eta_{\mu\nu} = n\)), and let \(g = \eta + h\).

  1. Prove that \(g^{\mu\nu} = \eta^{\mu\nu} - h^{\mu\nu} + O(h^2)\), where \(h^{\mu\nu} = \eta^{\mu\alpha}\eta^{\nu\beta}h_{\alpha\beta}\).
  2. Define \(\bar h_{\mu\nu} = h_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}h\). Compute the trace \(\bar h\) and then \(\bar{\bar h}_{\mu\nu}\). Prove that trace reversal is an involution if and only if \(n = 4\).
  3. Find the constant \(\alpha_n\) for which \(\tilde h_{\mu\nu} = h_{\mu\nu} - \alpha_n\eta_{\mu\nu}h\) has trace \(-h\) in \(n\) dimensions, and check that \(\alpha_4 = \tfrac12\).
  4. Check the trace reversal in the Newtonian limit of unit 35: \(h_{00} = -2\Phi/c^2\), \(h_{ij} = -2\Phi\delta_{ij}/c^2\), \(h_{0i} = 0\), in four dimensions. Compute \(\bar h_{\mu\nu}\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Part 1 is a claim about a matrix inverse to first order; parts 2 and 3 are one-line trace computations; part 4 is a substitution. Everything reduces to one number: \(\eta^{\mu\nu}\eta_{\mu\nu}\).

Rung 2

For part 1, multiply your candidate by \(\eta_{\nu\lambda} + h_{\nu\lambda}\) and show the product is \(\delta^\mu_\lambda\) up to terms quadratic in \(h\). For part 2, take the trace with \(\eta^{\mu\nu}\) and use \(\eta^{\mu\nu}\eta_{\mu\nu} = n\).

Rung 3

\(\bar h = h - \tfrac n2h = \big(1 - \tfrac n2\big)h\). Now apply the bar again: \(\bar{\bar h}_{\mu\nu} = \bar h_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}\bar h = \ldots\)

Worked solution

Idea. Everything is linear algebra at a point; the dimension enters only through the trace of the identity, \(\eta^{\mu\nu}\eta_{\mu\nu} = \delta^\mu_\mu = n\).

1. \((\eta^{\mu\nu} - h^{\mu\nu})(\eta_{\nu\lambda} + h_{\nu\lambda}) = \delta^\mu_\lambda + \eta^{\mu\nu}h_{\nu\lambda} - h^{\mu\nu}\eta_{\nu\lambda} - h^{\mu\nu}h_{\nu\lambda}\). The middle two terms are both \(h^\mu{}_\lambda\) and cancel; the last is \(O(h^2)\). The inverse of a matrix is unique, so \(g^{\mu\nu} = \eta^{\mu\nu} - h^{\mu\nu} + O(h^2)\). (For small enough \(h\) the inverse exists, because the invertible matrices form an open set.)

2. \(\bar h = \eta^{\mu\nu}\bar h_{\mu\nu} = h - \tfrac12nh = (1 - \tfrac n2)h\). Then \[\bar{\bar h}_{\mu\nu} = \bar h_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}\bar h = h_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12\eta_{\mu\nu}h - \tfrac12\big(1 - \tfrac n2\big)\eta_{\mu\nu}h = h_{\mu\nu} - \big(1 - \tfrac n4\big)\eta_{\mu\nu}h.\] This equals \(h_{\mu\nu}\) for every \(h\) if and only if \(1 - n/4 = 0\), i.e. \(n = 4\) (take \(h = \eta\), whose trace is \(n\ne0\), to see that "for every \(h\)" forces the coefficient to vanish). \(\square\)

3. \(\mathrm{tr}\,\tilde h = h - \alpha_nnh\). Setting this to \(-h\) gives \(\alpha_n = 2/n\), and \(\alpha_4 = \tfrac12\) ✓. In \(n\) dimensions the version of the linearised theory with trace \(-h\) uses \(2/n\); the one whose Einstein tensor has the four-term form uses \(\tfrac12\) regardless, and then the operation is not an involution. That is one of several places where \(n = 4\) is quietly special.

4. \(h = \eta^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu} = -h_{00} + h_{ii} = 2\Phi/c^2 - 6\Phi/c^2 = -4\Phi/c^2\). Then \(\bar h_{00} = h_{00} - \tfrac12(-1)h = -2\Phi/c^2 - 2\Phi/c^2 = -4\Phi/c^2\) and \(\bar h_{ij} = -2\Phi\delta_{ij}/c^2 - \tfrac12\delta_{ij}(-4\Phi/c^2) = 0\).

Check. Part 4 reproduces unit 35: only \(\bar h_{00} = -4\Phi/c^2\) survives, which is the deck's static check of the retarded solution. And \(\bar h = -\bar h_{00} = 4\Phi/c^2 = -h\) ✓, as part 2 says for \(n = 4\). Dimensions: \(\Phi/c^2\) is dimensionless ✓.

P2 Proof: curvature does not see the gauge medium

exercises: gauge invariance by Clairaut, the linearised Riemann tensor, geodesic deviation in TT gauge

The linearised Riemann tensor, with all indices down, is

\[R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} = \tfrac12\big(\partial_\nu\partial_\rho h_{\mu\sigma} + \partial_\mu\partial_\sigma h_{\nu\rho} - \partial_\mu\partial_\rho h_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\partial_\sigma h_{\mu\rho}\big).\]
  1. Prove that it is unchanged by \(h_{\mu\nu}\to h_{\mu\nu} + \partial_\mu\xi_\nu + \partial_\nu\xi_\mu\) for any smooth \(\xi\).
  2. Compute \(R_{i0j0}\) for a wave in TT gauge, and show that unit 33's geodesic deviation equation, \(\ddot\xi^i = -c^2R^i{}_{0j0}\,\xi^j\) for slowly moving masses, becomes \(\ddot\xi^i = \tfrac12\ddot h_{ij}\xi^j\).
  3. Compute \(R_{i0j0}\) instead for the static Newtonian field \(h_{00} = -2\Phi/c^2\) (other components irrelevant for this component — say why), and recover unit 05's tidal equation.
  4. The electromagnetic analogue: prove that \(\vec E = -\nabla\varphi - \partial_t\vec A\) and \(\vec B = \nabla\times\vec A\) are unchanged by unit 24's gauge transformation. Name the one theorem both proofs use.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Part 1 is a cancellation: the change in \(R\) is a sum of eight terms, each a third derivative of \(\xi\). Show they cancel in pairs. Parts 2 and 3 are substitutions; part 4 is the same argument with fewer indices.

Rung 2

Write \(\delta h_{\mu\sigma} = \partial_\mu\xi_\sigma + \partial_\sigma\xi_\mu\) and substitute into the first term: \(\partial_\nu\partial_\rho\partial_\mu\xi_\sigma + \partial_\nu\partial_\rho\partial_\sigma\xi_\mu\). Do the same for the other three and look for each third derivative with its opposite sign. For part 2, which components of \(h\) vanish in TT gauge?

Rung 3

In TT gauge \(h_{0\mu} = 0\), so in \(R_{i0j0} = \tfrac12(\partial_0\partial_jh_{i0} + \partial_i\partial_0h_{0j} - \partial_i\partial_jh_{00} - \partial_0\partial_0h_{ij})\) three terms die, leaving \(R_{i0j0} = -\tfrac12\partial_0^2h_{ij}\).

Worked solution

Idea. Gauge invariance of a field strength is always Clairaut: the gauge part of the potential is a derivative, and the field strength is an antisymmetrised derivative, so second (here third) derivatives meet with opposite signs.

1. The four terms of \(2\,\delta R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\) are

\[\begin{aligned} &\partial_\nu\partial_\rho(\partial_\mu\xi_\sigma + \partial_\sigma\xi_\mu) + \partial_\mu\partial_\sigma(\partial_\nu\xi_\rho + \partial_\rho\xi_\nu)\\ &- \partial_\mu\partial_\rho(\partial_\nu\xi_\sigma + \partial_\sigma\xi_\nu) - \partial_\nu\partial_\sigma(\partial_\mu\xi_\rho + \partial_\rho\xi_\mu). \end{aligned}\]

Pair them by which component of \(\xi\) is differentiated: \(\partial_\nu\partial_\rho\partial_\mu\xi_\sigma\) against \(-\partial_\mu\partial_\rho\partial_\nu\xi_\sigma\); \(\partial_\nu\partial_\rho\partial_\sigma\xi_\mu\) against \(-\partial_\nu\partial_\sigma\partial_\rho\xi_\mu\); \(\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma\partial_\nu\xi_\rho\) against \(-\partial_\nu\partial_\sigma\partial_\mu\xi_\rho\); \(\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma\partial_\rho\xi_\nu\) against \(-\partial_\mu\partial_\rho\partial_\sigma\xi_\nu\). Each pair is the same third derivative with the order of differentiation changed, and cancels by Clairaut's theorem (unit 03) for \(C^3\) functions. \(\square\)

2. With \(h_{0\mu} = 0\), \(R_{i0j0} = -\tfrac12\partial_0^2h_{ij} = -\frac{1}{2c^2}\ddot h_{ij}\). Raising \(i\) with \(\delta\) changes nothing, so \(\ddot\xi^i = -c^2R^i{}_{0j0}\xi^j = \tfrac12\ddot h_{ij}\xi^j\). Integrating twice with \(\xi = \xi_0\) when \(h = 0\) and keeping first order, \(\xi^i = \xi_0^i + \tfrac12h_{ij}\xi_0^j\): the deck's proper-distance result, now from a gauge-invariant equation.

3. \(R_{i0j0}\) involves \(h_{i0}\), \(h_{0j}\) (differentiated in time), \(h_{00}\) and \(h_{ij}\) (differentiated twice in time). For a static field the time derivatives vanish, so only \(-\tfrac12\partial_i\partial_jh_{00}\) survives, whatever \(h_{ij}\) is. With \(h_{00} = -2\Phi/c^2\): \(R_{i0j0} = \partial_i\partial_j\Phi/c^2\), and the deviation equation reads \(\ddot\xi^i = -(\partial_i\partial_j\Phi)\xi^j\) — unit 05's tidal equation.

4. \(\varphi\to\varphi - \partial_t\chi\), \(\vec A\to\vec A + \nabla\chi\). Then \(\vec E\) changes by \(\nabla\partial_t\chi - \partial_t\nabla\chi = 0\) and \(\vec B\) by \(\nabla\times\nabla\chi\), whose components are \(\partial_j\partial_k\chi - \partial_k\partial_j\chi = 0\). Both proofs are Clairaut's theorem, and nothing else.

Check. Dimensions in part 2: \([R] = \mathrm{m^{-2}}\), and \(\ddot h/c^2\) is \(\mathrm{s^{-2}}/(\mathrm{m^2s^{-2}}) = \mathrm{m^{-2}}\) ✓. Sign in part 3: two masses separated along the line to a point mass (\(\partial_r^2\Phi = -2GM/r^3 \lt 0\)) accelerate apart, the tidal stretch ✓ — which fixes the sign in part 2 too, since both come from one formula.

P3 Count to two medium

exercises: the degree-of-freedom count done by hand, residual gauge, finding the TT representative

A vacuum plane wave along \(z\) in Lorenz gauge, \(\bar h_{\mu\nu} = e_{\mu\nu}\cos(k\cdot x)\), with units chosen so that \(\omega/c = 1\): \(k_\mu = (-1, 0, 0, 1)\).

  1. Write out the four Lorenz conditions \(k^\mu e_{\mu\nu} = 0\) and show they leave six free components. Say which six you choose as free.
  2. A residual gauge transformation \(\xi_\mu = \epsilon_\mu\sin(k\cdot x)\) changes \(e\) by \(p_{\mu\nu} = k_\mu\epsilon_\nu + k_\nu\epsilon_\mu - \eta_{\mu\nu}(k\cdot\epsilon)\). Show that \(\Box\xi = 0\), write out the ten components of \(p\), and identify the two combinations of \(e\) that no \(\epsilon\) can change.
  3. Take \[e = \begin{pmatrix}2&1&0&-2\\1&3&4&-1\\0&4&1&0\\-2&-1&0&2\end{pmatrix}.\] Check that it satisfies the Lorenz conditions, then find \(\epsilon\) bringing it to TT gauge, and read off \(h_+\), \(h_\times\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

The answer to part 3 is a four-vector \(\epsilon\) and two numbers. The count in parts 1–2 is \(10 - 4 = 6\), then \(6 - 4 = 2\): make each stage explicit.

Rung 2

Raise the index on \(k\): \(k^\mu = (1, 0, 0, 1)\). So \(k^\mu e_{\mu\nu} = e_{0\nu} + e_{3\nu}\). And \(k\cdot\epsilon = k^\mu\epsilon_\mu = \epsilon_0 + \epsilon_3\). With these, \(p_{11} = p_{22} = -(\epsilon_0 + \epsilon_3)\) and \(p_{12} = 0\).

Rung 3

TT needs \(e_{11} + p_{11} = -(e_{22} + p_{22})\), i.e. \(3 - s = -(1 - s)\) with \(s = \epsilon_0 + \epsilon_3\): so \(s = 2\). Then set \(e_{01} + p_{01} = 0\) with \(p_{01} = k_0\epsilon_1 = -\epsilon_1\).

Worked solution

Idea. Do the deck's linear algebra in coordinates: the Lorenz conditions cut the ten components to six, the four residual gauge parameters move four of those six, and the two they cannot move are the polarisations.

1. \(k^\mu e_{\mu\nu} = e_{0\nu} + e_{3\nu} = 0\) for \(\nu = 0,1,2,3\): \(e_{00} + e_{30} = 0\), \(e_{01} + e_{31} = 0\), \(e_{02} + e_{32} = 0\), \(e_{03} + e_{33} = 0\). Four independent conditions (each involves a component of the third row the others do not), so six free components remain. Choose \(e_{00}, e_{01}, e_{02}, e_{11}, e_{12}, e_{22}\); then \(e_{30} = -e_{00}\), \(e_{31} = -e_{01}\), \(e_{32} = -e_{02}\) and \(e_{33} = -e_{03} = e_{00}\).

2. \(\Box\xi_\mu = \epsilon_\mu\Box\sin(k\cdot x) = -\epsilon_\mu(k^\alpha k_\alpha)\sin(k\cdot x) = 0\) since \(k^\alpha k_\alpha = -1 + 1 = 0\). With \(s = \epsilon_0 + \epsilon_3\) and \(\eta = \mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,1)\):

\[\begin{aligned} p_{00} &= -2\epsilon_0 + s, & p_{01} &= -\epsilon_1, & p_{02} &= -\epsilon_2, & p_{03} &= \epsilon_0 - \epsilon_3,\\ p_{11} &= -s, & p_{22} &= -s, & p_{12} &= 0, & p_{33} &= 2\epsilon_3 - s,\\ p_{13} &= \epsilon_1, & p_{23} &= \epsilon_2 . \end{aligned}\]

On the six free components, \((p_{00}, p_{01}, p_{02}, p_{11})\) can be set to anything by choosing \((\epsilon_0,\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2,\epsilon_3)\) — four independent shifts. But \(p_{11} = p_{22}\) and \(p_{12} = 0\) always, so \(\tfrac12(e_{11} - e_{22})\) and \(e_{12}\) are unchanged by every residual transformation: these are \(h_+\) and \(h_\times\). Six minus four is two.

3. Lorenz: \(e_{00} + e_{30} = 2 - 2 = 0\), \(e_{01} + e_{31} = 1 - 1 = 0\), \(e_{02} + e_{32} = 0\), \(e_{03} + e_{33} = -2 + 2 = 0\) ✓. For TT:

  • traceless spatial block: \(3 - s = -(1 - s)\), so \(s = 2\);
  • \(e_{01} + p_{01} = 1 - \epsilon_1 = 0\), so \(\epsilon_1 = 1\); \(e_{02} + p_{02} = -\epsilon_2 = 0\), so \(\epsilon_2 = 0\);
  • \(e_{00} + p_{00} = 2 - 2\epsilon_0 + 2 = 0\), so \(\epsilon_0 = 2\), and \(\epsilon_3 = s - \epsilon_0 = 0\).

So \(\epsilon = (2, 1, 0, 0)\). The rest follow without further choice: \(e_{03} + p_{03} = -2 + 2 = 0\), \(e_{33} + p_{33} = 2 - 2 = 0\), \(e_{13} + p_{13} = -1 + 1 = 0\), \(e_{23} + p_{23} = 0\). The TT amplitude is

\[e^{\rm TT} = \begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0\\0&1&4&0\\0&4&-1&0\\0&0&0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad h_+ = 1,\quad h_\times = 4.\]

Check. The invariants of part 2 computed from the original \(e\): \(\tfrac12(3 - 1) = 1\) and \(e_{12} = 4\) ✓ — the TT representative was determined before any \(\epsilon\) was found, which is the theorem \(L = P\oplus TT\). And the TT projector applied to the original spatial block, \(\Lambda(S)\) for \(n = \hat z\), gives \(\tfrac12(S_{11} - S_{22}) = 1\) and \(S_{12} = 4\) ✓. The residual freedom named at each stage: four functions to reach Lorenz (not needed here, since \(e\) was given in Lorenz gauge), four constants \(\epsilon_\mu\) — one plane-wave \(\xi\) with \(\Box\xi = 0\) — to reach TT, and after that none: \(\epsilon\) was unique.

Polarisations

P4 A binary seen from any angle medium

exercises: the TT projector in a rotated basis, the quadrupole formula, inclination dependence

The binary of worked example 2 orbits in the \(xy\) plane, and is seen by an observer at distance \(D\) in the direction \(n = (0, \sin\iota, \cos\iota)\), where \(\iota\) is the inclination.

  1. Show that \(e_1 = (1,0,0)\) and \(e_2 = (0, \cos\iota, -\sin\iota)\) form an orthonormal basis of \(n^\perp\), and that for a symmetric \(S\) the TT projection has components \(h_+ = \tfrac12(e_1^{\mathsf T}Se_1 - e_2^{\mathsf T}Se_2)\) and \(h_\times = e_1^{\mathsf T}Se_2\) in that basis (times the prefactor).
  2. Using \(\ddot Q_{11} = -\ddot Q_{22} = -2\mu a^2\Omega^2\cos2\Omega t\) and \(\ddot Q_{12} = -2\mu a^2\Omega^2\sin2\Omega t\), show \[h_+ = -h_0\,\frac{1 + \cos^2\iota}2\cos2\Omega t_{\rm r},\qquad h_\times = -h_0\cos\iota\,\sin2\Omega t_{\rm r},\qquad h_0 = \frac{4G\mu a^2\Omega^2}{c^4D}.\]
  3. Describe the polarisation seen face-on (\(\iota = 0\)), edge-on (\(\iota = 90^\circ\)), and at \(\iota = 60^\circ\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Two functions of time with angle-dependent amplitudes. The projector is basis-free, so work in the basis \((e_1, e_2, n)\), where it looks exactly as it did for \(n = \hat z\).

Rung 2

\(Q\) has no \(z\) components (the orbit is planar). So \(e_2^{\mathsf T}\ddot Qe_2 = \cos^2\iota\,\ddot Q_{22}\) and \(e_1^{\mathsf T}\ddot Qe_2 = \cos\iota\,\ddot Q_{12}\).

Rung 3

\(h_+ = \frac{2G}{c^4D}\cdot\tfrac12\big(\ddot Q_{11} - \cos^2\iota\,\ddot Q_{22}\big) = \frac{G}{c^4D}\ddot Q_{11}(1 + \cos^2\iota)\), using \(\ddot Q_{22} = -\ddot Q_{11}\).

Worked solution

Idea. The projector \(\Lambda(n)\) is a linear map defined without a basis; choose the basis adapted to \(n\) and it takes the form found for \(n = \hat z\). Then only the components of \(\ddot Q\) in the plane of the sky matter.

1. \(e_1\cdot e_1 = 1\), \(e_2\cdot e_2 = \cos^2\iota + \sin^2\iota = 1\), \(e_1\cdot e_2 = 0\), \(e_1\cdot n = 0\), \(e_2\cdot n = \sin\iota\cos\iota - \cos\iota\sin\iota = 0\). So \((e_1, e_2, n)\) is orthonormal. In it \(P = 1 - nn^{\mathsf T}\) is \(\mathrm{diag}(1,1,0)\), and the deck's computation for \(n = \hat z\) applies verbatim: \(\Lambda(S)\) has \(\tfrac12(S_{e_1e_1} - S_{e_2e_2})\) and \(S_{e_1e_2}\) in the \(+\) and \(\times\) slots, where \(S_{uv} = u^{\mathsf T}Sv\).

2. \(e_1^{\mathsf T}\ddot Qe_1 = \ddot Q_{11}\); \(e_2^{\mathsf T}\ddot Qe_2 = \cos^2\iota\,\ddot Q_{22}\) (the \(\sin\iota\) parts multiply \(z\) components, which vanish); \(e_1^{\mathsf T}\ddot Qe_2 = \cos\iota\,\ddot Q_{12}\). With the prefactor \(2G/c^4D\):

\[h_+ = \frac{G}{c^4D}\big(\ddot Q_{11} - \cos^2\iota\,\ddot Q_{22}\big) = \frac{G}{c^4D}(1 + \cos^2\iota)\,\ddot Q_{11} = -\frac{4G\mu a^2\Omega^2}{c^4D}\cdot\frac{1 + \cos^2\iota}{2}\cos2\Omega t_{\rm r},\] \[h_\times = \frac{2G}{c^4D}\cos\iota\,\ddot Q_{12} = -\frac{4G\mu a^2\Omega^2}{c^4D}\cos\iota\,\sin2\Omega t_{\rm r}.\]

3. Face-on: \(h_+ = -h_0\cos2\Omega t\), \(h_\times = -h_0\sin2\Omega t\): equal amplitudes a quarter period apart, circular polarisation (P5). Edge-on: \(h_+ = -\tfrac12h_0\cos2\Omega t\), \(h_\times = 0\): linear polarisation with half the amplitude, the plus pattern aligned with the orbital plane. At \(\iota = 60^\circ\): amplitudes \(0.625\,h_0\) and \(0.500\,h_0\), elliptical polarisation.

Check. \(\iota = 0\) reproduces the deck's axial wave ✓. \(\iota\to180^\circ\) (seen from below) gives the same \(h_+\) and \(h_\times\to+h_0\sin2\Omega t\): the rotation sense reverses, as it must when the orbit is viewed from the other side ✓. Dimensions: \(G\mu a^2\Omega^2/c^4D\) is \(\mathrm J/(\mathrm N\cdot\mathrm m) = 1\) ✓. Measuring both amplitudes gives \(\iota\), which is how an observed binary's inclination is found — and why the inclination and the distance are hard to separate when \(\iota\) is near \(0\).

P5 A ring under circular polarisation routine

exercises: the ring of free masses, helicity 2, reading a wave as a rotating pattern

A ring of free masses of radius \(\rho_0\) lies in the \(xy\) plane, and a wave travels along \(z\) with \(h_+ = h\cos\omega t\), \(h_\times = h\sin\omega t\).

  1. Write the proper displacement \(\delta x^i = \tfrac12h_{ij}x_0^j\) of the mass at angle \(\phi\), and show that the ring is always an ellipse with semi-axes \(\rho_0(1 \pm \tfrac12h)\).
  2. Find the direction of the major axis as a function of time, and the angular speed at which it turns.
  3. The wave comes from a binary seen face-on, so \(\omega = 2\Omega\). Compare the ellipse's angular speed with the binary's. Which particle, if any, moves round the ring?
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

The answer is a rotating ellipse: find its shape once and then its orientation angle as a function of \(t\). The spin-two slide's theorem does most of the work.

Rung 2

The matrix \(\begin{pmatrix}\cos\omega t&\sin\omega t\\\sin\omega t&-\cos\omega t\end{pmatrix}\) is symmetric with eigenvalues \(\pm1\). Find its eigenvector for \(+1\).

Rung 3

The eigenvector for \(+1\) is \((\cos\tfrac{\omega t}2, \sin\tfrac{\omega t}2)\): check with the double-angle formulas.

Worked solution

Idea. \(h_{ij}\) is \(h\) times a symmetric, traceless, orthogonal-like matrix with eigenvalues \(\pm1\); its eigenvectors turn at half the wave's angular frequency, because the wave has helicity 2.

1. \(h_{ij} = hH(t)\) with \(H = \begin{pmatrix}\cos\omega t&\sin\omega t\\\sin\omega t&-\cos\omega t\end{pmatrix}\). For \(x_0 = \rho_0(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)\): \(\delta x = \tfrac12h\rho_0\cos(\omega t - \phi)\), \(\delta y = \tfrac12h\rho_0\sin(\omega t - \phi)\), using the angle-difference formulas. \(H\) is symmetric with trace 0 and determinant \(-1\), so its eigenvalues are \(\pm1\), with orthonormal eigenvectors \(u_\pm\). In that basis \(x_0 + \delta x = (1 + \tfrac12hH)x_0\) scales the \(u_+\) component by \(1 + \tfrac12h\) and the \(u_-\) component by \(1 - \tfrac12h\): an ellipse with those semi-axes (times \(\rho_0\)).

2. \(Hu = u\) with \(u = (\cos\alpha,\sin\alpha)\): \(\cos\omega t\cos\alpha + \sin\omega t\sin\alpha = \cos(\omega t - \alpha)\) must equal \(\cos\alpha\), and \(\sin\omega t\cos\alpha - \cos\omega t\sin\alpha = \sin(\omega t - \alpha)\) must equal \(\sin\alpha\). Both hold for \(\alpha = \omega t/2\). So the major axis points along \(\omega t/2\) and turns at angular speed \(\omega/2\).

3. \(\omega/2 = \Omega\): the ellipse turns with the binary, its long axis following the line joining the stars (delayed by \(D/c\), and up to a sign convention in the phase). No mass moves round the ring: each mass moves on a small circle of radius \(\tfrac12h\rho_0\) about its rest position, at angular speed \(\omega\) — part 1's \(\delta x, \delta y\) — and the rotation is of the pattern, like a wave on a stadium crowd.

Multiply by \(y^i\) and integrate: \(\dot D^i = -\int y^i\partial_k(\rho v^k) = \int\delta^i_k\rho v^k = P^i\), since \(\partial_k(y^i\rho v^k) = \delta^i_k\rho v^k + y^i\partial_k(\rho v^k)\) and the divergence integrates to zero. \(\nu = i\): \(\partial_0T^{0i} + \partial_kT^{ki} = 0\), i.e. \(\partial_t(\rho v^i) = -\partial_kT^{ki}\); integrate: \(\dot P^i = 0\). The gravitational "string" pulling the stars together is exactly what restores it — the deck's warning slide, verified. Idea. The quadrupole chirp gives the order of magnitude of everything LIGO measured; where it disagrees it is by the known factors — orientation, and the breakdown near merger.
