The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: the Planck universe — flat FLRW with dust and a cosmological
constant, \(\Omega_{\mathrm m} = 0.315\), \(\Omega_\Lambda = 0.685\), \(H_0 =
67.4\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}Mpc^{-1}}\), so \(1/H_0 = 14.51\) Gyr. Its age is
\(t_0 = \frac{2}{3H_0\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}}\operatorname{arsinh}
\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda/\Omega_{\mathrm m}} = 13.80\) Gyr (13.79 with radiation);
matter and \(\Lambda\) were equally dense at \(z = 0.296\), 3.50 Gyr ago, and the
expansion began to accelerate at \(z = 0.632\).

* The assumption, and the geometry it forces

- cosmological principle :: The assumption that, on scales above roughly
  100 Mpc, space at each instant of cosmic time is homogeneous and isotropic
  about every fundamental observer. An assumption, not a result: isotropy is
  observed from here, homogeneity is inferred by adding that we are not special.
  /Slide: The cosmological principle./
- fundamental observers :: The family of observers moving with the mean motion
  of matter; operationally, those who see no dipole in the cosmic microwave
  background. Their worldlines are the curves of constant comoving coordinates.
  /Slide: The cosmological principle./
- homogeneous :: Of a Riemannian manifold: for any two points there is an
  isometry carrying one to the other. No point is special.
  /Slide: The cosmological principle./
- isotropic :: About a point \(p\): every rotation of \(T_p\Sigma\) is the
  differential of an isometry fixing \(p\). No direction is special.
  /Slide: The cosmological principle./
- maximally symmetric space :: A Riemannian \(n\)-manifold whose Killing fields
  form an algebra of the largest possible dimension, \(n(n+1)/2\); equivalently,
  homogeneous and isotropic about every point. Its curvature is
  \(R_{ijkl} = K(\gamma_{ik}\gamma_{jl} - \gamma_{il}\gamma_{jk})\) with \(K\)
  constant — a theorem, proved from isotropy, polarisation and homogeneity.
  /Slide: Maximally symmetric spaces./
- FLRW metric :: \(\mathrm ds^2 = -c^2\mathrm dt^2 + a(t)^2[\mathrm dr^2/(1-kr^2)
  + r^2\mathrm d\Omega^2]\), the only metric compatible with the cosmological
  principle. Named for Friedmann, Lemaître, Robertson and Walker.
  /Slide: The FLRW metric, and why there is no other./
- cosmic time :: The coordinate \(t\) in the FLRW metric: proper time along every
  fundamental observer, and the label of the homogeneous slices.
  /Slide: The FLRW metric, and why there is no other./
- comoving coordinates :: Spatial coordinates \((r,\theta,\phi)\) or
  \((\chi,\theta,\phi)\) that each fundamental observer keeps fixed for all time.
  In this course they carry units of length.
  /Slide: The FLRW metric, and why there is no other./
- scale factor :: The function \(a(t)\) in the FLRW metric, dimensionless, with
  \(a = 1\) today. Proper distances between fundamental observers are proportional
  to it. /Slide: The FLRW metric, and why there is no other./
- curvature constant :: The number \(k\) in the FLRW metric, in
  \(\mathrm{m^{-2}}\): the sectional curvature of the comoving space. Positive,
  zero or negative for the 3-sphere, Euclidean and hyperbolic cases; only its sign
  is qualitative. /Slide: The FLRW metric, and why there is no other./
- proper distance :: The length of the radial geodesic to a comoving object within
  one slice of constant cosmic time: \(d(t) = a(t)\chi\).
  /Slide: Proper distance, Hubble's law, conformal time./
- Hubble parameter :: \(H = \dot a/a\), in \(\mathrm s^{-1}\). Its present value
  is the Hubble constant \(H_0\); for the running example \(67.4\
  \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}Mpc^{-1}} = 2.184\times10^{-18}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\).
  /Slide: Proper distance, Hubble's law, conformal time./
- Hubble's law :: \(\dot d = Hd\) for proper distance, exact in FLRW because
  \(\chi\) is constant. At small redshift it becomes the observed \(cz \approx
  H_0d\). /Slide: Proper distance, Hubble's law, conformal time./
- conformal time :: \(\eta\) with \(\mathrm d\eta = \mathrm dt/a\). In it the
  FLRW metric is \(a^2\) times a static metric, radial light rays are \(45°\)
  lines, and the particle horizon is \(c\eta\).
  /Slide: Proper distance, Hubble's law, conformal time./

* Curvature of an expanding space

- Friedmann equations :: \(H^2 = \frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho - \frac{kc^2}{a^2} +
  \frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}\) and \(\frac{\ddot a}{a} = -\frac{4\pi G}{3}(\rho +
  3p/c^2) + \frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}\): the \(00\) and \(ij\) components of Einstein's
  equation for the FLRW metric with a comoving perfect fluid.
  /Slide: The Friedmann equations./
- acceleration equation :: The second Friedmann equation. Pressure appears as
  \(\rho + 3p/c^2\): positive pressure decelerates, and anything with \(p \lt
  -\rho c^2/3\) accelerates. /Slide: The Friedmann equations./
- equation of state :: A relation \(p = w\rho c^2\). With \(w\) constant the fluid
  equation \(\dot\rho + 3H(\rho + p/c^2) = 0\) gives \(\rho \propto a^{-3(1+w)}\):
  \(w = 0\) matter, \(\tfrac13\) radiation, \(-1\) a cosmological constant.
  /Slide: Equation of state, and how each component dilutes./

* Friedmann as an energy equation

- Einstein static universe :: Einstein's 1917 model: dust with \(\dot a = \ddot a
  = 0\), requiring \(\Lambda = 4\pi G\rho/c^2\) and closed space with \(k/a^2 =
  \Lambda\). It sits at the maximum of the effective potential and is unstable.
  /Slide: What the potential says./
- critical density :: \(\rho_{\mathrm c} = 3H^2/8\pi G\), the total density for
  which space is flat. Today \(8.53\times10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}\), about
  five protons per cubic metre.
  /Slide: Critical density and the density parameters./
- density parameter :: \(\Omega_i = \rho_{i,0}/\rho_{\mathrm c,0}\) for each
  component, with \(\Omega_\Lambda = \Lambda c^2/3H_0^2\) and \(\Omega_k =
  -kc^2/H_0^2\); they sum to \(1\).
  /Slide: Critical density and the density parameters./
- deceleration parameter :: \(q = -\ddot aa/\dot a^2\). Today \(q_0 =
  \tfrac12\Omega_{\mathrm m} + \Omega_{\mathrm r} - \Omega_\Lambda\), which is
  \(-0.53\) for the running example: the expansion accelerates.
  /Slide: Critical density and the density parameters./

* Solving it

- Einstein-de Sitter universe :: Flat, matter only: \(a = (\tfrac32H_0t)^{2/3}\),
  age \(2/3H_0\) (9.67 Gyr for \(H_0 = 67.4\)). The standard model before 1998.
  /Slide: Three pure universes./
- de Sitter universe :: Flat, \(\Lambda\) only: \(H\) constant, \(a =
  e^{H(t-t_0)}\), no beginning and a constant event horizon at proper distance
  \(c/H\). /Slide: Three pure universes./
- matter–radiation equality :: The moment \(\Omega_{\mathrm r}a^{-4} =
  \Omega_{\mathrm m}a^{-3}\): \(z = 3420\), about 50 000 years after the Big Bang.
  /Slide: Four histories through today./
- matter–Λ equality :: The moment \(\Omega_{\mathrm m}a^{-3} = \Omega_\Lambda\):
  \(z = 0.296\), 3.50 Gyr ago for the running example. Acceleration began earlier,
  at \(z = 0.632\), when matter was twice \(\Lambda\).
  /Slide: Four histories through today./

* What light tells us

- cosmological redshift :: \(1 + z = \lambda_0/\lambda_e = a(t_0)/a(t_e)\) for
  light between comoving source and observer: wavelengths stretch with space. Not
  a single Doppler shift, though it is a product of infinitesimal ones.
  /Slide: Cosmological redshift./
- particle horizon :: The comoving distance light has covered since \(t = 0\),
  \(\int_0^tc\,\mathrm dt'/a\). Finite whenever the early expansion decelerates;
  46.1 Gly (proper) today for the running example. /Slide: Horizons./
- event horizon :: The comoving distance light emitted now will ever cover,
  \(\int_t^\infty c\,\mathrm dt'/a\). Finite only if the expansion accelerates;
  16.7 Gly today for the running example. /Slide: Horizons./
- distance ladder :: The chain parallax, Cepheids, Type Ia supernovae, Hubble flow,
  each rung calibrating the next, by which \(H_0\) is measured directly.
  /Slide: The distance ladder./
- standard candle :: A class of source of known luminosity \(L\), whose distance
  follows from its flux: \(F = L/4\pi d_L^2\), with \(d_L = (1+z)\chi\) in flat
  FLRW. /Slide: The distance ladder./
- Hubble tension :: The disagreement between \(H_0 = 67.4 \pm 0.5\) inferred from
  the CMB through the flat \(\Lambda\)CDM model and \(73.0 \pm 1.0\) from the
  distance ladder, about \(4.9\sigma\). Open. /Slide: The Hubble tension./

* The early universe: a success and two puzzles

- Big Bang nucleosynthesis :: The formation of light nuclei in the first few
  minutes. With neutron-to-proton ratio \(x \approx 1/7\) it predicts a helium mass
  fraction \(Y = 2x/(1+x) \approx 0.25\); measured \(0.245\).
  /Slide: Big Bang nucleosynthesis./
- horizon problem :: At recombination the particle horizon subtends about a
  degree on today's sky (1.16° for the running example, 1.79° in
  Einstein-de Sitter), yet the whole sky has one temperature to \(10^{-5}\): the
  uniformity of some \(10^4\) causally disconnected patches must be assumed.
  /Slide: The horizon problem./
- flatness problem :: \(|\Omega - 1| \propto a^{1+3w}\) grows in every
  decelerating era, so today's \(|\Omega_k| \lesssim 0.002\) requires
  \(|\Omega - 1| \lesssim 10^{-18}\) at nucleosynthesis.
  /Slide: The flatness problem./
- inflation :: A hypothesised early era of accelerated, nearly de Sitter
  expansion, during which the comoving Hubble length \(c/aH\) shrinks, addressing
  both problems. Its generic predictions agree with data; its mechanism is
  unidentified. /Slide: Inflation, and its status./
- e-fold :: A factor \(e\) of growth in \(a\). Inflation needs about 60 of them if
  it ended near \(10^{15}\) GeV. /Slide: Inflation, and its status./
