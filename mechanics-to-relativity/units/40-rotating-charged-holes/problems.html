Rotating and charged holes, and where this course stops

the slides · the curriculum

Checkpoint. You show that inside the Kerr ergosphere no timelike worldline can hold \(r\), \(\theta\) and \(\varphi\) constant — from \(g_{tt}\), with the surface \(r_{\mathrm E}(\theta)\) produced; you compute the Penrose process ceiling, \(1 - 1/\sqrt2 = 29.3\%\), from the irreducible mass and the horizon inequality; and you state the Penrose singularity theorem with its energy condition named explicitly, and name a matter content that violates that condition.

Work each problem on paper first. Under it, Hints opens a ladder one rung at a time — each rung appears only once the one before it is open, and the last gives the first line of the working — and Worked solution has the whole thing: the idea, the working, then a check. Read the solution after your attempt, including when you got it right. F folds everything back; D switches dark and light.

Twelve problems, about six hours. Conventions are the deck's: signature \((-,+,+,+)\), factors of \(c\) kept, \(t\) in seconds. Throughout, \(r_g = GM/c^2\), \(a = J/Mc\), \(a_* = a/r_g\), \(\Sigma = r^2 + a^2\cos^2\theta\), \(\Delta = r^2 - 2r_gr + a^2\), and the Kerr metric in Boyer–Lindquist coordinates is

\[\mathrm ds^2 = -\Big(1 - \frac{2r_gr}{\Sigma}\Big)c^2\mathrm dt^2 - \frac{4r_gra\sin^2\theta}{\Sigma}\,c\,\mathrm dt\,\mathrm d\varphi + \frac{\Sigma}{\Delta}\mathrm dr^2 + \Sigma\,\mathrm d\theta^2 + \Big(r^2+a^2+\frac{2r_gra^2\sin^2\theta}{\Sigma}\Big)\sin^2\theta\,\mathrm d\varphi^2 .\]

Two identities save a great deal of algebra and may be used freely (both are asked for in P3):

\[g_{\varphi\varphi} = \frac{\big[(r^2+a^2)^2 - \Delta a^2\sin^2\theta\big]\sin^2\theta}{\Sigma}, \qquad g_{t\varphi}^2 - g_{tt}g_{\varphi\varphi} = \Delta\,c^2\sin^2\theta .\]

Momenta are covector components: \(E = -p_t\) in joules, \(L = p_\varphi\) in \(\mathrm{J\,s}\), and \(\mathcal E := E/c\). Per unit mass, \(\hat E = E/\mu c^2\), \(\hat L = L/\mu c\), \(\hat Q = Q/\mu^2c^2\).

The running example, used in several problems: \(M = 10M_\odot\), \(a_* = 0.9\), so \(r_g = 14\,766\) m and \(a = 13\,290\) m.

Constants: \(c = 2.99792458\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), \(G = 6.67430\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm{m^3kg^{-1}s^{-2}}\), \(GM_\odot = 1.32712440018\times10^{20}\ \mathrm{m^3s^{-2}}\), \(M_\odot = 1.98841\times10^{30}\) kg, \(\hbar = 1.054572\times10^{-34}\) J s, \(k_{\mathrm B} = 1.380649\times10^{-23}\ \mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}\), \(\varepsilon_0 = 8.8541878\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{F\,m^{-1}}\), \(e = 1.6021766\times10^{-19}\) C, \(m_{\mathrm e} = 9.1093837\times10^{-31}\) kg, \(m_{\mathrm p} = 1.6726219\times10^{-27}\) kg.

Horizon geometry

P1 The parameters of one Kerr hole routine

exercises: reading the Kerr metric, the identity \(2r_gr_+ = r_+^2+a^2\), dimensional bookkeeping

For the running hole (\(M = 10M_\odot\), \(a_* = 0.9\)):

  1. Compute \(r_+\), \(r_-\) and the ergosurface radius \(r_{\mathrm E}(\theta)\) at \(\theta = 0, 30°, 60°, 90°\), in kilometres.
  2. Compute the horizon area \(A\), and the radius of a Euclidean sphere with the same area. Compare it with \(r_+\) and say which is larger and why that is not a contradiction.
  3. Compute \(\Omega_{\mathrm H}\) in rad/s and in Hz, and \(\kappa\) in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\).
  4. Show that the proper circumference of the horizon around the equator is \(4\pi r_g\), independent of \(a\), and evaluate it. (This is the one measurement of a Kerr horizon that does not know about its spin.)
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Four lengths, an area, a length, an angular velocity, an acceleration, and a length again — all of them numbers, none of them requiring anything beyond substitution into formulas on the slides. Part 4 is the only one with an idea in it, and the idea is that a circumference is an integral of \(\sqrt{g_{\varphi\varphi}}\).

Rung 2

Everywhere you meet \(r_+^2+a^2\), replace it by \(2r_gr_+\): that is the statement \(\Delta(r_+) = 0\), and it shortens every part. For part 4, the equatorial circle is \(r = r_+\), \(\theta = \pi/2\), \(\varphi\) from \(0\) to \(2\pi\), so its length is \(\int_0^{2\pi}\sqrt{g_{\varphi\varphi}}\,\mathrm d\varphi\).

Rung 3

First line: \(\sqrt{1-a_*^2} = \sqrt{0.19} = 0.435890\), so \(r_\pm = r_g(1\pm0.435890)\). For part 4, use the preamble's \(g_{\varphi\varphi}\) identity at \(\Delta = 0\) and \(\theta = \pi/2\), where \(\Sigma = r_+^2\): it collapses to \((r_+^2+a^2)^2/r_+^2\), whose square root is \((r_+^2+a^2)/r_+\).

Worked solution

Idea. Everything is built from two lengths, \(r_g\) and \(a\), and one identity, \(2r_gr_+ = r_+^2+a^2\).

1. \(r_g = GM/c^2 = 1.32712\times10^{21}/8.98755\times10^{16} = 14\,766\) m and \(a = 0.9r_g = 13\,290\) m. With \(\sqrt{1-0.81} = 0.435890\),

\[r_+ = 1.435890\,r_g = 21\,203\ \mathrm m = 21.20\ \mathrm{km},\qquad r_- = 0.564110\,r_g = 8\,330\ \mathrm m = 8.33\ \mathrm{km}.\]

For the ergosurface, \(r_{\mathrm E}(\theta) = r_g\big(1 + \sqrt{1 - a_*^2\cos^2\theta}\big)\):

  • \(\theta = 0\): \(\sqrt{0.19} = 0.43589\), \(r_{\mathrm E} = 1.4359\,r_g = 21.20\) km — the horizon itself.
  • \(\theta = 30°\): \(\cos^2 = 0.75\), \(\sqrt{1-0.6075} = 0.62650\), \(r_{\mathrm E} = 1.6265\,r_g = 24.02\) km.
  • \(\theta = 60°\): \(\cos^2 = 0.25\), \(\sqrt{1-0.2025} = 0.89303\), \(r_{\mathrm E} = 1.8930\,r_g = 27.95\) km.
  • \(\theta = 90°\): \(\cos = 0\), \(r_{\mathrm E} = 2r_g = 29.53\) km.

2. \(A = 4\pi(r_+^2+a^2) = 8\pi r_gr_+ = 8\pi(14766)(21203) = 7.869\times10^{9}\ \mathrm{m^2}\). A Euclidean sphere of that area has radius \(\sqrt{A/4\pi} = 25\,023\) m \(= 25.02\) km, which is larger than \(r_+ = 21.20\) km. No contradiction: \(r\) is Boyer–Lindquist's radial coordinate, not an areal radius. In Schwarzschild the two coincided; in Kerr they do not, because \(g_{\theta\theta} = \Sigma\) varies with \(\theta\) and the horizon is oblate.

3. \(r_+^2+a^2 = 2r_gr_+ = 6.2617\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{m^2}\), so

\[\Omega_{\mathrm H} = \frac{ac}{r_+^2+a^2} = \frac{(1.3290\times10^{4})(2.99792\times10^{8})}{6.2617\times10^{8}} = 6363\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}},\] which is \(6363/2\pi = 1013\) Hz. And with \(r_+ - r_- = 1.2873\times10^{4}\) m,

\[\kappa = \frac{c^2(r_+-r_-)}{2(r_+^2+a^2)} = \frac{(8.98755\times10^{16})(1.2873\times10^{4})}{1.25234\times10^{9}} = 9.238\times10^{11}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}} .\]

4. On \(r = r_+\), \(\theta = \pi/2\): \(\Delta = 0\) and \(\Sigma = r_+^2\), so the preamble's identity gives \(g_{\varphi\varphi} = (r_+^2+a^2)^2/r_+^2\) and

\[\ell = \int_0^{2\pi}\sqrt{g_{\varphi\varphi}}\,\mathrm d\varphi = 2\pi\,\frac{r_+^2+a^2}{r_+} = 2\pi\,\frac{2r_gr_+}{r_+} = 4\pi r_g ,\] with \(a\) gone. Numerically \(4\pi(14766) = 1.8556\times10^{5}\) m \(= 185.6\) km.

Check. Dimensions: \(\Omega_{\mathrm H}\) is \(\mathrm{m\cdot m\,s^{-1}/m^2} = \mathrm{s^{-1}}\) ✓; \(\kappa\) is \(\mathrm{m^2s^{-2}\cdot m/m^2} = \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\) ✓. Limit \(a\to0\): \(r_+\to2r_g = r_s = 29.53\) km, \(r_-\to0\), \(r_{\mathrm E}\to r_s\) at every \(\theta\), \(A\to4\pi r_s^2 = 1.096\times10^{10}\ \mathrm{m^2}\), \(\Omega_{\mathrm H}\to0\), and \(\kappa\to c^4/4GM = 1.522\times10^{12}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\) ✓ — and the equatorial circumference \(4\pi r_g = 2\pi r_s\) ✓, as it must be for a round sphere. Special case: at \(\theta = 0\) the ergosurface coincides with \(r_+\), so the ergosphere has zero thickness on the axis ✓.

Another way

For part 2, avoid the area integral: the induced metric on the horizon is \(\Sigma\,\mathrm d\theta^2 + (r_+^2+a^2)^2\sin^2\theta\,\mathrm d\varphi^2/\Sigma\), whose determinant is \((r_+^2+a^2)^2\sin^2\theta\) with the \(\Sigma\) cancelling exactly. The area element is therefore \((r_+^2+a^2)\sin\theta\,\mathrm d\theta\,\mathrm d\varphi\), and the integral is immediate. That the \(\Sigma\) cancels is the reason \(A\) is so simple, and it is worth noticing rather than grinding through.

P2 The static limit, and the window of allowed rotations routine

exercises: timelike conditions on a Killing combination, the ergosphere, a quadratic in \(\Omega\)

  1. Show that a worldline of constant \(r, \theta, \varphi\) is timelike if and only if \(r^2 - 2r_gr + a^2\cos^2\theta \gt 0\), and hence derive \(r_{\mathrm E}(\theta) = r_g + \sqrt{r_g^2 - a^2\cos^2\theta}\).
  2. A stationary observer has \(u \propto \xi_\Omega = \partial_t + \Omega\partial_\varphi\) with \(\Omega\) constant. Show that such an observer exists exactly when \(\Omega_- \lt \Omega \lt \Omega_+\), and give \(\Omega_\pm\) in terms of the metric components.
  3. Evaluate \(\Omega_\pm\) at the point \(r = 2r_g\), \(\theta = \pi/2\) — that is, on the ergosurface at the equator — for the running hole, in rad/s. Interpret the lower value.
  4. Someone says: "inside the ergosphere nothing can escape." Say in two sentences what is true and what is not.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Parts 1 and 2 ask for inequalities, part 3 for two numbers in \(\mathrm{s^{-1}}\), part 4 for prose. The whole problem is one observation: "is this curve a possible worldline?" means "is its tangent timelike?", and the tangent is a combination of Killing fields with constant coefficients.

Rung 2

For part 2, \(g(\xi_\Omega,\xi_\Omega) = g_{tt} + 2\Omega g_{t\varphi} + \Omega^2g_{\varphi\varphi}\) is a quadratic in \(\Omega\) with positive leading coefficient, so it is negative between its roots and only there. For part 3, notice which of \(g_{tt}\), \(g_{t\varphi}\), \(g_{\varphi\varphi}\) is special at \(r = 2r_g\), \(\theta = \pi/2\).

Rung 3

First line of part 1: \(g_{tt} = -c^2(\Sigma - 2r_gr)/\Sigma\), and \(\Sigma - 2r_gr = r^2 - 2r_gr + a^2\cos^2\theta\). First line of part 3: \(r = 2r_g\), \(\theta = \pi/2\) is exactly the ergosurface, so \(g_{tt} = 0\) there and the quadratic factorises as \(\Omega(2g_{t\varphi} + \Omega g_{\varphi\varphi})\).

Worked solution

Idea. Every part is the timelike condition on \(\partial_t + \Omega\partial_\varphi\), with \(\Omega = 0\) in part 1 and \(\Omega\) free in parts 2 and 3.

1. A curve with \(r,\theta,\varphi\) constant has tangent proportional to \(\partial_t\), so it is timelike iff \(g(\partial_t,\partial_t) = g_{tt} \lt 0\). From the metric,

\[g_{tt} = -\Big(1 - \frac{2r_gr}{\Sigma}\Big)c^2 = -\frac{c^2}{\Sigma}\big(\Sigma - 2r_gr\big) = -\frac{c^2}{\Sigma}\big(r^2 - 2r_gr + a^2\cos^2\theta\big),\] and \(\Sigma \gt 0\) away from the ring, so \(g_{tt} \lt 0 \iff r^2 - 2r_gr + a^2\cos^2\theta \gt 0\). The quadratic in \(r\) has roots \(r_g \pm \sqrt{r_g^2 - a^2\cos^2\theta}\) and is positive outside them; the larger root is \(r_{\mathrm E}(\theta)\).

2. \(g(\xi_\Omega,\xi_\Omega) = g_{tt} + 2\Omega g_{t\varphi} + \Omega^2g_{\varphi\varphi}\). Since \(g_{\varphi\varphi} \gt 0\) outside the horizon, this parabola opens upwards and is negative exactly strictly between its roots,

\[\Omega_\pm = \frac{-g_{t\varphi}\pm\sqrt{g_{t\varphi}^2 - g_{tt}g_{\varphi\varphi}}}{g_{\varphi\varphi}} = \omega \pm \frac{c\sqrt\Delta\,\sin\theta}{g_{\varphi\varphi}}, \qquad \omega := -\frac{g_{t\varphi}}{g_{\varphi\varphi}},\] using the preamble's discriminant identity. Real roots need \(\Delta \ge 0\), i.e. \(r \ge r_+\), and the window closes at the horizon.

3. At \(r = 2r_g\), \(\theta = \pi/2\): \(\Sigma = 4r_g^2\), \(\Delta = 4r_g^2 - 4r_g^2 + a^2 = a^2\), and \(g_{tt} = 0\) (this is the ergosurface). Then

\[g_{t\varphi} = -\frac{2r_g(2r_g)a}{4r_g^2}c = -ac,\qquad g_{\varphi\varphi} = \frac{(4r_g^2+a^2)^2 - a^4}{4r_g^2} .\] With \(a = 0.9r_g\): \((4.81)^2 - 0.6561 = 23.1361 - 0.6561 = 22.480\), divided by \(4\), gives \(g_{\varphi\varphi} = 5.6200\,r_g^2\). Because \(g_{tt} = 0\) the quadratic factorises, \(\Omega(2g_{t\varphi} + \Omega g_{\varphi\varphi})\), so

\[\Omega_- = 0,\qquad \Omega_+ = -\frac{2g_{t\varphi}}{g_{\varphi\varphi}} = 2\omega = \frac{2(0.9)r_gc}{5.6200\,r_g^2} = 0.32028\,\frac{c}{r_g}.\] With \(c/r_g = 2.99792\times10^{8}/14766 = 2.0303\times10^{4}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\), that is \(\Omega_+ = 6.50\times10^{3}\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}\), and \(\omega = 3251\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}\). The lower value \(\Omega_- = 0\) is the static observer, who survives — just — precisely on the ergosurface and nowhere inside it.

4. True: inside the ergosphere every observer is dragged, \(\Omega \gt 0\), and no one can stand still relative to the distant stars. False: the ergosphere is outside the event horizon, and a rocket in it can leave and reach infinity. The static limit and the horizon are different surfaces, and they touch only at the poles.

Check. Dimensions: \(\Omega_\pm\) come from \(\sqrt{g_{t\varphi}^2}/g_{\varphi\varphi} = \mathrm{(m^2s^{-1})/m^2} = \mathrm{s^{-1}}\) ✓. Limit \(a\to0\): \(\omega\to0\) and \(\Omega_\pm \to \pm c\sqrt{f}/(r\sin\theta)\), symmetric about zero, so a static observer exists wherever \(f \gt 0\) ✓. Consistency with P1: at \(r = r_+\) the window shuts at \(\Omega_{\mathrm H} = 6363\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}\), which lies inside \((0, 6503)\) — the allowed band narrows as one descends, as it must ✓.

P3 Proof: the horizon rotates rigidly medium

exercises: metric algebra, \(\Delta(r_+) = 0\), what "rigid" means for a null surface

  1. Prove the preamble's identity \(g_{\varphi\varphi} = \big[(r^2+a^2)^2 - \Delta a^2\sin^2\theta\big]\sin^2\theta/\Sigma\) directly from the metric.
  2. Hence prove that \(\omega := -g_{t\varphi}/g_{\varphi\varphi}\) equals \(2r_gra\,c/\big[(r^2+a^2)^2 - \Delta a^2\sin^2\theta\big]\), and deduce that on \(r = r_+\) it takes the \(\theta\)-independent value \(\Omega_{\mathrm H} = ac/(r_+^2+a^2)\).
  3. Prove that \(\xi_{\mathrm H} = \partial_t + \Omega_{\mathrm H}\partial_\varphi\) is null on \(r = r_+\), using the discriminant identity rather than by direct substitution.
  4. Explain in three sentences why "rigid rotation" is a strong statement here: say what a rotating fluid body does instead, and which theorem says a stationary horizon has no choice.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Three algebraic identities and one paragraph. Nothing here needs curvature, Christoffel symbols or geodesics: it is the metric components and the single fact \(\Delta(r_+) = 0\).

Rung 2

For part 1, put the two terms of \(g_{\varphi\varphi}\) over the common denominator \(\Sigma\) and use \(\Sigma = r^2 + a^2 - a^2\sin^2\theta\). For part 3, recall that \(g(\xi_\Omega,\xi_\Omega) = g_{\varphi\varphi}(\Omega-\Omega_-)(\Omega-\Omega_+)\) and ask what the discriminant identity says about \(\Omega_\pm\) at \(\Delta = 0\).

Rung 3

First line of part 1: \(g_{\varphi\varphi}\Sigma/\sin^2\theta = (r^2+a^2)\Sigma + 2r_gra^2\sin^2\theta\), and now substitute \(\Sigma = r^2+a^2-a^2\sin^2\theta\) and \(2r_gr = r^2+a^2-\Delta\). First line of part 2: use part 1 and \(g_{t\varphi} = -2r_gra\sin^2\theta\,c/\Sigma\); the \(\Sigma\) and \(\sin^2\theta\) both cancel.

Worked solution

Idea. One substitution, \(2r_gr = r^2+a^2-\Delta\), does all three algebraic parts. It is just \(\Delta\)'s definition rearranged, and it is the only identity worth memorising about Kerr.

1. From the metric,

\[\frac{\Sigma\,g_{\varphi\varphi}}{\sin^2\theta} = (r^2+a^2)\Sigma + 2r_gra^2\sin^2\theta .\] Substitute \(\Sigma = r^2+a^2-a^2\sin^2\theta\) in the first term and \(2r_gr = r^2+a^2-\Delta\) in the second:

\[= (r^2+a^2)^2 - (r^2+a^2)a^2\sin^2\theta + (r^2+a^2)a^2\sin^2\theta - \Delta a^2\sin^2\theta = (r^2+a^2)^2 - \Delta a^2\sin^2\theta .\] The two middle terms cancel exactly, which is the whole content of the identity. \(\square\)

2. \(g_{t\varphi} = -2r_gra\sin^2\theta\,c/\Sigma\), so

\[\omega = -\frac{g_{t\varphi}}{g_{\varphi\varphi}} = \frac{2r_gra\sin^2\theta\,c/\Sigma}{\big[(r^2+a^2)^2-\Delta a^2\sin^2\theta\big]\sin^2\theta/\Sigma} = \frac{2r_gra\,c}{(r^2+a^2)^2 - \Delta a^2\sin^2\theta},\] with both \(\Sigma\) and \(\sin^2\theta\) cancelling — so \(\omega\) has no explicit \(\theta\) in it once written this way, only through \(r\). On \(r = r_+\), \(\Delta = 0\), so the denominator is \((r_+^2+a^2)^2\) and the numerator is \(2r_gr_+ac = (r_+^2+a^2)ac\), giving

\[\omega\big|_{r_+} = \frac{(r_+^2+a^2)ac}{(r_+^2+a^2)^2} = \frac{ac}{r_+^2+a^2} = \Omega_{\mathrm H},\] independent of \(\theta\). \(\square\)

3. \(g(\xi_\Omega,\xi_\Omega)\) is a quadratic in \(\Omega\) with roots \(\Omega_\pm = \omega \pm c\sqrt\Delta\sin\theta/g_{\varphi\varphi}\), so it factorises as \(g_{\varphi\varphi}(\Omega-\Omega_-)(\Omega-\Omega_+)\). At \(\Delta = 0\) the discriminant identity gives \(g_{t\varphi}^2 - g_{tt}g_{\varphi\varphi} = 0\), so the roots coincide at \(\Omega_- = \Omega_+ = \omega|_{r_+} = \Omega_{\mathrm H}\). Evaluating the quadratic at \(\Omega = \Omega_{\mathrm H}\) therefore gives \(g_{\varphi\varphi}(\Omega_{\mathrm H}-\Omega_{\mathrm H})^2 = 0\): \(\xi_{\mathrm H}\) is null. \(\square\) It is non-vanishing there (its \(\partial_t\) component is 1), so \(r = r_+\) is a Killing horizon of it.

4. A self-gravitating fluid in equilibrium cannot rotate rigidly unless it is specially prepared: differential rotation is the generic state, because different shells feel different effective gravity, and the Sun does exactly that. A horizon has no such freedom — part 2 shows every generator shares one \(\Omega_{\mathrm H}\). Hawking's rigidity theorem says this was never a property of Kerr in particular: any stationary, non-static, asymptotically flat horizon obeying the dominant energy condition is a Killing horizon of \(\partial_t + \Omega_{\mathrm H}\partial_\varphi\) with \(\Omega_{\mathrm H}\) constant.

Check. Dimensions: \(\omega\) is \(\mathrm{m\cdot m\cdot m\cdot m\,s^{-1}/m^4} = \mathrm{s^{-1}}\) ✓. Limit \(a\to0\): the numerator vanishes, \(\omega \equiv 0\), and Schwarzschild has no preferred sense of rotation ✓. Special case \(\theta = \pi/2\), \(r\) large: the denominator is \(\approx r^4\) and \(\omega \approx 2r_gac/r^3 = 2GJ/c^2r^3\), the Lense–Thirring rate ✓. Number: at \(r_+\) for the running hole the formula returns \(6363\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}\), as in P1 ✓.

P4 Surface gravity, two ways medium

exercises: Killing's equation, proper acceleration, redshift, the definition of \(\kappa\)

Work in Schwarzschild, \(f = 1 - r_s/r\), with \(\xi = \partial_t\) and the useful Christoffel symbols \(\Gamma^r_{tt} = \tfrac12ff'c^2\) and \(\Gamma^t_{tr} = f'/2f\) (unit 36). Throughout \(\kappa\) is the deck's — an acceleration, in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\) — so the defining relations carry a \(1/c\).

  1. Prove, for any Killing field, that \(\nabla^\mu(\xi_\nu\xi^\nu) = -2\xi^\nu\nabla_\nu\xi^\mu\), and hence that the two definitions of \(\kappa\) on the slides agree.
  2. Derive, from the deck's \(\xi^\nu\nabla_\nu\xi^\mu = (\kappa/c)\xi^\mu\), the scalar form \(\kappa^2 = -\tfrac12c^2(\nabla^\mu\xi^\nu)(\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu)\), and use it to compute \(\kappa\) and show it equals \(c^4/4GM\) on \(r = r_s\). (Use the scalar form: in Schwarzschild coordinates the vector relation is vacuous at \(r = r_s\), because the chart itself fails there.)
  3. Compute the proper acceleration \(|a|\) of a static observer at radius \(r\), show that it diverges as \(r\to r_s\), and show that \(\sqrt f\,|a|\) does not, with the same limit as part 2.
  4. Evaluate \(\kappa\) for a ten-solar-mass Schwarzschild hole, and say in one sentence what a winch at infinity would feel.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Part 1 is one line of index gymnastics. Parts 2 and 3 are two routes to the same number, an acceleration in \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). Part 4 is arithmetic plus a sentence.

Rung 2

Part 1: \(\nabla^\mu(\xi^\nu\xi_\nu) = 2\xi^\nu\nabla^\mu\xi_\nu\) by the product rule and metric compatibility; now use the antisymmetry of \(\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu\). Part 2 has two halves. The scalar form is an identity, and the missing ingredient is that \(\xi\) is hypersurface orthogonal on the horizon — which is a theorem of unit 31, not an assumption. For the evaluation, \(\xi_\nu = g_{\nu t}\) has the single component \(\xi_t = -fc^2\), so \(\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu\) has only the antisymmetric pair \(\nabla_r\xi_t = -\nabla_t\xi_r\), and the scalar is a single term.

Rung 3

Part 2, first line of the identity: contract Frobenius' \(\xi_{[\mu}\nabla_\nu\xi_{\lambda]} = 0\) with \(\nabla^\nu\xi^\lambda\), and use \(\xi^\nu\nabla_\nu\xi^\mu = (\kappa/c)\xi^\mu\) wherever it appears. First line of the evaluation: \(\nabla_r\xi_t = \partial_r\xi_t - \Gamma^t_{rt}\xi_t = -f'c^2 + \tfrac{f'}{2f}fc^2 = -\tfrac12f'c^2\). Then \((\nabla^\mu\xi^\nu)(\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu) = 2g^{rr}g^{tt}(\nabla_r\xi_t)^2\). Part 3, first line: \(u^\mu = (f^{-1/2},0,0,0)\) and \(a^r = \Gamma^r_{tt}(u^t)^2\).

Worked solution

Idea. \(\kappa\) is a limit of "local force times redshift", and the two definitions differ only by Killing's equation.

1. By the product rule and metric compatibility, \(\nabla^\mu(\xi^\nu\xi_\nu) = 2\xi^\nu\nabla^\mu\xi_\nu\). Killing's equation is \(\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu + \nabla_\nu\xi_\mu = 0\), so \(\nabla^\mu\xi_\nu = -\nabla_\nu\xi^\mu\) after raising and lowering. Hence \(\nabla^\mu(\xi\cdot\xi) = -2\xi^\nu\nabla_\nu\xi^\mu\), and setting this equal to \(-(2\kappa/c)\xi^\mu\) gives \(\xi^\nu\nabla_\nu\xi^\mu = (\kappa/c)\xi^\mu\). \(\square\) The second form says the generators are geodesics run with a non-affine parameter, the non-affinity being \(\kappa\).

3 (do this first; it is the concrete route). A static observer has \(u^\mu = (u^t,0,0,0)\) with \(g_{tt}(u^t)^2 = -c^2\), so \(u^t = f^{-1/2}\). Its acceleration is

\[a^\mu = u^\nu\nabla_\nu u^\mu \Rightarrow a^r = \Gamma^r_{tt}(u^t)^2 = \tfrac12ff'c^2\cdot\frac1f = \tfrac12f'c^2 ,\] with the other components zero (nothing depends on \(t\), and \(u^r = u^\theta = u^\varphi = 0\)). Its magnitude is

Worked solution

Idea. Integrability is a counting statement with three conditions attached, and the value of the problem is in checking all three rather than the count alone.

1. Liouville–Arnold: let \((\mathcal P, \omega)\) be a \(2n\)-dimensional symplectic manifold and \(f_1 = H, f_2, \dots, f_n\) smooth functions with \(\{f_i, f_j\} = 0\) for all \(i, j\) and \(\mathrm df_1, \dots, \mathrm df_n\) linearly independent on a level set \(M_c = \{f_i = c_i\}\). If \(M_c\) is compact and connected it is diffeomorphic to an \(n\)-torus; the flow of \(H\) on it is linear in suitable coordinates, and there exist action–angle variables in a neighbourhood. For geodesics on a 4-manifold the phase space is \(T^*\mathcal M\), of dimension \(8 = 2n\), so \(n = 4\) and four constants are needed.

2. In canonical coordinates \((x^\mu, p_\mu)\), \(\{f, g\} = \partial_{x^\mu}f\,\partial_{p_\mu}g - \partial_{p_\mu}f\,\partial_{x^\mu}g\). Take \(f = E = -p_t\): then \(\partial_{x^\mu}E = 0\) and \(\partial_{p_\mu}E = -\delta^\mu_t\), so \(\{E, g\} = \partial_tg\). Hence \(\{E, H\} = \partial_tH = \tfrac12(\partial_tg^{\mu\nu})p_\mu p_\nu = 0\), because Boyer–Lindquist components are \(t\)-independent; and \(\{E, L\} = \partial_tp_\varphi = 0\). Identically with \(\varphi\): \(\{L, g\} = -\partial_\varphi g\), so \(\{L, H\} = 0\) by axisymmetry. \(\square\)

3. \(Q = K_{\mu\nu}p^\mu p^\nu\) up to the constant combination \((L - a\mathcal E)^2\), which commutes with everything in sight since \(E\) and \(L\) do. By the same computation as part 2, \(\{E, Q\} = \partial_tQ = (\partial_tK_{\mu\nu})p^\mu p^\nu\), which vanishes because \(\mathcal L_{\partial_t}K = 0\); likewise \(\{L, Q\} = 0\). And \(\{H, Q\} = 0\) is exactly the statement that \(Q\) is constant along the flow of \(H\), which is the Killing-tensor theorem. \(\square\)

4. \(\mathrm dE\) and \(\mathrm dL\) involve only \(p_t, p_\varphi\) and are always independent of each other. \(\mathrm dH\) and \(\mathrm dQ\) both involve \(p_\theta\), but only \(H\) involves \(p_r\), so a dependence needs the \(p_\theta\)-parts to align — which happens where \(p_\theta = 0\) and \(\cos\theta = 0\) simultaneously, that is on the equatorial orbits, \(Q = 0\). That is a measure-zero set, and nothing is lost: equatorial Kerr geodesics live on the invariant submanifold \(\theta = \pi/2\), \(p_\theta = 0\), which is symplectic of dimension \(6 = 2n\) with \(n = 3\), and there \(H, E, L\) are three independent constants in involution — exactly the number Liouville–Arnold asks for — so those orbits are solved by quadrature directly, as unit 37 did for Schwarzschild.

5. First: put a spin on the test body. The Mathisson–Papapetrou equations are not integrable and their numerics show unit 17's islands and chaotic sea. Second: the two-body problem. exercises: the area theorem on real numbers, \(A = 8\pi r_gr_+\), what a merger conserves

LIGO's first detection merged black holes of \(36\,M_\odot\) and \(29\,M_\odot\) into one of \(62\,M_\odot\), radiating \(3\,M_\odot c^2\) as gravitational waves. Take the initial holes as non-spinning and the final hole as Kerr with \(a_* = 0.67\).

  1. Write \(A\) in terms of \(M\) and \(a_*\) alone, and check it gives \(16\pi G^2M^2/c^4\) at \(a_* = 0\).
  2. Compute the total initial horizon area and the final horizon area, in \(\mathrm m^2\).
  3. By what percentage did the total area change? By what percentage did the total mass change? Are these compatible with the second law of black-hole mechanics?
  4. Compute the entropy \(S/k_{\mathrm B}\) before and after, and state which of the four laws the comparison is testing.
  5. What would the final spin have to be for the area to have decreased? Is that value allowed?
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Two areas in \(\mathrm m^2\), two percentages, two entropies, and one spin value. Everything is \(A = 8\pi r_gr_+\) with \(r_+ = r_g(1+\sqrt{1-a_*^2})\); nothing about the merger dynamics is needed.

Rung 2

It is easiest to work in units of \(r_{g,\odot}^2\), where \(r_{g,\odot} = GM_\odot/c^2 = 1476.6\) m: then \(r_g = M/M_\odot\) in those units and \(A/r_{g,\odot}^2 = 8\pi(M/M_\odot)^2(1+\sqrt{1-a_*^2})\). Convert once at the end. Part 5 is the same formula solved for \(a_*\).

Rung 3

First line: \(A = 8\pi r_gr_+ = 8\pi r_g^2(1+\sqrt{1-a_*^2})\), and with \(a_* = 0\) that is \(16\pi r_g^2 = 16\pi G^2M^2/c^4\) ✓. Initial total: \(16\pi r_{g,\odot}^2(36^2 + 29^2)\).

Worked solution

Idea. Area is not mass. Mass adds and can be radiated away; area adds and cannot decrease, and the merger is the place where the two come apart visibly.

1. \(A = 4\pi(r_+^2+a^2) = 8\pi r_gr_+\) and \(r_+ = r_g(1+\sqrt{1-a_*^2})\), so

\[A = 8\pi r_g^2\big(1 + \sqrt{1-a_*^2}\big),\qquad r_g = \frac{GM}{c^2}.\] At \(a_* = 0\), \(A = 16\pi r_g^2 = 16\pi G^2M^2/c^4\) ✓, which is \(4\pi r_s^2\) as it must be.

2. Work in units of \(r_{g,\odot}^2\) with \(r_{g,\odot} = GM_\odot/c^2 = 1.32712\times10^{20}/8.98755\times10^{16} = 1476.6\) m, so \(r_{g,\odot}^2 = 2.18034\times10^{6}\ \mathrm{m^2}\).

  • Initial: \(A_{\mathrm i}/r_{g,\odot}^2 = 16\pi(36^2 + 29^2) = 16\pi(1296 + 841) = 16\pi(2137) = 1.07417\times10^{5}\), so \(A_{\mathrm i} = 2.342\times10^{11}\ \mathrm{m^2}\).
  • Final: \(\sqrt{1 - 0.67^2} = \sqrt{0.5511} = 0.742361\), so \(A_{\mathrm f}/r_{g,\odot}^2 = 8\pi(62)^2(1.742361) = 8\pi(3844)(1.742361) = 1.68330\times10^{5}\), so \(A_{\mathrm f} = 3.670\times10^{11}\ \mathrm{m^2}\).

3. The area rose by \(A_{\mathrm f}/A_{\mathrm i} = 1.5671\), that is \(\mathbf{+56.7\%}\). The mass fell from \(65\,M_\odot\) to \(62\,M_\odot\), that is \(\mathbf{-4.6\%}\). Entirely compatible: the second law constrains \(A\), not \(M\), and the merger is the cleanest demonstration that the two are independent. Three solar masses of rest energy left as radiation while the horizon grew by more than half.

4. \(S/k_{\mathrm B} = c^3A/4G\hbar\) with \(c^3/4G\hbar = 2.69440\times10^{25}/(2.81549\times10^{-44}) = 9.5702\times10^{68}\ \mathrm{m^{-2}}\):

\[\frac{S_{\mathrm i}}{k_{\mathrm B}} = 2.242\times10^{80},\qquad \frac{S_{\mathrm f}}{k_{\mathrm B}} = 3.513\times10^{80}.\] The comparison tests the second law — Hawking's area theorem, in its thermodynamic reading. It is a measurement, not a calculation: LIGO published a direct test of the area law from the inspiral and ringdown portions of this event in 2021 and found it satisfied at \(95\%\) confidence.

5. Set \(A_{\mathrm f} = A_{\mathrm i}\): \(8\pi(3844)(1+\sqrt{1-a_*^2}) = 16\pi(2137)\), so \(1 + \sqrt{1-a_*^2} = 2(2137)/3844 = 1.11186\) and \(\sqrt{1-a_*^2} = 0.11186\), giving \(a_* = 0.99372\). Any \(a_*\) above that would mean a decrease. Such a spin is allowed by the Kerr bound (\(a_* \le 1\)) but is not what a merger of two non-spinning holes produces: the final spin comes from the orbital angular momentum at plunge and sits near \(0.69\) for equal masses. So the area theorem is not violated, and — importantly — it could have been, had nature chosen differently. The test has teeth.

Check. Dimensions: \(A\) is \(\mathrm m^2\) ✓, \(c^3/G\hbar\) is \(\mathrm m^{-2}\) ✓, \(S/k_{\mathrm B}\) dimensionless ✓. Limit: if the final hole were non-spinning, \(A_{\mathrm f}/r_{g,\odot}^2 = 16\pi(3844) = 1.932\times10^{5}\), a rise of \(80\%\) — larger, as expected, since spin shrinks the horizon at fixed mass ✓. Sanity: \(M_{\mathrm{irr,f}} = 62\sqrt{1.742361/2} = 57.9\,M_\odot\), against \(\sqrt{36^2+29^2} = 46.2\,M_\odot\) for the initial pair combined in quadrature — and quadrature is the right combination, because area adds and \(A \propto M_{\mathrm{irr}}^2\) ✓.
