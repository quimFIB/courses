Rotating and charged holes, and where this course stops

Every hole that has ever formed rotates. Kerr's solution has an ergosphere you can mine, a hidden symmetry nobody expected, and — at the end — a temperature this course cannot derive.

needs 36, 15~3 h slides + 6 h problemsproblems: ./mr problems 40

Unit 36 solved the vacuum equations for a round, static star, and unit 37 read three measurements off the answer. But nothing round and static has ever existed. Every star rotates, so every hole that forms from one rotates, and the rotating solution was not found until 1963 — forty-eight years after Schwarzschild's, by Roy Kerr, who was looking for algebraically special metrics rather than for a black hole. It is not obtainable by the method of unit 36: no ansatz with two unknown functions of one variable contains it.

This unit has two jobs, and they pull in different directions. The first is to do the Kerr geometry honestly — the ergosphere, the two horizons, the energy you can take out, the fourth constant of motion that makes its orbits integrable. The second is to say where this course's methods stop. That is not a rhetorical flourish: there is a precise line, and it is crossed on the slide where the horizon acquires a temperature. Everything before that line is classical differential geometry and can be derived here. Everything after needs quantum field theory in curved spacetime, which this course has deliberately not taught, and so is stated with its source named.

Conventions

Unit 36's, unchanged: signature $(-,+,+,+)$, factors of $c$ kept, $t$ in seconds so $g_{tt}$ carries a $c^2$, $\mathrm d\Omega^2 = \mathrm d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,\mathrm d\varphi^2$. Why $a_* = 0.9$ and not $a_* = 1$. Extremal holes are the clean limiting case and the place the 29.3% ceiling is attained, but they are not attainable: Thorne showed in 1974 that photon capture from a thin accretion disc caps the spin-up at $a_* \approx 0.998$, and measured spins of stellar-mass holes scatter over the whole range (GRS 1915+105 sits near the top, Cygnus X-1 at $a_* \gt 0.95$, others near zero). $0.9$ is a real value, and keeping $a_* \lt 1$ means every square root in this deck is of a positive number, which extremal geometry quietly stops being true of.

Why a branch and not a spine unit. Nothing later in this course needs Kerr. It is here because it is the only other exact solution of comparable importance, because its hidden symmetry is the single best advertisement for unit 15's Hamilton–Jacobi machinery, and because the honest place to stop a general-relativity course is at the point where the classical theory starts predicting things it cannot explain.

40 · the metric

Kerr's solution, written down

the Kerr metric, in Boyer–Lindquist coordinates $$\begin{aligned}\mathrm ds^2 = &-\Big(1 - \frac{2r_gr}{\Sigma}\Big)c^2\mathrm dt^2 \;-\; \frac{4r_gra\sin^2\theta}{\Sigma}\,c\,\mathrm dt\,\mathrm d\varphi\\ &+ \frac{\Sigma}{\Delta}\,\mathrm dr^2 + \Sigma\,\mathrm d\theta^2 + \Big(r^2 + a^2 + \frac{2r_gra^2\sin^2\theta}{\Sigma}\Big)\sin^2\theta\,\mathrm d\varphi^2 ,\end{aligned}$$

$\Sigma = r^2 + a^2\cos^2\theta$, $\Delta = r^2 - 2r_gr + a^2$. It solves $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ for every $M$ and every $a$. Cited: Kerr 1963; the verification is mechanical and long, and the derivation needs machinery this course has not taught.

dimensions

$r_g$, $a$, $r$ are lengths, so $\Sigma$ and $\Delta$ are areas and every term is an area. $c\,\mathrm dt$ is a length; $g_{t\varphi}$ carries one factor of $c$, $g_{tt}$ two.

limit $a \to 0$

$\Sigma \to r^2$, $\Delta \to r^2(1 - r_s/r)$, the cross term vanishes, and every component collapses to unit 36's metric exactly.

limit $M \to 0$

$\mathrm ds^2 \to -c^2\mathrm dt^2 + \frac{\Sigma}{r^2+a^2}\mathrm dr^2 + \Sigma\mathrm d\theta^2 + (r^2+a^2)\sin^2\theta\,\mathrm d\varphi^2$: flat, in oblate spheroidal coordinates.

Two new features, both from the cross term: $g_{t\varphi} \ne 0$ means the geometry distinguishes $\varphi$ from $-\varphi$, and $g_{tt}$ vanishes somewhere other than $\Delta = 0$.

The $M \to 0$ check, in full. Set $x = \sqrt{r^2+a^2}\sin\theta\cos\varphi$, $y = \sqrt{r^2+a^2}\sin\theta\sin\varphi$, $z = r\cos\theta$. Differentiating and summing $\mathrm dx^2 + \mathrm dy^2 + \mathrm dz^2$ gives, after the cross terms cancel, $$\frac{r^2+a^2\cos^2\theta}{r^2+a^2}\,\mathrm dr^2 + (r^2+a^2\cos^2\theta)\,\mathrm d\theta^2 + (r^2+a^2)\sin^2\theta\,\mathrm d\varphi^2,$$ which is exactly the spatial part above. The surfaces of constant $r$ are oblate spheroids of semi-axes $\sqrt{r^2+a^2}$ (equatorial) and $r$ (polar); $r = 0$ is not a point but the disc $x^2 + y^2 \le a^2$, $z = 0$, whose rim $x^2+y^2 = a^2$ is $r = 0, \theta = \pi/2$. This is worth holding on to: it is why $r = 0$ in Kerr is a ring and not a point.

What "Boyer–Lindquist" buys. Kerr wrote his solution in what are now called Kerr–Schild coordinates, $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + H\ell_\mu\ell_\nu$ with $\ell$ null — elegant, and regular across the horizon, but with three off-diagonal terms. Boyer and Lindquist (1967) found the chart above, in which only $g_{t\varphi}$ survives and the $a \to 0$ limit is manifestly Schwarzschild. The price is the same one unit 36 paid: the chart breaks down at $\Delta = 0$, and crossing the horizon needs a different one.

Why no ansatz finds it. Unit 36's method worked because sphericity plus staticity left two functions of one variable. Kerr is stationary and axisymmetric, which leaves four functions of two variables, and $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ for that ansatz is a coupled nonlinear PDE system (the Ernst equations, 1968) with no elementary route. Kerr found the metric by imposing an algebraic condition on the Weyl tensor — that the spacetime be of Petrov type D, meaning its Weyl tensor has two repeated principal null directions — which reduces the PDE system to something solvable. That the resulting metric described a rotating hole was not obvious and was not what he was looking for.

The ring singularity. $\Sigma = 0$ requires $r = 0$ and $\theta = \pi/2$ together. The Kretschmann scalar there is $K = 48r_g^2(r^2-a^2\cos^2\theta)\big[\Sigma^2 - 16r^2a^2\cos^2\theta\big]/\Sigma^6$ (cited; Chandrasekhar, The Mathematical Theory of Black Holes, §58), which reduces to unit 36's $48r_g^2/r^6 = 12r_s^2/r^6$ at $a = 0$ and diverges as $\Sigma \to 0$. So the ring is a genuine curvature singularity. Unlike Schwarzschild's, it is timelike: it can be avoided, and the maximal extension of Kerr lets a geodesic pass through the disc to a region with $r \lt 0$, where closed timelike curves exist near the ring. That region is almost certainly not physical, for reasons the collapse section gives.

What the cross term does: space is dragged

Drop a particle from rest at large $r$ with $L = p_\varphi = 0$: no angular momentum at all. Its angular velocity is

$$\omega(r,\theta) := \frac{\mathrm d\varphi}{\mathrm dt} = \frac{g^{t\varphi}}{g^{tt}} = -\,\frac{g_{t\varphi}}{g_{\varphi\varphi}} = \frac{2r_gra\,c}{(r^2+a^2)^2 - \Delta a^2\sin^2\theta} ,$$

and it is not zero. A zero-angular-momentum observer is nonetheless carried around the hole: this is frame dragging.

far field

The denominator $\to r^4$ as $r\to\infty$, so

$$\omega \;\longrightarrow\; \frac{2r_gac}{r^3} \;=\; \frac{2GJ}{c^2r^3} .$$

Mass alone never produces this; only $J$ does. It is Lense–Thirring precession, 1918.

checks

Dimensions: $GJ/c^2r^3$ is $\mathrm{(m^3kg^{-1}s^{-2})(kg\,m^2s^{-1})/(m^2s^{-2}\cdot m^3)} = \mathrm{s^{-1}}$ ✓. Limit $J \to 0$: $\omega \to 0$, and Schwarzschild has no preferred sense of rotation ✓. Sign: $a \gt 0$ gives $\omega \gt 0$, dragging with the hole ✓.

the Earth does it too

$J_\oplus = 5.85\times10^{33}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2s^{-1}}$. A gyroscope in a polar circular orbit at $r = 7013$ km precesses at $GJ_\oplus/2c^2r^3 = 6.29\times10^{-15}$ rad/s, which is 41 milliarcseconds per year.

Gravity Probe B measured $37.2 \pm 7.2$ mas/yr against a full prediction of $39.2$ (cited: Everitt et al., PRL 106, 221101, 2011).

Where $\omega = -g_{t\varphi}/g_{\varphi\varphi}$ comes from. "Zero angular momentum" means $p_\varphi = 0$. Raise the index: $\dot\varphi = p^\varphi/m = (g^{\varphi t}p_t + g^{\varphi\varphi}p_\varphi)/m = g^{\varphi t}p_t/m$, and likewise $\dot t = g^{tt}p_t/m$. Hence $\mathrm d\varphi/\mathrm dt = g^{\varphi t}/g^{tt}$. For a metric whose only off-diagonal entry in the $(t,\varphi)$ block is $g_{t\varphi}$, inverting the $2\times2$ block gives $g^{t\varphi} = -g_{t\varphi}/D$ and $g^{tt} = g_{\varphi\varphi}/D$ with $D = g_{tt}g_{\varphi\varphi} - g_{t\varphi}^2$, so the ratio is $-g_{t\varphi}/g_{\varphi\varphi}$. The closed form uses the identity $g_{\varphi\varphi} = \big[(r^2+a^2)^2 - \Delta a^2\sin^2\theta\big]\sin^2\theta/\Sigma$, which is two lines of algebra from the metric and is worth keeping: it makes the $\sin^2\theta$ cancel, so $\omega$ has no $\theta$ in it at all once written this way, and it makes the horizon value obvious — set $\Delta = 0$ and use $2r_gr_+ = r_+^2+a^2$ to get $ac/(r_+^2+a^2)$. The single factor of $c$ is inherited from $g_{t\varphi}$, which carries one because the metric is written with $c\,\mathrm dt$ while $t$ is measured in seconds.

The Earth number, honestly. The Lense–Thirring precession rate of a gyroscope at position $\vec r$ is $\vec\Omega = (G/c^2r^3)\big[3(\vec J\cdot\hat r)\hat r - \vec J\big]$ (cited; it follows from the gravitomagnetic form of unit 38's linearised metric). Averaging over a circular polar orbit, $\langle(\hat J\cdot\hat r)\hat r\rangle = \tfrac12\hat J$, so $\langle\vec\Omega\rangle = G\vec J/2c^2r^3$ — the formula used in the box. With $I_\oplus = 0.3307M_\oplus R_\oplus^2$ and GP-B's 642 km altitude that is 41 mas/yr, about 5% above the mission's 39.2, the difference being the Earth's real moment of inertia distribution and the orbit's small eccentricity. The much larger geodetic (de Sitter) precession, $\tfrac32(GM_\oplus/c^2r)\sqrt{GM_\oplus/r^3}$, comes out at 6638 mas/yr against the published 6606.1 — the same kind of agreement. Both are unit 32's parallel transport, measured.

Why this is not a force. Nothing pushes the particle round. Its worldline is a geodesic; $\varphi$ increases because the metric's notion of "not rotating" is itself rotating relative to the stars. This is Mach's objection made quantitative, and it is the reason "inertial frame" is a local statement in general relativity and a global one in Newton's.

Worked example 1 — the ergosphere, and the end of standing still

where can an observer hold $r$, $\theta$, $\varphi$ fixed?

Step 1 — what "static" is. A worldline with $r, \theta, \varphi$ constant has tangent $u\propto\partial_t$, so it is an observer's worldline iff $g_{tt} \lt 0$.

Step 2 — read off $g_{tt}$.

$$g_{tt} = -\Big(1 - \frac{2r_gr}{\Sigma}\Big)c^2 = -\frac{c^2}{\Sigma}\big(\Sigma - 2r_gr\big).$$

Step 3 — substitute $\Sigma$. $\Sigma - 2r_gr = r^2 - 2r_gr + a^2\cos^2\theta$, so $g_{tt} \lt 0 \iff$ that quadratic is positive.

Step 4 — solve the quadratic. Its larger root is

$$\boxed{\;r_{\mathrm E}(\theta) = r_g + \sqrt{r_g^2 - a^2\cos^2\theta}\;}$$

so $g_{tt} \gt 0$ just inside it: outside $r_{\mathrm E}$ static observers exist, inside none do.

Step 5 — the running hole. $a = 0.9\,r_g$:

Step 1, said carefully. "Static" here means: the observer's spatial Boyer–Lindquist coordinates never change. That is a statement relative to the coordinates, but not an empty one, because $\varphi$ is fixed by the axial Killing field and $r,\theta$ by the geometry of the orbit spheroids — a static observer is one at rest with respect to the distant stars. The condition for such a curve to be a possible worldline is that its tangent be timelike, and its tangent is proportional to $\partial_t$ because $\dot r = \dot\theta = \dot\varphi = 0$.

The surface $r_{\mathrm E}$ has several names. Static limit (Penrose's), ergosurface, or the outer boundary of the ergoregion. "Ergo" is Greek for work, and the name was chosen because of what section 3 does there. The region between $r_+$ and $r_{\mathrm E}$ is the ergosphere, and despite the name it is not a sphere: it is the volume between a spheroid and the horizon, pinched to zero thickness at the poles.

Why the poles are special. At $\theta = 0$, $\cos^2\theta = 1$ and $r_{\mathrm E} = r_g + \sqrt{r_g^2 - a^2} = r_+$: the ergosurface meets the horizon. Physically, the dragging is a rotational effect and the rotation axis is the one place it has no direction to drag you in. Mathematically, $g_{tt}$ and $\Delta$ vanish together there because $\sin\theta = 0$ kills the difference $\Sigma - 2r_gr$ versus $\Delta$, which is $a^2\cos^2\theta - a^2 = -a^2\sin^2\theta$.

The smaller root. The quadratic's other root is $r_g - \sqrt{r_g^2 - a^2\cos^2\theta}$, which lies inside $r_-$ and bounds an inner ergoregion around the ring. It matters for the maximal extension and not for anything an infalling observer from outside will meet before the Cauchy horizon, so this deck does not use it.

What is not true. Being unable to stand still is not the same as being unable to escape. The ergosphere is outside the horizon; a rocket in it can leave, and must simply also be moving in $\varphi$ while it does. Confusing the static limit with the horizon is the single most common error about Kerr, and the next slide but one is the repair: inside the ergosphere the set of allowed angular velocities is a nonempty interval that does not contain zero.

Two horizons, and a bound on spin

$g_{rr} = \Sigma/\Delta$ blows up at the roots of $\Delta$:

$$r_\pm = r_g \pm \sqrt{r_g^2 - a^2} .$$

Both are coordinate singularities — $\Sigma$, and every curvature invariant, is finite there — and $r_+$ is a null hypersurface, hence an event horizon, by exactly unit 36's argument.

the Kerr bound

If $a \gt r_g$, i.e. $|J| \gt GM^2/c$, then $\Delta \gt 0$ everywhere: no horizon, and the ring singularity is naked and visible from infinity.

$a = r_g$ is extremal: the two horizons merge. Our hole has $a_* = 0.9$, so $r_+ = 1.436\,r_g = 21.20$ km and $r_- = 0.564\,r_g = 8.33$ km.

the inner horizon is not benign

$r_-$ is a Cauchy horizon: past it, data on a spacelike slice outside no longer determine the future. Infalling perturbations are infinitely blueshifted against it, and the backreaction ("mass inflation", Poisson–Israel 1990) is believed to turn it into a curvature singularity.

checks

$a \to 0$: $r_+ \to 2r_g = r_s$ and $r_- \to 0$ ✓, one horizon, as Schwarzschild has. Dimensions: lengths ✓. Product $r_+r_- = a^2$ and sum $r_+ + r_- = 2r_g$, straight from the quadratic — both useful later.

Cosmic censorship (Penrose 1969, a conjecture): generic collapse from regular data never produces a naked singularity. Every attempt to spin a hole past $a_* = 1$ by feeding it angular momentum has failed; the hole refuses the last particles.

Why $\Delta = 0$ is a null surface. The surface $r = r_+$ has normal covector $n_\mu = \partial_\mu(r - r_+) = \delta^r_\mu$, so $n\cdot n = g^{rr} = \Delta/\Sigma$, which vanishes exactly on it. A hypersurface whose normal is null is itself null, and unit 36 showed that a null hypersurface is one-way: its generators are null geodesics lying in it, and no causal curve crosses it outwards. The inverse metric component $g^{rr} = \Delta/\Sigma$ is the honest object to use here, since $g_{rr} = \Sigma/\Delta$ is the one that diverges and divergence of a component proves nothing (unit 36's whole point).

A chart across the horizon. As in unit 36, define a Kerr analogue of the ingoing Eddington–Finkelstein coordinates by $\mathrm dv = c\,\mathrm dt + (r^2+a^2)\mathrm dr/\Delta$ and $\mathrm d\tilde\varphi = \mathrm d\varphi + a\,\mathrm dr/\Delta$. In $(v, r, \theta, \tilde\varphi)$ the metric is smooth and non-degenerate at $\Delta = 0$; the twist in $\tilde\varphi$ is the new feature, and it is the coordinate statement that an infaller is dragged.

The over-spinning question, and what actually happens. Take an extremal hole and throw in a particle with enough angular momentum to push $a$ past $r_g$. Wald showed in 1974 that the particles that would do it are precisely the ones the hole will not absorb: the centrifugal and spin-curvature repulsion turns them away. The calculation has been sharpened many times since — for near-extremal holes it needs second-order self-force terms to work out — and no counterexample has survived. This is evidence for cosmic censorship, not a proof of it, and the conjecture remains open.

Mass inflation, briefly. An observer crossing $r_-$ sees the entire infinite future history of the external universe compressed into finite proper time, so any radiation tail falling in is blueshifted without bound. The Ori model shows the resulting mass function diverging while tidal distortion stays integrable — a "weak" singularity a body might survive crossing. Whether that counts as censorship holding is a live question; recent work on Reissner–Nordström–de Sitter has produced parameter ranges where it appears to fail.

Inside the ergosphere: a window of allowed rotations

A stationary observer is one with constant $r,\theta$ and $\varphi = \varphi_0 + \Omega t$, so $u \propto \xi_\Omega := \partial_t + \Omega\,\partial_\varphi$. It is a worldline iff

$$g(\xi_\Omega,\xi_\Omega) = g_{tt} + 2\Omega\,g_{t\varphi} + \Omega^2 g_{\varphi\varphi} \;\lt\; 0 .$$

A quadratic in $\Omega$ opening upwards ($g_{\varphi\varphi} \gt 0$), so it is negative exactly between its roots:

$$\Omega_\pm = \frac{-g_{t\varphi} \pm \sqrt{g_{t\varphi}^2 - g_{tt}g_{\varphi\varphi}}}{g_{\varphi\varphi}} = \omega \pm \sqrt{\omega^2 - \frac{g_{tt}}{g_{\varphi\varphi}}} .$$
three regimes

Outside $r_{\mathrm E}$: $g_{tt} \lt 0$, so $\Omega_- \lt 0 \lt \Omega_+$ and $\Omega = 0$ is allowed.
In the ergosphere: $g_{tt} \gt 0$, so $0 \lt \Omega_- \lt \Omega_+$ — you must co-rotate.
On $r_+$: the discriminant vanishes, the window shuts, and $\Omega_- = \Omega_+ = \Omega_{\mathrm H}$.

the angular velocity of the horizon $$\Omega_{\mathrm H} = \frac{ac}{r_+^2 + a^2} ,$$

independent of $\theta$: the horizon turns rigidly. For the hole, $6363\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$ — 1013 revolutions a second, a 0.988 ms period.

checks

$\mathrm{m}\cdot\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}/\mathrm{m^2} = \mathrm{s^{-1}}$ ✓. $a \to 0$: $\Omega_{\mathrm H}\to 0$, and a Schwarzschild horizon does not turn ✓. Extremal $a = r_g$: $\Omega_{\mathrm H} = c/2r_g$, the largest it can be ✓.

The rigidity is the geometric fact behind the zeroth law, two slides on.

Why $\Omega_{\mathrm H}$ has no $\theta$ in it — the computation. On $r = r_+$ we have $r_+^2 - 2r_gr_+ + a^2 = 0$, i.e. $2r_gr_+ = r_+^2 + a^2$. Then $$-g_{t\varphi}\big|_{r_+} = \frac{2r_gr_+a\sin^2\theta}{\Sigma}c = \frac{(r_+^2+a^2)a\sin^2\theta}{\Sigma}c,$$ and $$g_{\varphi\varphi}\big|_{r_+} = \Big(r_+^2+a^2 + \frac{(r_+^2+a^2)a^2\sin^2\theta}{\Sigma}\Big)\sin^2\theta = (r_+^2+a^2)\frac{\Sigma + a^2\sin^2\theta}{\Sigma}\sin^2\theta = \frac{(r_+^2+a^2)^2\sin^2\theta}{\Sigma},$$ using $\Sigma + a^2\sin^2\theta = r_+^2 + a^2$. Dividing, every $\theta$ cancels and $\Omega_{\mathrm H} = ac/(r_+^2+a^2)$. A Newtonian fluid body cannot rotate rigidly and stay in equilibrium; a horizon has no choice.

The discriminant on the horizon. $g_{t\varphi}^2 - g_{tt}g_{\varphi\varphi} = \Delta\sin^2\theta\,c^2$ for the Kerr metric — a one-line computation from the two displayed expressions plus $g_{tt} = -c^2(\Delta - a^2\sin^2\theta)/\Sigma$. So the window's width is $2c\sqrt{\Delta}\sin\theta/g_{\varphi\varphi}$, which closes exactly at $\Delta = 0$. That is the precise sense in which the horizon is the last surface on which anything can be stationary at all, and it forces every stationary observer there to share one angular velocity.

Reading $\omega$ again. The midpoint of the window is $\omega = -g_{t\varphi}/g_{\varphi\varphi}$, the ZAMO angular velocity from the frame-dragging slide. So the allowed window is "the dragging rate, plus or minus what your rocket can do against the local light cone", and it closes when the light cone has tipped so far that its whole width is on one side of $\Omega = 0$ — the ergosphere — and then to a single ray — the horizon.

Killing horizon: the definition

Killing horizon

Let $\xi$ be a Killing vector field (unit 31). A null hypersurface $\mathcal N$ is a Killing horizon of $\xi$ if $\xi$ is null and non-vanishing on $\mathcal N$.

On a null hypersurface the normal is also tangent, so $\xi$ generates $\mathcal N$. No chart appears anywhere in that.

Kerr's horizon is one

$r = r_+$ is a Killing horizon of

$$\xi_{\mathrm H} := \partial_t + \Omega_{\mathrm H}\,\partial_\varphi .$$
proof

$g(\xi_\Omega,\xi_\Omega) = g_{\varphi\varphi}(\Omega - \Omega_-)(\Omega - \Omega_+)$, and on $r_+$ the two roots coincide at $\Omega_{\mathrm H}$ (previous slide), so the value at $\Omega = \Omega_{\mathrm H}$ is zero. $\xi_{\mathrm H}$ is a Killing field as a constant-coefficient combination of two Killing fields. $\square$

event horizon $\ne$ Killing horizon, in general

An event horizon is defined globally — the boundary of the past of future null infinity — and deciding where it is needs the whole future history. A Killing horizon is defined locally, from a symmetry. They coincide here because of a theorem, not a definition.

Hawking's rigidity theorem (cited)

In a stationary, analytic, asymptotically flat spacetime obeying the dominant energy condition, the event horizon is a Killing horizon; if not static, its Killing field is $\partial_t + \Omega_{\mathrm H}\partial_\varphi$ and the spacetime is axisymmetric. Hawking 1972; Hawking & Ellis §9.3.

So a rotating stationary hole must be axisymmetric and its horizon must turn rigidly. Both were checked by hand for Kerr; there was never a choice.

Why a null hypersurface's normal is tangent to it. Let $\mathcal N = \{f = 0\}$ with $n_\mu = \partial_\mu f$. Null means $g^{\mu\nu}n_\mu n_\nu = 0$, i.e. $n\cdot n = 0$, i.e. $n$ is orthogonal to itself. A tangent vector to $\mathcal N$ is exactly one orthogonal to $n$; so $n$ is tangent. This is the peculiarity of Lorentzian signature that makes horizons possible at all, and unit 36 used it to argue that $r = r_s$ is one-way.

Where the analyticity in the rigidity theorem hurts. Hawking's original proof assumes the metric is real-analytic, which is a much stronger hypothesis than smoothness and has no physical justification — an analytic function is determined everywhere by its behaviour in any open set, so assuming it is close to assuming the answer. Removing it is a substantial research programme (Alexakis–Ionescu–Klainerman have removed it in the near-Kerr regime); the general case is open. This is typical of black-hole uniqueness results: physically compelling, technically fragile.

The bifurcation surface. Where $\xi_{\mathrm H}$ vanishes — the two-sphere at the crossing point of the two branches of the horizon in the maximally extended spacetime, analogous to the origin of unit 36's Kruskal diagram — the definition above excludes the surface, since $\xi$ must be non-vanishing. A horizon containing such a surface is called a bifurcate Killing horizon, and on one the zeroth law is a theorem of pure differential geometry (Rácz–Wald 1996) rather than a consequence of the field equations. A hole formed by collapse has no bifurcation surface, which is why the zeroth law for real holes is stated with an energy condition.

Surface gravity

On $\mathcal N$, $\xi\cdot\xi = 0$; just off it, $\xi\cdot\xi \ne 0$. So $\nabla^\mu(\xi\cdot\xi)$ is normal to $\mathcal N$ — and on a null hypersurface, normal means proportional to $\xi$.

surface gravity

$\kappa$ is the function on $\mathcal N$ defined by

$$\nabla^\mu(\xi_\nu\xi^\nu) = -\frac{2\kappa}{c}\,\xi^\mu \quad\text{on }\mathcal N .$$
equivalently: the generators are geodesic, badly parametrised $$\xi^\nu\nabla_\nu\xi^\mu = \frac{\kappa}{c}\,\xi^\mu .$$
proof, one line

Killing means $\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu = -\nabla_\nu\xi_\mu$. So $\nabla^\mu(\xi^\nu\xi_\nu) = 2\xi^\nu\nabla^\mu\xi_\nu = -2\xi^\nu\nabla_\nu\xi^\mu$. Compare with the definition. $\square$

$\kappa$ depends on a normalisation

$\xi \mapsto \lambda\xi$ sends $\kappa\mapsto\lambda\kappa$. The convention is fixed at infinity: $\xi_{\mathrm H} = \partial_t + \Omega_{\mathrm H}\partial_\varphi$ with $g(\partial_t,\partial_t)\to-c^2$ as $r\to\infty$. Without that sentence $\kappa$ is meaningless. The $1/c$ is the price of keeping $t$ in seconds; it makes $\kappa$ an acceleration rather than an inverse time.

physical reading

$\kappa$ is the redshifted proper acceleration of a stationary observer in the limit $r\to r_+$: the force per unit mass that must be applied at infinity, through an ideal rope, to hold a unit mass just above the horizon. The local force diverges; the force at the far end does not.

Dimensions: an acceleration, $\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$.

Full proof that $\nabla^\mu(\xi\cdot\xi)$ is normal. $\xi\cdot\xi$ is constant (zero) along $\mathcal N$, so its gradient annihilates every vector tangent to $\mathcal N$; a covector annihilating the tangent space of a hypersurface is a multiple of its normal. On a null hypersurface the normal direction is spanned by $\xi$ itself, whence the proportionality. The factor $-2$ in the definition is chosen so that the second, more useful, form comes out with no factor.

The Schwarzschild value, derived. Take a static observer at radius $r$ in unit 36's metric, $f = 1 - r_s/r$. Its four-velocity is $u^\mu = (f^{-1/2}, 0,0,0)$ — normalised by $g_{tt}(u^t)^2 = -fc^2/f = -c^2$. Its proper acceleration is $$a^r = u^\nu\nabla_\nu u^r = \Gamma^r_{tt}(u^t)^2 = \frac{ff'c^2}{2}\cdot\frac1f = \frac{f'c^2}{2},$$ using unit 36's $\Gamma^r_{tt} = ff'c^2/2$. Its magnitude is $|a| = \sqrt{g_{rr}}\,|a^r| = f^{-1/2}f'c^2/2$, which diverges as $r\to r_s$. Multiplying by the redshift factor $\sqrt f$ — the ratio of the force felt at infinity to the force felt locally, unit 36's gravitational redshift — gives $$\sqrt f\,|a| = \frac{f'c^2}{2} = \frac{c^2r_s}{2r^2}\ \xrightarrow{\ r\to r_s\ }\ \frac{c^2}{2r_s} = \frac{c^4}{4GM},$$ finite. That limit is $\kappa$. The geometric definition agrees: for a Killing field the scalar $\kappa^2 = -\tfrac12c^2(\nabla^\mu\xi^\nu)(\nabla_\mu\xi_\nu)$ follows from the boxed relation, and for $\xi = \partial_t$ in Schwarzschild the only independent component is $\nabla_r\xi_t = -\tfrac12f'c^2$, giving $\kappa = \tfrac12f'c^2$ at every $r$ — which is the redshifted acceleration above, not merely its limit. Problem P4 does that computation.

Why the rope is the right picture. Lower a unit mass on a massless rope from infinity. The tension at the bottom is the local proper acceleration and grows without bound; the tension at the top is redshifted by $\sqrt f$ and tends to $\kappa$. The work recovered at the top as the mass is lowered to the horizon is therefore finite — in fact the whole rest energy, which is the classical reason a black hole is the most efficient engine there is, and the thought experiment Bekenstein was analysing when he proposed that horizons carry entropy.

The Penrose process: taking energy out

Energy is what a Killing field measures. Inside the ergosphere that Killing field is spacelike, and the measurement can come out negative.

40 · penrose process

Negative energy, and why it is not a paradox

For a particle with four-momentum $p$, the conserved energy measured at infinity is

$$E = -g(\partial_t, p) = -p_t .$$

That is a pairing with a Killing field, not a local energy: every observer measures $-g(u_{\text{obs}}, p) \gt 0$, because $u_{\text{obs}}$ is timelike.

in the ergosphere, $E$ can be negative

Inside the ergosphere, $\partial_t$ is spacelike. There exist future-directed timelike $p$ at every such point with $E \lt 0$.

proof

Write $X = \partial_t$, $g(X,X) = s^2 \gt 0$. Pick a future-directed unit timelike $u$ with $g(u,X) = 0$ (possible: $X^\perp$ is a Lorentzian 3-space). Put $p = m(u + \beta X/s)$ with $0 \lt \beta \lt c$. Then $g(p,p) = m^2(-c^2 + \beta^2) \lt 0$, so $p$ is timelike and future-directed, and $E = -g(X,p) = -m\beta s \lt 0$. $\square$

what is not happening

No local observer sees a negative energy; the particle's rest mass and locally measured energy are positive. $E \lt 0$ means: the work needed to carry it to infinity exceeds what it has. It never gets there — such orbits are confined to the ergosphere and must cross the horizon.

sanity check against Newton

A bound Newtonian orbit has negative total energy too. The difference: here the rest energy $\mu c^2$ is part of $E$, so $E \lt 0$ means the binding exceeds the entire rest mass — impossible outside the ergosphere, routine inside.

The consequence: a particle that falls in with $E_2 \lt 0$ reduces the hole's mass.

Why $X^\perp$ is Lorentzian. If $X$ is spacelike, the restriction of $g$ to the three-dimensional subspace $X^\perp$ has signature $(-,+,+)$: the orthogonal complement of a positive-norm vector in a $(-,+,+,+)$ space keeps the one negative direction. So $X^\perp$ contains timelike vectors, and we may pick a unit one and orient it to the future. If $X$ were timelike, as it is outside the ergosphere, $X^\perp$ would be spacelike and no such $u$ would exist — which is exactly why the construction fails there, and why $E \gt 0$ outside.

The upper bound on how negative. Not arbitrarily: the construction needs $|\beta| \lt c$, so $|E| \lt mcs = mc\sqrt{g_{tt}}$, and $g_{tt}$ is bounded on the ergosphere. Its maximum over the ergosphere of a Kerr hole is attained at the equator on the horizon and is finite, which is ultimately why the extractable fraction has a ceiling at all.

Penrose's own telling. Penrose proposed the process in 1969 in Rivista del Nuovo Cimento, in the paper that also proposed cosmic censorship, and told it as a parable about an advanced civilisation living on a scaffold around a Kerr hole, lowering rubbish into the ergosphere and arranging for it to split so that the useful half comes back up hot. It is the cleanest demonstration that "the energy of a black hole" is not all locked away — that a hole has a mass and, separately, a rotational energy that is on the table.

The process, and the inequality that caps it

the Penrose process

Send a particle with $E_0 \gt 0$ into the ergosphere and split it: $p_0 = p_1 + p_2$, so $E_0 = E_1 + E_2$. Arrange $E_2 \lt 0$ and let fragment 2 fall in; then $E_1 = E_0 - E_2 \gt E_0$, and the hole absorbs $\delta(Mc^2) = E_2 \lt 0$, $\delta J = L_2 \lt 0$: it loses mass and spin.

the horizon inequality

Every causal, future-directed $p$ crossing $r_+$ satisfies

$$\delta(Mc^2) \;\ge\; \Omega_{\mathrm H}\,\delta J .$$
proof, three lines

$\xi_{\mathrm H}$ is future-directed null there and $p$ future-directed causal, so $-g(\xi_{\mathrm H}, p) \ge 0$. Expand: it is $E - \Omega_{\mathrm H}L$. The hole's increments are $\delta(Mc^2) = E$, $\delta J = L$. $\square$

checks

$\Omega_{\mathrm H}\delta J$ is $\mathrm{s^{-1}\cdot kg\,m^2s^{-1}} = \mathrm J$ ✓. At $a = 0$ the inequality is $\delta M \ge 0$: a Schwarzschild hole only grows, and has no ergosphere to mine ✓.

where the negative energy must go

Fragment 2 cannot escape or orbit: an $E \lt 0$ curve stays inside the ergosphere, and every timelike curve confined there falls in. The bookkeeping closes because the hole's mass is defined at infinity too — same Killing field, same pairing.

In one line: what is taken from the hole is charged against its spin, at the rate $\Omega_{\mathrm H}$.

The split is ordinary four-momentum conservation. At a single event, conservation of four-momentum in a local inertial frame is unit 27's law, unchanged: general relativity has no global momentum conservation, but it has a local one at each point, and that is all the argument uses. $E = -p_t$ is then conserved separately along each fragment's geodesic by unit 36's Killing argument — which is unit 13's Noether theorem, again.

Why $-g(\xi_{\mathrm H},p)\ge0$. For two future-directed causal vectors $k$ (null) and $p$ (causal) in a Lorentzian vector space, $g(k,p) \le 0$, with equality only if $p$ is null and parallel to $k$. Proof: in an orthonormal frame with $k = (1,1,0,0)$ up to scale and $p = (p^0, \vec p)$ future-directed causal so $p^0 \ge |\vec p|$, one has $g(k,p) = -p^0 + p^1 \le -p^0 + |\vec p| \le 0$. This is the same inequality that makes the future light cone convex, and it is the entire content of the horizon constraint.

Why the process is not an engine anyone will build. Bardeen, Press and Teukolsky showed in 1972 that the split must be relativistic: the fragments need a relative speed above about $c/2$ to put one of them on a negative-energy orbit. A chemical or nuclear explosion is nowhere near that. The astrophysically relevant versions do not use particles at all — they use fields, which is the next slide but one.

Irreducible mass: the part you cannot have

The horizon's area is $A = 4\pi(r_+^2+a^2) = 8\pi r_gr_+$ (the $\sin^2\theta$ in $g_{\varphi\varphi}$ and the $\Sigma$'s cancel — see the notes).

irreducible mass $$M_{\mathrm{irr}} := \frac{c^2}{G}\sqrt{\frac{A}{16\pi}} \;=\; M\sqrt{\frac{r_+}{2r_g}} .$$
Christodoulou mass formula $$M^2 = M_{\mathrm{irr}}^2 + \Big(\frac{cJ}{2GM_{\mathrm{irr}}}\Big)^{\!2} .$$

The hole's mass splits into an irreducible part and a rotational energy $\;(M - M_{\mathrm{irr}})c^2$.

the second law, from the first

$\delta(Mc^2) \ge \Omega_{\mathrm H}\delta J \iff \delta M_{\mathrm{irr}} \ge 0 \iff \delta A \ge 0$.

proof of the equivalence

Work in $G = c = 1$ for the algebra, where $M_{\mathrm{irr}}^2 = \tfrac12\big(M^2 + \sqrt{M^4 - J^2}\big)$. Writing $x = \sqrt{M^4-J^2}$ and differentiating,

$$2M_{\mathrm{irr}}\,\delta M_{\mathrm{irr}} = \frac{M(M^2+x)\,\delta M - \tfrac12 J\,\delta J}{x} ,$$

so $\delta M_{\mathrm{irr}}\ge0 \iff \delta M \ge \dfrac{J\,\delta J}{2M(M^2+x)}$. And $r_+ = (M^2+x)/M$, so $\Omega_{\mathrm H} = a/(2Mr_+) = J/\big(2M(M^2+x)\big)$ — the same coefficient. $\square$

checks

$J = 0$: $M_{\mathrm{irr}} = M$, nothing extractable ✓. Extremal $J = GM^2/c$: $r_+ = r_g$, $M_{\mathrm{irr}} = M/\sqrt2$ ✓, and the formula returns $M^2/2 + M^2/2 = M^2$ ✓. Dimensions: $cJ/GM_{\mathrm{irr}}$ is $\mathrm{(m\,s^{-1})(kg\,m^2s^{-1})/(m^3kg^{-1}s^{-2}\,kg)} = \mathrm{kg}$ ✓.

The area, computed. The horizon is $r = r_+$, $t$ constant. Its induced metric is $\Sigma\,\mathrm d\theta^2 + g_{\varphi\varphi}\mathrm d\varphi^2$ with $g_{\varphi\varphi}|_{r_+} = (r_+^2+a^2)^2\sin^2\theta/\Sigma$ (computed two slides back), so the area element is $\sqrt{\Sigma\cdot(r_+^2+a^2)^2\sin^2\theta/\Sigma} = (r_+^2+a^2)\sin\theta$ — the $\Sigma$ cancels exactly. Integrating, $A = 2\pi\cdot2\cdot(r_+^2+a^2) = 4\pi(r_+^2+a^2)$. Then $2r_gr_+ = r_+^2+a^2$ gives the second form. Note that the horizon's intrinsic geometry is not that of a round sphere: for $a_* \gt \sqrt3/2 \approx 0.866$ its Gaussian curvature is negative near the poles, and it cannot be isometrically embedded in $\mathbb R^3$ (Smarr 1973). Our $a_* = 0.9$ hole is just past that threshold.

Why "irreducible". Christodoulou's 1970 paper introduced $M_{\mathrm{irr}}$ precisely as the quantity no Penrose process can decrease. Hawking's area theorem (1971) came a year later and showed the same monotone quantity is the horizon area, for any process obeying the null energy condition — not just particle splittings. That coincidence is the first hint that $A$ is thermodynamic, and Bekenstein took it seriously within two years.

The formula's shape. $M^2 = M_{\mathrm{irr}}^2 + (J/2M_{\mathrm{irr}})^2$ (geometrised) is a Pythagorean relation, and the two legs are "what is stored in the horizon" and "what is stored in the rotation". Its differential is exactly the first law of section 7: $\delta M = (\kappa/8\pi)\delta A + \Omega_{\mathrm H}\delta J$. One can go either way; we derived the inequality first because it is the physical constraint, and the equality is what the inequality saturates for a reversible process.

Fields do it better: superradiance

Replace the particle by a classical wave. A mode $\propto e^{-i\omega t + im\varphi}$ carries energy and angular momentum into the horizon in the fixed ratio $\mathrm dE/\mathrm dL = \omega/m$ — a statement about unit 29's stress tensor, and nothing quantum.

superradiance

Put that ratio into the horizon inequality $\mathrm dE - \Omega_{\mathrm H}\mathrm dL \ge 0$:

$$\mathrm dL\Big(\frac{\omega}{m} - \Omega_{\mathrm H}\Big) \ge 0 .$$

So if $\;\omega \lt m\,\Omega_{\mathrm H}$, then $\mathrm dL \lt 0$ and $\mathrm dE \lt 0$: the hole loses energy, and the reflected wave comes back amplified.

Zel'dovich predicted it in 1971 for a rotating absorbing cylinder, before anyone connected it to holes.

checks

$\omega/m$ is a rad/s, like $\Omega_{\mathrm H}$ ✓. An axisymmetric mode ($m = 0$) would need $\omega \lt 0$: impossible ✓. Schwarzschild ($\Omega_{\mathrm H} = 0$): never ✓.

Blandford–Znajek process

The astrophysical version (1977): a magnetic field anchored in an accretion disc threads the horizon, the same inequality applies to its angular-momentum flux, and the hole drives a Poynting-flux jet along its axis. The leading model for the jets of active galactic nuclei — the Penrose process, run with fields rather than rubbish.

For the running hole, the superradiant band is $\omega \lt m\times6363\ \mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$.

Why $\mathrm dE/\mathrm dL = \omega/m$. For a field with time dependence $e^{-i\omega t}$ and angular dependence $e^{im\varphi}$, the stress-energy fluxes through the horizon are $\mathrm dE/\mathrm dt = -\oint T^r{}_t$ and $\mathrm dL/\mathrm dt = \oint T^r{}_\varphi$, and for a single mode the integrands are proportional with ratio $\omega/m$, because the only $t$- and $\varphi$-dependence is in the phase $-\omega t + m\varphi$ and $T$ is quadratic in the field. Unit 29 derived $T^{\mu\nu}$ for a field by varying the action with respect to the metric; this is that tensor, evaluated on a mode.

The black-hole bomb. Press and Teukolsky (1972) noticed that surrounding a Kerr hole with a reflecting mirror turns superradiance into an exponential instability: the wave is amplified, reflected, amplified again. A massive field supplies its own mirror — modes with $\omega$ below the mass gap cannot escape to infinity — so a light boson of the right mass would make astrophysical Kerr holes unstable and spin them down. Measured black-hole spins therefore exclude ranges of ultralight boson masses, which is one of the stranger particle-physics constraints in existence and a live line of work with LIGO data.

Where the quantum version would go. Replace the classical incident wave by the vacuum, and the same amplification acts on vacuum fluctuations: that is spontaneous superradiance, and it is the rotational part of Hawking radiation. The step from "a wave is amplified" to "the vacuum radiates" is exactly the step this course cannot take, and section 7 says why.

Reissner–Nordström

the Reissner–Nordström metric $$\mathrm ds^2 = -f(r)c^2\mathrm dt^2 + \frac{\mathrm dr^2}{f(r)} + r^2\mathrm d\Omega^2,$$ $$f(r) = 1 - \frac{r_s}{r} + \frac{r_Q^2}{r^2},\qquad r_Q^2 := \frac{GQ^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0c^4} .$$

Sourced by a point charge: unit 36's derivation with $R_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac{8\pi G}{c^4}T_{\mu\nu}$ and unit 29's Maxwell stress tensor. No trace reversal, since $T^\mu{}_\mu = 0$ in four dimensions.

checks

$[r_Q^2] = \mathrm{(m^3kg^{-1}s^{-2})(J\,m)/(m^4s^{-4})} = \mathrm{m^2}$ ✓. $Q\to0$: Schwarzschild ✓. The $1/r^2$ term is the field energy outside $r$, gravitating — hence the $+$ sign, and it reduces the attraction ✓.

why RN is studied at all

Nothing astrophysical is charged. RN is the laboratory for the inner horizon: spherically symmetric, so mass inflation can be analysed in closed form there and then believed about Kerr.

Charge and spin together: Kerr with $\Delta = r^2 - 2r_gr + a^2 + r_Q^2$, plus $A = -\dfrac{Qr\,(c\,\mathrm dt - a\sin^2\theta\,\mathrm d\varphi)}{4\pi\varepsilon_0\Sigma}$. Newman et al., 1965.

The source, in full. The electromagnetic field of the charge is $F = \mathrm dA$ with $A = -\dfrac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r}\,\mathrm dt$, so the only nonzero component is $F_{tr} = -Q/(4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2)$ — Coulomb, unchanged, because unit 34's Maxwell equations $\mathrm dF = 0$, $\mathrm d\star F = 0$ are conformally invariant in four dimensions and the spherical ansatz does the rest. Unit 29's stress tensor $T_{\mu\nu} = \varepsilon_0(F_{\mu\alpha}F_\nu{}^\alpha - \tfrac14g_{\mu\nu}F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta})$ then has $T^t{}_t = T^r{}_r = -T^\theta{}_\theta = -T^\varphi{}_\varphi$, and feeding that into unit 36's $R_{\mu\nu}$ computation changes one line of the algebra: instead of $(rA)' = 1$ one gets $(rA)' = 1 - r_Q^2/r^2$, integrating to $A = 1 - r_s/r + r_Q^2/r^2$.

Tracelessness, and why it matters twice. $T^\mu{}_\mu = \varepsilon_0(F^{\mu\alpha}F_{\mu\alpha} - \tfrac44F_{\alpha\beta}F^{\alpha\beta}) = 0$ only because the spacetime dimension is 4 — the $\tfrac14$ in the definition and the $\delta^\mu_\mu = 4$ must cancel. It is the same fact that makes electromagnetism conformally invariant in four dimensions, and it is why $R = 0$ for RN and why the trace-reversed field equation of unit 35 collapses to $R_{\mu\nu} \propto T_{\mu\nu}$.

The singularity is still spacelike-free. Like Kerr's ring and unlike Schwarzschild's, RN's $r=0$ is timelike: the $+r_Q^2/r^2$ term dominates at small $r$ and makes $f \to +\infty$, so an infaller is repelled rather than crushed, and can miss the singularity and re-emerge through a white hole into another universe in the maximal extension. The Cauchy horizon at $r_-$ is exactly where that picture stops being trustworthy.

Why no real hole is charged

two numbers that settle it

1. An electron is over-extremal by a factor $2\times10^{21}$. The ratio for a body of charge $Q$ and mass $m$ is

For an electron, $\sqrt{4\pi\varepsilon_0G} = 8.617\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm{C\,kg^{-1}}$ and

$$\frac{r_Q}{r_g} = \frac{1.602\times10^{-19}}{9.109\times10^{-31}\cdot 8.617\times10^{-11}} = 2.04\times10^{21}.$$

No hair: three numbers, and that is the whole list

the no-hair theorem (cited)

Let $(\mathcal M, g, F)$ be a four-dimensional, asymptotically flat, stationary solution of the Einstein–Maxwell equations, containing a black hole whose event horizon is connected and non-degenerate, with the exterior regular. Then it is a Kerr–Newman solution, determined by $(M, J, Q)$ alone.

Israel 1967/68 (static case); Carter 1971 and Robinson 1975 (the rotating vacuum case); Hawking 1972 (rigidity and horizon topology). Wald §12.3; Heusler, Black Hole Uniqueness Theorems.

Everything else the collapsing star had — its shape, composition, magnetic field, baryon number, the colour of its light — is radiated away or swallowed. Unit 38's ringdown is that radiation.

four caveats, all real

Analyticity. The rigidity step assumes it, and nobody can justify it.
Electrovac only. With Yang–Mills fields there are hairy solutions (Bartnik–McKinnon 1988); with a conformally coupled scalar too. Hair is forbidden for ordinary matter, not for all matter.
Four dimensions. In five, black rings (Emparan–Reall 2002) have horizon topology $S^1\times S^2$ and the theorem is simply false.
Degenerate horizons — the extremal case — are excluded and need separate work.

test

If the theorem holds, every multipole moment of a hole is fixed by $M$ and $J$: $M_\ell + iS_\ell = M(ia)^\ell$. The quadrupole is then $-Ma^2$, and LIGO's ringdown spectroscopy and the Event Horizon Telescope both test it. So far it holds.

Wheeler's phrase and Bekenstein's objection. "Black holes have no hair" is Wheeler's, from around 1970; the story is that Feynman found it indecent. The physical content is an information puzzle from the start: a star has an enormous number of possible internal configurations, and the hole it collapses to has three parameters. Where did the rest go? Bekenstein's answer — the horizon area is an entropy counting them — is the subject of section 7, and the question of whether the information is really gone is the one the last slide leaves open.

Why the multipole relation is the sharp test. Geroch and Hansen showed that a stationary asymptotically flat vacuum spacetime is determined by its multipole moments $M_\ell$ (mass) and $S_\ell$ (current). For Kerr they collapse onto a single line: $M_\ell + iS_\ell = M(ia)^\ell$, so $M_0 = M$, $S_1 = Ma = J/c$, $M_2 = -Ma^2$, and every higher moment follows. Measuring $M$, $J$ and $M_2$ independently and finding $M_2 \ne -J^2/M c^2$ would falsify the Kerr hypothesis in one measurement. Extreme-mass-ratio inspirals seen by LISA are expected to do this to a part in $10^{4}$; they are also why the next section matters, since modelling those orbits needs Kerr geodesics to be integrable.

"Non-degenerate" is not a technicality. Extremal holes have $\kappa = 0$, no bifurcation surface, and an infinite throat; the uniqueness proofs' boundary analysis fails on them. They are also where the entropy counting of string theory works, so the excluded case is the interesting one — a recurring pattern.

what being one short would mean

the empirical fact

Carter found it in 1968 by the one method available: try to separate the Hamilton–Jacobi equation, and see what falls out.

Why $SO(3)$ collapses the problem and $SO(2)$ does not. Spherical symmetry gives three Killing vectors whose conserved quantities are the three components of $\vec L$; the vector $\vec L$ is constant, and $\vec r\cdot\vec L = 0$ confines the orbit to a plane. Axial symmetry gives one, so only the projection $L_z$ is conserved, and nothing stops the orbit wandering in $\theta$. Unit 08's central-force reduction used precisely the planarity that Kerr destroys.

"In involution" is the extra condition. Unit 17's theorem needs the $n$ constants to Poisson-commute, not merely to be independent. $\{H, E\} = \{H, L\} = \{E, L\} = 0$ here because $E$ and $L$ generate commuting flows (translations in $t$ and rotations in $\varphi$) that preserve $H$. Whether the fourth constant is in involution with the other three is a thing to check, and it is — see the Liouville–Arnold slide.

Kerr geodesics are integrable; Kerr is not integrable for everything. Two caveats worth holding. First, the statement is about geodesics: add a spin to the test body (the Mathisson–Papapetrou equations) and integrability is lost, with genuine chaos appearing in the numerics. Second, the two-body problem in general relativity is not integrable at all, which is why unit 38's waveforms need numerical relativity. Integrability is a property of this particular one-body problem, and it is a gift.

Killing tensors: the object that does it

Killing tensor

A Killing tensor of rank 2 on $(\mathcal M, g)$ is a symmetric $(0,2)$-tensor field $K$ — that is, a smooth field of symmetric bilinear maps $T_p\mathcal M\times T_p\mathcal M\to\mathbb R$, unit 16's definition — satisfying

$$\nabla_{(\lambda}K_{\mu\nu)} = 0 ,$$

the round brackets denoting the symmetrisation over all three indices.

what it conserves

$\;\mathcal K := K_{\mu\nu}p^\mu p^\nu$ is constant along every geodesic.

proof, one line

$\dfrac{\mathrm d\mathcal K}{\mathrm d\lambda} = p^\lambda\nabla_\lambda(K_{\mu\nu}p^\mu p^\nu) = (\nabla_\lambda K_{\mu\nu})p^\lambda p^\mu p^\nu = (\nabla_{(\lambda}K_{\mu\nu)})p^\lambda p^\mu p^\nu = 0$, the second step because $p^\lambda\nabla_\lambda p^\mu = 0$ and the third because $p^\lambda p^\mu p^\nu$ is totally symmetric. $\square$

rank 1 is a Killing vector

$\nabla_{(\mu}\xi_{\nu)} = 0$ is Killing's equation (unit 31), and it conserves $\xi_\mu p^\mu$ — unit 36's $E$ and $L$. The metric itself is always a Killing tensor, since $\nabla g = 0$; it conserves $g_{\mu\nu}p^\mu p^\nu = -\mu^2c^2$.

no group behind it

A Killing vector generates a one-parameter group of isometries, and its constant is unit 13's Noether charge for that symmetry. A Killing tensor generates nothing on $\mathcal M$: it is a symmetry of the geodesic flow on $T^*\mathcal M$ that is invisible in the isometry group. Hence the name hidden symmetry.

Walker and Penrose (1970): Kerr admits one, and it is built from the two repeated principal null directions that Kerr's Petrov type D supplies.

The symmetrisation, written out. $\nabla_{(\lambda}K_{\mu\nu)} = \tfrac13(\nabla_\lambda K_{\mu\nu} + \nabla_\mu K_{\nu\lambda} + \nabla_\nu K_{\lambda\mu})$, using that $K$ is already symmetric in its two indices so only three of the six permutations are distinct. The condition is weaker than $\nabla K = 0$ (a covariantly constant tensor, which forces reducible holonomy and is far too strong) and is exactly what the one-line proof needs.

Kerr's Killing tensor, explicitly. Let $\ell$ and $n$ be the two principal null directions, $$\ell^\mu = \Big(\frac{r^2+a^2}{\Delta},\,1,\,0,\,\frac{a}{\Delta}\Big),\qquad n^\mu = \frac{1}{2\Sigma}\Big(r^2+a^2,\,-\Delta,\,0,\,a\Big)$$ in Boyer–Lindquist $(t,r,\theta,\varphi)$ up to the usual factors of $c$, normalised so $\ell\cdot n = -1$. Then $K_{\mu\nu} = 2\Sigma\,\ell_{(\mu}n_{\nu)} + r^2g_{\mu\nu}$ is a Killing tensor, and $K_{\mu\nu}p^\mu p^\nu = Q + (L - aE/c)^2$ — Carter's constant, up to the piece built from the Killing vectors. Verifying $\nabla_{(\lambda}K_{\mu\nu)} = 0$ is a page of Christoffel symbols and is left to the reference (Chandrasekhar §62; Walker & Penrose, Commun. Math. Phys. 18, 265).

The Noether reading, for the learner who wants one. There is a Noether theorem here, but not on configuration space. On $T^*\mathcal M$, the function $\mathcal K$ generates a Hamiltonian flow that commutes with the geodesic flow; that is the symmetry. It is not induced by any diffeomorphism of $\mathcal M$, because it moves points of $T^*\mathcal M$ in a way that depends quadratically on the momentum. Unit 13's theorem was for point transformations; this is the canonical-transformation generalisation, and it is the reason "symmetry" in Hamiltonian mechanics is a larger notion than "symmetry of the space".

Where else hidden symmetries show up. The Runge–Lenz vector of the Kepler problem is the other famous one: it is conserved, it is not the Noether charge of any point symmetry of configuration space, and it is what makes the Kepler ellipse close. Kerr's Carter constant is its closest relative in this course.

unit 15's method on unit 14's geodesic Hamiltonian. Write $\mathcal E := E/c$ and $P := (r^2+a^2)\mathcal E - aL$

$$g^{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\mu W\,\partial_\nu W + \mu^2c^2 = 0 .$$

$$W = -E\,t + L\,\varphi + W_r(r) + W_\theta(\theta) .$$

$$\Delta W_r'^2 + W_\theta'^2 + \mu^2c^2\Sigma = \frac{P^2}{\Delta} - \frac{N^2}{\sin^2\theta},$$

where $N := L - a\mathcal E\sin^2\theta$.

Step 5 — expand the $\theta$ terms. The second fraction plus $\mu^2c^2a^2\cos^2\theta$ is

$$\Theta\cos^2\theta + (L - a\mathcal E)^{2},$$

where $\Theta := a^2(\mu^2c^2 - \mathcal E^2) + L^2/\sin^2\theta$.

Step 6 — it splits. What is left is a function of $r$ plus a function of $\theta$, so each is constant:

Carter's constant $$Q := W_\theta'^2 + \Theta\cos^2\theta$$

and then, automatically,

$$\Delta W_r'^2 = \frac{P^2}{\Delta} - \mu^2c^2r^2 - Q - (L - a\mathcal E)^{2}.$$

Inverting the $(t,\varphi)$ block and the two diagonal entries gives $$\Sigma g^{tt} = -\frac{(r^2+a^2)^2 - \Delta a^2\sin^2\theta}{\Delta c^2},\quad \Sigma g^{t\varphi} = -\frac{2r_gra}{\Delta c},\quad \Sigma g^{rr} = \Delta,\quad \Sigma g^{\theta\theta} = 1,$$ $$\Sigma g^{\varphi\varphi} = \frac{\Delta - a^2\sin^2\theta}{\Delta\sin^2\theta} .$$ Substituting, collecting and completing the square in $E$ and $L$ gives the displayed line exactly; the identity was checked symbolically and the check is the one the learner should repeat, since it is the only step in this example that is not two lines.

Four constants: Liouville–Arnold, and no chaos

Kerr geodesics are completely integrable

On $T^*\mathcal M$, $\dim = 8$, the four functions

$$H = \tfrac12g^{\mu\nu}p_\mu p_\nu,\quad E = -p_t,\quad L = p_\varphi,\quad Q$$

are independent and pairwise in involution. By unit 17's Liouville–Arnold theorem, the compact level sets are 4-tori carrying action–angle variables, and the motion is quasi-periodic.

why they commute

$\{H,E\} = \{H,L\} = 0$: Killing vectors preserve $H$. $\{E,L\} = 0$: $\partial_t$ and $\partial_\varphi$ commute. $\{H,Q\} = 0$: the Killing-tensor theorem. $\{E,Q\} = \{L,Q\} = 0$: $\mathcal L_{\partial_t}K = \mathcal L_{\partial_\varphi}K = 0$, since $K$ is built from $\Sigma$, $r$, $g$ and the principal null directions. $\square$

what it buys

Three physical frequencies $\Omega_r$, $\Omega_\theta$, $\Omega_\varphi$. Perihelion precession is $\Omega_\varphi - \Omega_r$, unit 37's $43''$ generalised; nodal precession is $\Omega_\varphi - \Omega_\theta$, zero exactly when $a = 0$.

the honest caveat

"No chaos" concerns compact level sets. Plunging and scattering orbits are not compact, and the theorem says nothing about them — but nor is there anything chaotic to find, since the separated equations solve those by quadrature too.

This is unit 15's payoff: a method introduced for its elegance is the only way anyone has found the fourth constant.

Independence, checked. The four differentials $\mathrm dH$, $\mathrm dE$, $\mathrm dL$, $\mathrm dQ$ must be linearly independent on a dense subset of $T^*\mathcal M$. $\mathrm dE$ and $\mathrm dL$ involve only $p_t$ and $p_\varphi$; $\mathrm dH$ and $\mathrm dQ$ both involve $p_r$ and $p_\theta$, and they are independent because $H$ contains $\Delta p_r^2/\Sigma$ while $Q$ contains no $p_r$ at all. They degenerate exactly on the equatorial orbits ($Q = 0$, $p_\theta = 0$), which is a measure-zero set and the standard place Liouville–Arnold tori degenerate.

Contrast with unit 17. Hénon–Heiles has two degrees of freedom and one constant too few, and the Poincaré section shows islands drowning in a chaotic sea as the energy rises. Kerr has four degrees of freedom and exactly four constants, so the section is a clean set of curves at every energy and spin. The difference is one function, and it exists here for a reason — the Killing tensor — that has no analogue in Hénon–Heiles. Unit 17's lesson was that integrability is exceptional; Kerr is one of the exceptions, and nobody predicted it.

Replace the region below the star's surface with the FLRW interior and the extension terminates there instead: $r = 0$ inside the star is a regular centre, a perfectly ordinary timelike worldline, until the surface passes it. So III and IV never appear, and no white hole and no second universe are predicted by any collapse.

Reading the right-hand diagram. Time runs upwards, light at 45°, and every point is a 2-sphere except on $r = 0$. The star's surface starts at $i^-$ (past timelike infinity: it has been sitting there forever in this idealisation), falls, crosses the horizon — the solid 45° line — and reaches the singularity. Region I is everything outside; region II is the hole. $\mathcal I^+$ is where light that escapes ends up; the horizon is exactly the boundary of the past of $\mathcal I^+$, which is the definition of an event horizon and is manifestly a global, teleological one: you cannot tell where it is without knowing the whole future.

The apparent horizon, which is local and different. The boundary of the trapped region on each slice is the apparent horizon, defined by $\theta_{\text{out}} = 0$ and computable from data on one slice. For Oppenheimer–Snyder it forms inside the star, before the surface crosses $r_s$, and it is a spacelike surface that coincides with the event horizon only after the collapse settles. Numerical relativity locates apparent horizons, because event horizons cannot be found until the simulation is over.

Kerr's diagram, for completeness. The maximal extension of Kerr is much worse: an infinite ladder of regions, a timelike ring singularity that can be avoided, a passage through $r = 0$ to $r \lt 0$, and closed timelike curves near the ring. Every one of those features lies beyond the Cauchy horizon $r_-$, and every one of them is believed to be destroyed by the mass-inflation instability of section 1. Congruences, and what expansion means

null congruence and its expansion

Let $k$ be a null geodesic field, $k\cdot k = 0$, $k^\nu\nabla_\nu k^\mu = 0$, filling a region. Pick an auxiliary null $n$ with $k\cdot n = -1$; the screen is the spacelike 2-plane orthogonal to both, with projector $h^\mu{}_\nu$. Decompose

$$h^\alpha{}_\mu h^\beta{}_\nu\nabla_\beta k_\alpha = \underbrace{\tfrac12\theta\,h_{\mu\nu}}_{\text{trace}} + \underbrace{\sigma_{\mu\nu}}_{\text{trace-free sym}} + \underbrace{\omega_{\mu\nu}}_{\text{antisym}} .$$

$\theta$ is the expansion, $\sigma$ the shear, $\omega$ the twist; the $\tfrac12$ is $1/(D-2)$.

what $\theta$ measures

If $\mathcal A(\lambda)$ is the cross-sectional area of an infinitesimal bundle of the congruence, then

$$\theta = \frac{1}{\mathcal A}\frac{\mathrm d\mathcal A}{\mathrm d\lambda} .$$
three examples

Flat, outgoing from a point. A sphere of radius $\lambda$: $\mathcal A\propto\lambda^2$, so $\theta = 2/\lambda \gt 0$.
Flat, ingoing toward a centre a distance $\lambda$ away: $\theta = -2/\lambda \lt 0$.
Schwarzschild, outgoing from radius $r$. $\theta \propto (1 - r_s/r)$: positive outside the horizon, zero on it, negative within.

checks

With $\lambda$ of dimension length, $k$ is dimensionless and $\theta$ is $\mathrm m^{-1}$ ✓. At $r\to\infty$ the Schwarzschild value $\to 2/r$, recovering flat space ✓.

Both expansions negative at once is the whole content of "trapped", three slides on.

Why the screen, and why $k$ is not enough. For a timelike congruence the orthogonal complement of the tangent is a 3-space and the decomposition is the familiar one (unit 22's rate-of-strain tensor, in fact, with $\theta$ the divergence). For a null congruence $k$ is orthogonal to itself, so the orthogonal complement contains $k$ and is three-dimensional and degenerate. Quotienting by $k$ — equivalently, projecting with the help of an auxiliary $n$ — gives a genuine 2-dimensional Euclidean space. The quantities $\theta$, $\sigma$, $\omega$ turn out not to depend on the choice of $n$, which is the technical fact that makes the construction worth doing.

Full proof that $\theta = \dot{\mathcal A}/\mathcal A$. Let $\eta_1,\eta_2$ be two deviation vectors in the screen, Lie-dragged along $k$, so $\mathcal L_k\eta_i = 0$ and hence $k^\nu\nabla_\nu\eta_i^\mu = \eta_i^\nu\nabla_\nu k^\mu$ (unit 31's $\mathcal L_XY = [X,Y]$). The area they span is $\mathcal A = \sqrt{\det(h(\eta_i,\eta_j))}$, and Jacobi's formula (unit 22, unit 35) gives $\dot{\mathcal A}/\mathcal A = \tfrac12\mathrm{tr}(h^{-1}\dot h)$, which on substituting the deviation equation is the trace of the projected $\nabla k$ — that is, $\theta$. The $\tfrac12\theta h_{\mu\nu}$ normalisation is chosen so this comes out with no factor.

The twist and Frobenius. $\omega = 0$ if and only if the congruence is hypersurface orthogonal — that is unit 31's Frobenius theorem, applied to the distribution orthogonal to $k$. The null geodesics normal to a closed 2-surface are automatically twist-free, since they are orthogonal to the family of surfaces obtained by dragging it. That is why the focusing theorem's hypothesis $\omega = 0$ is free in every application here.

Raychaudhuri, and focusing

Raychaudhuri's equation (null form; cited, Wald §9.2) $$\frac{\mathrm d\theta}{\mathrm d\lambda} = -\tfrac12\theta^2 - \sigma^2 + \omega^2 - R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu ,$$

writing $\sigma^2 = \sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}$ and $\omega^2 = \omega_{\mu\nu}\omega^{\mu\nu}$.

Term by term. $-\tfrac12\theta^2$: converging bundles converge faster. $-\sigma^2$: shear focuses. $+\omega^2$: rotation defocuses, the only term with the wrong sign. $-R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu$: gravity focuses, if matter obliges.

where the physics enters

Einstein's equation turns the last term into matter, the trace term dropping out on null $k$:

$$R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu = \tfrac{8\pi G}{c^4}\,T_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu ,$$

so "$\ge 0$" is the null energy condition.

focusing theorem

If $\omega = 0$ and $R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu\ge0$, and $\theta(0) = \theta_0 \lt 0$ at some point, then $\theta\to-\infty$ within affine parameter

$$\lambda \le \frac{2}{|\theta_0|} .$$
proof, three lines

The hypotheses give $\theta' \le -\tfrac12\theta^2$. Put $u = 1/\theta$: then $u' = -\theta'/\theta^2 \ge \tfrac12$, so $u(\lambda) \ge u(0) + \lambda/2$. But $u(0) = 1/\theta_0 \lt 0$, so $u$ reaches $0$ — that is, $|\theta|\to\infty$ — by $\lambda = -2/\theta_0$. $\square$

checks

$\theta$ is $\mathrm m^{-1}$, $\theta'$ and $R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu$ both $\mathrm m^{-2}$ ✓. Flat space, ingoing from a sphere of radius $r$: $\theta_0 = -2/r$, and the bound gives $\lambda\le r$ — exactly the distance to the focus ✓.

Why the last check is exact and not merely consistent. In flat space $\sigma = \omega = 0$ and $R = 0$, so the inequality is an equality: $\theta' = -\tfrac12\theta^2$ integrates to $\theta(\lambda) = -2/(r-\lambda)$, which blows up at $\lambda = r$. The bound $2/|\theta_0|$ is attained exactly when nothing but the $-\tfrac12\theta^2$ term acts, and every additional non-negative term makes focusing happen sooner. That is the whole logic of the singularity theorems: they never need to compute anything, only to bound.

What "$\theta\to-\infty$" is and is not. It is a caustic — a conjugate point, where neighbouring geodesics of the congruence cross and the cross-sectional area goes to zero. Nothing physical goes wrong there: the crossing point of a bundle of light rays at a focus is a perfectly ordinary event, and in flat space it is the centre of the sphere. The equation says the congruence fails to be a congruence past that point, not that spacetime ends. Turning caustics into incompleteness needs a global argument, which is the next slide but two.

Why the rotation term escapes. $+\omega^2$ is why a rotating dust cloud need not collapse to a point in Newtonian gravity, and it was the standing hope through the 1950s — Lifshitz and Khalatnikov argued that realistic collapse would be generic, rotating, and singularity-free. Penrose's theorem killed that hope by using null congruences orthogonal to a closed surface, for which Frobenius forces $\omega = 0$. Choosing the right congruence is the entire trick.

Raychaudhuri's own paper. Amal Kumar Raychaudhuri published the timelike version in 1955, working alone in Calcutta and motivated by exactly the question of whether the Big Bang singularity was an artefact of symmetry. Landau found it independently. It is the one equation in this section that is pure geometry — it is an identity about congruences, true on any pseudo-Riemannian manifold, with no field equation in it until you substitute for $R_{\mu\nu}$.

The four energy conditions

Each is a statement about $T_{\mu\nu}$ alone, with no field equation in it. For unit 35's perfect fluid, $T_{\mu\nu} = (\rho + p/c^2)u_\mu u_\nu + p\,g_{\mu\nu}$, they reduce to the right-hand column.

SEC, derived

With $u\cdot u = -c^2$: $T_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu = \rho c^4$ and $T = -\rho c^2+3p$, so the combination is $\rho c^4 + \tfrac12(-\rho c^2+3p)c^2$, that is

SEC $\not\Rightarrow$ WEC and WEC $\not\Rightarrow$ SEC

They are not ordered. DEC $\Rightarrow$ WEC $\Rightarrow$ NEC is the only chain. SEC implies NEC but says nothing about $\rho\ge0$.

NEC, derived. For null $k$, $g_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu = 0$, so $T_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu = (\rho + p/c^2)(u\cdot k)^2$, and $(u\cdot k)^2 \gt 0$ for any timelike $u$ and non-zero null $k$. Hence NEC $\iff \rho + p/c^2 \ge 0 \iff \rho c^2 + p \ge 0$.

WEC, derived. Take $u_{\text{obs}}$ timelike, boosted relative to the fluid with $u\cdot u_{\text{obs}} = -\gamma c^2$. Then $T_{\mu\nu}u^\mu_{\text{obs}}u^\nu_{\text{obs}} = (\rho+p/c^2)\gamma^2c^4 - pc^2 = c^4[\gamma^2\rho + (\gamma^2-1)p/c^2]$. As $\gamma\to1$ this is $\rho c^4$, requiring $\rho \ge 0$; as $\gamma\to\infty$ it is dominated by $\gamma^2(\rho c^4 + pc^2)$, requiring $\rho c^2 + p \ge 0$. Both together are also sufficient.

Why DEC has the odd second clause. $-T^\mu{}_\nu u^\nu$ is the energy–momentum flux measured by the observer $u$; requiring it to be causal (timelike or null, future-directed) says the energy does not stream past the observer faster than light. For a perfect fluid the flux in the rest frame is $(\rho c^2, 0,0,0)$ and in a boosted frame it acquires momentum $\propto(\rho c^2+p)v$; the causality requirement works out to $|p| \le \rho c^2$. DEC is the condition Hawking's area theorem and the positive-energy theorem use, and it is the strongest of the four for ordinary matter.

Their status, honestly. None of these is derived from anything. They are hypotheses imposed on $T_{\mu\nu}$ because all known classical matter satisfies them and because without some such hypothesis Einstein's equation has no content — any metric whatever solves it for a suitably chosen $T$. Which condition a theorem uses is therefore the most important thing about it, and the next slide is why.

quantum fields break all of them

Casimir's plates have negative energy density between them, and so do squeezed states of light. All four pointwise conditions fail; what survives is averaged: $\int T_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu\,\mathrm d\lambda\ge0$ along a complete achronal null geodesic.

Which condition a theorem uses decides whether it survives contact with the real universe.

The inflation entry, derived. A homogeneous scalar field has $\rho c^2 = \tfrac12\dot\phi^2 + V(\phi)$ and $p = \tfrac12\dot\phi^2 - V(\phi)$. Then $\rho c^2 + p = \dot\phi^2 \ge 0$ (NEC holds, always) and $\rho c^2 + 3p = 2\dot\phi^2 - 2V$, which is negative whenever $V \gt \dot\phi^2$ — the slow-roll condition. So inflation is, by construction, a SEC-violating epoch, and it has to be: unit 39 showed that $\ddot a \gt 0$ and $\rho c^2 + 3p \ge 0$ are incompatible.

The Casimir entry. Between two conducting plates a distance $d$ apart the renormalised vacuum energy density is $-\pi^2\hbar c/720d^4$, negative, and measured to a few per cent (Lamoreaux 1997, Mohideen–Roy 1998). The energy is negative relative to the unbounded vacuum, which is what the energy conditions compare against, so the violation is real and not a bookkeeping choice. This is the standing reason nobody believes the pointwise conditions are fundamental.

What is at stake. If NEC can be violated over macroscopic regions, traversable wormholes and warp drives become solutions of Einstein's equation — Morris and Thorne pointed this out in 1988 and it is why quantum energy inequalities became a research field. The known quantum energy inequalities (Ford–Roman) bound how negative an energy density can be and for how long: the larger the magnitude, the shorter the interval, with the product fixed by $\hbar$. Every bound proved so far is tight enough to forbid a macroscopic traversable wormhole, and none is a proof that all of them are. The subject remains open.

Trapped surfaces, and Penrose's theorem

trapped surface

A compact spacelike 2-surface without boundary, both of whose orthogonal null congruences have $\theta \lt 0$. If $\theta_{\text{out}} = 0$, it is marginally trapped.

None exists in flat space: a round sphere has $\theta_{\text{out}} = +2/r$. In Schwarzschild every sphere with $r \lt r_s$ is trapped — the outward front shrinks, not merely slows.

Penrose singularity theorem, 1965 (cited)

Let $(\mathcal M,g)$ be globally hyperbolic with a non-compact Cauchy surface, let $R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu\ge0$ for all null $k$ (the NEC), and let $\mathcal M$ contain a closed trapped surface. Then $\mathcal M$ is future null geodesically incomplete.

read the conclusion exactly

Some null geodesic cannot be extended past a finite affine parameter. It does not say a curvature invariant diverges, nor where, nor that the singularity is hidden.

why it was a shock

Before 1965 the consensus was that Schwarzschild's singularity came from exact spherical symmetry, which no star has. Penrose's proof uses no symmetry — only a trapped surface, an open condition that survives perturbation. Nobel Prize, 2020.

In one sentence: focusing makes $\partial J^+(S)$ compact; a non-compact Cauchy surface makes it non-compact. Notes for the full argument.

Where each hypothesis does its work. Trapped surface: supplies $\theta_0 \lt 0$ for both congruences, so the focusing theorem applies to $\partial J^+(S)$. NEC: makes the $-R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu$ term in Raychaudhuri non-positive, which is what lets focusing run. Global hyperbolicity: makes $\partial J^+(S)$ a well-behaved 3-manifold generated by null geodesics leaving $S$ orthogonally. Non-compact Cauchy surface: $\partial J^+(S)$ would project homeomorphically into it, and a compact set cannot map onto a non-compact one — the topological contradiction. Take away any one and the theorem is false: de Sitter space with a compact Cauchy surface has trapped surfaces and no singularity.

The Schwarzschild computation, since it is the example. The outgoing radial null geodesics from a sphere of areal radius $r$ have $\theta_{\text{out}} \propto (1-r_s/r)/r$ and the ingoing ones $\theta_{\text{in}} \propto -1/r$, up to a positive normalisation depending on the choice of affine parameter. So outside the horizon one is positive and one negative — an ordinary sphere — on the horizon $\theta_{\text{out}} = 0$, marginally trapped, and inside both are negative. The trapped region is exactly the black-hole interior, which is not a coincidence but is also not a theorem in general: the boundary of the trapped region is the apparent horizon, and it coincides with the event horizon only in stationary spacetimes.

How much matter it takes. Schoen and Yau (1983) proved that a trapped surface forms whenever enough mass is concentrated in a small enough region, with an explicit constant — so the hypothesis is not merely plausible but checkable from initial data. Combined with the theorem, that is the modern statement of gravitational collapse: concentrate mass past a threshold and a singularity is unavoidable, whatever the matter is doing, provided it obeys the NEC.

Christodoulou's refinement. In 2009 Christodoulou proved trapped-surface formation from vacuum initial data — a strong enough pulse of pure gravitational radiation focuses itself into a hole, no matter at all. That closes the last gap: general relativity produces singularities from its own field, without borrowing an energy condition from matter that might violate it.

Hawking's theorem, and where $\Lambda$ hits it

Hawking's cosmological theorem, 1967 (cited)

Suppose $R_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu\ge0$ for every timelike $u$ (the SEC, via Einstein); no closed timelike curves; and a compact spacelike hypersurface with $\theta \le -C \lt 0$ everywhere. Then every past-directed timelike geodesic is incomplete: the universe has a beginning.

the hypothesis $\Lambda$ breaks

$\Lambda \gt 0$ violates the SEC and satisfies the NEC. So:

Penrose's theorem is untouched — black holes in our accelerating universe still contain singularities.
Hawking's is not applicable — and the failure is not a technicality, because de Sitter space is geodesically complete. The conclusion is genuinely false under the violated hypothesis.

check the two conditions on $\Lambda$

NEC: $\rho_\Lambda c^2 + p_\Lambda = 0 \ge 0$ ✓, marginal — so Raychaudhuri's gravity term vanishes for null rays and focusing still runs on $-\tfrac12\theta^2$ alone.
SEC: $\rho_\Lambda c^2 + 3p_\Lambda = -2\rho_\Lambda c^2 \lt 0$ ✗ — and the geodesic congruence of de Sitter space defocuses, forever.

The cleanest illustration in the course of why a theorem's hypotheses are its content. "The universe began" follows from general relativity plus the SEC — and the SEC is measured to be false today.

what survives

The Borde–Guth–Vilenkin theorem (2003) replaces the SEC by a kinematic condition — positive average expansion along a past-directed causal geodesic — and still concludes past-incompleteness. No energy condition at all.

Why an inflating universe evades Hawking and not BGV. Hawking's argument focuses timelike geodesics using SEC; with $\Lambda \gt 0$ they defocus and no conjugate point forms. BGV instead argues that a past-directed geodesic observer in an expanding region sees the comoving matter blueshift without bound, and the affine parameter to the point where that blueshift diverges is finite. It is a much weaker statement — it says the inflating description breaks down, not that curvature diverges — and it holds in the multiverse scenarios inflation produces. It is the honest modern successor to the phrase "the universe had a beginning".

The de Sitter counterexample, made concrete. De Sitter space is the maximally symmetric vacuum solution with $\Lambda \gt 0$: $\mathrm ds^2 = -c^2\mathrm dt^2 + e^{2Ht}\mathrm d\vec x^2$ with $H = c\sqrt{\Lambda/3}$ in flat slicing, or a closed slicing that contracts and re-expands. In the global (closed) form it is geodesically complete, has compact Cauchy surfaces, and has no singularity anywhere. It satisfies every hypothesis of Hawking's theorem except the strong energy condition, and it is not singular. That single example is why the SEC clause cannot be dropped, and why the theorem's statement must carry it explicitly.

A caution about "the SEC is violated, so cosmology is fine". The SEC is violated today and during inflation. It is comfortably satisfied during the radiation and matter eras, which is where the classical Big Bang singularity would be. So the Hawking theorem remains a good argument about the hot early universe and a bad one about the universe as a whole. Distinguishing the two is exactly the kind of care that stating the hypotheses out loud enforces.

Why the area cannot decrease

Hawking's area theorem (1971)

In a globally hyperbolic spacetime obeying the NEC, with no naked singularities to the future, the area of the event horizon is non-decreasing along any spacelike foliation.

the argument, in four steps

1. The horizon is a null hypersurface generated by null geodesics with no future endpoint (they cannot leave it).
2. Suppose $\theta \lt 0$ at some point of a generator. By the focusing theorem — NEC supplies the hypothesis — a caustic forms within affine parameter $2/|\theta|$.
3. A generator that reaches a caustic leaves the horizon and enters its interior, so it has a future endpoint. Contradiction with 1.
4. Hence $\theta \ge 0$ everywhere on the horizon, and $\theta = \dot{\mathcal A}/\mathcal A$ gives $\delta A\ge0$. $\square$

the merger check, a real measurement

GW150914 merged holes of $36$ and $29$ solar masses into one of $62$ with $a_* = 0.67$: the mass fell by $3\,M_\odot$, radiated as waves. The area did not. In units of $16\pi(GM_\odot/c^2)^2$ the initial areas sum to $36^2+29^2 = 2137$ and the final one is $62^2\times\tfrac12(1+\sqrt{1-0.67^2}) = 3348$ — up by $57\%$ while the mass fell by $4.6\%$ ✓. P12 does this in full.

what breaks it

The NEC. Hawking radiation violates it — the horizon-crossing flux has negative energy — and an evaporating hole's area does decrease. The area theorem is a classical theorem, and the second law it stands for has to be generalised, which is the next slide.

Step 3, made precise. A point on a null generator of $\partial J^+$ beyond a caustic is connected to the surface by a timelike curve (the crossing of two null geodesics can be smoothed into a timelike one), so it lies in the interior of $J^+$, not on its boundary. The horizon being the boundary of the past of $\mathcal I^+$, its generators cannot have future endpoints — once on the horizon, always on the horizon. Those two facts contradict each other unless no caustic forms, hence $\theta\ge0$. The argument is Penrose's from the singularity theorem, run backwards: there the caustic produced the contradiction that proved incompleteness; here it produces the contradiction that proves monotonicity.

The GW150914 numbers, honestly. The masses are $36^{+5}_{-4}$ and $29^{+4}_{-4}$ solar masses with a final mass of $62\pm4$ and $3.0\pm0.5$ radiated (Abbott et al., PRL 116, 061102, 2016). The slide's inequality uses $A \ge 16\pi G^2M_{\mathrm{irr}}^2/c^4$ with $M_{\mathrm{irr}} \le M$, so writing $M^2$ for the initial holes overestimates their area and the conclusion is conservative — the true increase is larger. The LIGO collaboration published a direct test of the area law from the ringdown in 2021 and found it satisfied at $95\%$ confidence.

Why "no naked singularities" is in the hypotheses. The proof needs the horizon's generators to be well-defined and the spacetime to the future to be predictable; a naked singularity can emit anything, including generators that end. The area theorem therefore rests on cosmic censorship, which is a conjecture. This is the standard state of affairs in the subject: the physically important results are theorems modulo a conjecture nobody can prove.

Bekenstein and Hawking: the analogy becomes an identity

Bekenstein, 1972. Drop a box of entropy $S$ into a hole. The outside world's entropy falls and nothing outside compensates: the second law fails, unless the hole has an entropy of its own. The only candidate that never decreases is $A$. Dimensional analysis leaves one free number:

$$S_{\mathrm{BH}} = \alpha\,\frac{k_{\mathrm B}c^3A}{G\hbar} .$$

Hawking, 1974. Computed the spectrum a collapsing body radiates, using quantum field theory on the collapse background. It is thermal, at

Hawking temperature and Bekenstein–Hawking entropy (cited) $$T_{\mathrm H} = \frac{\hbar\kappa}{2\pi c\,k_{\mathrm B}},\qquad S_{\mathrm{BH}} = \frac{k_{\mathrm B}c^3A}{4G\hbar} .$$
the two results determine each other

Put $T_{\mathrm H}$ and $S_{\mathrm{BH}}$ into $\mathrm dE = T\,\mathrm dS$:

$$\frac{\hbar\kappa}{2\pi ck_{\mathrm B}}\cdot\frac{k_{\mathrm B}c^3}{4G\hbar}\,\delta A = \frac{\kappa c^2}{8\pi G}\,\delta A ,$$

which is exactly the first law's first term. So $\alpha = 1/4$ is forced once $T_{\mathrm H}$ is known, and $\hbar$ cancels out of the combination — the classical law survives untouched.

dimensions

$\hbar\kappa/ck_{\mathrm B}$: $\mathrm{(J\,s)(m\,s^{-2})/(m\,s^{-1}\cdot J\,K^{-1})} = \mathrm K$ ✓. $c^3A/G\hbar$: $\mathrm{(m^3s^{-3})(m^2)/(m^3kg^{-1}s^{-2}\cdot J\,s)}$, dimensionless ✓.

$\hbar$ appears in each of $T$ and $S$ and cancels in their product. That is the signature of a classical relation with a quantum origin.

Bekenstein's argument, in more detail. Lower a box of energy $E$ and entropy $S$ slowly to just outside the horizon and release it. By the rope argument of section 2 almost all of $E$ can be extracted as work, so the hole's mass barely rises and $\delta A$ is nearly zero; yet $S$ has left the universe. If $S_{\mathrm{BH}} = \alpha k_{\mathrm B}c^3A/G\hbar$, the requirement that $S_{\text{outside}} + S_{\mathrm{BH}}$ never decrease — the generalised second law — bounds $\alpha$ from below. Bekenstein got $\alpha \sim \ln2/8\pi$ from an information-theoretic argument about one bit; Hawking's calculation replaced the estimate with $1/4$.

The Euclidean argument, as an indication of how it goes. Continue $t \to -i\tau$ in the near-horizon Schwarzschild metric; the $(\tau, r)$ part becomes $\mathrm d\rho^2 + \rho^2(\kappa\,\mathrm d\tau/c)^2$ in a suitable radial variable $\rho$, which is flat polar coordinates — and is smooth at $\rho = 0$ only if the angle $\kappa\tau/c$ has period $2\pi$, i.e. $\tau$ has period $2\pi c/\kappa$. In quantum statistical mechanics, a field theory periodic in imaginary time with period $\beta\hbar$ is a thermal state at $T = 1/k_{\mathrm B}\beta$. Matching gives $T = \hbar\kappa/2\pi ck_{\mathrm B}$ immediately. It is a beautiful argument, it is three lines, and it is not available here: every step after "in quantum statistical mechanics" needs machinery this course has not built.

Three independent derivations, all agreeing. (i) Hawking's original Bogoliubov computation: the in-vacuum of the collapsing star is not the out-vacuum, and the mismatch is a Planck spectrum. (ii) The Euclidean periodicity argument above (Gibbons–Hawking 1977). (iii) The Unruh effect plus the equivalence principle: a uniformly accelerated detector in Minkowski space sees a thermal bath at $T = \hbar\alpha/2\pi ck_{\mathrm B}$ (Unruh 1976), and unit 30's Rindler observer is the near-horizon static observer. The third is the one this course comes closest to touching, since unit 30 built Rindler coordinates in full — and it still needs a quantum field to put in them.

Why the $T\propto1/M$ sign is the strange one. The hole's heat capacity is negative: absorbing energy makes it colder. That is the same instability as a self-gravitating gas sphere (unit 39's Jeans problem in disguise), and it is why a hole in equilibrium with a heat bath is unstable and why the microcanonical rather than canonical ensemble is the right one. It is also why evaporation runs away at the end: the last gram goes in under a nanosecond.

40 · the edge

Where this course stops, precisely

everything in this unit up to the four laws

Killing horizons, $\kappa$, $\Omega_{\mathrm H}$, the ergosphere, the Penrose ceiling, Carter's constant, Raychaudhuri, the energy conditions, the singularity theorems, the area theorem: all Lorentzian differential geometry plus Einstein's equation, all available with units 31–35 in hand.

$T_{\mathrm H}$ and $S_{\mathrm{BH}}$ are not

They need a quantum field on a curved background; a vacuum, which in a non-stationary spacetime is not unique; a Bogoliubov transformation between in- and out-modes; and a renormalised $\langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle$. That is a second course, and this one has deliberately not taught any of it.

three roads out, named

Quantum field theory in curved spacetime. Unruh's effect, then Hawking's. Birrell & Davies; Wald, QFT in Curved Spacetime and Black Hole Thermodynamics.
Numerical relativity. The two-body problem, not integrable and with no closed form; it produces unit 38's templates. Baumgarte & Shapiro.
Quantum gravity. What $A/4$ counts. String theory answers for extremal charged holes, loop quantum gravity with a tunable parameter, nobody for Kerr.

the open problem this leaves

Hawking radiation is thermal, so it depends only on $(M,J,Q)$: a hole formed from a book and one formed from a bomb of the same mass evaporate identically, and the difference is gone — which unitary quantum mechanics forbids. Fifty years on, the information paradox is unresolved.

Why a vacuum is the hard part. In Minkowski space the vacuum is picked out by Poincaré invariance: it is the state annihilated by the positive-frequency annihilation operators, and "positive frequency" means with respect to the global timelike Killing field. A general curved spacetime has no timelike Killing field, so there is no preferred splitting into positive and negative frequency, so there is no preferred vacuum and no observer-independent notion of a particle. Hawking's calculation works precisely because a collapse spacetime is stationary before and after — there are two natural vacua, and they disagree. The disagreement is the radiation. Everything in that paragraph requires a quantum field, and none of it can be faked classically.

What the paradox is not. It is not a claim that information loss is impossible; Hawking himself argued for thirty years that it happens and that quantum mechanics must give. It is not resolved by "the information is in the correlations", since Hawking's leading-order calculation shows the radiation is exactly thermal to all orders in the semiclassical expansion. The live proposal — the Page curve computed from replica wormholes and "islands", 2019–2020 — reproduces unitarity from a gravitational path integral, and whether it is a derivation or a consistency check is itself disputed.

An honest note on what a course can do. It would have been possible to state Hawking's formula on slide two and treat this unit as an exposition of results. The reason not to is the contract this course has kept for forty units: every claim proved or explicitly labelled with its source. Honouring it at the end means saying plainly that the last two formulas are borrowed, and naming what would have to be learnt to stop borrowing them. That is the difference between knowing physics and having read about it.

The running example, closed

what it is

A Kerr hole, $M = 10M_\odot$, $a_* = 0.9$. $r_g = 14.77$ km, $a = 13.29$ km, $J = 7.92\times10^{43}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2s^{-1}}$.

Horizons at $21.20$ and $8.33$ km; an ergosphere reaching $29.53$ km at the equator and pinching to the horizon at the poles.

what it does

Turns rigidly at $1013$ Hz. Drags every zero-angular-momentum infaller round with it. Forbids any observer inside its ergosurface from standing still.

Holds $15.27\%$ of its rest energy — $2.73\times10^{47}$ J — in a form that can be taken out, against a ceiling of $29.3\%$ for a hole spun to extremality.

what it hides

A fourth constant of motion, $Q$, from a Killing tensor with no isometry behind it. Its geodesics are integrable in unit 17's exact sense.

A temperature of $3.75$ nK and an entropy of $7.5\times10^{78}k_{\mathrm B}$, neither of which this course can derive — and a singularity that Penrose's theorem guarantees, from the NEC alone.

One object, seven sections, and one honest boundary. Unit 36 solved a metric; unit 37 measured three numbers with it; this unit set it spinning and found that the classical theory, pushed to its limit, hands you a thermodynamic system and no way to explain it.

Every number above was computed in the deck: $r_\pm$ in worked example 1 and the horizon slide, $\Omega_{\mathrm H}$ and $\kappa$ in worked example 2, the extractable fraction in worked example 3, $Q$ in the Carter section, $T$ and $S$ in worked example 5. None was quoted from anywhere.

A last thought about the shape of Part F. Unit 35 produced an equation from two independent routes; unit 36 solved it once, exactly; unit 37 turned the solution into three measurements; units 38, 39 and 40 each took the theory somewhere it was not designed to go — to radiation, to the whole universe, and to rotation. The third of those is where the theory stops being self-contained. That is not a defect of general relativity. It is the most interesting thing about it.
