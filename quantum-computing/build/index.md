# Every unit, in order: what it teaches. Nothing may be used before the unit that introduces it.

- **00 Why complex numbers: unbiased bases, and the spectral theorem for normal operators** (needs —; spine) — Complex inner-product spaces, sesquilinearity, the adjoint. Dirac notation as notation for the Riesz isomorphism V → V*: a bra is a dual vector, nothing more, and |u⟩⟨v| is a rank-one map. Hermitian, unitary and normal operators. Schur triangularisation, then the spectral theorem for normal operators proved from it; simultaneous diagonalisation of commuting normal operators. Functional calculus: f(A) for normal A, and e^iH unitary for Hermitian H.
- **01 The postulates, as mathematics: states, measurement, and evolution** (needs 00; spine) — Spectral projections and the Born rule as a probability measure on the spectrum; expectation and variance as ⟨ψ|A|ψ⟩ and its square. Stone's theorem in finite dimension, proved: every continuous one-parameter group of unitaries is e^−iHt for a unique Hermitian H — so the Schrödinger equation is not a separate postulate but the generator of the evolution postulate. The Robertson uncertainty relation ΔA ΔB ≥ ½|⟨[A, B]⟩| from Cauchy–Schwarz, and why it is a statement about preparations, not about disturbance.
- **02 One qubit: the Bloch sphere, and the group that rotates it twice** (needs 01; spine) — SU(2) and SO(3); the homomorphism SU(2) → SO(3) via conjugation of the Pauli vector, proved surjective with kernel {±I}. Consequently a 2π rotation multiplies a spinor by −1, which is observable in interference with an unrotated reference (neutron interferometry, 1975, cited). Every single-qubit unitary is e^iαR_z(β)R_y(γ)R_z(δ), proved.
- **03 Two systems: the tensor product, and the states that are not products** (needs 01; spine) — The tensor product defined by its universal property — the bilinear map V × W → V ⊗ W through which every bilinear map factors uniquely — then constructed, with uniqueness up to unique isomorphism proved. Bases and dimension; A ⊗ B on operators and the Kronecker matrix. The Schmidt decomposition, proved from the singular value decomposition, and Schmidt rank as the criterion: a pure bipartite state is entangled if and only if its rank exceeds one.
- **04 Density operators: what a subsystem knows, and why Alice cannot signal** (needs 03, 02; spine) — The partial trace, defined as the unique linear map with tr((A ⊗ I)ρ) = tr(A ρ_A) for all A, and uniqueness proved. Purification, proved to exist. The Schrödinger–HJW theorem: two ensembles give the same density operator if and only if they are related by an isometry, proved. The no-signalling theorem: nothing Alice does locally — unitary or measurement — changes ρ_B, proved in two lines from the definition of partial trace.
- **05 No-cloning, and how well two states can be told apart** (needs 03; spine) — Non-orthogonal states cannot be perfectly distinguished, proved. The Helstrom bound for two equiprobable pure states, ½(1 + √(1 − |⟨ψ|φ⟩|²)), proved by reducing to the plane the two states span. The optimal universal cloner's fidelity 5/6 stated (Bužek–Hillery, cited). Unambiguous discrimination stated here and proved in unit 21, when POVMs exist.
- **06 Bell, CHSH and Tsirelson: the classical bound, the quantum bound, and the gap between** (needs 04; spine) — Tsirelson's bound, proved: for ±1-valued observables C² = 4I − [A, A′][B, B′], so ‖C‖ ≤ 2√2. The CHSH game as a nonlocal game: 3/4 classically, cos²(π/8) quantumly. The Popescu–Rohrlich box reaches 4 while respecting no-signalling — so no-signalling is not what stops quantum mechanics at 2√2, and why it stops there remains a live question.
- **07 Teleportation and dense coding: the exchange rate between qubits, bits and ebits** (needs 04, 05; spine) — Resource inequalities as bookkeeping: 1 ebit + 2 cbits ≥ 1 qubit; 1 ebit + 1 qubit ≥ 2 cbits. The Holevo bound stated (cited here, proved in branch 08): one qubit alone never carries more than one bit, so dense coding is optimal. Average fidelity over the Bloch sphere as the figure of merit, and the classical measure-and-prepare benchmark of 2/3.
- **08 Entropy and the Holevo bound: how many bits a qubit holds** (needs 07; branch) — Relative entropy and Klein's inequality, proved. Typical sequences and typical subspaces; Schumacher compression stated with its rate S(ρ) and the proof's structure given. Entanglement entropy of a pure bipartite state as the unique measure of pure-state entanglement (stated).
- **09 Quantum key distribution: disturbance as a security guarantee** (needs 06, 05; branch) — The information–disturbance trade-off for BB84 states, proved for intercept-resend. The Shor–Preskill key rate 1 − 2h(Q) for quantum bit error rate Q, stated with its proof cited, and its reading: error correction costs h(Q), privacy amplification costs another. What QKD does not do: authenticate, which still needs a shared secret.
- **10 Circuits: reversible classical computation, and exact universality** (needs 03, 02; spine) — Every unitary on ℂ^d is a product of two-level unitaries, proved; Gray codes move each onto adjacent basis states; so CNOT plus single-qubit gates is exactly universal, proved (Barenco et al. 1995). Controlled-U from single-qubit gates and two CNOTs via the ZYZ form of unit 02. The counting lower bound: a generic n-qubit unitary has 4^n − 1 real parameters, so exact synthesis needs Ω(4^n) gates — most unitaries are not efficiently implementable, and the interesting ones are the exceptions.
- **11 Finite gate sets: density, Solovay–Kitaev, and why the T gate is the expensive one** (needs 10; spine) — The Solovay–Kitaev theorem: any gate to accuracy ε in O(log^c(1/ε)) gates, c ≈ 4 in the Dawson–Nielsen version, with the balanced group-commutator step proved and the recursion's analysis cited. Exact synthesis over Clifford + T (Kliuchnikov–Maslov–Mosca: entries in ℤ[1/√2, i]) and optimal z-rotations at about 3 log₂(1/ε) T gates (Ross–Selinger), both cited — the number fault tolerance will later charge for.
- **12 BQP, and where it sits among the classes you already know** (needs 11; spine) — Path sums and GapP functions; restricting to gates with rational or dyadic entries without loss. What is not known: BQP versus NP in either direction. What is known relative to oracles: BQP ⊄ PH for some oracle (Raz–Tal 2018, cited), and unit 18's lower bound gives an oracle with NP ⊄ BQP. The honest summary: quantum computing is a hypothesis about BQP ≠ BPP, and no proof of it is in sight, since it would separate P from PSPACE.
- **13 The query model, phase kickback, and the first separations** (needs 10; spine) — The Hadamard transform H^⊗n as the Fourier transform over ℤ₂^n, written via characters (−1)^x·y — the first appearance of the structure unit 15 generalises. Adversary-free classical lower bounds by counting information.
- **14 Simon's problem: the first exponential separation, and the algorithm Shor read** (needs 13; spine) — Linear algebra over 𝔽₂: orthogonal complements, rank, elimination. The probability that n − 1 uniform vectors span an (n−1)-dimensional space, bounded below by ∏(1 − 2^−k) ≈ 0.289, proved; expected number of runs below n + 1.
- **15 Fourier on finite abelian groups, the quantum Fourier transform, and phase estimation** (needs 14; spine) — The approximate QFT: dropping rotations below 2π/2^m costs at most O(n2^−m), so O(n log n) gates suffice (Coppersmith 1994, proved). Phase estimation: given U and an eigenvector, t ancillas give the eigenphase to the nearest t-bit fraction with probability at least 4/π² ≈ 0.405, and n + ⌈log₂(2 + 1/2ε)⌉ ancillas give n bits with probability 1 − ε, both proved.
- **16 Shor: factoring is order finding, and order finding is phase estimation** (needs 15; spine) — Continued fractions, and Legendre's theorem, proved: if |x − p/q| < 1/(2q²) then p/q is a convergent of x. Recovering r when gcd(s, r) > 1, by repetition and least common multiples. The hidden subgroup problem as the frame for Simon, Shor and discrete log; the abelian case solved, the dihedral and symmetric cases — lattices and graph isomorphism — stated as open.
- **17 Grover: search as a rotation in a plane** (needs 13; spine) — Unknown M: exponentially growing random iteration counts reach expected O(√(N/M)) (Boyer–Brassard–Høyer–Tapp 1998, proved). Quantum counting as phase estimation on the Grover rotation, joining this unit to unit 15. Applied to key search: AES-128 in about 2⁶⁴ sequential iterations, which is why the response is longer keys rather than new ciphers — and why the sequential depth, not the count, is the real obstacle.
- **18 Lower bounds: what quantum computers provably cannot do** (needs 17, 12; spine) — Symmetrisation (Minsky–Papert), proved, reducing symmetric functions to univariate polynomials. Approximate degree of OR at Θ(√N) (Nisan–Szegedy, Paturi, cited). For total functions, deterministic query complexity is at most a fourth power of quantum (Aaronson–Ben-David–Kothari–Rao–Tal 2021, cited): exponential speedups need a promise — which is exactly what Simon and Shor have and search does not.
- **19 Hamiltonian simulation: Feynman's reason, made into an algorithm** (needs 15, 11; spine) — Commutator scaling of Trotter error (Childs–Su–Tran–Wiebe–Zhu 2021, cited). Gate counts for a k-local Hamiltonian on n qubits. Qubitisation and quantum signal processing named as the optimal methods. Branch 20 is the other side of the coin: dynamics is easy for a quantum computer, ground energies are not. (Mechanics to Relativity unit 14 is the classical picture: a Hamiltonian generates a flow, and splitting it is how symplectic integrators work.)
- **20 QMA and the local Hamiltonian problem: Cook–Levin, quantum** (needs 12, 19; branch) — Membership: Arthur measures one randomly chosen term on Merlin's state, proved. Hardness, Kitaev's theorem that 5-local Hamiltonian is QMA-complete, proved: the Feynman–Kitaev history state Σ_t U_t⋯U₁|ξ⟩ ⊗ |t⟩ with a unary clock; the input, output, clock and propagation terms; the propagation term unitarily equivalent to the Laplacian of a path, with spectral gap 1 − cos(π/(T+1)) = Θ(1/T²), proved; soundness from the geometric lemma on the angle between two null spaces, proved. 2-local completeness (Kempe–Kitaev–Regev 2006) and one-dimensional chains (Aharonov–Gottesman–Irani–Kempe 2009) cited; the quantum PCP conjecture and the NLTS theorem (Anshu–Breuckmann–Nirkhe 2023) named.
- **21 Open systems: measurements and channels in full generality** (needs 04; spine) — The standard noise models — bit flip, phase flip, depolarising, amplitude damping (T₁), dephasing (T₂) — as Kraus families, and T₂ ≤ 2T₁ within this model, proved. Trace distance and fidelity; trace distance contracts under every channel, proved, so no processing can make two states more distinguishable.
- **22 The first quantum codes, and the conditions that say when any code works** (needs 21, 10; spine) — The Knill–Laflamme conditions, proved necessary and sufficient: P E_i†E_j P = α_ijP. The discretisation of errors, proved from linearity: a code correcting X, Y, Z on any one qubit corrects every single-qubit channel. Distance, and degenerate codes.
- **23 Stabilisers: codes as abelian groups, and the circuits a classical computer can run** (needs 22; spine) — The binary symplectic representation of Paulis, turning stabiliser questions into linear algebra over 𝔽₂. The Gottesman–Knill theorem, proved: Clifford circuits on stabiliser states with Pauli measurements are simulable classically in polynomial time — so entanglement alone does not make a computation hard, and the non-Clifford T gate is where the power is. The quantum Hamming bound for non-degenerate codes.
- **24 Fault tolerance: the threshold theorem, magic states, and the surface code** (needs 23, 11; spine) — Eastin–Knill, stated: no code has a transversal universal gate set. So T is injected: gate teleportation with the magic state T|+⟩, proved, and 15-to-1 distillation taking error p to 35p³ (Bravyi–Kitaev 2005, cited). The surface code on its lattice, drawn: distance d, about 2d² − 1 physical qubits, logical error roughly A(p/p_th)^(d+1)/2 with a threshold near 1 per cent. Google's 2024 below-threshold experiment, distance 3 to 7, cited with its suppression factor.
- **25 The physical qubit: driving a transmon, and the third level that will not stay empty** (needs 21, 02; branch) — Time-dependent Hamiltonians, evolved as a product of short-time exponentials with the error of the piecewise-constant approximation bounded. The truncated ladder operator, whose |1⟩ → |2⟩ element is √2: the drive couples to the leakage transition more strongly than to the qubit one, detuned only by α. DRAG (Motzoi–Gambetta–Rebentrost–Wilhelm 2009): a second quadrature proportional to the envelope's derivative over α, derived to first order. The drive also shifts the qubit's own frequency — an AC Stark shift — repaired by a detuning proportional to Ω²/α; the correction that minimises leakage is not the one that minimises gate error (Gambetta et al. 2011, cited). Unit 21's amplitude damping as a gate error: average infidelity t/3T₁ for short gates, proved.
- **26 Hardware and the near term: what exists, what it has shown, and what it has not** (needs 24, 17, 21; branch) — QAOA at depth one on triangle-free 3-regular graphs, analysed exactly: expected cut fraction ½ + 1/(3√3) ≈ 0.6925, against Goemans–Williamson's 0.878. Dequantisation (Tang 2018, cited): a claimed exponential speedup for recommendation systems that turned out to be a better classical algorithm waiting to be found.
