## Why this unit exists, and what it must not become
Unit 01 deferred the fourth postulate. This unit states it: the joint space of two systems is the tensor product H_A ⊗ H_B — not the direct sum — and the difference between 2n and 2ⁿ dimensions for n qubits is the whole of quantum computing's promise and its difficulty. The tensor product is defined the way a mathematician wants, by its universal property, then constructed, then computed with as Kronecker products. Entanglement is defined for pure states and decided by the Schmidt decomposition.

It must not become a course on multilinear algebra, and it must not reach for density operators: partial trace, mixed states and reduced states are unit 04's. Bell inequalities are unit 06's; teleportation and dense coding are unit 07's; say so where the singlet tempts. Measurement on a subsystem is projective, P ⊗ I, and nothing more general.

## Physics
Spontaneous parametric down-conversion, from nothing: a laser photon entering a nonlinear crystal occasionally splits into two lower-energy photons; with the right crystal cut (type II), the pair emerges in the polarisation singlet state (Kwiat et al., *PRL* 75, 4337, 1995, cited). Polarisation is a qubit — horizontal and vertical as |0⟩, |1⟩ — and a polarising beam splitter at any angle measures it in a basis; circular polarisers give unit 00's y basis. The experimental fact: measured in the same basis, whichever basis it is, the two photons always disagree. **Note the correction to the card's draft wording:** it is the singlet |Ψ⁻⟩ = (|01⟩ − |10⟩)/√2 that is perfectly *anti*correlated in every basis, because (U ⊗ U)|Ψ⁻⟩ = det(U)|Ψ⁻⟩; the state |Φ⁺⟩ = (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2 is perfectly correlated in every *real* basis but not in every basis — in the y basis it is anticorrelated. Say this on a slide; it is exactly the U ⊗ Ū invariance the problems prove.

Then the claim the unit can prove: no assignment of a separate pure state to each photon reproduces perfect anticorrelation in two bases (if |a⟩|b⟩ never agrees in z, one of a, b is |0⟩ and the other |1⟩; then in x they agree half the time), and neither does a random choice among such assignments, since each one would have to anticorrelate on its own. The full-strength version — no local hidden-variable model at all — is Bell's theorem, unit 06; say so, and do not claim more here.

## Mathematical tools
The tensor product of finite-dimensional complex vector spaces defined by its universal property: a pair (T, ⊗) with ⊗: V × W → T bilinear such that every bilinear map V × W → U factors uniquely through a linear map T → U. Uniqueness up to unique isomorphism proved (the standard two-diagram argument, every step). Existence by construction — for finite dimension, as the space of bilinear forms on V* × W* (with v ⊗ w the form (f, g) ↦ f(v)g(w)), or as the formal span modulo relations; one construction done fully, the other named. Bases: {eᵢ ⊗ f_j} is a basis, so dim V ⊗ W = dim V · dim W, proved. Not every tensor is a pure tensor: the rank of a tensor, and for ℂ² ⊗ ℂ² the 2×2 coefficient matrix whose rank it is.

Hilbert-space structure: ⟨v ⊗ w | v′ ⊗ w′⟩ = ⟨v|v′⟩⟨w|w′⟩ extends to an inner product, well-defined (via the universal property applied to a sesquilinear-in-pairs map, or via bases with an independence check). Operators: A ⊗ B defined by the universal property, (A ⊗ B)(C ⊗ D) = AC ⊗ BD, (A ⊗ B)† = A† ⊗ B†, tensor products of unitaries and of projectors are unitaries and projectors. The Kronecker matrix of A ⊗ B in the basis ordered by AUTHORING.md's convention (qubit 0 leftmost and most significant: |01⟩ is basis vector 1), with the warning that Qiskit's order is the reverse.

The Schmidt decomposition: every |ψ⟩ ∈ H_A ⊗ H_B is Σ_k s_k |a_k⟩|b_k⟩ with orthonormal {a_k}, {b_k} and s_k > 0, Σ s_k² = 1 — proved from the singular value decomposition of the coefficient matrix. The learner has the real SVD; derive the complex one in two lines from unit 00's spectral theorem applied to C†C. Schmidt rank and coefficients are uniquely determined (the s_k² are the eigenvalues of C†C); the Schmidt vectors are unique up to phases when the coefficients are distinct. Definition: a pure bipartite state is a product state if it is v ⊗ w, entangled otherwise; theorem: entangled iff Schmidt rank > 1. For two qubits, entangled iff det C ≠ 0.

The four Bell states, an orthonormal basis of ℂ² ⊗ ℂ² of maximally entangled states (both Schmidt coefficients 1/√2). Measuring one subsystem: the projective measurement {P_λ ⊗ I}; outcome probabilities and the post-measurement state of the other system, computed for the singlet.

The dimension count: product states of n qubits form a family with 2n real parameters (each qubit's pure state has two), inside a state space of real dimension 2^{n+1} − 2 (unit vectors in ℂ^{2ⁿ} modulo phase). For n = 2: 4 against 6; n = 10: 20 against 2046; n = 50: 100 against about 2.25 × 10¹⁵. Prove the count (a smooth-parametrisation dimension argument at the level of "count free real parameters" is enough; say so).

## The running example
**The photon pair from the crystal.** Overview: the anticorrelation in every basis, posed as a fact demanding a model. Section 1 (why not the direct sum): the direct sum ℂ² ⊕ ℂ² has dimension 4 too, but its states are "photon A *or* photon B"; joint outcomes need pairs, hence bilinearity. Section 2 (the tensor product): the singlet written in the product basis and as a Kronecker vector. Section 3 (product versus entangled): proof that the singlet is not a product and that no random choice of product states reproduces its two-basis anticorrelation. Section 4 (Schmidt): the singlet's Schmidt form in every basis at once, (U ⊗ U)|Ψ⁻⟩ = det(U)|Ψ⁻⟩. Section 5 (measuring one photon): measure A in any basis, and B is left in the orthogonal state — the anticorrelation derived. Close: the dimension count, and what it means that the singlet sits outside the product family.

## Worked examples (three to five, in `box ex`)
1. Kronecker products: X ⊗ Z and Z ⊗ X as 4×4 matrices in the course's basis order, and their action on |01⟩ — the ordering convention made concrete.
2. The singlet's anticorrelation in an arbitrary basis {|n⟩, |n^⊥⟩}, from (U ⊗ U)|Ψ⁻⟩ = det(U)|Ψ⁻⟩.
3. A Schmidt decomposition worked fully: |ψ⟩ = (|00⟩ + |01⟩ + |10⟩)/√3, coefficient matrix [[1, 1],[1, 0]]/√3, Schmidt coefficients φ/√3 ≈ 0.9342 and (φ − 1)/√3 ≈ 0.3568 with φ the golden ratio — squares (3 ± √5)/6 ≈ 0.8727, 0.1273 summing to one; the Schmidt vectors found.
4. The universal property used once in anger: the trace-pairing or a contraction map defined on V ⊗ V* by specifying it on pure tensors, with the check that it is well-defined.
5. (optional) The two-qubit determinant criterion applied to (|00⟩ + |01⟩ + |10⟩ + |11⟩)/2 (product: det 0, it is |+⟩|+⟩) and to |Φ⁺⟩ (det ≠ 0).

## What the problem sheet exercises
Ten to twelve problems; at least one proof and one ending in a number; referee checks where natural (Kronecker products, Schmidt coefficients).
- "Universal property": prove V ⊗ W is determined up to unique isomorphism; prove V ⊗ W ≅ W ⊗ V and (U ⊗ V) ⊗ W ≅ U ⊗ (V ⊗ W) by universal properties.
- "The determinant test": prove a two-qubit state is entangled exactly when its 2×2 coefficient matrix has non-zero determinant, and that |det C| ∈ [0, ½] with ½ exactly for maximally entangled states (2|det C| is the concurrence of a pure state — name it).
- "Schmidt-decompose" two or three given states, including one with equal coefficients where the Schmidt vectors are not unique.
- "|Φ⁺⟩ is invariant under U ⊗ Ū": prove it for every unitary U — the algebraic heart of unit 07 — and deduce the correlations of |Φ⁺⟩ in real bases and the anticorrelation in the y basis.
- "The singlet and det U": prove (U ⊗ U)|Ψ⁻⟩ = det(U)|Ψ⁻⟩.
- "No product, no mixture of products": prove no product state, and no random choice among product states, is perfectly anticorrelated in both z and x.
- "Counting": the real dimension of the product family and of the full pure-state space for n qubits; for n = 50, the ratio.
- "Operators on a factor": show (A ⊗ I) and (I ⊗ B) commute, and that a measurement on A alone never changes the probabilities of outcomes on B — the first glimpse of no-signalling, proved here for projective measurements only (unit 04 does it in general).
- "Kronecker bookkeeping": the matrix of CNOT₀₁ = |0⟩⟨0| ⊗ I + |1⟩⟨1| ⊗ X in the course's order, and the same gate in Qiskit's order — show the two matrices differ by the swap permutation.
- "Bell basis": show the four Bell states are orthonormal, each obtained from |Φ⁺⟩ by I, X, Z or XZ on qubit 1 (unit 00's matrices; unit 02 names them Pauli, and this unit does not need the name), and that Schmidt coefficients are invariant under local unitaries U ⊗ V.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You prove the universal property determines V ⊗ W up to unique isomorphism, and for any two-qubit state you decide entanglement and compute the Schmidt coefficients.
