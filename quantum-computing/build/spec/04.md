# Unit 04 — Density operators: what a subsystem knows, and why Alice cannot signal

- directory: `units/04-density-operators/`

- part thesis: Stern–Gerlach forces complex amplitudes, the Mach–Zehnder interferometer forces relative phase, the precessing spin forces unitary evolution, and two photons from one crystal force the tensor product. Part A ends with the density operator, the object that says what a subsystem knows when it is entangled with something you cannot see.
- tag: spine — the last unit of Part A

## Why this unit exists, and what it must not become
Half of a Bell pair is in no pure state at all, so the state space of unit 01 is too small. This unit enlarges it to density operators and proves the enlargement is forced, not chosen: two different preparation stories with the same density operator are indistinguishable by every measurement, so the operator is the state and the story is not. Then the partial trace, purification, the Schrödinger–HJW theorem and the no-signalling theorem — the tools Parts B and E stand on.

It must not become the theory of channels or generalised measurements: the evolution of an open system (Kraus, Choi, Stinespring) and POVMs are unit 21's, and the deck says so where it is tempting. Entropy is unit 08's. Distinguishing states (trace distance, Helstrom) is unit 05's — the deck may note a number in passing as a forward pointer, nothing more. Mixed-state entanglement (separability of density operators) is named and left open; it is not in the curriculum.

## Physics
Two preparation devices: one emits |0⟩ or |1⟩ by a fair coin, the other |+⟩ or |−⟩ by a fair coin. No measurement on the output — in any basis, any number of times on fresh copies — tells them apart: compute the statistics for an arbitrary basis on a slide and show they depend only on Σ p_i |ψ_i⟩⟨ψ_i| = I/2. The second source of mixedness: Bob's photon from unit 03's crystal, about which Bob knows everything that can be known locally — and it is in no pure state. Both lead to the same object.

Then the scenario that makes the stakes physical: Alice and Bob share |Φ⁺⟩ across a lab. Alice measures her qubit in z or in x. If she measures z, Bob holds |0⟩ or |1⟩ with probability ½ each; if x, |+⟩ or |−⟩ (|Φ⁺⟩ = (|++⟩ + |−−⟩)/√2 — check on the slide). Bob's density operator is I/2 either way, so Alice's choice is invisible to him: no signal travels, whatever the distance. That is the no-signalling theorem, and it is why entanglement does not contradict relativity — say so in one sentence, without developing relativity.

## Mathematical tools
Positive operators (A = A†, ⟨v|Av⟩ ≥ 0, equivalently non-negative spectrum — unit 00's spectral theorem; unit 00's sheet began this). Density operators: positive, trace one; the set is convex and its extreme points are exactly the pure states |ψ⟩⟨ψ|, proved; pure iff tr ρ² = 1, and tr ρ² ≥ 1/d always, with equality only at I/d. The postulates of unit 01 rewritten: Born rule Pr(λ) = tr(P_λ ρ), post-measurement state P_λρP_λ / tr(P_λρ), evolution UρU† — each reducing to unit 01's statement when ρ is pure. Ensembles {p_i, |ψ_i⟩} and the density operator they define; the same operator from different ensembles; the operator determines every measurement statistic (proof: statistics are traces against ρ).

For a qubit, the Bloch ball: ρ = (I + r·σ)/2 with r ∈ ℝ³, positive iff |r| ≤ 1 (eigenvalues (1 ± |r|)/2), pure iff |r| = 1 — unit 02's sphere is the boundary, I/2 the centre, and mixing is taking chords. Purity tr ρ² = (1 + |r|²)/2.

The partial trace, defined as the unique linear map tr_B: L(H_A ⊗ H_B) → L(H_A) with tr((A ⊗ I)ρ) = tr(A tr_B ρ) for all A; uniqueness proved from the non-degeneracy of the Hilbert–Schmidt pairing (A, B) ↦ tr(A†B), existence by the formula tr_B ρ = Σ_j (I ⊗ ⟨j|)ρ(I ⊗ |j⟩) and the check that it is basis-independent. tr_B maps density operators to density operators. For a pure state with coefficient matrix C (unit 03), ρ_A = CC† — which makes the Schmidt coefficients the square roots of ρ_A's eigenvalues. Hence a pure bipartite state is entangled iff its reduced state is mixed.

Purification: every ρ_A is tr_B of a pure state on H_A ⊗ H_B with dim H_B ≥ rank ρ_A, constructed from the spectral decomposition; any two purifications on the same H_B differ by a unitary on H_B (proved from Schmidt). The Schrödinger–HJW theorem (Schrödinger 1936; Hughston, Jozsa and Wootters 1993): {√p_i |ψ_i⟩} and {√q_j |φ_j⟩} give the same ρ iff √p_i |ψ_i⟩ = Σ_j u_ij √q_j |φ_j⟩ for a matrix u with orthonormal columns (padding the shorter ensemble with zero vectors makes u unitary), proved via purifications. Corollary, the steering form: every ensemble of ρ_B can be produced at Bob's end by Alice measuring her half of a fixed purification — for projective measurements when the ensemble has at most dim H_A members (general POVMs are unit 21's).

The no-signalling theorem: nothing Alice does locally — a unitary U ⊗ I, or a projective measurement whose outcome Bob is not told — changes ρ_B. Two lines from the defining property of the partial trace: tr_A((U ⊗ I)ρ(U† ⊗ I)) = tr_A ρ, and Σ_λ tr_A((P_λ ⊗ I)ρ(P_λ ⊗ I)) = tr_A ρ because Σ P_λ = I.

## The running example
**Alice and Bob sharing |Φ⁺⟩.** Overview: Bob's qubit, and the question "what state is it in?". Section 1 (density operators): the two coin-flip sources, and the theorem that Bob cannot tell them apart. Section 2 (the Bloch ball): Bob's state at the centre; the two ensembles as two diameters through it. Section 3 (partial trace): tr_A|Φ⁺⟩⟨Φ⁺| = I/2 by the definition, and all four Bell states give I/2. Section 4 (purification and HJW): |Φ⁺⟩ as the purification of I/2; the two ensembles related by the Hadamard matrix; Alice's choice of basis as her choice of ensemble at Bob's end. Section 5 (no-signalling): the theorem, and why Alice's measurement changes Bob's ensemble but not his state. Close: "the operator is the state, the story is not", with Bob's I/2 as the example.

## Worked examples (three to five, in `box ex`)
1. Two sources, one operator: statistics of both coin-flip sources in an arbitrary basis {|n⟩, |n^⊥⟩}, both ½, ½.
2. The partial trace of each Bell state by the defining formula: I/2 four times, on both sides.
3. A mixture not at the centre: ρ = (|0⟩⟨0| + |+⟩⟨+|)/2 = [[3/4, 1/4],[1/4, 1/4]]; Bloch vector (½, 0, ½), purity ¾; eigenvalues cos²(π/8) ≈ 0.8536 and sin²(π/8) ≈ 0.1464 (a forward pointer: unit 05's Helstrom number); a purification √0.8536|e₁⟩|0⟩ + √0.1464|e₂⟩|1⟩ written with its eigenvectors, and tr_B of it checked.
4. HJW in the smallest case: {|0⟩, |1⟩} and {|+⟩, |−⟩}, each with weights ½, related by u = the Hadamard matrix.
5. (optional) No-signalling computed rather than proved: Alice measures z or x on |Φ⁺⟩; Bob's post-measurement ensembles, each averaging to I/2.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Ten to twelve problems; at least one proof and one ending in a number; referee checks where natural (partial traces, purity, the HJW isometry).
- "Purify a given qubit state": for ρ = [[3/4, 1/4],[1/4, 1/4]] and for a second state, with the reduced state checked.
- "Find the unitary relating two ensembles" of I/2: {|0⟩, |1⟩} (weights ½) and the trine {|t_k⟩ = cos(πk/3)|0⟩ + sin(πk/3)|1⟩, k = 0, 1, 2} (weights ⅓). The isometry u, with rows (√(2/3), 0), (1/√6, 1/√2), (−1/√6, 1/√2) — check u†u = I and the relation entry by entry. This is the checkpoint's computation.
- "Entangled iff mixed": prove a pure bipartite state is entangled iff its reduced state is mixed.
- "The Bloch ball is exactly the qubit density operators": prove both inclusions.
- "Extreme points": prove the pure states are exactly the extreme points of the set of density operators.
- "Uniqueness of the partial trace": prove it from the non-degeneracy of the trace pairing.
- "No-signalling, both cases": prove it for unitaries and for non-selective projective measurements; then show that a *selective* measurement whose outcome Bob is told can change ρ_B — and explain why that is not a signal.
- "Purity bounds": tr ρ² ∈ [1/d, 1], equality cases; for the reduced state of (|00⟩ + |01⟩ + |10⟩)/√3 (unit 03's example), purity λ₁² + λ₂² = 1 − 2 det ρ_A = 1 − 2|det C|² = 7/9 ≈ 0.7778, checked against unit 03's Schmidt coefficients ((3 ± √5)/6 squared and summed).
- "Two purifications": given two purifications of the same ρ_A on the same H_B, find the unitary on H_B relating them.
- "Steering": from |Φ⁺⟩, which projective measurement must Alice make for Bob's conditional states to be |±i⟩? Show that Alice measuring in the y basis gives Bob the *other* member of the y basis than her outcome — because |Φ⁺⟩ is invariant under U ⊗ Ū, not U ⊗ U (unit 03).

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You prove the no-signalling theorem from the defining property of the partial trace, and exhibit the HJW isometry between two given ensembles for the maximally mixed qubit.
