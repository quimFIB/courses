## Why this unit exists, and what it must not become
Unit 03 showed what a pair of systems can hold; this unit says what a single unknown state withholds. Two theorems with one root, non-orthogonality: an unknown state cannot be copied, and two non-orthogonal states cannot be told apart with certainty — with the exact optimal success probability, the Helstrom bound, proved for two pure states. Both feed unit 07 (teleportation does not clone), unit 09 (eavesdropping disturbs) and unit 12's model of what a quantum algorithm can read out.

It must not become the general theory of state discrimination: mixed states, trace distance and POVMs are unit 21's. Unambiguous discrimination is stated here with its optimum and proved there. Approximate cloning is stated (Bužek–Hillery), not derived.

## Physics, and the history that is the argument
Herbert's FLASH (*Found. Phys.* 12, 1171, 1982): Alice and Bob share entangled photons; Alice measures her photon in one of two bases, which leaves Bob's photon in one of two kinds of state (|0⟩/|1⟩ or |+⟩/|−⟩ in unit 03's notation); a device that made many copies of Bob's photon would let him tell which kind, and so read Alice's choice instantly, at any distance. Within months, Wootters and Zurek (*Nature* 299, 802) and Dieks (*Phys. Lett. A* 92, 271) showed no such device can exist. Peres, a referee of Herbert's paper who recommended publication knowing it was wrong, later told the story ("How the no-cloning theorem got its name", 2003, cited) — worth one paragraph of notes: the error was productive. The physics of a "copier": a laser amplifier, which cannot clone a single photon's polarisation without noise (spontaneous emission; cited, one sentence).

## Mathematical tools
The no-cloning theorem: no unitary U on H ⊗ H and fixed blank state |s⟩ satisfies U(|ψ⟩|s⟩) = |ψ⟩|ψ⟩ for two non-orthogonal, non-identical states — proved twice. (1) Linearity: cloning |0⟩ and |1⟩ forces the wrong output on (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2. (2) Inner products: unitarity gives ⟨ψ|φ⟩ = ⟨ψ|φ⟩², so ⟨ψ|φ⟩ ∈ {0, 1}. The second proof is the sharper statement: a cloner for a set of states exists iff the states are pairwise orthogonal or equal. Then the converse: orthogonal states copy freely, and the unit 03 matrix CNOT₀₁ copies the z basis onto a blank |0⟩ — a classical copier; the theorem is precisely about non-orthogonality. Ancillas and general devices: allowing U to act on an extra environment |e⟩ changes nothing (same inner-product argument), which covers every physical device modelled as unitary on something.

Distinguishing states: non-orthogonal states cannot be perfectly distinguished by any projective measurement, proved (a measurement that always identified |ψ⟩ would need its "ψ" projector to give probability 1 on ψ and 0 on φ, forcing orthogonality). The Helstrom bound (Helstrom 1976, cited for the general theory) for two equiprobable pure states: the best success probability is ½(1 + √(1 − |⟨ψ|φ⟩|²)), proved by reducing to the two-dimensional span (any measurement's projectors restricted to the span; the optimum is a basis of the span symmetric about the bisector of the two states), with the optimal measurement exhibited. Unambiguous discrimination stated (Ivanovic 1987, Dieks 1988, Peres 1988): never wrong, sometimes "don't know", best success 1 − |⟨ψ|φ⟩| — 0.2929 for |0⟩ and |+⟩ — proved in unit 21 when POVMs exist. The optimal universal symmetric 1 → 2 cloner reaches fidelity 5/6 (Bužek and Hillery, *PRA* 54, 1844, 1996, cited).

## The running example
**|0⟩ versus |+⟩**, the two states Bob's photon can be left in under FLASH. Overview: "Bob holds one of these two; can he copy it, and how well can he tell which?". Section 1 (no-cloning): the two proofs, run on this pair — ⟨0|+⟩ = 1/√2 ≠ (1/√2)². Section 2 (what copies): CNOT copies |0⟩ and |1⟩ and fails on |+⟩, producing an entangled state instead of |+⟩|+⟩ — computed. Section 3 (distinguishing): measure in z (success ¾), then the Helstrom-optimal basis, the real unit vectors at angles −π/8 and 3π/8, which succeeds with probability cos²(π/8) = ½(1 + 1/√2) ≈ 0.8536; drawn in the plane. Section 4 (FLASH killed): why a copier would have let Bob signal, and why its impossibility closes the loophole. Close: 0.8536, the measurement, and the remark that 0.8536 reappears in unit 06 as the CHSH game value — the same angle π/8 for a related reason.

## Worked examples (three to five, in `box ex`)
1. No-cloning by inner products, on |0⟩ and |+⟩, every step.
2. CNOT on |+⟩|0⟩: (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2, not |+⟩|+⟩ = (|00⟩ + |01⟩ + |10⟩ + |11⟩)/2; the overlap between them, 1/√2, computed.
3. Helstrom for |0⟩ versus |+⟩: the z-basis measurement's ¾, then the optimal basis and 0.8536, with the error probability 0.1464 and the sum checked.
4. The general Helstrom formula for |0⟩ versus cos(θ/2)|0⟩ + sin(θ/2)|1⟩: success ½(1 + sin(θ/2)), checked at θ = 0 (½) and θ = π (1).

## What the problem sheet exercises
Ten to twelve problems; at least one proof and one ending in a number; referee checks where natural.
- "Finish off FLASH": show that a perfect cloner would let Alice signal through a shared entangled pair, completing the refutation with unit 03's statement that a local projective measurement does not change the other side's outcome probabilities. (Not unit 04's theorem — this unit does not need 04.)
- "No-deleting" (Pati and Braunstein, *Nature* 404, 164, 2000): no unitary takes |ψ⟩|ψ⟩|A⟩ to |ψ⟩|0⟩|A_ψ⟩ for all ψ — prove it by the inner-product method.
- "Copying a known set": for which finite sets of states does a cloner exist? (Pairwise orthogonal or equal — prove both directions.)
- "Helstrom at angle θ": the success probability for two states with |⟨ψ|φ⟩| = cos(θ/2), and the optimal measurement as a function of θ.
- "Unequal priors": with priors p and 1 − p, the optimal success ½(1 + √(1 − 4p(1 − p)|⟨ψ|φ⟩|²)) — derive it in the two-dimensional span; evaluate for |0⟩, |+⟩ and p = 0.9 (0.9528, against 0.9 for always guessing |0⟩).
- "Many copies": with N copies of the unknown state the overlap becomes |⟨ψ|φ⟩|^N; how many copies of |0⟩ or |+⟩ are needed for 99 per cent success? (Success ½(1 + √(1 − 2^{−N})): 0.8536, 0.9330, 0.9677, 0.9841 for N = 1 to 4, and 0.9921 at N = 5, the least N reaching 0.99.)
- "Ancillas do not help copying": prove the inner-product argument survives an environment.
- "Unambiguous discrimination, stated": verify, for |0⟩ and |+⟩, that the claimed optimum 1 − 1/√2 is below Helstrom's 0.8536, and explain in words why "never wrong" costs success.
- "Cloning fidelity": the trivial strategy (measure in a random basis and prepare two copies of the outcome) and its average fidelity; compare with the cited 5/6.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You prove no-cloning both ways and prove the Helstrom bound, producing 0.8536 and the optimal measurement for |0⟩ versus |+⟩.
