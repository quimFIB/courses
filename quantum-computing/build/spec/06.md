## Why this unit exists, and what it must not become
Unit 03 showed the singlet's correlations cannot come from separate pure states; this unit proves the far stronger statement — no local hidden-variable model at all reproduces them — and then proves the matching upper bound that quantum mechanics itself obeys. Both bounds are short, exact proofs, and the gap between 2 and 2√2 is the unit's subject. Unit 00's caveat (that real quantum theory is ruled out only by a multi-party experiment) is cashed here in one slide, cited.

It must not become a debate about interpretations (excluded by the curriculum: the unit takes Bell's theorem as far as its hypotheses and stops) or a survey of all Bell inequalities. Measurements are projective (±1-valued observables A with A² = I); say that general measurements are unit 21's and that Tsirelson's bound survives them (via Naimark dilation, cited forward).

## Physics
Two labs, a source sending one particle of a singlet to each, and at each lab a choice between two measurement settings, made after the particles left. Describe polarisation analysers at angles as the measurement of cos θ Z + sin θ X on a polarisation qubit (unit 02's rotation of the measurement axis, restricted to a plane). The experiments, honestly: Clauser and Freedman 1972 (the first test); Aspect, Dalibard and Roger 1982 (*PRL* 49, 1804, settings switched during flight); the three loophole-free tests of 2015 — Delft (Hensen et al., *Nature* 526, 682: electron spins 1.3 km apart, 245 trials, S = 2.42 ± 0.20, p = 0.039), Vienna and NIST (Giustina et al. and Shalm et al., *PRL* 115, 250401 and 250402); the 2022 Nobel prize to Aspect, Clauser and Zeilinger. Name the loopholes the 2015 tests closed (locality: settings chosen space-like separated; detection: no fair-sampling assumption) and the one no experiment can close (free choice of settings — "superdeterminism"), as a hypothesis of the theorem, not a hole in its proof.

## Mathematical tools
A local hidden-variable model, as mathematics: a set Λ with a probability distribution (finite or countable is enough for the proof; say that the general measure-theoretic version is the same proof with integrals), and response functions A(a, λ), B(b, λ) ∈ {±1} depending only on the local setting and λ. The correlation E(a, b) = 𝔼_λ[A(a, λ)B(b, λ)]. The CHSH quantity S = E(a, b) + E(a, b′) + E(a′, b) − E(a′, b′) (Clauser, Horne, Shimony and Holt, *PRL* 23, 880, 1969), |S| ≤ 2 in every such model — proved by bounding pointwise (A(b + b′) + A′(b − b′) = ±2 for ±1 values) and averaging; and equivalently by reducing to deterministic strategies (the distribution is a mixture of them, and S is linear). Stochastic local responses reduce to deterministic ones by enlarging λ — proved.

The quantum side. For ±1-valued observables (Hermitian, A² = I) on each side, the CHSH operator C = A⊗B + A⊗B′ + A′⊗B − A′⊗B′, and its value ⟨ψ|C|ψ⟩ (or tr(ρC), unit 04). The singlet's correlation for coplanar settings, E(θ_a, θ_b) = −cos(θ_a − θ_b), derived. The optimal angles (a = 0, a′ = π/2, b = π/4, b′ = −π/4, or their negatives) giving |S| = 2√2, derived by maximising — not just exhibited. Tsirelson's bound (Cirel'son, *Lett. Math. Phys.* 4, 93, 1980): C² = 4I − [A, A′]⊗[B, B′], so ‖C‖² ≤ 4 + ‖[A, A′]‖‖[B, B′]‖ ≤ 8, proved for every state including mixed and every dimension. For the qubit settings above, C has eigenvalues 2√2, −2√2, 0, 0, the two extreme ones non-degenerate — so each of S = ±2√2 is attained by exactly one state, and with these settings the singlet is the one giving −2√2 (sign conventions: say once that |S| is what matters). Unit 09 uses this uniqueness.

The CHSH game as a nonlocal game: referee sends uniform bits x, y; players return a, b; they win if a ⊕ b = x ∧ y. Classical value 3/4 (from S ≤ 2 via win = ½ + S/8, or directly by checking the 16 deterministic strategies); quantum value ½ + √2/4 = cos²(π/8) ≈ 0.8536, optimal by Tsirelson. The Popescu–Rohrlich box (*Found. Phys.* 24, 379, 1994): a correlation with S = 4 that respects no-signalling — so no-signalling (unit 04) is not what stops quantum mechanics at 2√2, and why it stops there (information causality, macroscopic locality, and others — named, cited) remains a live question.

Real versus complex, paying unit 00's debt as a citation: Renou et al. (*Nature* 600, 625, 2021) showed a three-party network Bell-type experiment whose quantum prediction no real-Hilbert-space theory reproduces; its 2022 tests (*PRL* 128, 040402 and 040403). One slide, stated, not proved.

## The running example
**The CHSH game played with a singlet.** Overview: two players who cannot communicate, a referee, the rules; "how often can they win?". Section 1 (local hidden variables): the model as mathematics; the deterministic strategies; 3/4. Section 2 (the bound): |S| ≤ 2 proved; win = ½ + S/8. Section 3 (the singlet): E = −cos(θ_a − θ_b), the angles, S = 2√2, win 0.8536 — drawn as the four analyser directions in the plane. Section 4 (Tsirelson): nothing quantum does better; the operator identity. Section 5 (the experiments and the PR box): Delft's S = 2.42 ± 0.20 as a game won 0.8025 of the time (½ + 2.42/8), and the box that would win always. Close: 3/4 < 0.8536 < 1, and which hypothesis each gap breaks.

## Worked examples (three to five, in `box ex`)
1. The classical bound by enumerating deterministic strategies for the CHSH game: all 16 checked to win at most 3 of the 4 input pairs.
2. The singlet's correlation E(θ_a, θ_b) = −cos(θ_a − θ_b), from the singlet's invariance (unit 03) or by direct computation; checked at θ_a = θ_b (−1) and at a right angle (0).
3. Choosing the angles: maximise |S| over coplanar settings, reducing to two angle differences; 2√2 at the angles above, and for contrast the value 0 at the "obvious" choice a = b = 0, a′ = b′ = π/2 (computed: −1 + 0 + 0 + 1) — the naive settings show no violation at all.
4. Tsirelson's identity C² = 4I − [A, A′]⊗[B, B′] multiplied out, every term.
5. (optional) The PR box: its conditional distribution, the no-signalling marginals checked, S = 4.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Ten to twelve problems; at least one proof and one ending in a number; referee checks where natural (expectation values, operator norms).
- "Deterministic strategies suffice": prove that any local hidden-variable model's CHSH value is an average of deterministic models' values, hence |S| ≤ 2.
- "The PR box is no-signalling": prove it, and show its S = 4.
- "The game value is optimal": prove cos²(π/8) ≈ 0.8536 is the best quantum winning probability, from Tsirelson.
- "Read Delft critically": with S = 2.42 ± 0.20, how many standard deviations above 2 (2.1); why the authors reported a p-value of 0.039 from a test that does not assume independent, identically distributed trials rather than a Gaussian sigma count; what 245 trials can and cannot establish.
- "Mixed states never beat 2√2, and noise kills the violation": for the Werner state p|Ψ⁻⟩⟨Ψ⁻| + (1 − p)I/4, S_max = 2√2 p, so a violation needs p > 1/√2 ≈ 0.7071 (computed).
- "Non-coplanar settings": E(a, b) = −a·b for unit vectors a, b on the Bloch sphere (unit 02), and why the optimum is coplanar.
- "Uniqueness at the top": for the unit's four qubit observables, show that ±2√2 are non-degenerate eigenvalues of C, so each extreme value is attained by exactly one state, and identify both.
- "Bell's original inequality" (Bell, *Physics* 1, 195, 1964): derive it from the CHSH bound under perfect anticorrelation, and find settings where the singlet violates it.
- "Stochastic responses": show that letting A(a, λ) be random given λ adds nothing.
- "Product states never violate": prove |S| ≤ 2 for every product state and every separable mixture (unit 04's convex combinations of product states).

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You prove CHSH ≤ 2 for every local hidden-variable model and ≤ 2√2 for every quantum strategy, and produce measurement directions on the singlet that attain 2√2.
