## Why this unit exists, and what it must not become
Entanglement as a resource with an exchange rate. One shared ebit plus two classical bits moves one qubit (teleportation); one ebit plus one qubit moves two classical bits (dense coding); and neither trade can be improved. The derivations are one identity each, and the unit's work is in what they do not do: teleportation does not clone (unit 05), and does not signal (unit 04). The classical benchmark of 2/3 makes "something quantum happened" a measurable claim.

It must not become a resource-theory course or a survey of quantum networks. Channels (noisy pairs as channels) are unit 21's; here a noisy pair is a density operator, a mixture, and nothing more. The Holevo bound is cited, and proved in branch 08.

## Physics
Teleportation (Bennett et al., *PRL* 70, 1895, 1993); dense coding (Bennett and Wiesner, *PRL* 69, 2881, 1992). The experiments: Innsbruck 1997 (Bouwmeester et al., *Nature* 390, 575 — photon polarisation, with the honest caveat that its Bell measurement succeeded a quarter of the time, a limit of linear optics); Micius 2017 (Ren et al., *Nature* 549, 70 — ground to satellite, up to about 1400 km). Entanglement swapping (Żukowski et al., *PRL* 71, 4287, 1993; first demonstrated by Pan et al., 1998) as the primitive of a quantum repeater, one sentence. Nothing travels faster than light: Bob's state before the two bits arrive is I/2 whatever Alice's input — unit 04's no-signalling, now with a protocol to apply it to.

## Mathematical tools
The teleportation identity: |ψ⟩_C ⊗ |Φ⁺⟩_{AB} = ½ Σ over the four Bell states |β⟩_{CA} ⊗ (σ_β|ψ⟩)_B, where σ_β ∈ {I, X, Z, XZ} up to signs — derived by expanding |ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩ and regrouping in the Bell basis, every term shown. The protocol: Alice's Bell measurement (as CNOT then H then a z-basis measurement, which is the Bell basis rotated to the computational basis — derived), two bits sent, Bob's correction σ_β^{-1}. Each of the four outcomes has probability ¼ independent of ψ — which is why Alice learns nothing about ψ and the original is destroyed, so no-cloning survives. Bob's density operator before the bits arrive: the average over the four branches, ¼ Σ σ_β|ψ⟩⟨ψ|σ_β† = I/2, proved (the twirl over the Pauli group) — so no-signalling survives.

Dense coding: Alice applies one of I, X, Z, XZ to her half of |Φ⁺⟩, producing the four Bell states; Bob's Bell measurement reads two bits. Resource inequalities as bookkeeping: 1 ebit + 2 cbits ≥ 1 qubit; 1 ebit + 1 qubit ≥ 2 cbits; and 1 qubit ≥ 1 cbit trivially. Optimality: the Holevo bound (stated here, proved in branch 08) says one qubit alone carries at most one bit, so dense coding's two bits need the ebit; and teleportation needs two bits, because otherwise combining it with dense coding would send more than two bits per qubit — the argument given in full.

Average fidelity as the figure of merit: F = average over input states ψ of ⟨ψ|ρ_out|ψ⟩, with ψ uniform on the Bloch sphere (unit 02). Compute averages over the sphere without integrals where possible: by symmetry ⟨x²⟩ = ⟨y²⟩ = ⟨z²⟩ = ⅓ for a uniform unit vector — proved from rotation invariance plus x² + y² + z² = 1, with the integral as a check in the notes. The classical measure-and-prepare benchmark: measuring in z and sending the outcome gives fidelity cos⁴(θ/2) + sin⁴(θ/2) = 1 − ½ sin²θ for an input at polar angle θ, averaging to 1 − ½ · ⅔ = 2/3; Massar and Popescu (*PRL* 74, 1259, 1995) proved no classical strategy (no shared entanglement, classical message only) beats 2/3 — cited. So a measured average fidelity above 2/3 certifies that entanglement was used.

## The running example
**Teleporting one qubit, |ψ⟩ = cos(θ/2)|0⟩ + e^{iφ} sin(θ/2)|1⟩, from Alice to Bob.** Overview: the problem — move an unknown qubit with only a phone line and a shared Bell pair. Section 1 (the identity): the regrouping, in full. Section 2 (the protocol): the diagram, all four branches traced to Bob's corrected state. Section 3 (what does not happen): Alice's outcomes uniform, the original destroyed (no-cloning), Bob at I/2 until the bits arrive (no-signalling). Section 4 (the reverse trade): dense coding, and the resource inequalities. Section 5 (benchmarks): the 2/3 fidelity of measure-and-prepare, and Innsbruck's and Micius's measured fidelities against it (read the papers' reported values; quote them only with their sources). Close: teleportation as the exchange 1 ebit + 2 cbits → 1 qubit, with each of the three resources shown to be necessary.

## Worked examples (three to five, in `box ex`)

3. Bob's state before the bits: ¼ Σ σ_β|ψ⟩⟨ψ|σ_β† = I/2, multiplied out for ψ = |0⟩ and then in general via the Bloch vector (each Pauli flips two components).
4. Measure-and-prepare's average fidelity 2/3 by the symmetry argument.
5. (optional) Dense coding of the bits 10: the operation, the Bell state, Bob's measurement.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Ten to twelve problems; at least one proof and one ending in a number; referee checks where natural (the four branches, swapping, the noisy pair).
- "The classical benchmark": show the measure-in-z-and-resend strategy has average fidelity 1 − ½⟨sin²θ⟩ = 2/3, and that measuring in a random basis does no better; cite Massar–Popescu for the optimality of 2/3.
- "Entanglement swapping": teleport half of a Bell pair and show the two never-interacting particles end up in a Bell state — every branch.
- "Teleporting through a noisy pair": with the Werner state p|Φ⁺⟩⟨Φ⁺| + (1 − p)I/4 as the shared resource and the standard protocol, the average fidelity is (1 + p)/2, beating 2/3 exactly when p > 1/3; at p = 0.5 it is 0.75 (computed).
- "Why two bits": prove that teleportation with one classical bit is impossible, via the dense-coding/Holevo argument.
- "Dense coding is optimal": with Holevo (cited), no protocol sends three bits with one ebit and one qubit.
- "Uniform outcomes": prove Alice's four outcomes are equiprobable for every input, and explain why this is necessary for no-signalling.
- "Teleporting an entangled half": teleport qubit C of a state entangled with a fourth system R, and show the entanglement with R is transferred to Bob.
- "The Bell measurement as a circuit": show CNOT then H maps the Bell basis to the computational basis, giving the bit labels.
- "Fidelity of doing nothing": Bob outputs I/2 regardless; average fidelity ½ — the floor below the classical benchmark.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You derive teleportation with all four corrections, prove Bob's state is I/2 until the bits arrive, and compute the 2/3 classical benchmark.
