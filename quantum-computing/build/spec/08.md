## Why this unit exists, and what it must not become
A qubit has a continuum of states and yields one bit. This unit makes that precise: Shannon entropy as the answer to "how many bits" for a classical source, von Neumann entropy as its quantum successor, and the Holevo bound — the theorem unit 07 cited for the optimality of dense coding. Its proof is the unit's spine: strong subadditivity, stated and cited, is used in the form "discarding a subsystem never increases mutual information", and the Holevo bound follows by modelling a measurement as a unitary followed by discarding.

It must not become a course in quantum Shannon theory: channel capacities, the HSW theorem and distillation rates are excluded by the curriculum. Schumacher compression is stated with its rate and the shape of its proof, not proved in full. Strong subadditivity is cited (Lieb and Ruskai 1973), never proved — its proof needs Lieb's concavity theorem, which is beyond the course; say so plainly.

## Physics, and the motivating question
Alice encodes a classical message into qubit states and sends them; Bob measures. How many bits per qubit can get through? Unit 07 claimed one, without entanglement. Make the question concrete with the ensemble the unit runs on: Alice sends |0⟩ or |+⟩ with probability ½ each. Bob's best single-shot guess succeeds with probability 0.8536 (unit 05). How much *information*, in bits, can he extract? The Holevo quantity says at most χ = S(ρ) = h(cos²(π/8)) ≈ 0.601 bits — and the Helstrom measurement extracts 1 − h(0.8536) ≈ 0.399 bits, which is in fact the best single-letter measurement (Levitin 1995, cited). The gap between 0.399 and 0.601 is real, and is closed only asymptotically by collective measurements on many copies (the HSW theorem — named, excluded).

Schumacher's interpretation: S(ρ) qubits per symbol suffice and are necessary to compress a source emitting the ensemble (Schumacher, *PRA* 51, 2738, 1995) — here 0.601 qubits per symbol.

## Mathematical tools
Classical: Shannon entropy H(p) = −Σ pᵢ log pᵢ (base 2), its meaning via typical sequences — for n i.i.d. symbols, about 2^{nH} sequences carry almost all the probability, proved from the weak law of large numbers (Chebyshev; Chernoff–Hoeffding is assumed background). Joint, conditional and mutual information; H(X|Y) ≥ 0 classically.

Quantum: von Neumann entropy S(ρ) = −tr ρ log ρ (unit 00's functional calculus), equal to the Shannon entropy of the spectrum; 0 iff pure, log d iff ρ = I/d. Proved: concavity (from the concavity of η(x) = −x log x via a spectral argument, or from Klein); subadditivity S(AB) ≤ S(A) + S(B) (from Klein's inequality applied to ρ_AB and ρ_A ⊗ ρ_B); the Araki–Lieb inequality |S(A) − S(B)| ≤ S(AB) (from subadditivity plus purification, unit 04). Relative entropy D(ρ‖σ) = tr ρ(log ρ − log σ) and Klein's inequality D ≥ 0 with equality iff ρ = σ, proved (from the convexity of x log x and the double sum over eigenbases). Quantum mutual information I(A:B) = S(A) + S(B) − S(AB) = D(ρ_AB ‖ ρ_A ⊗ ρ_B) ≥ 0.

Strong subadditivity, S(ABC) + S(B) ≤ S(AB) + S(BC) — stated, cited (Lieb and Ruskai, *J. Math. Phys.* 14, 1938, 1973), and rewritten as I(A:BC) ≥ I(A:B): discarding a subsystem never increases mutual information. Say explicitly that this is the special case of the monotonicity of relative entropy under partial trace (Lindblad 1975, Uhlmann 1977, cited for the general case) that the course needs, and that the two are equivalent — not that one is derived from the other here. Local unitaries leave mutual information unchanged (proved).

The Holevo bound (Holevo 1973): for an ensemble {pₓ, ρₓ} and any measurement with outcome Y, the mutual information I(X:Y) ≤ χ = S(Σ pₓρₓ) − Σ pₓ S(ρₓ). Proof, every step: a classical register P holding x, the quantum system Q, a measurement apparatus M initially |0⟩; the joint state Σ pₓ |x⟩⟨x| ⊗ ρₓ ⊗ |0⟩⟨0|; I(P:Q) = χ; the measurement as a unitary on QM copying the outcome into M (for projective measurements — unit 21 extends to general ones); I(P:QM) unchanged by the unitary; then discard Q: I(P:M) ≤ I(P:QM) by strong subadditivity; and I(P:M) is the classical mutual information I(X:Y). Corollary: χ ≤ log d, so a qubit carries at most one bit — unit 07's citation discharged.

Entanglement entropy: for pure ρ_AB, S(A) = S(B) = the Shannon entropy of the squared Schmidt coefficients (unit 03), and it is the unique measure of pure-state entanglement under local operations and classical communication in the asymptotic sense (Popescu and Rohrlich 1997, cited, stated). The whole can have less entropy than a part: S(AB) = 0 < S(A) = 1 for a Bell state; conditional entropy S(A|B) can be negative, which no classical distribution allows.

Schumacher compression: typical subspaces of ρ^{⊗n}, of dimension about 2^{nS(ρ)}, capture almost all the weight; rate S(ρ) achievable and optimal — stated with the proof's structure given (the achievability half sketched in the notes from the classical typical-sequence lemma applied to the spectrum).

## The running example
**The ensemble {|0⟩, |+⟩} with probabilities ½.** Overview: one bit in, how many bits out? Section 1 (Shannon): one bit of classical input entropy. Section 2 (von Neumann): ρ = (|0⟩⟨0| + |+⟩⟨+|)/2 (unit 04's example), eigenvalues cos²(π/8), sin²(π/8), S = 0.601. Section 3 (inequalities): the joint state of register and qubit, its subadditivity and mutual information computed — I(P:Q) = 0.601. Section 4 (Holevo): the bound proved; the Helstrom measurement's 0.399 bits against 0.601. Section 5 (compression): 0.601 qubits per symbol; what that means for 1000 symbols (about 601 qubits). Close: the orthogonal ensemble {|0⟩, |1⟩} gives χ = 1 — non-orthogonality is what costs 0.399 bits of capacity per qubit even before measurement costs more.

## Worked examples (three to five, in `box ex`)
1. χ for the running ensemble: ρ, its eigenvalues, S(ρ) = h(0.8536) = 0.601 bits, each state pure so the second term vanishes.
2. Mutual information of the Helstrom measurement on the same ensemble: the 2×2 joint distribution, I(X:Y) = 1 − h(0.8536) = 0.399 bits.
3. Klein's inequality proved and then checked on two 2×2 density operators with numbers.
4. Superdense coding saturates Holevo: the four Bell states with probability ¼ on a two-qubit system give χ = 2, and Bob's Bell measurement attains it.
5. (optional) S(AB) < S(A) for |Φ⁺⟩; and S(AB) ≥ S(A) for a separable state, via concavity of conditional entropy (a consequence of strong subadditivity — proved as a corollary).

## What the problem sheet exercises
Ten to twelve problems; at least one proof and one ending in a number; referee checks where natural (entropies, χ).
- "Holevo for |0⟩ and |+⟩": χ = 0.601 bits against one bit for orthogonal states; the general formula h(½(1 + |⟨ψ|φ⟩|)) for two equiprobable pure states — derive it.
- "Dense coding saturates Holevo with an ebit": prove it.
- "The whole can be smaller than a part": S(AB) < S(A) for an entangled pure state; prove S(AB) ≥ S(A) for every separable state.
- "Klein and subadditivity": prove Klein's inequality, then subadditivity from it.
- "Araki–Lieb": prove it from subadditivity and purification; when is it tight?
- "Typical sequences": for a biased coin with p = 0.11, h(p) ≈ 0.5 bits (compute it: 0.4999); how many sequences of length 1000 are typical, to leading order in the exponent?
- "Concavity": prove S(Σ pᵢρᵢ) ≥ Σ pᵢ S(ρᵢ), and the upper bound S(Σ pᵢρᵢ) ≤ Σ pᵢS(ρᵢ) + H(p) with equality iff the ρᵢ have orthogonal supports.
- "Mutual information of a Bell pair": I(A:B) = 2 bits, twice the classical maximum for two bits' worth of correlation — and what that has to do with dense coding.
- "Entanglement entropy of unit 03's state": for (|00⟩ + |01⟩ + |10⟩)/√3, S(A) = h((3 + √5)/6) ≈ 0.5500 bits.
- "Accessible information is less than χ": for the trine ensemble of unit 04 (three states, probability ⅓, average I/2), compute χ = 1 bit; state (cited: Sasaki et al., *PRA* 59, 3325, 1999) that the best measurement extracts only log 3 − 1 ≈ 0.585 bits, and that it is a three-outcome generalised measurement — unit 21's — so no projective measurement on a qubit attains it; compute the best a two-outcome projective measurement can do for comparison (about 0.459 bits, found by optimising the measurement angle).
