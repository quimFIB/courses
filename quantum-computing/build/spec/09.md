## Why this unit exists, and what it must not become
No-cloning (unit 05) turned from a limitation into a weapon: an eavesdropper cannot copy a qubit she does not know the basis of, so she must disturb it, and the disturbance is measurable. BB84 makes that a protocol; E91 makes the same key out of Bell pairs with unit 06's CHSH test as the alarm, the seed of device-independent cryptography. The unit's honest edges matter as much as its theorems: QKD does not authenticate, its full security proofs are long and cited, and real devices leak.

It must not become a cryptography course or a full security proof. Composable security, finite-key analysis and the general (coherent-attack) proofs are cited (Shor–Preskill 2000; Renner 2005), not developed. Error-correcting codes are named by function here; quantum codes are Part E's.

## Physics
Single photons in polarisation (unit 03's qubit), sent through fibre or free space; Alice prepares, Bob measures in a basis he chooses at random. Real systems use attenuated laser pulses, which sometimes carry two photons — the photon-number-splitting attack and the decoy-state fix (Hwang 2003; Lo, Ma and Chen 2005), named in the notes as the gap between the ideal protocol and hardware. The experiments: BB84 first demonstrated over 32 cm of air (Bennett et al., 1992, the "Bennett–Bessette" demonstration, cited); Micius 2017, satellite-to-ground decoy-state QKD over up to 1200 km (Liao et al., *Nature* 549, 43, 2017); device-independent QKD demonstrated in 2022 (Nadlinger et al., *Nature* 607, 682; Zhang et al., *Nature* 607, 687).

## Mathematical tools
BB84 (Bennett and Brassard, 1984), each step's job named: Alice sends random bits in randomly chosen z or x bases; Bob measures in random bases; sifting (announce bases, keep the half where they agree); parameter estimation (reveal a random sample, estimate the quantum bit error rate Q); classical error correction (cost about h(Q) bits per bit, by Shannon's coding theorem — cited); privacy amplification (hash down by Eve's information, Bennett, Brassard and Robert 1988 — cited, with the universal-hashing idea in one paragraph); authentication of the classical channel throughout (Wegman–Carter 1981 — cited), which needs a short pre-shared secret: QKD is key *expansion*, not key creation from nothing.

The information–disturbance trade-off for BB84 states, proved for intercept-resend: Eve measures each photon in a random basis and resends what she saw. On the sifted key, she guessed Alice's basis half the time (no error, full information) and the other basis half the time (her outcome is uniform, Bob's result is wrong with probability ½) — so Q = 25 per cent, and Eve knows each sifted bit with probability ¾. More generally, an intercept-resend attack on a fraction f of photons causes Q = f/4. Then the Shor–Preskill key rate r = 1 − 2h(Q) per sifted bit (Shor and Preskill, *PRL* 85, 441, 2000), stated with its proof cited, and read: one h(Q) pays for error correction, the other for privacy amplification against the most general attack. r(0.05) ≈ 0.427; r = 0 at Q ≈ 11.0 per cent. Intercept-resend's 25 per cent is far beyond the threshold — which is why a sophisticated Eve uses gentler attacks, and why the 11 per cent figure, not 25, is the operational line.

E91 (Ekert, *PRL* 67, 661, 1991): a source distributes singlets; Alice and Bob measure in randomly chosen bases, use matching-basis rounds as key and the others for a CHSH test. The reason a Bell violation protects the key: with unit 06's qubit observables, S = 2√2 is attained by exactly one state, so ρ_AB is pure; a pure ρ_AB is uncorrelated with anything else (unit 04: a pure reduced state cannot be entangled with a third system — purification), so Eve's system is independent of the key. Device independence — the same conclusion without trusting that the devices measure the stated observables — needs self-testing (Summers–Werner, Popescu–Rohrlich 1992, cited) and is stated, not proved. BBM92 (Bennett, Brassard and Mermin, *PRL* 68, 557, 1992): the entanglement version of BB84 without a Bell test, equivalent to BB84 — one sentence.

## The running example
**Alice, Bob and an eavesdropper running BB84 on a 16-photon toy transmission** (a fixed table of bases and bits, written out once in the refs template). Overview: the table, and the question "how would Alice and Bob notice Eve?". Section 1 (the protocol): sifting the 16 to about 8; the steps named. Section 2 (intercept-resend): Eve's attack on the same 16, the errors it produces in the sifted key (exactly computed for the table, and 25 per cent in expectation). Section 3 (the rate): from Q to the final key length via 1 − 2h(Q). Section 4 (E91): the same story with Bell pairs and a CHSH alarm. Section 5 (what QKD does not do): authentication, the pre-shared secret, the hardware gap. Close: the key that survives, and what an observed Q of 5 per cent buys (0.427 key bits per sifted bit).

## Worked examples (three to five, in `box ex`)
1. The toy transmission: sifting, the sifted key, error estimation on a sample.
2. Intercept-resend's 25 per cent, computed over all eight cases (Alice's basis × bit × Eve's basis), probabilities summed to one.
3. The Shor–Preskill rate at Q = 5 per cent: h(0.05) ≈ 0.2864, r ≈ 0.427; and the threshold Q ≈ 0.1100 by solving 2h(Q) = 1 (bisection, each step shown or tabulated).
4. E91's alarm: CHSH on the singlet (2√2, unit 06) against the CHSH value of the state Eve leaves behind after measuring each of Bob's photons in z: |S| = √2 at the protocol's settings, and no choice of settings exceeds 2, because the state is now a separable mixture (unit 06's sheet) — computed.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Ten to twelve problems; at least one proof and one ending in a number; referee checks where natural (the intercept-resend average, h(Q)).
- "Intercept-resend causes 25 per cent": prove it; then for an attack on a fraction f of photons, Q = f/4 and Eve's information.
- "The rate": compute 1 − 2h(Q) at Q = 5 per cent (0.427 bits per sifted bit) and find where it reaches zero (Q ≈ 11.0 per cent).
- "Eve in the Breidbart basis": Eve measures in the basis halfway between z and x (unit 05's Helstrom basis for |0⟩ versus |+⟩); her probability of guessing each sifted bit (0.8536) and the error rate she causes (25 per cent again) — compute both.
- "A CHSH value of 2√2 leaves Eve nothing": with unit 06's qubit observables fixed, prove that S = 2√2 forces ρ_AB to be the extremal pure state, hence Eve's system is independent — the argument above, every step.
- "What a lower S allows": with the Werner state of unit 06, S = 2√2 p; find p at which S drops to 2 (p = 1/√2) and explain why that is not the BB84 threshold.
- "Authentication": show that without an authenticated classical channel a man-in-the-middle runs BB84 separately with Alice and with Bob, undetected.
- "Sifting efficiency": the expected fraction of photons kept; with biased basis choice (z with probability 0.9 on both sides — Lo, Chau and Ardehali 2005, cited), the sifting rate 0.82 and why security survives.
- "Error correction's cost": for Q = 5 per cent, how many bits of a 10⁶-bit sifted key are spent on error correction and privacy amplification, to leading order? (2h(0.05) × 10⁶ ≈ 5.73 × 10⁵.)
- "Two-photon pulses": if a pulse carries two photons, Eve can keep one and forward one; show she then learns the bit after sifting with no disturbance — the photon-number-splitting attack.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You prove intercept-resend causes 25 per cent error, and produce the 11.0 per cent threshold from the Shor–Preskill rate.
