## Why this unit exists, and what it must not become
Parts A and B used unitaries one at a time. A computer is a recipe for building a big unitary from small ones, and this unit says exactly which recipes suffice: CNOT plus single-qubit gates build every unitary exactly — and most unitaries need exponentially many gates, so efficiency, not possibility, is the real question. The unit also brings classical computation inside the model, reversibly, which is what makes every oracle in Part D a polynomial-size circuit.

It must not become a compiler course. Finite gate sets, approximation and the T gate's cost are unit 11's; complexity classes are unit 12's. Gate-count optimisation beyond the textbook constructions is out. No hardware: which gates a device natively has is unit 26's.

## The model
Qubits as wires, time left to right, qubit 0 on top (AUTHORING.md); gates as unitaries acting on one or two wires, extended by identities (unit 03's tensor products); measurement in the computational basis; classical control. Circuit size and depth. A circuit as a product of unitaries; the diagram as notation for that product, read right to left when written as a formula — say so and show the reversal once.

The principle of deferred measurement, proved: a measurement whose outcome classically controls a later gate can be replaced by the corresponding quantum-controlled gate followed by a measurement at the end, with identical output statistics — by computing both distributions from unit 01's postulates. Corollary: measuring only at the end loses nothing; and a qubit no longer used may as well be measured (the principle of implicit measurement, stated).

## Classical computation inside the model
Reversible gates as permutations of basis states, hence unitary. Toffoli (Toffoli 1980) is universal for reversible computation: it computes NAND with an ancilla and copies with fan-out, proved. Garbage, and Bennett's uncomputation (Bennett, *IBM J. Res. Dev.* 17, 525, 1973): compute f with garbage, copy the output with CNOTs, run the computation backwards — proved to leave |x⟩|y ⊕ f(x)⟩|0…0⟩ with ancillas restored; cost a constant factor in time. So every polynomial-time f yields a polynomial-size circuit U_f: |x⟩|y⟩ ↦ |x⟩|y ⊕ f(x)⟩ — the oracle of Part D. Why the garbage must be removed: left entangled with the output, it destroys interference (shown on a two-qubit example).

## Exact universality
Every unitary on ℂ^d is a product of at most d(d − 1)/2 two-level unitaries (acting non-trivially on the span of two basis vectors), proved by zeroing entries column by column. Gray codes move any two-level unitary onto adjacent basis states with CNOT-like permutations, and a two-level unitary on adjacent states is a multiply-controlled single-qubit gate; multiply-controlled gates from Toffolis and controlled-U; controlled-U from single-qubit gates and two CNOTs via unit 02's Z-Y-Z form (the A, B, C construction with ABC = I and AXBXC = e^{−iα}U, plus a phase on the control). Hence CNOT plus single-qubit gates are exactly universal (Barenco et al., *PRA* 52, 3457, 1995).

The counting lower bound: SU(2ⁿ) has real dimension 4ⁿ − 1; a two-qubit gate has at most 16 real parameters; a circuit of g gates is the image of a smooth map from a space of dimension at most 16g, so exact synthesis of a generic unitary needs at least (4ⁿ − 1)/16 gates — Ω(4ⁿ). State the dimension fact used (a smooth image of a lower-dimensional manifold has measure zero — Sard's theorem, cited) and keep the argument at the level of counting parameters. Most unitaries are not efficiently implementable; the interesting ones are the exceptions.

## The running example
**The Toffoli gate.** Overview: the reversible AND, |a, b, c⟩ ↦ |a, b, c ⊕ ab⟩, and the question "how do you build it from one- and two-qubit gates?". Section 1 (the model): Toffoli as an 8×8 permutation matrix, drawn as a circuit symbol. Section 2 (reversible classical computation): Toffoli computes NAND and copies, so it is universal for reversible circuits; a one-bit adder with uncomputation. Section 3 (deferred measurement): measuring a control early versus late. Section 4 (exact universality): Toffoli as a two-level unitary (it swaps |110⟩ and |111⟩), decomposed by the general construction — and then the economical textbook circuit: six CNOTs, two H and seven T or T† gates, verified on all eight inputs (Nielsen and Chuang Fig. 4.9, cited for the circuit; the verification is the unit's). Section 5 (counting): the eight-input check is cheap; a generic three-qubit unitary has 63 parameters. Close: the seven T gates flagged as the cost unit 11 will price.

## Worked examples (three to five, in `box ex`)
1. Deferred measurement on a two-qubit example: measure-then-classically-control versus controlled gate then measure, both output distributions computed and equal.
2. Bennett's trick on a small f (e.g. f(x₁, x₂, x₃) = x₁x₂ ⊕ x₃ with one ancilla), garbage computed and uncomputed, ancilla restored on all eight inputs.
3. The two-level decomposition of a 4×4 unitary (e.g. the two-qubit Fourier matrix of unit 15's convention, or a simpler one), every zeroing step.
4. The seven-T Toffoli verified on all eight basis inputs — tabulate the phases each path accumulates and show they cancel except where they should.
5. (optional) Controlled-H from single-qubit gates and two CNOTs via the ABC construction.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Ten to twelve problems; at least one proof and one ending in a number; referee checks where natural (every circuit identity: `Circuit(...).unitary()` against the target, up to phase).
- "Controlled-controlled-U": build it from controlled-V gates with V² = U and two CNOTs (Barenco et al.'s construction), and count gates.
- "Compile a two-qubit unitary": a given one (e.g. the controlled-S gate, or SWAP: three CNOTs, proved minimal is cited), with the circuit checked.
- "Deferred measurement": prove it in general.
- "Uncompute": a reversible adder for two-bit numbers with its garbage removed; ancilla count and gate count.
- "Two-level count": the d(d − 1)/2 bound, and why it is attained by a generic unitary.
- "Gray codes": the Gray-code path between |000⟩ and |111⟩ and the circuit it gives.
- "Counting": the parameter count for n = 1, 2, 3, 10 and the resulting gate lower bound; for n = 10, (4¹⁰ − 1)/16 ≈ 6.6 × 10⁴ gates.
- "Garbage destroys interference": with f(x) = x on one bit and the garbage left behind, show the phase-kickback interference of Part D fails.
- "Toffoli is not enough for quantum": prove Toffoli plus classical permutations cannot create superposition from a basis state, so a non-classical gate (H) is needed.
- **Optional, marked so:** "Write your own statevector simulator" — apply a k-qubit gate by reshaping the state to (2,)*n and contracting the gate's axes (the tensor trick, about ten lines of NumPy); test it against qclib's `Circuit(...).run()` on random circuits. Its worked solution is a short NumPy listing — the one place in the course where a solution is code (AUTHORING.md). Mind the qubit order (qubit 0 most significant).
