## Why this unit exists, and what it must not become
The learner knows P, NP and BPP and has proved Cook–Levin. This unit adds one class and places it exactly: BQP, defined by uniform families of quantum circuits, sits between BPP and PP, and every inclusion is proved. The unit's honest conclusion is as important as its theorems: whether BQP is larger than BPP is unknown, and a proof that it is would separate P from PSPACE, so none is in sight. "Quantum computers are faster" is a hypothesis about BQP, supported by oracle separations (Part D) and by the absence of classical algorithms for factoring — not a theorem.

It must not become a complexity-theory course. PSPACE, PP, #P and GapP are defined here in a slide each, because the assumed background stops at BPP; nothing beyond them (no PH beyond its definition, no interactive proofs) is developed. QMA is branch 20's. Oracle separations are stated here and proved in Part D (unit 18 for NP ⊄ BQP relative to an oracle).

## Mathematical tools
Definitions, one slide each, since the background does not include them: PSPACE (polynomial space, any time); #P (functions counting accepting paths of a polynomial-time nondeterministic machine); GapP (differences of two #P functions, closed under subtraction, products and polynomial sums — proved or cited, whichever is shorter); PP (accept iff more than half the paths accept, equivalently iff a GapP function is positive); the polynomial hierarchy named, for the oracle result.

BQP: languages decided by a polynomial-time uniform family of circuits over a fixed universal gate set, with error at most ⅓ on every input. Uniformity stated precisely (a polynomial-time classical machine outputs the circuit for each input length) and why it matters (non-uniform families decide undecidable languages). Independence of the gate set: by unit 11, any other universal set approximates each gate to accuracy 1/(poly) with polylogarithmic overhead (the approximations themselves must be efficiently computable — Solovay–Kitaev's construction is), and errors add, so the acceptance probability moves by at most a small constant. Amplification by majority vote over k independent runs, error at most exp(−k/18) by Hoeffding (assumed background: Chernoff–Hoeffding stated) — proved, with the reversible bookkeeping (each run's garbage uncomputed, unit 10). BQP is closed under complement and under polynomial-time reductions; BQP is low for itself, BQP^BQP = BQP (Bennett, Bernstein, Brassard and Vazirani, *SIAM J. Comput.* 26, 1510, 1997), stated.

The placement:
- **BPP ⊆ BQP**: coin flips from Hadamards on fresh qubits measured (or not — deferred measurement, unit 10), the deterministic part made reversible by unit 10; proved.
- **BQP ⊆ PSPACE** (Bernstein and Vazirani, *SIAM J. Comput.* 26, 1411, 1997): the amplitude of an output string as Feynman's sum over paths — a sum over all intermediate basis strings of products of gate entries — evaluated depth-first, reusing space, in polynomial space and exponential time; the acceptance probability as a sum of squared amplitudes, also in polynomial space; proved, with the space accounting done carefully.
- **BQP ⊆ PP** (Adleman, DeMarrais and Huang, *SIAM J. Comput.* 26, 1524, 1997): restrict without loss to the gate set {H, Toffoli} — universal for BQP with real amplitudes (Shi 2003; Aharonov 2003, cited), complex amplitudes being simulable with one extra qubit (cited) — so every path's amplitude is ±2^{−h/2} for a circuit with h Hadamards. Then the acceptance probability is p_acc = g(x)/2^h for a GapP function g (pairs of paths ending at the same accepting output, counted with sign), and x ∈ L iff 2g(x) − 2^h > 0, a GapP condition, hence PP. Proved in full.

What is and is not known: BQP versus NP unknown in both directions; relative to an oracle, NP ⊄ BQP (Bennett, Bernstein, Brassard and Vazirani 1997 — unit 18 proves it) and BQP ⊄ PH (Raz and Tal, STOC 2019, cited). If BQP ≠ BPP were proved, then P ≠ PSPACE would follow, since P ⊆ BPP ⊆ BQP ⊆ PSPACE: the one-paragraph argument the checkpoint asks for. PostBQP = PP (Aaronson, *Proc. R. Soc. A* 461, 3473, 2005), stated, with NP ⊆ PostBQP as the problem-sheet first step.

## The running example
**One small circuit, accepted three ways.** The three-qubit circuit H₀ H₁ · Toffoli(0, 1 → 2) · H₀ H₁ on |000⟩, accepting when the output is 000. Overview: the circuit, and the claim that its acceptance probability is 9/16 = 0.5625. Section 1 (the class): what it would mean for a family of such circuits to decide a language. Section 2 (simulation): the 8-dimensional state vector, P(000) = 9/16. Section 3 (paths, PSPACE): the 16 paths through the four Hadamards; the amplitude of 000 as ¼ × (the number of paths with x₀x₁ = 0, each contributing +1) = ¾, computed depth-first with only a counter and a path index in memory. Section 4 (GapP, PP): the same number as a difference of two path counts over 2^h, h = 4; the PP condition. Section 5 (placement): the inclusions drawn as a chain BPP ⊆ BQP ⊆ PP ⊆ PSPACE, with what is known and unknown marked. Close: the circuit decided nothing hard, but every quantum computation's acceptance probability is a number of exactly this form.

## Worked examples (three to five, in `box ex`)
1. Amplification: from error ⅓ to 2^{−100} needs k with exp(−k/18) ≤ 2^{−100}, k ≥ 1800 ln 2 ≈ 1248 repetitions (about 1250).
2. The running circuit's acceptance probability by direct simulation and by the path sum, both 9/16.
3. The space accounting for BQP ⊆ PSPACE: what is stored at each recursion level, totalling polynomial space.
4. The GapP expression for the running circuit, and the PP condition checked numerically.
5. (optional) BPP ⊆ BQP on a toy randomised algorithm (e.g. a two-coin test), the coins as Hadamards.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Ten to twelve problems; at least one proof and one ending in a number; referee checks where natural (acceptance probabilities of small circuits).
- "Repetitions": how many runs push error from ⅓ to 2^{−100} (about 1250 by Hoeffding); and from 0.49 to 2^{−100} (compute: exp(−2k(0.01)²) ≤ 2^{−100}, k ≈ 3.47 × 10⁵).
- "Complement": prove BQP is closed under complement.
- "Postselection contains NP": show NP ⊆ PostBQP, the first step toward Aaronson's PostBQP = PP (cited).
- "Uniformity matters": a non-uniform family of circuits deciding a unary version of the halting problem.
- "Gate-set independence": with Solovay–Kitaev's overhead, show a circuit of size s over one universal set becomes size O(s polylog s) over another with acceptance probability moved by at most 1/12.
- "Path sums by hand": the amplitude of a given output of a small {H, Toffoli} circuit as a signed path count.
- "P = PSPACE would collapse it": write the one-paragraph argument that a proof of BQP ≠ BPP proves P ≠ PSPACE.
- "GapP closure": prove GapP is closed under subtraction and multiplication (or cite, and use).
- "The real-amplitude trick": show a circuit with complex gates is simulated by a real one on one extra qubit (a + ib ↦ a ⊗ I + b ⊗ (the 2×2 rotation by 90°)) — the step the {H, Toffoli} reduction needs.

## Checkpoint (falsifiable, and the last slide)
You prove BQP ⊆ PP, and explain in one paragraph why a proof that BQP ≠ BPP would also prove P ≠ PSPACE.
