## Why this unit exists, and what it must not become
Feynman's argument (talk 1981; *Int. J. Theor. Phys.* 21, 467, 1982) was the reason the field exists: simulating n spins classically tracks 2ⁿ amplitudes, so build the simulator out of spins. Lloyd (*Science* 273, 1073, 1996) made it an algorithm for local Hamiltonians: split the evolution into steps of each local term and pay a Trotter error. This unit proves the first-order Trotter bound, derives the second-order symmetric splitting, counts gates for a k-local Hamiltonian, and reads off energies with unit 15's phase estimation. It is the honest counterweight to branch 20: dynamics is easy for a quantum computer; ground energies, in the worst case, are not.

It must not become a survey of simulation algorithms. Commutator-scaling bounds (Childs, Su, Tran, Wiebe and Zhu, *PRX* 11, 011020, 2021), qubitisation (Low and Chuang, *Quantum* 3, 163, 2019) and quantum signal processing (Low and Chuang, *PRL* 118, 010501, 2017) are named and cited with their scaling, not derived. Quantum chemistry is named as the application, without molecular Hamiltonians.

## Physics
A chain of n spins (unit 02's qubits) with nearest-neighbour coupling in a transverse field: the transverse-field Ising model H = −J Σ Z_iZ_{i+1} − h Σ X_i, explained from nothing — neighbouring spins prefer to align (the J term), the field tries to flip them (the h term), and the two compete; at J = h the infinite chain has a quantum phase transition (stated, cited, not developed). Local Hamiltonians as the physically relevant class: interactions are between few, nearby particles, so H = Σ_j H_j with each H_j acting on k = O(1) qubits and the number of terms polynomial in n. What one wants from a simulator: e^{−iHt}|ψ⟩ for a prepared state, then measured observables — correlation functions, magnetisation — and energies via phase estimation.

## Mathematical tools
Lie–Trotter, proved: for Hermitian A, B and t > 0, ‖e^{−i(A+B)t} − (e^{−iAt/n}e^{−iBt/n})ⁿ‖ ≤ ‖[A, B]‖t²/(2n). Proof: the one-step error ‖e^{−i(A+B)δ} − e^{−iAδ}e^{−iBδ}‖ ≤ ‖[A, B]‖δ²/2 from the integral representation (differentiate W(s) = e^{iAs}e^{−i(A+B)s}… or the variation-of-parameters formula; the learner has unit 01's derivative of e^{−iHt} and unit 11's error accumulation), then n steps by telescoping (unit 11). Many terms: Σ_j over the k-local terms with the bound by the sum of pairwise commutator norms. Strang's symmetric splitting e^{−iAδ/2}e^{−iBδ}e^{−iAδ/2}, second order: error O(t³/n²) with the nested-commutator constant — proved at the order-of-magnitude level, the exact constant cited. Higher-order Suzuki formulas named.

Gate counts: each e^{−iH_jδ} for a k-local Pauli-product term is a rotation conjugated by Cliffords (e^{−iθZ⊗Z} = CNOT·(I ⊗ R_z(2θ))·CNOT, derived); with L terms and n_T steps, L·n_T such blocks; each rotation costs about 3 log₂(1/ε) T gates (unit 11). For the Ising chain, L = 2n − 1.

Energies by phase estimation (unit 15) on U = e^{−iHτ}, with the eigenphase −Eτ/2π mod 1: choose τ so the spectrum fits in one period; precision from the number of counting qubits; the input must overlap the ground state — the catch, named (preparing a state with good ground-state overlap can be hard; branch 20 shows how hard in the worst case).

## The running example
**The transverse-field Ising chain.** Overview: H for n spins, J = h = 1, and the question "how many gates to simulate it for time t to accuracy ε?". Section 1 (Feynman): 2ⁿ amplitudes, 2⁵⁰ ≈ 10¹⁵ at n = 50. Section 2 (Trotter): A = −Σ ZZ and B = −Σ X, each a sum of commuting terms so e^{−iAδ} is exact; [A, B] computed, ‖[A, B]‖ ≤ 4(n − 1) by counting non-commuting pairs (each ‖[Z_iZ_{i+1}, X_j]‖ = 2). Section 3 (the numbers): for n = 8, t = 5, the actual first-order error against the bound and the Strang error — 10 steps: 1.99 (useless); 100 steps: 0.147 (bound 2.38); 1000 steps: 0.0144 (bound 0.238), Strang 0.00025 — computed and tabulated, with ‖[A, B]‖ = 19.0 against the counting bound 28. Section 4 (gates): the ZZ block as CNOT–R_z–CNOT; total rotations and T-count for the 1000-step simulation. Section 5 (energies): phase estimation on e^{−iHτ}. Close: the bound is pessimistic by about 16× here, which is why commutator-scaling analyses matter; and the pointer to branch 20.

## Worked examples (three to five, in `box ex`)

3. e^{−iθZ⊗Z} as CNOT–R_z(2θ)–CNOT, multiplied out.
4. The Ising chain's commutator norm by counting, and the step count for a target accuracy.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Ten to twelve problems; at least one proof and one ending in a number; referee checks where natural (Trotter errors on small systems).
- "One qubit": for H = X + Z at t = 1, compute the first-order Trotter error at n = 10 (0.070) against the bound ‖[X, Z]‖/(2n) = 0.1.
- "Size an Ising simulation": count the Trotter steps for an n-spin chain to accuracy ε from the bound; for n = 50, t = 10, ε = 10⁻³: n_T ≥ 4·49·100/(2·10⁻³) = 9.8 × 10⁶ steps; then with Strang's O(t³/n²) behaviour, the order of magnitude instead.
- "Commuting terms": prove e^{−i(A+B)t} = e^{−iAt}e^{−iBt} exactly when [A, B] = 0, and that each of the Ising chain's A and B is a sum of commuting terms.
- "Strang is second order": prove the symmetric splitting's error is O(δ³) per step.
- "Many terms": generalise the first-order bound to L terms.
- "Gate count": the total number of CNOTs and rotations for the n = 8, t = 5, 1000-step simulation; with 3 log₂(1/ε) T gates per rotation at ε = 10⁻⁶, the T-count.
- "Energies": for H = X + Z (eigenvalues ±√2), the choice of τ and the number of counting qubits to read the ground energy to three decimal places.
- "Feynman's count": the memory needed to store a 50-qubit state vector in double precision complex numbers (2⁵⁰ × 16 bytes ≈ 18 PB).
- "The classical picture" (optional, cross-reference only): Mechanics to Relativity's unit 14 treats a Hamiltonian as generating a flow; splitting the flow is how symplectic integrators work — the same Lie–Trotter idea, one level down.
