## Why this unit exists, and what it must not become
The learner has proved Cook–Levin. This unit proves its quantum analogue: the k-local Hamiltonian problem is complete for QMA, the quantum version of NP, by Kitaev's construction — a computation encoded as the ground state of a Hamiltonian, with a clock. The payoff is a sobering theorem with physical content: unit 19 showed a quantum computer simulates dynamics efficiently, and this unit shows that finding a ground energy — what a chemist most wants — is QMA-hard in the worst case, so (unless QMA = BQP) not even a quantum computer does it efficiently in general. Cook–Levin's tableau is set beside the history state, row for clock step, so the learner sees exactly what changes and what it costs: a promise gap of 1/poly instead of a constant.

It must not become a survey of Hamiltonian complexity. 2-local completeness (Kempe, Kitaev and Regev, *SIAM J. Comput.* 35, 1070, 2006) and one-dimensional chains (Aharonov, Gottesman, Irani and Kempe, *Commun. Math. Phys.* 287, 41, 2009) are cited; the quantum PCP conjecture and the NLTS theorem (Anshu, Breuckmann and Nirkhe, STOC 2023) are named in one slide.

## Mathematical tools
QMA (Kitaev 1999; Kitaev, Shen and Vyalyi, *Classical and Quantum Computation*, AMS 2002, ch. 14): a language L with a uniform polynomial-size verifier circuit V and polynomial witness length such that for x ∈ L some witness |ξ⟩ makes V accept with probability ≥ ⅔ (completeness), and for x ∉ L every witness is accepted with probability ≤ ⅓ (soundness). NP ⊆ QMA and BQP ⊆ QMA, proved. Amplification is no longer free: the witness cannot be copied (the two-line no-cloning argument reproved, unit 05 named). Parallel repetition with k copies of the witness and a majority vote works, and entangled cheating witnesses across the copies do not help, proved (the acceptance operator for each copy is checked independently; bound via the product structure of the verifier's measurement). Marriott and Watrous's in-place amplification (*Comput. Complexity* 14, 122, 2005), stated and cited.

The k-local Hamiltonian problem: H = Σ_j H_j on n qubits, each term acting on at most k qubits with 0 ≤ H_j ≤ I (or ‖H_j‖ ≤ 1), polynomially many terms; promise that the ground energy is ≤ a or ≥ b with b − a ≥ 1/poly(n); decide which. The classical case first: a k-SAT formula as a diagonal k-local Hamiltonian, one projector per clause onto its violating assignment, counting violated clauses — so the problem is NP-hard before anything quantum happens, proved.

Membership in QMA, proved: Merlin sends the ground state (amplified by copies); Arthur picks a term j uniformly and measures the two-outcome measurement {H_j, I − H_j} (via a small circuit — a k-qubit unitary from unit 10), accepting on I − H_j; acceptance probability 1 − ⟨ξ|H|ξ⟩/m for m terms, so the gap b − a becomes a 1/poly acceptance gap, then amplified.

Hardness — Kitaev's theorem that 5-local Hamiltonian is QMA-complete, proved in the following parts:
- **The history state** Σ_{t=0}^{T} U_t⋯U₁|ξ, 0⟩ ⊗ |t⟩/√(T + 1) for a verifier circuit U_T⋯U₁, with a unary clock on T qubits (|t⟩ = |1^t 0^{T−t}⟩), so that clock transitions are checked by 3-local terms and legality by 2-local ones.
- **The terms**: H_in (ancillas start in |0⟩ at t = 0), H_out (the output qubit reads accept at t = T), H_clock (illegal unary strings penalised), H_prop = Σ_t H_t with H_t = ½(I ⊗ |t⟩⟨t| + I ⊗ |t − 1⟩⟨t − 1| − U_t ⊗ |t⟩⟨t − 1| − U_t† ⊗ |t − 1⟩⟨t|); each is 5-local with the unary clock (a gate on 2 qubits plus 3 clock qubits).
- **The propagation term's spectrum**: conjugating by W = Σ_t U_t⋯U₁ ⊗ |t⟩⟨t| makes H_prop = I ⊗ (the Laplacian of the path graph on T + 1 vertices, scaled by ½·2), whose eigenvalues are 1 − cos(πk/(T + 1)), k = 0, …, T, each with multiplicity the witness dimension — proved (the path Laplacian's eigenvectors are cosines; check on a slide). The spectral gap is 1 − cos(π/(T + 1)) = Θ(1/T²); for T = 9, 0.0489.
- **Completeness**: the history state of an accepting computation has zero propagation, input and clock energy and output energy ≤ (1 − acceptance)/(T + 1) — proved.
- **Soundness**: if every witness is accepted with probability ≤ ε, the ground energy is at least Ω((1 − √ε)/T³), via Kitaev's geometric lemma — for positive semidefinite A, B with null spaces at angle θ, A + B ≥ min(λ_A, λ_B)·2 sin²(θ/2), where λ are the smallest non-zero eigenvalues — proved; applied with A = H_prop + H_clock (null space: history states) and B = H_in + H_out.

The comparison with Cook–Levin: a classical tableau's consistency is checked locally, row against row, and a single bad cell is caught by one clause; a history state's consistency is enforced by energy penalties spread over a superposition of T + 1 clock steps, so a bad step costs energy only of order 1/T² — which is why the promise gap is 1/poly instead of a constant. Quantum PCP asks whether a constant gap is possible; NLTS (2023) is evidence; both named.

## The running example
**The Feynman–Kitaev Hamiltonian of a nine-gate circuit.** Overview: a verifier circuit with T = 9 gates, and the question "can its acceptance be read off a ground energy?". Section 1 (QMA): the circuit as a verifier, a witness, completeness and soundness. Section 2 (the classical shadow): a small 3-SAT instance as a diagonal 3-local Hamiltonian, its ground energy the minimum number of violated clauses (e.g. 0 for a satisfiable instance, 1 for (x)(¬x) padded to 3-local). Section 3 (the history state): the tableau and the history state side by side, row for clock step (drawn). Section 4 (the propagation term): its matrix for T = 9 after the change of basis W, the path graph on ten vertices, eigenvalues 1 − cos(πk/10), gap 0.0489 — drawn as the ten eigenvalues on a line. Section 5 (soundness and the gap): the geometric lemma and the 1/poly price. Close: dynamics is easy (unit 19), ground energies are QMA-hard; what that means for quantum chemistry, stated carefully (worst case, not typical).

## What the problem sheet exercises
Ten to twelve problems; at least one proof and one ending in a number; referee checks where natural (small propagation Hamiltonians, as `tests/test_known_results.py` does — but the sheet's snippet is its own).
- "3-SAT as a Hamiltonian": write a given 3-SAT instance as a diagonal 3-local Hamiltonian and read off its ground energy.
- "The gap for nine gates": build the propagation term for a nine-gate circuit and find its gap, 1 − cos(π/10) ≈ 0.0489.
- "Zero propagation energy": show the history state of an accepting computation has zero propagation energy, and compute its output energy.
- "Membership": prove the local Hamiltonian problem is in QMA.
- "NP ⊆ QMA and BQP ⊆ QMA": prove both.
- "Entangled witnesses do not help repetition": prove it for k = 2 copies.
- "The geometric lemma": prove it.
- "Gap scaling": for T = 100 and T = 1000, the propagation gap (about 4.8 × 10⁻⁴ and 4.9 × 10⁻⁶), and what that does to the promise gap.
- "Why unary": show a binary clock would need O(log T)-local terms to check one transition, and why 5-locality needs the unary clock.
- "Clock legality": the 2-local terms penalising illegal unary strings, and the dimension of the legal subspace (T + 1).
