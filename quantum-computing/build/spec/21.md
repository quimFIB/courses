## Why this unit exists, and what it must not become
Every earlier unit treated evolution as unitary and measurement as projective. Neither is what happens to a qubit in a real device: it leaks energy and loses phase to surroundings nobody controls, and the most informative measurements are not projective. This unit gives both their full generality and proves the generality is no more than unitaries and projections on a larger space: POVMs by Naimark's dilation, channels by Choi, Kraus and Stinespring. It fixes the standard noise models as Kraus families — the vocabulary of Part E — and proves the one inequality every later unit leans on: no channel makes two states more distinguishable.

It must not become a course on master equations or open-systems physics. The Lindblad equation is named (one sentence, with T₁ and T₂ as its parameters) and not derived. Channel capacities are excluded by the curriculum.

## Physics
A qubit's two characteristic times, explained from nothing. T₁: an excited qubit relaxes to |0⟩, giving its energy to the environment — the probability of still being in |1⟩ decays as e^{−t/T₁}. T₂: a superposition's relative phase wanders because the environment nudges the qubit's frequency — the off-diagonal element of ρ decays as e^{−t/T₂}. Typical numbers named (superconducting qubits tens to hundreds of microseconds; trapped ions seconds for T₂), as orders of magnitude, with the platforms left to unit 26. Neither process is unitary on the qubit: the qubit's state goes from pure to mixed (unit 04), which no unitary does.

## Mathematical tools
POVMs: positive operators E_m summing to I, outcome probability tr(E_mρ); why they are needed (a three-outcome measurement on a qubit). Naimark's dilation, proved: every POVM is a projective measurement on a larger space after an isometry, V: H → H ⊗ ℂ^M, V|ψ⟩ = Σ_m √E_m|ψ⟩ ⊗ |m⟩.

Channels: linear maps on operators that take density operators to density operators even when applied to part of a larger system — completely positive and trace-preserving (CPTP). Positivity alone is not enough: the transpose is positive but (T ⊗ id)(|Φ⁺⟩⟨Φ⁺|) has eigenvalue −½ (the swap divided by 2), proved; normalised to the Choi convention the minimum eigenvalue is −1 (as in `tests/test_known_results.py`'s convention — state the convention used). The Choi matrix J(Φ) = Σ_{ij} |i⟩⟨j| ⊗ Φ(|i⟩⟨j|); Choi's theorem, proved: Φ is CP iff J(Φ) ≥ 0; trace-preserving iff the partial trace of J over the output is I. The Kraus representation Φ(ρ) = Σ_k A_kρA_k† with Σ A_k†A_k = I, proved from the Choi matrix's spectral decomposition; two Kraus families give the same channel iff related by an isometry (unit 04's HJW, applied to the Choi matrix — proved). Stinespring dilation, proved in finite dimension: every channel is an isometry into system ⊗ environment followed by a partial trace (unit 04).

The standard noise models as Kraus families, each checked to be trace-preserving: bit flip, phase flip, depolarising (ρ ↦ (1 − p)ρ + p I/2, and its Kraus form with the four Paulis), amplitude damping with γ = 1 − e^{−t/T₁} (A₀ = diag(1, √(1 − γ)), A₁ = √γ|0⟩⟨1|), and pure dephasing. Their action on the Bloch ball (unit 04): bit flip squashes toward the x axis, depolarising shrinks uniformly, amplitude damping shrinks and shifts toward |0⟩ — drawn. T₂ ≤ 2T₁ within this model, proved: amplitude damping alone already decays the coherence as e^{−t/2T₁}, and adding pure dephasing (rate 1/T_φ) only adds, so 1/T₂ = 1/(2T₁) + 1/T_φ.

Trace distance D(ρ, σ) = ½‖ρ − σ‖₁ and its meaning (the best probability of telling ρ from σ in one shot is ½(1 + D) — the mixed-state Helstrom bound, proved here since unit 05 is not on the path, or proved for the projective case and the POVM case reduced to it by Naimark); fidelity F(ρ, σ) = ‖√ρ√σ‖₁, Uhlmann's theorem stated (cited); the Fuchs–van de Graaf inequalities stated. Trace distance contracts under every channel, proved (via the Helstrom characterisation: a measurement after Φ is a measurement before it). So no processing makes two states more distinguishable.

## The running example
**A qubit in |+⟩ left alone in a noisy device.** Overview: |+⟩ at t = 0, and the question "what is it at t, and could anything recover it?". Section 1 (generalised measurement): a three-outcome measurement on the qubit — the unambiguous test for |0⟩ versus |+⟩ — and its Naimark dilation. Section 2 (channels): the device as a channel; why the map must be completely positive (the qubit may be entangled with another — the transpose's failure shown on |Φ⁺⟩). Section 3 (Kraus and Stinespring): amplitude damping as the qubit coupled to an environment qubit by a partial swap, then the environment discarded. Section 4 (T₁ and T₂): |+⟩'s Bloch vector shrinking and sliding toward |0⟩, drawn as a trajectory in the Bloch ball, with T₂ = 2T₁ when there is no pure dephasing. Section 5 (distinguishability): |+⟩ and |−⟩ under the noise; their trace distance falls and can never rise again. Close: what the device took cannot be recovered by any later processing on the qubit alone — which is exactly what error correction (unit 22) must circumvent by never letting the information sit in one qubit.

## Worked examples (three to five, in `box ex`)
1. The transpose is not completely positive: (T ⊗ id)|Φ⁺⟩⟨Φ⁺| = SWAP/2, eigenvalue −½, every entry shown.
2. Amplitude damping's Kraus operators from a Stinespring dilation with one environment qubit, and the Choi matrix computed and checked positive.
3. |+⟩ under amplitude damping for time t: ρ(t), its Bloch vector, the coherence e^{−t/2T₁}, normalisation and positivity checked.
4. Trace distance of |+⟩ and |−⟩ before and after depolarising noise of strength p: 1 → 1 − p.
5. (optional) The unambiguous POVM for |0⟩ versus |+⟩ with c = √2/(1 + √2): E₊ = c|1⟩⟨1|, E₀ = c|−⟩⟨−|, E_? = I − E₊ − E₀ ≥ 0; success 1 − 1/√2 ≈ 0.293.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Ten to twelve problems; at least one proof and one ending in a number; referee checks where natural (`channels.choi_of_map`, `channels.amplitude_damping`, Kraus checks).
- "Unambiguous discrimination": for |0⟩ and |+⟩, never wrong and sometimes inconclusive; the optimal three-outcome POVM succeeds with probability 1 − 1/√2 ≈ 0.293, proved optimal for equal priors (the Ivanovic–Dieks–Peres bound, proved for this pair); Naimark turns it into a projective measurement on a qutrit — construct it.
- "Amplitude damping from Stinespring": derive its Kraus operators from a unitary on qubit ⊗ environment.
- "Two Kraus families": find two Kraus families for the phase-flip channel and the unitary relating them.
- "Choi's theorem": prove it.
- "T₂ ≤ 2T₁": prove it in the model; for T₁ = 100 µs and T_φ = 150 µs, T₂ = 1/(1/200 + 1/150) µs ≈ 85.7 µs (compute).
- "Depolarising in Kraus form": show the Pauli-twirl form and the Bloch-ball shrinking factor.
- "Contractivity": prove trace distance contracts under channels; show fidelity does not decrease.
- "Positive but not CP": the transpose; and a CP map that is not trace-preserving (a projection), and how a channel completes it.
- "Measurements as channels": a projective measurement whose outcome is forgotten is a channel — write its Kraus operators (unit 04's non-selective measurement).
- "Naimark for a trine": the three-outcome trine POVM on a qubit (unit 04's states, E_k = ⅔|t_k⟩⟨t_k|) and its dilation.
