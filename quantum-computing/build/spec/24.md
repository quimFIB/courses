## Why this unit exists, and what it must not become
Unit 22's codes assumed perfect correction circuits. Real ones are made of the same faulty gates as everything else, and a CNOT spreads errors: an X error on its control is copied forward to the target, a Z error on its target is copied back to the control. Fault tolerance is the discipline of building every gadget so that one fault causes at most one error per code block, and the threshold theorem is its payoff: below a constant physical error rate, concatenation suppresses logical errors doubly exponentially. Then the two facts that set the shape of every real architecture: no code has a transversal universal gate set (Eastin–Knill), so the T gate must be injected via magic states, at a cost; and the surface code, with a threshold near 1 per cent and only nearest-neighbour connections, is what the experiments now run.

It must not become a full proof of the threshold theorem (cited — Aharonov and Ben-Or 1997/2008; Kitaev 1997; Knill, Laflamme and Zurek 1998; Aliferis, Gottesman and Preskill 2006 for the modern proof) or a surface-code decoder course (minimum-weight matching named, not developed). The 2048-bit resource estimate is the capstone's; this unit sizes one logical qubit.

## Mathematical tools
Error propagation through CNOT, proved by conjugation: CNOT(X ⊗ I)CNOT = X ⊗ X and CNOT(I ⊗ Z)CNOT = Z ⊗ Z — drawn as arrows on the circuit. Fault-tolerant gadgets: one fault produces at most one error in each output block; transversal gates (applied qubit-by-qubit across blocks) are automatically fault-tolerant for this reason; on Steane's code (unit 23) H, S and CNOT are transversal, so the whole Clifford group is. Fault-tolerant syndrome extraction (cat-state or Steane-style ancillas, named; the reason repeated measurement is needed, shown).

The threshold theorem in a simplified model, proved: suppose a level-1 encoded gadget fails only if at least two of its A fault locations fail, so p₁ ≤ C(A, 2)p² = cp²; then k levels of concatenation give p_k ≤ (cp)^{2^k}/c — doubly exponential suppression whenever p < p_th = 1/c — while the size grows only exponentially in k (a factor of about the gadget size per level), so the overhead to reach logical error ε is polylogarithmic in 1/ε, proved. With p = 10⁻³ and p_th = 10⁻²: one level 10⁻⁴, two 10⁻⁶, three 10⁻¹⁰, four 10⁻¹⁸; on Steane's code, 7, 49, 343, 2401 data qubits per logical qubit (ancillas extra). The full theorem cited with its hypotheses (local, weakly correlated noise; fresh ancillas; parallel operations; classical computation fast and reliable).

Eastin–Knill (Eastin and Knill, *PRL* 102, 110502, 2009), stated: no code that detects every single-qubit error has a transversal universal gate set. So T is injected: the magic state |A⟩ = T|+⟩ = (|0⟩ + e^{iπ/4}|1⟩)/√2, and gate teleportation — CNOT from the data qubit into |A⟩'s qubit (or the reverse, as in the standard circuit), measure in Z, apply S conditioned on the outcome — implements T up to Pauli frames, proved line by line (S is Clifford, so the correction is cheap). Magic-state distillation (Bravyi and Kitaev, *PRA* 71, 022316, 2005), cited: 15 noisy copies with error p yield one with error about 35p³; e.g. 10⁻³ → 3.5 × 10⁻⁸ in one round, 1.5 × 10⁻²¹ in two (computed). So the T-count of unit 11 converts into distillation factories, which dominate the area of a fault-tolerant computer.

The surface code (Kitaev 1997/2003; Dennis, Kitaev, Landahl and Preskill 2002; Fowler et al., *PRA* 86, 032324, 2012): data qubits on a square lattice, X-type and Z-type stabilisers on alternating plaquettes, each of weight four (two or three at boundaries), measured by nearest-neighbour circuits; logical operators as strings crossing the lattice, distance d; the rotated layout with d² data and d² − 1 measurement qubits, 2d² − 1 in total — drawn for d = 3 and d = 5. Its threshold near 1 per cent under circuit-level noise, and the heuristic logical error per cycle p_L ≈ A(p/p_th)^{(d+1)/2} with A ≈ 0.1 (Fowler et al.), stated as heuristic and cited. The experiment: Google Quantum AI, "Quantum error correction below the surface code threshold" (*Nature*, 2025; preprint 2024) — distances 3, 5 and 7 on a superconducting processor, logical error suppressed by a factor Λ ≈ 2 per increase of d by 2; quote Λ and the distance-7 error rate only as the paper reports them, with the reference, and say what "below threshold" means operationally (Λ > 1).

## The running example
**One logical qubit, from a physical error rate of 10⁻³ to a target of 10⁻¹² per cycle.** Overview: the target, and two ways to reach it. Section 1 (faulty correction): CNOT propagation drawn; why a naive syndrome circuit makes things worse. Section 2 (concatenation): the threshold in the simplified model; four levels of Steane's code, 2401 data qubits. Section 3 (the surface code): the lattice for d = 3; p_L ≈ 0.1·(0.1)^{(d+1)/2} ≤ 10⁻¹² gives d = 21; 2·21² − 1 = 881 physical qubits (computed). Section 4 (T gates): Eastin–Knill; the magic state; one round of 15-to-1 distillation from 10⁻³ to 3.5 × 10⁻⁸ — not yet enough for 10⁻¹², two rounds overshoot to 10⁻²¹. Section 5 (the experiment): Google's below-threshold result and what Λ ≈ 2 would mean at d = 21. Close: 881 qubits per logical qubit is the number the capstone multiplies.

## What the problem sheet exercises
Ten to twelve problems; at least one proof and one ending in a number; referee checks where natural (CNOT conjugations, the teleported T, the sizing arithmetic).
- "Size a logical qubit": with p = 10⁻³, p_th = 10⁻² and A = 0.1, the distance for logical error at most 10⁻¹² per cycle (d = 21) and the physical cost per logical qubit (881).
- "Concatenation does the same job": the levels needed (four) and the qubit count on Steane's code (2401 data qubits); compare with 881.
- "Magic-state teleportation": prove the circuit implements T up to a Clifford correction.
- "Propagation": prove the CNOT conjugation rules for X and Z, and work out where a Y error goes.
- "Transversal Cliffords on Steane": show transversal CNOT implements logical CNOT.
- "Doubly exponential": prove the overhead for logical error ε is polylog(1/ε) in the simplified model.
- "Distillation": rounds of 15-to-1 needed to go from 10⁻³ to below 10⁻¹⁵ (two), and the raw magic states consumed per output (225).
- "What Λ buys": with Λ = 2 per step of 2 in distance, from a measured d = 7 rate (given), the distance for 10⁻¹²; compare with the heuristic's d = 21.
- "Below threshold means": show that for p > p_th increasing d makes the heuristic p_L worse.
- "Eastin–Knill's consequence": explain why no amount of code choice removes the need for magic states or another non-transversal trick (code switching, named).
