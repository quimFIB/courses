The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: a proton in a 1.5 T MRI magnet, \(H =
-\frac{\gamma\hbar}{2}\mathbf B\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\) with \(\gamma/2\pi =
42.577\) MHz/T. It precesses about the field at \(\omega_0/2\pi = 63.87\) MHz,
one turn every 15.66 ns, and a full turn multiplies its state by \(-1\). A
field \(B_1 = 11.74\ \mu\)T turning with it gives \(\Omega/2\pi = 499.9\) Hz: a
\(\pi\)-pulse of 1.000 ms flips \(\ket0\) to \(i\ket1\), with \(\Omega/\omega_0 =
7.8\times10^{-6}\). A spin echo, \(\pi/2\) – wait – \(\pi\) – wait, is the single
rotation \(R_x(-3\pi/2) = e^{i\pi}R_z(-\frac\pi2)R_y(\frac\pi2)R_z(\frac\pi2)\)
for every detuning. Unit 00's test state \(\frac15(3\ket0 + 4i\ket1)\) has
Bloch vector \((0, \frac{24}{25}, -\frac7{25})\).

* The Pauli matrices, named

- magnetic moment :: The vector \(\boldsymbol\mu\) giving a small magnet's
  strength and direction; in a field \(\mathbf B\) its energy is \(E =
  -\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\mathbf B\), least when aligned. For a proton the moment
  along any axis takes two values, \(\pm\gamma\hbar/2\). /Slide: A proton is a
  small magnet./
- gyromagnetic ratio :: The constant \(\gamma\) relating a particle's magnetic
  moment to its spin: the moment along \(\mathbf n\) is the observable
  \(\frac{\gamma\hbar}{2}\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\). For the proton
  \(\gamma/2\pi = 42.577\,478\,518\) MHz/T (CODATA 2018), and \(\gamma \gt 0\).
- Pauli matrices :: \(X = \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\), \(Y =
  \begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\i&0\end{pmatrix}\), \(Z =
  \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}\), also \(\sigma_1, \sigma_2,
  \sigma_3\): Hermitian, unitary, traceless, with \(\sigma_j\sigma_k =
  \delta_{jk}I + i\sum_l\varepsilon_{jkl}\sigma_l\) — so \(XY = iZ\) and
  cyclically, distinct ones anticommute, and \(\operatorname{tr}(\sigma_j\sigma_k)
  = 2\delta_{jk}\). /Slide: The Pauli algebra./
- Levi-Civita symbol :: \(\varepsilon_{jkl}\) is \(+1\) if \((j, k, l)\) is a
  cyclic shift of \((1, 2, 3)\), \(-1\) if it is a shift of \((2, 1, 3)\), and
  \(0\) if an index repeats. The structure constants of the cross product:
  \(\mathbf e_j\times\mathbf e_k = \sum_l\varepsilon_{jkl}\mathbf e_l\).
- Hilbert–Schmidt inner product :: \(\braket{A, B} = \operatorname{tr}(A^\dagger
  B) = \sum\overline{A_{ij}}B_{ij}\) on matrices. \(\{I, X, Y, Z\}\) is an
  orthogonal basis of \(M_2(\mathbb C)\) for it, so \(A =
  \frac12\sum_\mu\operatorname{tr}(\sigma_\mu A)\sigma_\mu\), and \(A\) is
  Hermitian iff the four coefficients are real. /Slide: A basis of all 2×2
  matrices./
- Pauli vector :: The triple \(\boldsymbol\sigma = (X, Y, Z)\), with
  \(\mathbf a\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma = a_1X + a_2Y + a_3Z\). Product rule:
  \((\mathbf a\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)(\mathbf b\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma) =
  (\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b)I + i(\mathbf a\times\mathbf b)\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\).
  For a unit \(\mathbf n\), \(\mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\) has eigenvalues
  \(\pm1\) with projectors \(\frac12(I \pm \mathbf n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)\),
  and is the Stern–Gerlach measurement along \(\mathbf n\). /Slide: The Pauli
  vector, and measuring along any axis./

* States are points of a sphere

- projective line :: \(\mathbb{CP}^1\): unit vectors of \(\mathbb C^2\) modulo
  a global phase, equivalently the lines through the origin — the pure states of
  a qubit. Every class has a representative \(\cos\frac\theta2\ket0 +
  e^{i\varphi}\sin\frac\theta2\ket1\), \(0 \le \theta \le \pi\), with
  \(\varphi\) undefined at \(\theta = 0, \pi\). /Slide: Every state, in two
  angles./
- Bloch sphere :: The unit sphere \(S^2\subset\mathbb R^3\) as the space of
  pure qubit states: \(\ket0, \ket1\) at the poles, unit 00's \(x\) and \(y\)
  bases on the equator, the real states on the great circle of the
  \(xz\)-plane. /Slide: The Bloch map./
- Bloch vector :: \(\mathbf r(\psi) = \braket{\psi|\boldsymbol\sigma|\psi} =
  (\braket X, \braket Y, \braket Z) = (\sin\theta\cos\varphi,
  \sin\theta\sin\varphi, \cos\theta)\); its components are the Stern–Gerlach
  differences \(p_+ - p_-\) along \(x, y, z\). It depends only on the ray, and
  \(\ket\psi\!\bra\psi = \frac12(I + \mathbf r\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)\).
- Bloch map :: \([\psi]\mapsto\mathbf r(\psi)\), proved a bijection from
  \(\mathbb{CP}^1\) onto \(S^2\): injective because \(\mathbf r\) fixes the
  projector \(\frac12(I + \mathbf r\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)\), surjective because
  that projector has rank one for every unit \(\mathbf r\). No special case at
  the poles. /Slide: The Bloch map is a bijection onto the sphere./
- transition probability :: \(|\braket{\chi|\psi}|^2 = \frac12(1 + \mathbf
  r\cdot\mathbf s) = \cos^2\frac\alpha2\) for Bloch vectors at angle \(\alpha\).
  Orthogonal states are antipodes; unbiased states are at right angles; so two
  bases are mutually unbiased iff their axes are perpendicular, and
  \(\mathbb C^2\) holds exactly three. /Slide: Overlaps are angles./

* Unitaries are rotations

- rotation operator :: \(R_{\mathbf n}(\theta) = e^{-i\theta\,\mathbf
  n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma/2} = \cos\frac\theta2 I - i\sin\frac\theta2\,\mathbf
  n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\), unitary with determinant one. It turns the Bloch
  sphere by \(\theta\) about \(\mathbf n\): \(R_{\mathbf n}(\theta)(\mathbf
  r\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)R_{\mathbf n}(\theta)^\dagger = (R\mathbf
  r)\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\). \(S = e^{i\pi/4}R_z(\frac\pi2)\), \(T =
  e^{i\pi/8}R_z(\frac\pi4)\). /Slide: The rotation operators./
- Hadamard gate :: \(H = \frac1{\sqrt2}(X + Z) =
  \frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}\), the half-turn about
  \(\frac1{\sqrt2}(1, 0, 1)\) up to global phase: \(H = iR_{\mathbf n}(\pi)\).
  It swaps the \(z\) and \(x\) axes and reverses \(y\). /Slide: Worked: the
  Hadamard gate is a half-turn./
- Larmor precession :: The free motion of a spin in a static field: \(H =
  -\frac{\hbar\omega_0}{2}Z\) gives \(e^{-iHt/\hbar} = R_z(-\omega_0t)\), the
  Bloch vector turning about the field, clockwise from \(+z\) for \(\gamma \gt
  0\). An instance of: every \(aI + \mathbf b\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\) precesses
  about \(\hat{\mathbf b}\) at angular frequency \(2|\mathbf b|\). /Slide:
  Larmor precession: the proton in its field./
- Larmor frequency :: \(\omega_0 = \gamma B_0\), the angular frequency of
  Larmor precession and the energy gap over \(\hbar\). For the proton,
  \(\omega_0/2\pi = 63.87\) MHz at 1.5 T and 127.73 MHz at 3 T.

* SU(2) covers the rotations twice

- special unitary group :: \(SU(2) = \{V\in U(2) : \det V = 1\}\). Every
  element is a rotation operator \(R_{\mathbf n}(\theta)\), and every \(U\in
  U(2)\) is \(e^{i\alpha}V\) with \(V\in SU(2)\), unique up to a sign. /Slide:
  SU(2), and why every element is a rotation operator./
- rotation group :: \(SO(3)\), the real \(3\times3\) matrices with \(R^{\mathsf
  T}R = I\) and \(\det R = 1\). The homomorphism \(\Phi: SU(2)\to SO(3)\),
  \(U(\mathbf r\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)U^\dagger = (\Phi(U)\mathbf
  r)\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\), sends \(R_{\mathbf n}(\theta)\) to the rotation by
  \(\theta\) about \(\mathbf n\). /Slide: The homomorphism from SU(2) to
  rotations./
- Euler's rotation theorem :: Every \(R\in SO(3)\) is a rotation by some angle
  about some axis (Euler, 1775). Proof: \(\det(R - I) = -\det(R - I)\) in
  dimension three, so \(1\) is an eigenvalue; \(R\) then turns the
  perpendicular plane. /Slide: Euler's rotation theorem./
- double cover :: \(\Phi: SU(2)\to SO(3)\) is onto with kernel \(\{\pm I\}\),
  so \(SO(3)\cong SU(2)/\{\pm I\}\) and every rotation has exactly two lifts,
  \(\pm U\). Up to phase, qubit gates and rotations of the sphere are the same
  thing. /Slide: Φ is onto, and its kernel is ±I./
- spinor :: A vector of \(\mathbb C^2\) acted on by \(SU(2)\): a full turn
  \(R_{\mathbf n}(2\pi) = -I\) multiplies it by \(-1\), and it takes \(4\pi\) to
  return. The sign is a global phase, invisible on its own, and was seen
  relative to an unrotated reference by neutron interferometry (Rauch et al.;
  Werner et al.; 1975). /Slide: A turn by 2π is −I./

* Driving: resonance and the rotating frame

- Rabi frequency :: \(\Omega = \gamma B_1\) for a drive field of strength
  \(B_1\) turning in the plane perpendicular to the static field: the angular
  speed of the driven rotation on resonance. For the proton at \(B_1 =
  11.74\ \mu\)T, \(\Omega/2\pi = 499.9\) Hz. /Slide: Driving the proton./
- rotating frame :: The state \(\tilde\psi = V(t)^\dagger\psi\) with \(V(t) =
  R_z(-\omega t)\), turning with the drive. In it the Hamiltonian is constant:
  \(\tilde H = -\frac\hbar2(\Delta Z + \Omega X)\). Level populations are the
  same in both frames. /Slide: The rotating frame, derived./
- detuning :: \(\Delta = \omega_0 - \omega\), the drive's distance from
  resonance, with this course's proton signs. Probabilities depend only on
  \(\Delta^2\).
- effective field :: \(\mathbf B_{\text{eff}} = (B_1, 0, B_0 - \omega/\gamma)\):
  the fixed field the spin sees in the rotating frame, \(\tilde H =
  -\frac{\gamma\hbar}{2}\mathbf B_{\text{eff}}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\). On
  resonance only \(B_1\) is left.
- Rabi oscillation :: From \(\ket0\), \(P_1(t) =
  \frac{\Omega^2}{\Omega'^2}\sin^2\frac{\Omega't}{2}\) with \(\Omega' =
  \sqrt{\Omega^2 + \Delta^2}\) (Rabi, 1937): a full swing on resonance, a
  partial and faster one off it. /Slide: Rabi oscillation./
- π/2-pulse :: A resonant drive for time \(\pi/2\Omega\): \(R_x(-\frac\pi2)\),
  taking \(\ket0\) to \(\frac1{\sqrt2}(\ket0 + i\ket1) = \ket{+i}\), an equal
  superposition. /Slide: On resonance: the π/2-pulse and the π-pulse./
- π-pulse :: A resonant drive for time \(\pi/\Omega\): \(R_x(-\pi) = iX\), a
  flip \(\ket0\mapsto i\ket1\). For the proton above, 1.000 ms.
- counter-rotating term :: The half of a linearly oscillating drive that turns
  against the precession; in the rotating frame it turns at \(2\omega\):
  \(-\frac{\hbar\Omega}{2}(\cos2\omega t\,X + \sin2\omega t\,Y)\). /Slide: A
  linear drive is two rotating ones./
- rotating-wave approximation :: Dropping the counter-rotating term, justified
  when \(\Omega, |\Delta| \ll \omega_0\). For the proton \(\Omega/\omega_0 =
  7.8\times10^{-6}\) and the error is of order \(10^{-11}\); for a
  superconducting qubit it is five hundred times larger. /Slide: The
  rotating-wave approximation, and its price./
- Bloch–Siegert shift :: The first correction to the rotating-wave
  approximation: the resonance moves up by about \(\Omega^2/4\omega_0\) (Bloch
  and Siegert, 1940; cited). For the proton, 0.98 mHz.

* Every unitary in three turns

- ZYZ decomposition :: Every \(U\in U(2)\) is \(e^{i\alpha}R_z(\beta)R_y(\gamma)
  R_z(\delta)\), proved from the matrix entries. The Hadamard gate is
  \(iR_y(\frac\pi2)R_z(\pi)\). /Slide: The Z-Y-Z decomposition./
- Euler angles :: The \(\beta, \gamma, \delta\) of the ZYZ form. \(\gamma\) and
  \(\beta\) are the polar angle and longitude of the image of the north pole;
  at \(\gamma = 0\) only \(\beta + \delta\) is determined, at \(\gamma = \pi\)
  only \(\beta - \delta\) — gimbal lock.
- spin echo :: \(\pi/2\)-pulse, wait \(\tau\), \(\pi\)-pulse, wait \(\tau\)
  (Hahn, 1950): the \(\pi\)-pulse reverses the fan of spins with different
  detunings \(\delta\), and the product is \(R_x(-\frac{3\pi}2)\) for every
  \(\delta\). /Slide: The close: a spin echo./
