The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: the photon pair from a down-conversion crystal. Analysers at
the same setting on both sides — any linear angle \(\theta\), or circular —
always give opposite results, each way half the time. The model is the singlet
\(\ket{\Psi^-} = \tfrac1{\sqrt2}(\ket{01} - \ket{10})\) in \(\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^2\), with
\(\ket0\) horizontal and \(\ket1\) vertical: not a product state, not a random
choice of product states, and \((U\otimes U)\ket{\Psi^-} = \det(U)\ket{\Psi^-}\) for every
\(2\times2\) matrix \(U\), so it has the same Schmidt form in every basis.

* Two photons, and why not the direct sum

- polarisation :: The direction, across the beam, in which a light wave's
  electric field swings: horizontal, vertical, any angle \(\alpha\) between, or
  round a circle. Modelled as a unit vector of \(\mathbb C^2\): \(\ket H = \ket0\),
  \(\ket V = \ket1\), linear at \(\alpha\) is \(\cos\alpha\ket0 + \sin\alpha\ket1\), and
  circular is \(\ket{\pm i} = \tfrac1{\sqrt2}(\ket0 \pm i\ket1)\). /Slide: Polarisation, from nothing./
- polarising beam splitter :: A glass cube cut along a diagonal and coated, which
  transmits one polarisation and reflects the perpendicular one. Turned
  through \(\theta\), it measures in the basis \(\{\ket\theta, \ket{\theta + 90^\circ}\}\): an
  analyser. /Slide: Polarisation, from nothing./
- Malus's law :: Light polarised at \(\alpha\) through an analyser at \(\theta\): a fraction
  \(\cos^2(\alpha - \theta)\) is transmitted (1809). For single photons it is a
  probability, and the model reproduces it as \(|\braket{\theta|\alpha}|^2\). /Slide:
  Polarisation, from nothing./
- spontaneous parametric down-conversion :: In a non-linear crystal, a pump
  photon occasionally becomes two photons whose energies add to its own. Cut for
  type II, the pair has one H and one V photon on two crossing cones; where the
  cones cross, nothing tells which is which (Kwiat et al., 1995, cited).
  /Slide: Down-conversion: one photon in, two out./
- perfect anticorrelation :: Two outcomes, labelled by the same pair of exits,
  that are always different. The photon pair shows it for analysers at the same
  setting, whatever the setting. /Slide: The fact that needs a model./
- direct sum :: \(V\oplus W\), pairs \((v, w)\) with componentwise operations, of
  dimension \(\dim V + \dim W\). The right space for alternatives (one photon in
  beam A or B), the wrong one for conjunctions: it has no amplitude for a joint
  outcome, and it makes one photon's global phase visible. /Slide: The first
  guess: the direct sum./
- composite-system postulate :: The state space of systems A and B together is
  \(H_A\otimes H_B\); independent preparations \(\psi\), \(\varphi\) give \(\psi\otimes\varphi\). The
  fourth postulate, deferred by unit 01. Forced by asking joint amplitudes to be
  bilinear. /Slide: Joint amplitudes are bilinear./

* The tensor product

- bilinear map :: \(h: V\times W \to U\), linear in each argument when the other is
  fixed. /Slide: The universal property./
- tensor product :: A vector space \(V\otimes W\) with a bilinear map
  \(\otimes: V\times W \to V\otimes W\) such that every bilinear map out of
  \(V\times W\) is \(L\circ\otimes\) for exactly one linear map \(L\). Unique up to unique
  isomorphism; dimension \(\dim V\cdot\dim W\). /Slide: The universal property./
- universal property :: For every bilinear \(h: V\times W \to U\) there is exactly
  one linear \(L: V\otimes W \to U\) with \(h = L\circ\otimes\). The definition of the
  tensor product; it determines the space up to a unique isomorphism compatible
  with \(\otimes\). /Slide: The universal property./
- pure tensor :: An element \(v\otimes w\) of \(V\otimes W\); also called a simple
  tensor. Not a pair: \((2v)\otimes w = v\otimes(2w)\) and \(v\otimes0 = 0\). Pure tensors
  span, but most tensors are not pure. /Slide: The universal property./
- coefficient matrix :: For \(t = \sum_{ij}C_{ij}\,e_i\otimes f_j\), the matrix \(C\).
  A pure tensor has \(C = vw^{\mathsf T}\), with no conjugation; an operator
  \(A\otimes B\) sends \(C\) to \(ACB^{\mathsf T}\). /Slide: Computing in coordinates:
  matrices, and rank./
- tensor rank :: The least \(r\) with \(t = \sum_{k=1}^rv_k\otimes w_k\). For two factors
  it equals the rank of the coefficient matrix, so the pure tensors are the
  rank-one matrices. /Slide: Computing in coordinates: matrices, and rank./
- computational basis :: The orthonormal basis \(\ket{x_0x_1\cdots x_{n-1}} =
  \ket{x_0}\otimes\cdots\otimes\ket{x_{n-1}}\) of \(n\) qubits, \(x\) a bit string; \(2^n\) vectors.
  /Slide: n qubits, and the order of the bits./
- qubit order :: The course's convention: qubit 0 is the leftmost symbol of a ket
  and the most significant bit of an index, so \(\ket{01}\) is basis vector 1, and
  qubit 0 is the top wire of a circuit. Qiskit reverses it. /Slide: n qubits,
  and the order of the bits./
- tensor product of operators :: \(A\otimes B\), the unique linear map with \((A\otimes
  B)(v\otimes w) = Av\otimes Bw\). Obeys \((A\otimes B)(C\otimes D) = AC\otimes BD\) and \((A\otimes
  B)^\dagger = A^\dagger\otimes B^\dagger\); tensor products of unitaries, Hermitian
  operators and projectors are again such. /Slide: Operators on a tensor
  product./
- Kronecker product :: The matrix of \(A\otimes B\) with the first factor most
  significant: entry \(A_{ik}B_{jl}\) in row \((i, j)\), column \((k, l)\); in blocks,
  \(\begin{pmatrix}a_{00}B & a_{01}B\\ a_{10}B & a_{11}B\end{pmatrix}\). /Slide: The Kronecker matrix./

* Product or entangled

- product state :: A unit vector of \(H_A\otimes H_B\) of the form \(\psi\otimes\varphi\).
  For two qubits, exactly those with \(\det C = 0\). /Slide: Product states and
  entangled states./
- entangled state :: A pure state of two systems that is not a product state;
  equivalently, of Schmidt rank greater than one. Not the same as being a
  superposition: \(\ket+\ket+\) is a superposition of all four basis states and is a
  product. /Slide: Product states and entangled states./
- Bell states :: \(\ket{\Phi^\pm} = \tfrac1{\sqrt2}(\ket{00} \pm \ket{11})\), \(\ket{\Psi^\pm} =
  \tfrac1{\sqrt2}(\ket{01} \pm \ket{10})\): an orthonormal basis of \(\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^2\)
  of maximally entangled states, all obtained from \(\ket{\Phi^+}\) by \(I\), \(X\), \(Z\),
  \(XZ\) on qubit 1. \(\ket{\Phi^+}\) is invariant under \(U\otimes\bar U\). /Slide: The
  four Bell states./
- singlet :: \(\ket{\Psi^-} = \tfrac1{\sqrt2}(\ket{01} - \ket{10})\), the antisymmetric Bell
  state. \((U\otimes U)\ket{\Psi^-} = \det(U)\ket{\Psi^-}\), so it gives opposite results in
  every basis — the running example. /Slide: The four Bell states./

* The Schmidt decomposition

- singular value decomposition :: Every complex matrix of rank \(r\) is \(\sum_{k\le
  r}s_k\ket{u_k}\!\bra{v_k}\) with \(s_k > 0\) and orthonormal \(\{u_k\}\), \(\{v_k\}\); the
  \(s_k^2\) are the nonzero eigenvalues of \(C^\dagger C\). Proved from the spectral
  theorem applied to \(C^\dagger C\). /Slide: The singular value decomposition over
  C./
- Schmidt decomposition :: Every unit vector of \(H_A\otimes H_B\) is \(\sum_ks_k\,a_k\otimes
  b_k\) with orthonormal \(\{a_k\}\), \(\{b_k\}\), \(s_k > 0\) and \(\sum s_k^2 = 1\). From the SVD
  of the coefficient matrix, with \(b_k\) the conjugate of the right singular
  vector. /Slide: The Schmidt decomposition./
- Schmidt coefficients :: The \(s_k\) of a Schmidt decomposition. Determined by the
  state: their squares are the nonzero eigenvalues of \(CC^\dagger\). Unchanged by
  local unitaries \(U\otimes V\). /Slide: The Schmidt decomposition./
- Schmidt rank :: The number of Schmidt coefficients, equal to the rank of the
  coefficient matrix and at most \(\min(\dim H_A, \dim H_B)\). The state is a
  product if and only if it is one. /Slide: The Schmidt decomposition./
- maximally entangled :: For two qubits, Schmidt coefficients \(s_1 = s_2 =
  \tfrac1{\sqrt2}\); equivalently \(|\det C| = \tfrac12\), its largest possible value. Then
  every orthonormal basis of either qubit is a Schmidt basis. /Slide: What is
  unique, and the test for entanglement./

* Measuring one photon

- measurement on a subsystem :: Measuring an observable \(\sum\lambda P_\lambda\) of A alone,
  on a state of \(H_A\otimes H_B\), is the projective measurement \(\{P_\lambda\otimes I\}\):
  probability \(\braket{\psi|(P_\lambda\otimes I)|\psi}\), state afterwards
  \((P_\lambda\otimes I)\psi\) normalised. On the singlet it leaves photon B in the
  state orthogonal to A's result, and B's own statistics never change. /Slide: A
  measurement on one subsystem./
