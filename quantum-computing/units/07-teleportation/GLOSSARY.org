The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: Alice teleporting \(\ket\psi = \cos\frac\theta2\ket0 +
e^{i\varphi}\sin\frac\theta2\ket1\), unknown to her, to Bob, using one shared
\(\ket{\Phi^+}\) and two classical bits. Rewritten with Alice's pair in the Bell
basis, \(\ket\psi_C\ket{\Phi^+}_{AB} = \frac12\sum_{jk}\ket{\mathcal B_{jk}}_{CA}\otimes
X^kZ^j\ket\psi_B\); Alice's outcome \(jk\) has probability \(\frac14\) whatever
\(\psi\) is, and Bob's correction is \(Z^jX^k\). Until the bits arrive Bob's qubit
is \(I/2\). Without the shared pair, measuring in \(z\) and phoning the result
gives fidelity \(1 - \frac12\sin^2\theta\), which averages to \(\frac23\) over the
Bloch sphere.

* The identity

- cbit :: One classical bit delivered from one lab to another: a phone call
  carrying 0 or 1. A unit of the resource bookkeeping. /Slide: Three qubits,
  three resources./
- ebit :: One copy of \(\ket{\Phi^+} = (\ket{00} + \ket{11})/\sqrt2\) shared in
  advance, one qubit in each lab: a unit of entanglement. On its own it carries
  no message (no-signalling); spent with two cbits it moves a qubit, spent with a
  qubit it moves two cbits. /Slide: Three qubits, three resources./
- teleportation identity :: For every \(\ket\psi\in\mathbb C^2\),
  \(\ket\psi_C\ket{\Phi^+}_{AB} = \frac12\sum_{j,k}\ket{\mathcal B_{jk}}_{CA}\otimes
  X^kZ^j\ket\psi_B\), where \(\ket{\mathcal B_{jk}} = (I\otimes
  X^kZ^j)\ket{\Phi^+}\) labels the Bell states (\(\mathcal B_{00} = \Phi^+\),
  \(\mathcal B_{01} = \Psi^+\), \(\mathcal B_{10} = \Phi^-\), \(\mathcal B_{11} =
  \Psi^-\)). A rewriting of the starting state, linear in \(\psi\), not an event.
  /Slide: The teleportation identity./

* The protocol

- Bell measurement :: The projective measurement
  \(\{\ket{\mathcal B_{jk}}\!\bra{\mathcal B_{jk}}\}\) on two qubits, with outcome
  the two bits \(jk\): \(j\) the sign, \(k\) the parity. Realised by
  \(\mathrm{CNOT}_{01}\), then \(H\) on the first qubit, then a \(z\) measurement
  of each, because \((H\otimes I)\mathrm{CNOT}_{01}\ket{\mathcal B_{jk}} =
  \ket{jk}\). /Slide: The Bell measurement, rotated to the computational basis./
- teleportation :: The protocol that moves an unknown qubit state using one ebit
  and two cbits: Alice makes a Bell measurement on her input and her half of the
  pair and phones the outcome \(jk\); Bob applies \(Z^jX^k\) to his half, which
  then holds \(\ket\psi\) exactly. The original is left in Alice's lab in
  \(\ket{jk}\), so nothing is cloned. Bennett et al. 1993. /Slide: The
  teleportation circuit./

* What does not happen

- Pauli twirl :: The map \(M \mapsto \frac14\sum_{jk}\sigma_{jk}M\sigma_{jk}^\dagger\)
  with \(\sigma_{jk}\in\{I, X, Z, XZ\}\). It sends every \(2\times2\) matrix to
  \(\frac12\operatorname{tr}(M)\,I\), so every qubit state to \(I/2\): each Pauli
  flips two Bloch components and each component is flipped by two of the four.
  It is why Bob's qubit is \(I/2\) before the bits arrive. /Slide: Before the
  bits arrive, Bob holds I/2./

* The reverse trade

- dense coding :: Two classical bits sent through one qubit with the help of one
  ebit: Alice applies \(Z^jX^k\) to her half of \(\ket{\Phi^+}\), giving exactly
  \(\ket{\mathcal B_{jk}}\), sends it to Bob, and his Bell measurement reads
  \(jk\). Bennett and Wiesner 1992; also called superdense coding. /Slide: Dense
  coding: two bits in one qubit./
- resource inequality :: \(\mathcal R \ge \mathcal S\): a protocol consuming the
  resources \(\mathcal R\), with free local operations, delivers \(\mathcal S\)
  exactly. Teleportation: 1 ebit + 2 cbits \(\ge\) 1 qubit; dense coding: 1 ebit
  + 1 qubit \(\ge\) 2 cbits; trivially 1 qubit \(\ge\) 1 cbit and 1 qubit
  \(\ge\) 1 ebit. Both protocols are tight. /Slide: Resource inequalities as
  bookkeeping./
- Holevo bound :: A message encoded in \(n\) qubits, with no entanglement shared
  beforehand, gives at most \(n\) bits of information about the message, whatever
  measurement is made (Holevo 1973; cited here, proved in unit 08). Its zero-error
  case — at most \(d\) states on \(\mathbb C^d\) can be told apart with certainty —
  is proved in the deck. /Slide: Dense coding cannot be beaten./

* Benchmarks

- fidelity :: Of an output \(\rho\) with a pure target \(\psi\):
  \(F = \braket{\psi|\rho|\psi}\), the probability that \(\rho\) passes the test
  "is it \(\psi\)?". In Bloch terms \(F = \frac12(1 + \mathbf r\cdot\mathbf s)\).
  /Slide: Average fidelity: one number for a whole teleporter./
- average fidelity :: \(\bar F = \int F(\psi, \rho_{\text{out}}(\psi))\,\mathrm
  d\mu(\psi)\) with \(\mu\) uniform on the Bloch sphere, \(\mathrm d\mu =
  \sin\theta\,\mathrm d\theta\,\mathrm d\varphi/4\pi\). The figure of merit of a
  teleporter: \(1\) ideally, \(\frac12\) for doing nothing, at most \(\frac23\)
  without entanglement. /Slide: Average fidelity: one number for a whole
  teleporter./
- measure-and-prepare :: The strategy without entanglement: Alice measures the
  input and sends the outcome; Bob prepares the corresponding state. In the \(z\)
  basis its fidelity is \(1 - \frac12\sin^2\theta\) and its average fidelity
  \(\frac23\). /Slide: Worked: measure and prepare scores two-thirds./
- classical benchmark :: \(\bar F \le \frac23\) for every strategy with no shared
  entanglement and one copy of the input (Massar and Popescu 1995, cited), met by
  measure-and-prepare. A measured average fidelity above \(\frac23\) certifies that
  entanglement was used. /Slide: The classical benchmark: two-thirds./
- entanglement swapping :: Teleporting half of a Bell pair: from
  \(\ket{\Phi^+}_{12}\ket{\Phi^+}_{34}\), a Bell measurement on 2 and 3 with outcome
  \(jk\) leaves 1 and 4, which never interacted, in \(\ket{\mathcal B_{jk}}_{14}\).
  Żukowski et al. 1993; demonstrated by Pan et al. 1998. /Slide: Teleporting
  half a pair: entanglement swapping./
- quantum repeater :: A chain of short ebits joined by entanglement swapping into
  one long ebit, to beat the exponential loss of photons over distance without
  copying them. Briegel, Dür, Cirac and Zoller 1998. /Slide: Teleporting half a
  pair: entanglement swapping./
