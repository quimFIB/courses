The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: the Toffoli gate, \(\ket{a, b, c}\mapsto\ket{a, b, c\oplus
ab}\) on three qubits — an \(8\times8\) permutation matrix that swaps
\(\ket{110}\) and \(\ket{111}\) and fixes the rest. It computes NAND and copies
bits, so it runs every classical circuit reversibly; as a two-level unitary on
neighbouring basis states it is \(C^2(X)\). Barenco's general construction
builds it from 8 CNOTs, 9 \(T\)-type gates and 2 \(H\); the textbook circuit
from 6 CNOTs, 2 \(H\) and 7 \(T\) or \(T^\dagger\), verified by its phase
table: the seven \(T\)-type phases sum to \(4abx\) (units of \(\pi/4\)), so the
middle of the circuit is \(C^2(Z)\) and the two \(H\)'s make it the Toffoli. A
generic three-qubit unitary, by contrast, has 63 real parameters.

* The circuit model

- quantum circuit :: A finite list of gates \(G_1, \dots, G_m\), each a unitary
  on a few named qubits (its wires) and the identity on the rest, acting on
  \((\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes n}\). It denotes the product \(U = G_m\cdots G_1\):
  the diagram is read left to right in time, the formula right to left. Qubit 0
  is the top wire and the most significant bit. /Slide: Wires, gates, and the
  product they denote./
- gate :: A unitary on one, two or three qubits, placed on chosen wires of a
  circuit. A gate set is the list of gates allowed: in this unit, all one-qubit
  gates and CNOT.
- controlled-U :: For a unitary \(U\), the gate \(C(U) = \ket0\!\bra0\otimes I +
  \ket1\!\bra1\otimes U\), block diagonal \(\mathrm{diag}(I, U)\), so unitary: it
  applies \(U\) to the target when the control reads 1. With \(k\) controls,
  \(C^k(U)\) applies \(U\) when all read 1. A controlled global phase
  \(C(e^{i\alpha}I)\) is a relative phase on the control, not a global one.
  /Slide: Controlled gates./
- CNOT :: The controlled-\(X\) gate, \(\ket{c, t}\mapsto\ket{c, t\oplus c}\);
  \(\mathrm{CNOT}_{01}\) has control 0 and target 1. A permutation matrix, its
  own inverse.
- open control :: A control drawn as an open circle, which fires when its
  qubit reads 0: an ordinary control conjugated by \(X\) on that qubit.
- Toffoli gate :: \(C^2(X)\): \(\ket{a, b, c}\mapsto\ket{a, b, c\oplus ab}\).
  The running example; universal for reversible classical computation.
- permutation matrix :: A matrix with one entry 1 in each row and column and 0
  elsewhere, sending each basis vector to a basis vector. Its columns are
  orthonormal, so it is unitary. /Slide: The Toffoli gate, as a matrix./
- classically controlled gate :: A gate applied only if a measured bit, carried
  by a double line, is 1. Not a single operator but one per measurement branch;
  deferred measurement replaces it by a controlled gate. /Slide: Measurement and
  classical control./
- size :: The number of gates in a circuit: the total work. /Slide: Size and
  depth./
- depth :: The least number of layers of gates on disjoint wires a circuit can
  be cut into; equivalently the longest chain of gates each sharing a wire with
  the next. The textbook Toffoli circuit has size 15 and depth 11.

* Classical computation, reversibly

- reversible gate :: A bijection of \(\{0, 1\}^k\). Its matrix is a permutation
  matrix, so every reversible gate is a quantum gate. AND is not reversible;
  \((x, y)\mapsto(x, y\oplus f(x))\) is, for every \(f\). /Slide: Reversible
  gates are permutations./
- garbage :: The intermediate values a gate-by-gate reversible translation
  leaves on its work bits, \(\ket x\ket0\mapsto\ket x\ket{f(x)}\ket{g(x)}\). On
  a superposition of inputs it is entangled with \(x\) and acts as a which-path
  record: interference between different \(x\) is lost. /Slide: Garbage
  destroys interference./
- uncomputation (Bennett's trick) :: Compute \(f\) with garbage by a reversible
  circuit \(C\), copy the output onto a target with CNOTs, then run
  \(C^{-1}\): the result is \(\ket x\ket0\ket0\ket y\mapsto\ket
  x\ket0\ket0\ket{y\oplus f(x)}\), with the work registers restored and
  unentangled, at about twice the gates (Bennett 1973). /Slide: Bennett's
  uncomputation./
- work qubit (ancilla) :: A qubit borrowed in a known state, usually
  \(\ket0\), and returned in it, so that it ends unentangled with the rest and
  can be reused.
- oracle :: For \(f:\{0, 1\}^n\to\{0, 1\}^m\), the unitary \(U_f\ket x\ket y =
  \ket x\ket{y\oplus f(x)}\), work qubits returned to \(\ket0\). If \(f\) is
  polynomial-time, \(U_f\) has polynomial-size circuits of Toffoli, CNOT and
  \(X\) gates. /Slide: Every polynomial-time function is a polynomial-size
  oracle./

* Deferred measurement

- deferred measurement :: A measurement whose outcome is used only to control
  later gates may be replaced by the corresponding quantum-controlled gate,
  \(W = \ket0\!\bra0\otimes U_0 + \ket1\!\bra1\otimes U_1\), with the
  measurement moved to the end: every joint outcome probability, and every
  post-measurement state, is unchanged. /Slide: The principle of deferred
  measurement./
- implicit measurement :: A qubit no longer acted on may be measured or not:
  the statistics of the other qubits are the same, since \(\sum_m\lVert(P_m\otimes
  Q_k)\phi\rVert^2 = \lVert(I\otimes Q_k)\phi\rVert^2\). /Slide: Measure at the
  end, or not at all./

* Exact universality

- exact universality :: A gate set is exactly universal if every unitary on
  any number of qubits is exactly a product of its gates. CNOT together with
  all one-qubit gates is (Barenco et al. 1995). /Slide: The plan: four
  reductions./
- two-level unitary :: A unitary on \(\mathbb C^d\) that fixes all basis
  vectors but two, \(\ket s, \ket t\), and acts on their span as a \(2\times2\)
  unitary. Every \(d\times d\) unitary is a product of at most \(d(d-1)/2\) of
  them. /Slide: Two-level unitaries, and the step that zeroes an entry./
- multiply-controlled gate :: A one-qubit gate on one wire, controlled by
  several others (filled or open controls): \(C^k(\tilde U)\) up to \(X\) gates.
  A two-level unitary on two basis states that differ in one bit is exactly
  such a gate. /Slide: Two-level on neighbours is a controlled one-qubit gate./
- Gray code :: A sequence of bit strings from \(s\) to \(t\) in which
  consecutive strings differ in one bit; flipping the differing bits one at a
  time gives one with at most \(n + 1\) terms. Swapping
  along it moves a two-level unitary onto neighbouring basis states. /Slide:
  Gray codes: walking two states together./
- ABC construction :: For a one-qubit \(U = e^{i\alpha}AXBXC\) with \(ABC = I\)
  (from the Z-Y-Z form: \(A = R_z(\beta)R_y(\gamma/2)\), \(B =
  R_y(-\gamma/2)R_z(-(\delta+\beta)/2)\), \(C = R_z((\delta-\beta)/2)\)), the
  controlled-\(U\) is \(C\), CNOT, \(B\), CNOT, \(A\) on the target and the phase
  gate \(\mathrm{diag}(1, e^{i\alpha})\) on the control. /Slide: Controlled-U
  from two CNOTs: the ABC construction./
- phase polynomial :: A circuit of CNOTs and \(T\), \(T^\dagger\) gates that
  returns every wire to its input acts diagonally, \(\ket v\mapsto e^{i\pi
  f(v)/4}\ket v\), where \(f\) adds \(\pm\) the XOR of inputs held on the wire
  at each \(T\) (\(+\)) or \(T^\dagger\) (\(-\)). For the Toffoli's middle,
  \(f = 4abx\). /Slide: The textbook Toffoli: six CNOTs, seven T gates./

* Counting

- generic unitary :: A property holds for a generic unitary if the unitaries
  failing it form a set of measure zero. A generic \(n\)-qubit unitary needs at
  least \((4^n - 1)/16\), so at least \(4^{n-2}\), gates on at most two qubits,
  because the circuits with fewer form the smooth image of a space of
  dimension below \(\dim U(2^n) = 4^n\). /Slide: The counting lower bound./
