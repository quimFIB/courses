The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: phase estimation of the eigenphase \(\varphi = \tfrac13\) of
\(U = \mathrm{diag}(1, e^{2\pi i/3})\), eigenvector \(\ket1\), with three
counting qubits. The register lives on \(\mathbb Z_8\), whose characters are
\(\chi_k(j) = \omega^{jk}\) with \(\omega = e^{2\pi i/8}\); the transform
\(F_8\) is a seven-gate circuit (three \(H\), three controlled phases, one
swap), and \(F_8\ket5 = 2^{-3/2}(\ket0 - \ket1)(\ket0 + i\ket1)(\ket0 +
\omega^5\ket1)\) is a product state. Since \(2^3\varphi = 8/3\) is not an
integer, no outcome is certain: the nearest fraction \(3/8\) appears with
probability \(0.688\), its neighbour \(2/8\) with \(0.175\), and the eight
probabilities sum to 1. The guarantee for the nearest fraction is
\(4/\pi^2\approx0.405\).

* Characters

- character :: For a finite abelian group \(G\), a homomorphism \(\chi: G\to
  U(1)\), \(\chi(g + h) = \chi(g)\chi(h)\). Its values are \(|G|\)-th roots
  of unity, so \(|\chi(g)| = 1\) and \(\chi(-g) = \overline{\chi(g)}\). The
  characters of \(\mathbb Z_N\) are \(\chi_k(j) = \omega^{jk}\), \(\omega =
  e^{2\pi i/N}\); those of \(\mathbb Z_2^n\) are \((-1)^{x\cdot y}\).
  /Slide: Characters, and the characters of \(\mathbb Z_N\)./
- dual group :: \(\hat G\), the characters of \(G\) under pointwise
  multiplication. \(\hat{\mathbb Z}_N\cong\mathbb Z_N\) via \(k\mapsto\chi_k\),
  \(\widehat{G\times H}\cong\hat G\times\hat H\), and so \(\hat G\cong G\) for
  every finite abelian \(G\) — but not canonically.
- trivial character :: \(\chi_0\equiv1\), the identity of \(\hat G\). Every
  other character sums to zero over the group.
- structure theorem :: Every finite abelian group is isomorphic to a product
  of cyclic groups \(\mathbb Z_{N_1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb Z_{N_r}\).
  Cited (Dummit and Foote §5.2); used only to get \(|\hat G| = |G|\) in
  general. /Slide: Products of groups, and \(\hat G\cong G\)./
- orthogonality of characters :: \(\sum_g\overline{\chi(g)}\psi(g) =
  |G|\delta_{\chi\psi}\). Proved by translation invariance: a non-trivial
  character's sum \(S\) satisfies \(S = \chi(h)S\) with \(\chi(h)\ne1\), so
  \(S = 0\). The vectors \(|G|^{-1/2}\chi\) are an orthonormal basis of
  \(\mathbb C^G\). /Slide: Orthogonality of characters./

* The Fourier transform

- Fourier transform :: Of a finite abelian group: \(F_G\ket g =
  |G|^{-1/2}\sum_{\chi}\chi(g)\ket\chi\), the change of basis to the
  characters; unitary because its rows are orthonormal and it is square. It
  turns translation into a diagonal phase: \(F_GT_a = D_aF_G\). On
  \(\mathbb Z_2^n\) it is \(H^{\otimes n}\). /Slide: The Fourier transform of
  a finite abelian group./
- quantum Fourier transform (QFT) :: \(F_N\ket j = N^{-1/2}\sum_k\omega^{jk}\ket
  k\), \(\omega = e^{+2\pi i/N}\), on \(\mathbb Z_N\); on qubits \(N =
  2^n\). Numerical libraries' DFT uses the opposite sign and no
  normalisation.
- binary fraction :: \(0.j_lj_{l+1}\cdots j_n = j_l/2 + j_{l+1}/4 + \cdots +
  j_n/2^{n-l+1}\). Dividing an integer by \(2^l\) and discarding the integer
  part leaves its last \(l\) bits as a binary fraction. /Slide: On \(\mathbb
  Z_{2^n}\), a basis state goes to a product state./
- product formula :: \(F_{2^n}\ket j = \bigotimes_{l=1}^n2^{-1/2}(\ket0 +
  e^{2\pi i\,0.j_{n-l+1}\cdots j_n}\ket1)\), factor \(l\) on qubit \(l - 1\):
  the transform of a basis state is a product state. Proved by expanding
  \(k\) in binary.

* The circuit

- controlled phase rotation :: \(R_k = \mathrm{diag}(1, e^{2\pi i/2^k}) =
  P(2\pi/2^k)\), so \(R_1 = Z\), \(R_2 = S\), \(R_3 = T\). Controlled, it is
  \(\mathrm{diag}(1, 1, 1, e^{2\pi i/2^k})\), symmetric in its two qubits;
  from a control holding bit \(b\) it adds \(b/2^k\) to the target's phase.
  The QFT uses \(n(n-1)/2\) of them. /Slide: Two gates build every factor./
- fast Fourier transform (FFT) :: The classical algorithm (Cooley–Tukey 1965,
  cited) computing the discrete Fourier transform of an \(N\)-vector in
  \(O(N\log N)\) operations — about \(n2^n\) for \(N = 2^n\). The QFT is
  exponentially cheaper but its output cannot be read out. /Slide:
  Exponentially faster at a task nobody wants./
- operator norm :: \(\lVert A\rVert = \max_{\lVert v\rVert = 1}\lVert
  Av\rVert\); for diagonal \(D\), the largest \(|d_i|\). Errors add along a
  circuit: \(\lVert\prod U_i - \prod V_i\rVert\le\sum\lVert U_i - V_i\rVert\),
  and an outcome probability moves by at most twice the norm error. /Slide:
  The approximate QFT: most rotations can be dropped./
- approximate QFT :: The QFT circuit with every \(C(R_k)\), \(k \gt m\),
  dropped. Its error is at most \(2\pi(n - m)/2^m\) in operator norm
  (Coppersmith 1994), so \(m = \log n + O(1)\) gives constant error with
  \(O(n\log n)\) gates.
- inverse QFT :: \(F_N^{-1}\ket k = N^{-1/2}\sum_j\omega^{-jk}\ket j\): the QFT
  circuit in reverse order with each gate replaced by its adjoint, so each
  \(R_k\) by \(R_k^\dagger\). /Slide: The inverse QFT./

* Phase estimation

- phase kickback :: If \(U\ket u = e^{2\pi i\varphi}\ket u\), then \(C(U)\)
  maps \((\alpha\ket0 + \beta\ket1)\ket u\) to \((\alpha\ket0 + \beta
  e^{2\pi i\varphi}\ket1)\ket u\): the eigenvector is unchanged and the phase
  moves to the control. Unit 13's kickback is the case \(U = X\), \(\ket u =
  \ket-\). /Slide: Kickback of an eigenphase./
- eigenphase :: The \(\varphi\in[0, 1)\) with \(U\ket u = e^{2\pi
  i\varphi}\ket u\); defined modulo 1, since every eigenvalue of a unitary
  has modulus 1.
- Hadamard test :: \(H\), \(C(U)\), \(H\) on a control qubit, with \(\ket u\)
  on the target: \(\Pr(0) = \cos^2\pi\varphi\). Phase estimation with one
  counting qubit; the interferometer of unit 01 with the eigenphase as the
  slab. For \(\varphi = \tfrac13\), \(\Pr(0) = \tfrac14\).
- phase estimation :: \(t\) counting qubits in \(\ket+\), counting qubit \(l\)
  controlling \(U^{2^{t-1-l}}\) on an eigenvector \(\ket u\), then
  \(F_{2^t}^{-1}\) and a measurement giving \(y\), the estimate \(y/2^t\).
  Exact when \(2^t\varphi\) is an integer; otherwise outcome \(y\) has
  probability \(\sin^2(\pi\delta)/(2^{2t}\sin^2(\pi\delta/2^t))\), \(\delta
  = 2^t\varphi - y\). On a superposition of eigenvectors it samples one
  phase with the superposition's weights (Kitaev 1995; Cleve, Ekert,
  Macchiavello and Mosca 1998). /Slide: The phase-estimation circuit./
- counting register :: The \(t\) qubits of phase estimation that control the
  powers of \(U\) and are measured; their label lives on \(\mathbb
  Z_{2^t}\).
- Parseval :: A unitary preserves norms, so the probabilities of the
  outcomes after the inverse QFT sum to 1. Applied to phase estimation it
  proves \(\sum_y\csc^2(x - \pi y/N) = N^2\csc^2(Nx)\). /Slide: The general
  case: the outcome distribution./

* Precision

- \(4/\pi^2\) bound :: The integer nearest \(2^t\varphi\) is the outcome with
  probability at least \(4/\pi^2\approx0.405\), for every \(\varphi\) and
  \(t\); proved from \(|\sin x|\le|x|\) and \(\sin x\ge2x/\pi\) on \([0,
  \pi/2]\). Approached when \(\varphi\) is halfway between two \(t\)-bit
  fractions and \(t\) grows. With \(t = n + \lceil\log(2 +
  1/2\varepsilon)\rceil\) counting qubits the estimate is within \(2^{-n}\)
  with probability at least \(1 - \varepsilon\). /Slide: The nearest
  fraction, with probability at least \(4/\pi^2\)./
