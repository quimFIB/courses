The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: the transverse-field Ising chain \(H = -J\sum_i Z_iZ_{i+1} -
h\sum_i X_i\) on \(n\) spins, with \(J = h = 1\), split as \(H = A + B\) with \(A\)
the \(ZZ\) part and \(B\) the \(X\) part. Classically it needs \(2^n\) amplitudes:
18 PB at \(n = 50\). Its commutator satisfies \(\lVert[A, B]\rVert \le 4(n - 1)\)
by counting (28 at \(n = 8\); exactly 19.0). At \(n = 8\), \(t = 5\), first-order
Trotter with 10, 100 and 1000 steps has error 1.99, 0.147 and 0.0144 against the
bounds 23.8, 2.38 and 0.238; Strang at 1000 steps, 0.00025. One step costs 15
rotations and 14 CNOTs, so 1000 steps cost 15,000 rotations, 14,000 CNOTs and
about \(9.0\times10^5\) T gates at \(10^{-6}\) per rotation. The ground energy
\(-9.838\) is read by phase estimation on \(e^{-iH\tau}\), \(\tau = \pi/16\), with 11
counting qubits as \(-9.844\); the input \(\ket{+}^{\otimes 8}\) has weight 0.565 on
the ground state, \(\ket{0}^{\otimes 8}\) only 0.116.

* Feynman's reason

- transverse-field Ising chain :: \(H = -J\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}Z_iZ_{i+1} -
  h\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}X_i\) with \(J, h > 0\): \(n\) spins in a row whose
  neighbours prefer to agree along \(z\) (the \(J\) term) while a magnetic field
  along \(x\) pulls each towards \(\ket{+}\) (the \(h\) term). The two parts do
  not commute. /Slide: Spins in a row./
- quantum phase transition :: An abrupt change in the character of the lowest
  state of a family of Hamiltonians as a parameter crosses a critical value,
  in the limit of infinitely many particles. For the infinite transverse-field
  Ising chain it happens at \(J = h\): ordered for \(h < J\), disordered for \(h >
  J\) (Pfeuty 1970, cited). /Slide: What the two terms want./
- Hamiltonian simulation :: Given \(H\) on \(n\) qubits, a time \(t\) and an
  accuracy \(\varepsilon\), produce a circuit \(W\) with \(\lVert W -
  e^{-iHt}\rVert \le \varepsilon\). Every outcome probability after \(W\) is then
  within \(2\varepsilon\) of the true one. /Slide: What a simulator must deliver./
- magnetisation :: \(M = \frac1n\sum_i Z_i\), the average moment along \(z\); its
  expectation is estimated by repeating a simulation and averaging the \(\pm1\)
  outcomes.
- k-local Hamiltonian :: \(H = \sum_{j=1}^L H_j\) with each \(H_j\) Hermitian and
  acting on at most \(k = O(1)\) qubits, and \(L\) polynomial in \(n\). The chain
  is 2-local with \(L = 2n - 1\). /Slide: Local Hamiltonians./

* Lie–Trotter, proved

- Trotter step :: The replacement of \(e^{-i(A+B)\delta}\) by
  \(e^{-iA\delta}e^{-iB\delta}\). The two agree to first order in \(\delta\) and
  differ at second by \(-\frac12[A, B]\delta^2\). /Slide: The error, to leading
  order./
- one-step bound :: For Hermitian \(A\), \(B\) and \(\delta > 0\),
  \(\lVert e^{-i(A+B)\delta} - e^{-iA\delta}e^{-iB\delta}\rVert \le
  \frac12\lVert[A, B]\rVert\delta^2\). Proved by differentiating \(F =
  U^\dagger V\), bounding \(\lVert F'(s)\rVert \le s\lVert[A, B]\rVert\), and
  integrating. /Slide: One step costs at most half a commutator./
- first-order Trotter bound :: \(\lVert e^{-i(A+B)t} -
  (e^{-iAt/r}e^{-iBt/r})^r\rVert \le \lVert[A, B]\rVert t^2/2r\): the one-step
  bound, added over \(r\) steps by the hybrid argument. For accuracy
  \(\varepsilon\), \(r \ge \lVert[A, B]\rVert t^2/2\varepsilon\). /Slide: r steps:
  the errors add./
- Lie product formula :: \(\lim_{r\to\infty}(e^{-iAt/r}e^{-iBt/r})^r =
  e^{-i(A+B)t}\); Lie for matrices, Trotter (1959) for operators.

* The numbers, and second order

- Strang splitting (second-order product formula) :: The symmetric step
  \(S(\delta) = e^{-iA\delta/2}e^{-iB\delta}e^{-iA\delta/2}\). Its error is
  \(O(\delta^3)\) per step, so \(O(t^3/r^2)\) over \(r\) steps, with the
  nested-commutator constant \(\frac{\delta^3}{12}\lVert[B, [B, A]]\rVert +
  \frac{\delta^3}{24}\lVert[A, [A, B]]\rVert\) (cited). Adjacent half-steps merge,
  so it costs almost exactly what first order does. /Slide: Strang's symmetric
  splitting./
- Suzuki formulas :: Product formulas of every even order \(2k\), built
  recursively from Strang's, with error \(O(\delta^{2k+1})\) per step (Suzuki
  1991, cited). /Slide: Higher orders, and sharper bounds./
- commutator scaling :: Bounding a product formula's error by norms of nested
  commutators of the terms rather than by their norms (Childs, Su, Tran, Wiebe
  and Zhu 2021, cited). For local Hamiltonians most pairs commute, so the
  constant grows like \(n\), not \(n^2\).
- quantum signal processing :: A method (Low and Chuang 2017) that transforms
  the eigenvalues of a unitary encoding \(H\) by a polynomial, using a sequence
  of single-qubit rotations; with qubitisation it simulates \(e^{-iHt}\) with
  optimal cost in \(t\) and \(\varepsilon\). Named, not developed.
- qubitisation :: Low and Chuang's (2019) construction of a walk operator from
  a block encoding of \(H/\lambda\), giving simulation in \(O(\lambda t +
  \log(1/\varepsilon))\) uses, \(\lambda\) the sum of the absolute Pauli
  coefficients of \(H\). Named, not developed.

* From steps to gates

- Pauli-product rotation :: \(e^{-i\theta P}\) for a Pauli product \(P = P_1
  \otimes\cdots\otimes P_k\). Built from one \(R_z(2\theta)\), \(2(k-1)\) CNOTs
  computing and uncomputing a parity, and single-qubit Cliffords changing each
  factor's basis to \(Z\). For \(k = 2\): \(e^{-i\theta Z\otimes Z} =
  \mathrm{CNOT}\,(I\otimes R_z(2\theta))\,\mathrm{CNOT}\). /Slide: Any Pauli product:
  change basis, compute the parity./

* Energies, by phase estimation

- ground state :: An eigenvector of \(H\) for its least eigenvalue. Every unit
  vector has \(\braket{\psi|H|\psi} \ge E_0\) (the variational principle).
  /Slide: Energies are eigenphases of the evolution./
- ground energy :: The least eigenvalue \(E_0\) of \(H\). For the chain at
  \(n = 8\), \(J = h = 1\): \(-9.838\). Read by phase estimation on
  \(e^{-iH\tau}\), whose eigenphase for energy \(E\) is \(-E\tau/2\pi \bmod 1\).
- ground-state overlap :: \(|\braket{E_0|\psi}|^2\) for the input \(\psi\) of
  phase estimation: the probability that a run samples the ground energy at
  all. It can be exponentially small in \(n\), and preparing a state with large
  overlap is, in the worst case, the hard part (unit 20). /Slide: The catch: the
  input must overlap the ground state./
