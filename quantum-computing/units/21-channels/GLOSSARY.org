The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: a qubit prepared in \(\ket+\) and left alone in a noisy
device. Under amplitude damping for time \(t\), with \(\gamma = 1 -
e^{-t/T_1}\), it becomes \(\rho(t) = \frac12\begin{pmatrix}1 + \gamma &
e^{-t/2T_1}\\ e^{-t/2T_1} & 1 - \gamma\end{pmatrix}\), Bloch vector
\((e^{-t/2T_1}, 0, \gamma)\): at \(t = T_1\), \((0.6065, 0, 0.6321)\), purity
\(0.8837\). With pure dephasing as well the coherence decays as
\(e^{-t/T_2}\), \(1/T_2 = 1/2T_1 + 1/T_\varphi\). Alongside it, the unambiguous
test for \(\ket0\) versus \(\ket+\) succeeds with probability \(1 -
\frac1{\sqrt2} \approx 0.293\) and is never wrong; and \(\ket+\), \(\ket-\)
in the device have trace distance \(1 - p\) after depolarising noise and
\(e^{-t/2T_1}\) after amplitude damping, which no later processing can raise.

* Generalised measurement

- POVM (positive-operator-valued measure) :: Operators \(E_1, \dots, E_M\)
  on \(H\) with \(E_m \ge 0\) and \(\sum_mE_m = I\); outcome \(m\) has
  probability \(\operatorname{tr}(E_m\rho)\). \(M\) may exceed \(\dim H\).
  Projective measurements are the case of projectors. Every projective
  measurement on the system together with a prepared helper induces one:
  \(E_m = (I\otimes\bra a)P_m(I\otimes\ket a)\). /Slide: POVMs: the
  measurements a larger system induces./
- effect :: One operator \(E_m\) of a POVM: \(0 \le E_m \le I\).
- unambiguous discrimination :: Telling two non-orthogonal states apart with a
  test that is never wrong but may answer "don't know". For \(\ket0\) versus
  \(\ket+\): effects \(c\ket1\!\bra1\), \(c\ket-\!\bra-\) and the rest, \(c = 2
  - \sqrt2\), success \(1 - \frac1{\sqrt2} \approx 0.293\) on each state,
  optimal (problem P1). /Slide: Worked: a test that is never wrong./
- Naimark's dilation :: Every POVM is a projective measurement on a larger
  space after an isometry: \(V\ket\psi = \sum_m\sqrt{E_m}\ket\psi\otimes\ket
  m\), \(V^\dagger V = I\), and \(\operatorname{tr}(P_mV\rho V^\dagger) =
  \operatorname{tr}(E_m\rho)\) with \(P_m = I\otimes\ket m\!\bra m\).
  Extending \(V\) to a unitary gives the circuit: ancilla in \(\ket0\), a
  unitary, a basis measurement of the ancilla. For rank-one effects
  \(\ket{w_m}\!\bra{w_m}\), \(V = \sum_m\ket m\!\bra{w_m}\) into
  \(\mathbb C^M\) suffices. Naimark 1940. /Slide: Naimark's dilation: every
  POVM is a projection on a larger space./

* Channels

- positive (positive map) :: A linear map \(\Phi\) on operators with \(X \ge
  0 \Rightarrow \Phi(X) \ge 0\). /Slide: The device as a map on states./
- trace-preserving :: \(\operatorname{tr}\Phi(X) = \operatorname{tr}X\) for
  all \(X\). With positivity: density operators go to density operators.
- completely positive :: \(\mathrm{id}_k\otimes\Phi\) is positive for every
  \(k\): the map still sends states to states when its input is half of an
  entangled pair. Abbreviated CP. /Slide: The qubit may have a partner./
- channel (CPTP map) :: A linear map that is completely positive and
  trace-preserving: the most general thing that can happen to a quantum system
  between preparation and measurement.
- transpose map :: \(T(X) = X^{\mathsf T}\) in a fixed basis. Positive and
  trace-preserving; on the Bloch ball the reflection \((x, y, z) \mapsto (x,
  -y, z)\). Not completely positive: \((\mathrm{id}\otimes
  T)(\ket{\Phi^+}\!\bra{\Phi^+}) = \frac12\mathrm{SWAP}\), eigenvalue
  \(-\frac12\) on \(\ket{\Psi^-}\). /Slide: The transpose is positive: a mirror
  of the Bloch ball./
- Choi matrix :: \(J(\Phi) = \sum_{ij}\ket i\!\bra j\otimes\Phi(\ket i\!\bra
  j) = d\,(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Phi)(\ket{\Phi^+_d}\!\bra{\Phi^+_d})\), with
  \(\Phi\) on the second factor and no \(1/d\) — this course's convention, in
  which the transpose has least eigenvalue \(-1\). It determines \(\Phi\).
  /Slide: The Choi matrix: a map as one operator./
- Choi's theorem :: \(\Phi\) is completely positive iff \(J(\Phi) \ge 0\), and
  trace-preserving iff \(\operatorname{tr}_2J(\Phi) = I\). The proof turns
  the eigenvectors of \(J\) into Kraus operators. Choi 1975. /Slide: Choi's
  theorem./

* Kraus and Stinespring

- Kraus representation :: \(\Phi\) is a channel iff \(\Phi(\rho) =
  \sum_kA_k\rho A_k^\dagger\) with \(\sum_kA_k^\dagger A_k = I\); at most
  \(\operatorname{rank}J(\Phi) \le dd'\) operators are needed. /Slide: The
  Kraus representation./
- Kraus operators :: The \(A_k\) of a Kraus representation. Not unique; only
  the channel is physical.
- unitary freedom :: Two Kraus families \(\{A_k\}\), \(\{B_l\}\) give the same
  channel iff \(A_k = \sum_lu_{kl}B_l\) with \(u^\dagger u = I\): unit 04's
  Schrödinger–HJW theorem applied to the Choi matrix. Example: \(\{I/\sqrt2,
  Z/\sqrt2\}\) and \(\{\ket0\!\bra0, \ket1\!\bra1\}\), related by \(H\).
  /Slide: Two Kraus families, one channel: HJW for the Choi matrix./
- Stinespring dilation :: Every channel is \(\Phi(\rho) =
  \operatorname{tr}_E(V\rho V^\dagger)\) for an isometry \(V\colon H \to
  H'\otimes E\), e.g. \(V\ket\psi = \sum_kA_k\ket\psi\otimes\ket k\); as a
  circuit, an environment in \(\ket0\), a unitary, and a discard. Stinespring
  1955. /Slide: Stinespring: every channel is a unitary with an environment./
- partial swap :: The unitary on qubit and environment that fixes \(\ket{00}\),
  \(\ket{11}\) and rotates \(\ket{10} \mapsto \sqrt{1 - \gamma}\ket{10} +
  \sqrt\gamma\ket{01}\): the excitation leaks to the environment with
  amplitude \(\sqrt\gamma\). /Slide: Amplitude damping: a qubit hands its
  energy to its surroundings./
- amplitude damping :: The channel with Kraus operators \(A_0 = \mathrm{diag}(1,
  \sqrt{1 - \gamma})\), \(A_1 = \sqrt\gamma\ket0\!\bra1\): decay \(\ket1 \to
  \ket0\) with probability \(\gamma\). Bloch action \((x, y, z) \mapsto
  (\sqrt{1 - \gamma}x, \sqrt{1 - \gamma}y, \gamma + (1 - \gamma)z)\). Choi
  eigenvalues \(2 - \gamma, \gamma, 0, 0\). Over time \(t\), \(\gamma = 1 -
  e^{-t/T_1}\). /Slide: Worked: amplitude damping from one environment qubit./

* T₁ and T₂

- bit flip :: \(\rho \mapsto (1 - p)\rho + pX\rho X\), Kraus \(\sqrt{1 - p}I,
  \sqrt pX\); squashes the Bloch ball toward the \(x\) axis: \((x, (1 -
  2p)y, (1 - 2p)z)\). /Slide: The standard noise models./
- phase flip :: \(\rho \mapsto (1 - p)\rho + pZ\rho Z\), Kraus \(\sqrt{1 -
  p}I, \sqrt pZ\); squashes toward the \(z\) axis: \(((1 - 2p)x, (1 - 2p)y,
  z)\). The same channel as pure dephasing with \(\sqrt{1 - \lambda} = 1 -
  2p\).
- depolarising (depolarising channel) :: \(\rho \mapsto (1 - p)\rho + p\,I/2\),
  Kraus \(\sqrt{1 - 3p/4}\,I\) and \(\frac{\sqrt p}2X, \frac{\sqrt p}2Y,
  \frac{\sqrt p}2Z\); shrinks the ball uniformly by \(1 - p\). Completely
  positive exactly for \(0 \le p \le \frac43\) (problem P9). Some texts use
  \(q = 3p/4\), the probability that some Pauli error occurred.
- pure dephasing :: Kraus \(\mathrm{diag}(1, \sqrt{1 - \lambda})\),
  \(\mathrm{diag}(0, \sqrt\lambda)\): populations kept, coherence multiplied
  by \(\sqrt{1 - \lambda}\). Physically, a random frequency drift averaged over.
- energy relaxation time (T₁) :: The time scale on which an excited qubit
  gives its energy to the environment: \(\Pr(1) = e^{-t/T_1}\) after
  preparing \(\ket1\). Superconducting qubits: tens to hundreds of
  microseconds. /Slide: Two clocks on a real qubit./
- coherence time (dephasing time, T₂) :: The time scale on which a
  superposition's off-diagonal element decays: \(|\rho_{01}(t)| =
  \frac12e^{-t/T_2}\) from \(\ket+\). Trapped ions: seconds. Within the model
  \(1/T_2 = 1/2T_1 + 1/T_\varphi\), so \(T_2 \le 2T_1\); complete positivity
  alone forces the same bound.
- pure dephasing time (T_φ) :: The decay time of the coherence from pure
  dephasing alone, \(\sqrt{1 - \lambda(t)} = e^{-t/T_\varphi}\). Exponential
  decay follows from memorylessness: the channels compose, \(f(s + t) =
  f(s)f(t)\). /Slide: Why the decay is exponential./

* Distinguishability

- trace norm :: \(\lVert A\rVert_1 = \operatorname{tr}\sqrt{A^\dagger A}\),
  the sum of the singular values; \(\sum|\lambda_i|\) for Hermitian \(A\).
  /Slide: Trace distance./
- trace distance :: \(D(\rho, \sigma) = \frac12\lVert\rho - \sigma\rVert_1
  \in [0, 1]\). Pure states: \(\sqrt{1 - |\braket{\psi|\varphi}|^2}\). Qubits:
  \(\frac12|r - s|\), half the distance between Bloch vectors.
- Helstrom bound :: With equal priors, the best probability of identifying
  \(\rho\) versus \(\sigma\) from one copy, over all POVMs, is \(\frac12(1 +
  D(\rho, \sigma))\), attained by projecting onto the positive part of \(\rho
  - \sigma\). Helstrom 1976. /Slide: What trace distance means: the best
  one-shot guess./
- contractivity :: \(D(\Phi(\rho), \Phi(\sigma)) \le D(\rho, \sigma)\) for
  every channel: a measurement after \(\Phi\) is a measurement before it. No
  processing makes two states easier to tell apart. /Slide: No channel makes
  two states easier to tell apart./
- adjoint map (Heisenberg picture) :: \(\Phi^\dagger(E) =
  \sum_kA_k^\dagger EA_k\), with \(\operatorname{tr}(E\Phi(\rho)) =
  \operatorname{tr}(\Phi^\dagger(E)\rho)\). Positive and unital, \(\Phi^\dagger(I)
  = I\), when \(\Phi\) is a channel.
- fidelity :: \(F(\rho, \sigma) = \lVert\sqrt\rho\sqrt\sigma\rVert_1 =
  \operatorname{tr}\sqrt{\sqrt\rho\sigma\sqrt\rho}\); \(|\braket{\psi|\varphi}|\)
  for pure states. Some authors use \(F^2\). Never decreases
  under a channel. /Slide: Fidelity, the other yardstick./
- Uhlmann's theorem :: \(F(\rho, \sigma) = \max|\braket{\Psi|\Phi}|\) over
  purifications of \(\rho\) and \(\sigma\). Uhlmann 1976; cited, proved in
  Nielsen and Chuang, Theorem 9.4.
- Fuchs–van de Graaf inequalities :: \(1 - F \le D \le \sqrt{1 - F^2}\), with
  the right side an equality for pure states. Fuchs and van de Graaf 1999;
  cited.
