Conventions, as in the deck: \(\braket{u|v}\) is conjugate-linear in the first slot. \(\ket0 = (1, 0)\), \(\ket1 = (0, 1)\), \(\ket\pm = (\ket0 \pm \ket1)/\sqrt2\); \(X, Y, Z\) are the Pauli matrices and \(H = (X + Z)/\sqrt2\). In \(\ket{ab}\) the first symbol is qubit 0 and is the most significant: the order is \(00, 01, 10, 11\). A qubit state is \(\rho = \tfrac12(I + xX + yY + zZ)\) with Bloch vector \((x, y, z)\). A POVM is \(\{E_m \ge 0\}\) with \(\sum_mE_m = I\); a channel is a completely positive trace-preserving map. The Choi matrix is \(J(\Phi) = \sum_{ij}\ket i\!\bra j\otimes\Phi(\ket i\!\bra j)\), with \(\Phi\) on the second factor and no \(1/d\). Depolarising is \(\rho \mapsto (1 - p)\rho + p\,I/2\); amplitude damping has Kraus operators \(A_0 = \mathrm{diag}(1, \sqrt{1 - \gamma})\), \(A_1 = \sqrt\gamma\ket0\!\bra1\). Trace distance is \(D = \tfrac12\lVert\rho - \sigma\rVert_1\); fidelity is \(F = \lVert\sqrt\rho\sqrt\sigma\rVert_1\), not its square.

Generalised measurement

P1 Never wrong, sometimes silent hard

exercises: POVMs, positivity from a determinant, a constrained optimisation, the Ivanovic–Dieks–Peres bound for one pair, a number

A fair coin prepares a qubit in \(\ket0\) or in \(\ket+\). You measure it with a three-outcome POVM \(\{E_0, E_+, E_?\}\) and announce "\(0\)", "\(+\)" or "don't know". The test must be never wrong: \(E_+\) never fires on \(\ket0\), and \(E_0\) never fires on \(\ket+\). (If you took unit 05, this is the problem it promised; nothing from it is used.)

  1. Show that "never wrong" forces \(E_+ = a\ket1\!\bra1\) and \(E_0 = b\ket-\!\bra-\) for some \(a, b \ge 0\).
  2. Show the success probability is \(\tfrac14(a + b)\), and that \(E_? = I - E_+ - E_0 \ge 0\) holds iff \(1 - a - b + \tfrac{ab}2 \ge 0\) together with \(a + \tfrac b2 \le 1\) and \(\tfrac b2 \le 1\).
  3. Prove that the success probability is at most \(1 - \tfrac1{\sqrt2}\), with equality at \(a = b = 2 - \sqrt2\) — the deck's test is optimal. Give the number.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Part 1 is about positive operators with a zero on a given vector; part 2 is about when a \(2\times2\) Hermitian matrix is positive; part 3 is an inequality in two real variables.

Rung 2

A positive operator \(A\) with \(\braket{v|A|v} = 0\) satisfies \(Av = 0\) (unit 04). On \(\mathbb C^2\), an operator killing \(\ket0\) and positive is a multiple of the projector onto \(\ket1\). For part 3, use \(ab \le \big(\tfrac{a + b}2\big)^2\).

Rung 3

With \(\ket-\!\bra- = \tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}\), the first line is \(E_? = \begin{pmatrix}1 - \tfrac b2 & \tfrac b2\\ \tfrac b2 & 1 - a - \tfrac b2\end{pmatrix}\). Its determinant is \(1 - a - b + \tfrac{ab}2\).

Worked solution

Idea. "Never wrong" pins each definite effect to a single direction, leaving one number each; positivity of the leftover effect is one determinant; the optimisation is then two-variable calculus, done by AM–GM.

1. \(\Pr(+ \mid \ket0) = \braket{0|E_+|0} = 0\) with \(E_+ \ge 0\) gives \(E_+\ket0 = 0\) (unit 04: if \(A \ge 0\) and \(\braket{v|Av} = 0\) then \(Av = 0\)). \(E_+\) is Hermitian, so its range is orthogonal to its kernel, which contains \(\ket0\); so its range is inside \(\mathbb C\ket1\) and \(E_+ = a\ket1\!\bra1\), with \(a = \braket{1|E_+|1} \ge 0\). The same argument with \(\ket+\) and \(\ket-\) gives \(E_0 = b\ket-\!\bra-\).

2. With equal priors, success is \(\tfrac12\braket{0|E_0|0} + \tfrac12\braket{+|E_+|+} = \tfrac12\cdot\tfrac b2 + \tfrac12\cdot\tfrac a2 = \tfrac14(a + b)\), since \(|\braket{-|0}|^2 = |\braket{1|+}|^2 = \tfrac12\). Then \(E_+ + E_0 = \begin{pmatrix}\tfrac b2 & -\tfrac b2\\ -\tfrac b2 & a + \tfrac b2\end{pmatrix}\), so \(E_?\) is as in rung 3. A \(2\times2\) Hermitian matrix is positive iff both diagonal entries and the determinant are \(\ge 0\) (the eigenvalues have product \(\det\) and sum \(\operatorname{tr}\)). Diagonal: \(1 - \tfrac b2 \ge 0\) and \(1 - a - \tfrac b2 \ge 0\). Determinant: \((1 - \tfrac b2)(1 - a - \tfrac b2) - \tfrac{b^2}4 = 1 - a - b + \tfrac{ab}2\).

3. Let \(S = a + b\). From the determinant and \(ab \le S^2/4\): \(0 \le 1 - S + \tfrac{ab}2 \le 1 - S + \tfrac{S^2}8\), i.e. \(S^2 - 8S + 8 \ge 0\), so \(S \le 4 - 2\sqrt2\) or \(S \ge 4 + 2\sqrt2\). The second is impossible: the diagonal conditions give \(a + \tfrac b2 \le 1\) and \(b \le 2\), so \(S \le 1 + \tfrac b2 \le 2\). Hence \(S \le 4 - 2\sqrt2\) and

\[P_{\text{succ}} = \tfrac S4 \le 1 - \tfrac{\sqrt2}2 = 1 - \tfrac1{\sqrt2} \approx 0.2929 .\]

Equality needs \(ab = S^2/4\), so \(a = b = 2 - \sqrt2\); then the determinant is \(1 - 2c + \tfrac{c^2}2 = 0\) with \(c = 2 - \sqrt2\) (as \(c^2 - 4c + 2 = 0\)), and the diagonal entries \(1 - \tfrac c2 = 0.7071\) and \(1 - \tfrac{3c}2 = 0.1213\) are positive. So the deck's test is feasible and optimal.

Check. The general Ivanovic–Dieks–Peres bound for two equiprobable pure states is \(1 - |\braket{\psi|\varphi}|\) (cited: Peres, Phys. Lett. A 128, 19 (1988)). Here \(|\braket{0|+}| = \tfrac1{\sqrt2}\), giving \(1 - \tfrac1{\sqrt2}\) ✓. Limits: orthogonal states give \(1\), identical states \(0\), as they must. And the best basis (the deck's A question with three answers) gave \(\tfrac14 = 0.25 \lt 0.2929\) ✓.

P2 The unambiguous test on a qutrit medium

exercises: Naimark's dilation for rank-one effects, an isometry as a matrix, completing an isometry to a unitary

The deck's test has rank-one effects \(E_m = \ket{w_m}\!\bra{w_m}\) with \(c = 2 - \sqrt2\) and

\[w_+ = \sqrt c\,\ket1, \qquad w_0 = \sqrt c\,\ket-, \qquad w_? = \sqrt{2\sqrt2 - 2}\,\big(\cos\tfrac\pi8\ket0 + \sin\tfrac\pi8\ket1\big).\]
  1. Write \(V = \sum_m\ket{e_m}\!\bra{w_m}\colon\mathbb C^2 \to \mathbb C^3\) as a \(3\times2\) matrix (rows \(m = +, 0, ?\); \(\{e_m\}\) the qutrit's basis), with closed-form entries, and check \(V^\dagger V = I\).
  2. Find a third column making \(V\) a \(3\times3\) unitary \(U\).
  3. Embed the qubit in the qutrit as the span of its first two basis vectors, apply \(U\), and measure in the basis \(\{e_m\}\). Compute the outcome distribution for inputs \(\ket0\), \(\ket+\) and \(\ket1\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Row \(m\) of \(V\) is the row vector \(\bra{w_m}\): the conjugate transpose of \(w_m\), here just its transpose. Orthonormal columns is the same statement as \(V^\dagger V = I\).

Rung 2

\(\sqrt{2\sqrt2 - 2}\cos\tfrac\pi8\) and \(\sqrt{2\sqrt2 - 2}\sin\tfrac\pi8\) simplify: use \(\cos^2\tfrac\pi8 = \tfrac12(1 + \tfrac1{\sqrt2})\). For the third column, real columns in \(\mathbb R^3\) have an obvious orthogonal partner.

Rung 3

The squared entries of the bottom row are \((2\sqrt2 - 2)\cdot\tfrac12(1 \pm \tfrac1{\sqrt2})\), which are \(\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\) and \(\tfrac{(\sqrt2 - 1)^2}{\sqrt2}\). The third column is the cross product of the first two.

Worked solution

Idea. A POVM with rank-one effects is literally an orthonormal basis of a bigger space seen from a plane inside it; writing the plane's embedding as a matrix and completing it is Gram–Schmidt.

1. \(\sqrt c = \sqrt{2 - \sqrt2} \approx 0.7654\), \(\sqrt{c/2} = \sqrt{1 - \tfrac1{\sqrt2}} \approx 0.5412\). From rung 3 the bottom row is \(2^{-1/4} \approx 0.8409\) and \((\sqrt2 - 1)2^{-1/4} \approx 0.3483\). So

\[V = \begin{pmatrix}0 & \sqrt c\\ \sqrt{c/2} & -\sqrt{c/2}\\ 2^{-1/4} & (\sqrt2 - 1)2^{-1/4}\end{pmatrix} \approx \begin{pmatrix}0 & 0.7654\\ 0.5412 & -0.5412\\ 0.8409 & 0.3483\end{pmatrix}.\]

Column norms: \(0 + \tfrac c2 + \tfrac1{\sqrt2} = 1 - \tfrac1{\sqrt2} + \tfrac1{\sqrt2} = 1\) and \(c + \tfrac c2 + \tfrac{(\sqrt2 - 1)^2}{\sqrt2} = \tfrac32(2 - \sqrt2) + \tfrac{3 - 2\sqrt2}{\sqrt2} = 3 - \tfrac{3\sqrt2}2 + \tfrac{3\sqrt2}2 - 2 = 1\). Inner product: \(0 - \tfrac c2 + 2^{-1/2}(\sqrt2 - 1) = -(1 - \tfrac1{\sqrt2}) + 1 - \tfrac1{\sqrt2} = 0\). So \(V^\dagger V = I\) ✓ — equivalently \(\sum_mE_m = I\).

2. The cross product of the columns, normalised, is

\[u_3 = \big(\sqrt{\sqrt2 - 1},\ \sqrt{\sqrt2 - 1},\ 1 - \sqrt2\big) \approx (0.6436, 0.6436, -0.4142),\]

with \(\lVert u_3\rVert^2 = 2(\sqrt2 - 1) + (3 - 2\sqrt2) = 1\). Orthogonality, exactly: with \(\sqrt c = 2^{1/4}\sqrt{\sqrt2 - 1}\) and \(\sqrt{c/2} = 2^{-1/4}\sqrt{\sqrt2 - 1}\), column 1 gives \(\sqrt{\sqrt2 - 1}\cdot2^{-1/4}\sqrt{\sqrt2 - 1} + (1 - \sqrt2)2^{-1/4} = 0\), and column 2 gives \(\sqrt{\sqrt2 - 1}\big(\sqrt c - \sqrt{c/2}\big) + (1 - \sqrt2)(\sqrt2 - 1)2^{-1/4} = (\sqrt2 - 1)^2\,2^{-1/4} - (\sqrt2 - 1)^2\,2^{-1/4} = 0\) ✓ (numerically \(0.3483 - 0.3483\) and \(0.1443 - 0.1443\)). \(U = (V \mid u_3)\) is real orthogonal, hence unitary. (Its negative also works: the third column is fixed only up to a phase.)

3. The input \(\alpha\ket0 + \beta\ket1\) becomes the qutrit vector \((\alpha, \beta, 0)\), and \(U\) sends it to \(\alpha\,\mathrm{col}_1 + \beta\,\mathrm{col}_2 = V(\alpha, \beta)\): the third column never acts on an embedded qubit. Outcome probabilities are the squared entries:

  • \(\ket0\): \(V\ket0 = (0, 0.5412, 0.8409)\), probabilities \((0, 0.2929, 0.7071)\) for \((+, 0, ?)\);
  • \(\ket+\): \(V\ket+ = \tfrac1{\sqrt2}(0.7654, 0, 1.1892)\), probabilities \((0.2929, 0, 0.7071)\);
  • \(\ket1\): \(V\ket1 = (0.7654, -0.5412, 0.3483)\), probabilities \((0.5858, 0.2929, 0.1213)\).

Check. Each row sums to one. On \(\ket0\) outcome \(+\) is impossible and on \(\ket+\) outcome \(0\) is impossible — never wrong ✓ — and each succeeds with \(0.2929\), the deck's number. On \(\ket1\), neither of the two promised states, the test answers "\(0\)" with probability \(0.29\): unambiguity is a promise about the two inputs, not about all inputs.

P3 Naimark for the trine medium

exercises: a POVM from unit 04's trine, Naimark's dilation, completing an isometry, outcome distributions

Unit 04's trine is \(\ket{t_k} = \cos\tfrac{\pi k}3\ket0 + \sin\tfrac{\pi k}3\ket1\), \(k = 0, 1, 2\). Let \(E_k = \tfrac23\ket{t_k}\!\bra{t_k}\).

  1. Show \(\{E_0, E_1, E_2\}\) is a POVM.
  2. Write its rank-one Naimark isometry \(V\colon\mathbb C^2 \to \mathbb C^3\), check \(V^\dagger V = I\), and complete it to a \(3\times3\) unitary.
  3. Find the outcome distributions on \(\ket0\) and on \(\ket1\). Which outcome never occurs on \(\ket1\), and why, geometrically?
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Part 1 is unit 04's computation of the trine's density operator, scaled. Part 2 is P2's recipe with \(w_k = \sqrt{2/3}\,t_k\).

Rung 2

Unit 04 showed \(\tfrac13\sum_k\ket{t_k}\!\bra{t_k} = I/2\). The rows of \(V\) are \(\sqrt{2/3}(\cos\tfrac{\pi k}3, \sin\tfrac{\pi k}3)\).

Rung 3

The rows are \(\sqrt{2/3}\,(1, 0)\), \(\sqrt{2/3}\,(\tfrac12, \tfrac{\sqrt3}2)\), \(\sqrt{2/3}\,(-\tfrac12, \tfrac{\sqrt3}2)\). A third column orthogonal to both columns has \(a + \tfrac b2 - \tfrac c2 = 0\) and \(b + c = 0\).

Worked solution

Idea. The trine is three vectors \(120^\circ\) apart on the Bloch sphere; scaled by \(\tfrac23\) its projectors sum to \(I\), and the dilation is a \(3\times3\) rotation whose first two columns are the scaled trine.

1. Each \(E_k \ge 0\) as a positive multiple of a projector. Sum: \(\tfrac23\sum_k\ket{t_k}\!\bra{t_k} = \tfrac23\cdot\tfrac32I = I\), by unit 04 (\(\tfrac13\sum = I/2\)). Directly: the diagonals of \(\sum_k\ket{t_k}\!\bra{t_k}\) are \(1 + \tfrac14 + \tfrac14 = \tfrac32\) and \(0 + \tfrac34 + \tfrac34 = \tfrac32\), the off-diagonals \(0 + \tfrac{\sqrt3}4 - \tfrac{\sqrt3}4 = 0\) ✓.

2. With \(w_k = \sqrt{2/3}\,t_k\),

\[V = \sqrt{\tfrac23}\begin{pmatrix}1 & 0\\ \tfrac12 & \tfrac{\sqrt3}2\\ -\tfrac12 & \tfrac{\sqrt3}2\end{pmatrix}, \qquad U = \begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{2/3} & 0 & 1/\sqrt3\\ 1/\sqrt6 & 1/\sqrt2 & -1/\sqrt3\\ -1/\sqrt6 & 1/\sqrt2 & 1/\sqrt3\end{pmatrix}.\]

Column norms of \(V\): \(\tfrac23(1 + \tfrac14 + \tfrac14) = 1\), \(\tfrac23(\tfrac34 + \tfrac34) = 1\); inner product \(\tfrac23(0 + \tfrac{\sqrt3}4 - \tfrac{\sqrt3}4) = 0\): \(V^\dagger V = I\) ✓. Rung 3's equations give \(c = -b\), \(a = -b\): the third column \(\tfrac1{\sqrt3}(1, -1, 1)\), unit length, orthogonal to both (\(\sqrt{2/3}(1 - \tfrac12 - \tfrac12) = 0\), \(\sqrt{2/3}(0 - \tfrac{\sqrt3}2 + \tfrac{\sqrt3}2) = 0\)) ✓.

3. \(\Pr(k) = \tfrac23|\braket{t_k|\psi}|^2\). For \(\ket0\): \(\tfrac23(1, \tfrac14, \tfrac14) = (\tfrac23, \tfrac16, \tfrac16)\). For \(\ket1\): \(\tfrac23(0, \tfrac34, \tfrac34) = (0, \tfrac12, \tfrac12)\). Outcome \(0\) never occurs on \(\ket1\) because \(\ket{t_0} = \ket0 \perp \ket1\) — on the Bloch sphere, \(\ket1\) is antipodal to \(t_0\)'s point. In general a trine outcome \(k\) is impossible exactly on the state antipodal to \(t_k\): the trine POVM rules out one of three states with certainty.

Check. Both distributions sum to one. On \(I/2\) every outcome has \(\tfrac23\cdot\tfrac12 = \tfrac13\), as symmetry demands.

P4 Measurements as channels routine

exercises: Kraus operators of measurement processes, trace preservation, a Choi matrix, a Bloch-ball action

  1. Unit 04's non-selective measurement \(\rho \mapsto \sum_\lambda P_\lambda\rho P_\lambda\): give Kraus operators and check trace preservation. Show that doing it twice is the same as doing it once.
  2. Measure and record: the outcome is written into a classical register \(\mathbb C^L\), \(\rho \mapsto \sum_\lambda P_\lambda\rho P_\lambda\otimes\ket\lambda\!\bra\lambda\). Give Kraus operators \(H \to H\otimes\mathbb C^L\) and check them.
  3. For the qubit \(z\) measurement, compute the Choi matrix of the non-selective map and check it is positive.
  4. The trine POVM of P3, measured and forgotten with Kraus operators \(\sqrt{E_k}\): find its action on Bloch vectors.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Each map is already written as a sum of terms \(A\rho A^\dagger\); read the \(A\)'s off. The Bloch action is found by feeding in \(\tfrac12(I + X)\), \(\tfrac12(I + Y)\), \(\tfrac12(I + Z)\) and using linearity.

Rung 2

\(P_\lambda\rho P_\lambda\otimes\ket\lambda\!\bra\lambda = (P_\lambda\otimes\ket\lambda)\rho(P_\lambda\otimes\bra\lambda)\). For a projector, \(\sqrt{\tfrac23\ket t\!\bra t} = \sqrt{\tfrac23}\ket t\!\bra t\).

Rung 3

For part 4 the trine channel is \(\rho \mapsto \tfrac23\sum_k\braket{t_k|\rho|t_k}\ket{t_k}\!\bra{t_k}\). Start with \(\rho = \tfrac12(I + Y)\): every \(\braket{t_k|Y|t_k}\) is zero because \(t_k\) is real.

Worked solution

Idea. Every measurement whose result is not kept, or is kept classically, is a channel; the Kraus operators are the measurement operators themselves.

1. \(A_\lambda = P_\lambda\); \(\sum_\lambda P_\lambda^\dagger P_\lambda = \sum_\lambda P_\lambda = I\) ✓. Twice: \(\sum_\mu P_\mu\big(\sum_\lambda P_\lambda\rho P_\lambda\big)P_\mu = \sum_\lambda P_\lambda\rho P_\lambda\), since \(P_\mu P_\lambda = \delta_{\mu\lambda}P_\lambda\). Idempotent, as a measurement repeated should be.

2. \(B_\lambda = P_\lambda\otimes\ket\lambda\colon v \mapsto P_\lambda v\otimes\ket\lambda\), adjoint \(P_\lambda\otimes\bra\lambda\). Then \(\sum_\lambda B_\lambda^\dagger B_\lambda = \sum_\lambda P_\lambda\braket{\lambda|\lambda} = I\) ✓, and \(B_\lambda\rho B_\lambda^\dagger = P_\lambda\rho P_\lambda\otimes\ket\lambda\!\bra\lambda\) ✓. Tracing out the register recovers part 1; tracing out the system gives the classical distribution \(\sum_\lambda\operatorname{tr}(P_\lambda\rho)\ket\lambda\!\bra\lambda\).

3. \(\Phi(\ket i\!\bra j) = \delta_{ij}\ket i\!\bra i\), so \(J = \ket0\!\bra0\otimes\ket0\!\bra0 + \ket1\!\bra1\otimes\ket1\!\bra1 = \ket{00}\!\bra{00} + \ket{11}\!\bra{11}\): a projector, eigenvalues \(1, 1, 0, 0\), positive ✓; \(\operatorname{tr}_2J = \ket0\!\bra0 + \ket1\!\bra1 = I\) ✓. Compare the identity channel's \(J = \ket{00}\!\bra{00} + \ket{00}\!\bra{11} + \ket{11}\!\bra{00} + \ket{11}\!\bra{11}\): the measurement removed exactly the coherences between the blocks.

4. The channel is \(\Phi(\rho) = \tfrac23\sum_k\ket{t_k}\!\braket{t_k|\rho|t_k}\!\bra{t_k}\). The \(t_k\) are real, so \(\braket{t_k|Y|t_k} = 0\) and \(\Phi(\tfrac12(I + Y)) = \Phi(\tfrac I2) = \tfrac I2\): \(y\) is sent to \(0\) and so is everything, \((0, 1, 0) \mapsto (0, 0, 0)\). For \(\tfrac12(I + Z)\): \(\braket{t_k|0}^2 = 1, \tfrac14, \tfrac14\), so \(\Phi = \tfrac23(\ket{t_0}\!\bra{t_0} + \tfrac14\ket{t_1}\!\bra{t_1} + \tfrac14\ket{t_2}\!\bra{t_2})\), whose Bloch vector is \(\tfrac23\big((0,0,1) + \tfrac14(\tfrac{\sqrt3}2, 0, -\tfrac12) + \tfrac14(-\tfrac{\sqrt3}2, 0, -\tfrac12)\big) = \tfrac23(0, 0, \tfrac34) = (0, 0, \tfrac12)\). By the symmetry of the trine under \(120^\circ\) turns about \(y\), every direction in the \(xz\) plane is halved:

\[(x, y, z) \mapsto \big(\tfrac x2,\ 0,\ \tfrac z2\big).\]

Check. The centre is fixed (\(\Phi(I/2) = \tfrac23\cdot\tfrac12\sum_k\ket{t_k}\!\bra{t_k} = I/2\) ✓). The pure state \(\ket0\) goes to Bloch length \(\tfrac12\), a mixed state — measuring and forgetting loses information, as it should. Trace preservation: \(\sum_k\sqrt{E_k}^2 = \sum_kE_k = I\) ✓.

P5 Positive but not completely positive medium

exercises: the transpose in any dimension, the swap's spectrum, a CP map that is not trace-preserving, completing it to a channel, a number

  1. Show the transpose on \(L(\mathbb C^d)\) is positive and trace-preserving, and that \((\mathrm{id}\otimes T)(\ket{\Phi^+_d}\!\bra{\Phi^+_d}) = \tfrac1d\mathrm{SWAP}_d\) with \(\ket{\Phi^+_d} = d^{-1/2}\sum_i\ket{ii}\). Find its least eigenvalue and its multiplicity; give the value for \(d = 3\).
  2. Show \(\Phi(\rho) = P\rho P\) with \(P = \ket0\!\bra0\) on a qubit is completely positive but not trace-preserving.
  3. Find two different ways to add Kraus operators that make it a channel, and describe each in words.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

The answers to 1 are an operator identity and an eigenvalue. For 2 and 3: a map with Kraus form is CP; it is TP iff \(\sum A_k^\dagger A_k = I\).

Rung 2

\(\mathrm{SWAP}_d\) squares to \(I\), so its eigenvalues are \(\pm1\). Its \(-1\) eigenspace is spanned by the antisymmetric vectors \(\ket{ij} - \ket{ji}\), \(i \lt j\). In part 3 you need extra operators with \(\sum_kA_k^\dagger A_k = I - P\).

Rung 3

The first line of part 1: \(\ket{\Phi^+_d}\!\bra{\Phi^+_d} = \tfrac1d\sum_{ij}\ket i\!\bra j\otimes\ket i\!\bra j\). For part 3, try \(A_1 = \ket1\!\bra1\) and, separately, \(A_1 = \ket0\!\bra1\).

Worked solution

Idea. The transpose turns the maximally entangled state into the swap, whose antisymmetric eigenvectors carry a negative eigenvalue. Conversely, being CP is easy and being TP is a single operator equation, which can be completed in many ways.

1. Positivity: for \(\rho \ge 0\), \(\rho^{\mathsf T} = \bar\rho\) and \(\braket{v|\bar\rho|v} = \overline{\braket{\bar v|\rho|\bar v}} \ge 0\), as in the deck, in any dimension; the diagonal and so the trace are unchanged. Then \((\mathrm{id}\otimes T)\) of \(\tfrac1d\sum_{ij}\ket i\!\bra j\otimes\ket i\!\bra j\) is \(\tfrac1d\sum_{ij}\ket i\!\bra j\otimes\ket j\!\bra i\), which sends \(\ket{ab} \mapsto \tfrac1d\ket{ba}\): \(\tfrac1d\mathrm{SWAP}_d\). \(\mathrm{SWAP}_d^2 = I\), and it fixes the \(\tfrac{d(d+1)}2\) symmetric vectors (\(\ket{ii}\), \(\ket{ij} + \ket{ji}\)) and negates the \(\tfrac{d(d-1)}2\) antisymmetric ones; together they are an orthogonal basis of \(\mathbb C^{d^2}\). So the least eigenvalue is \(-\tfrac1d\), with multiplicity \(\tfrac{d(d-1)}2\). For \(d = 3\): \(-\tfrac13 \approx -0.3333\), three times. (In the Choi convention, \(J(T) = \mathrm{SWAP}_d\), least eigenvalue \(-1\) for every \(d\).)

2. One Kraus operator, \(P\), so CP by the deck's Kraus theorem (its "if" direction needs only the form). Trace: \(\operatorname{tr}(P\ket1\!\bra1P) = 0 \ne 1\). In operator form \(P^\dagger P = P \ne I\).

P6 Choi's theorem hard

exercises: the Choi matrix, unfolding vectors into operators, spectral decomposition, a full proof in both directions

Let \(\Phi\colon L(\mathbb C^d) \to L(\mathbb C^{d'})\) be linear, with \(J = \sum_{ij}\ket i\!\bra j\otimes\Phi(\ket i\!\bra j)\).

  1. Prove: \(\Phi\) is completely positive \(\iff\) \(J \ge 0\).
  2. Prove: \(\Phi\) is trace-preserving \(\iff\) \(\operatorname{tr}_2J = I_d\).
  3. Deduce that if \(\mathrm{id}_d\otimes\Phi\) is positive, then \(\mathrm{id}_k\otimes\Phi\) is positive for every \(k\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

One direction of part 1 is a single application of the definition to one particular state. The other direction must construct something that is obviously CP.

Rung 2

\(J = d(\mathrm{id}\otimes\Phi)(\ket{\Phi^+_d}\!\bra{\Phi^+_d})\). For the converse, decompose \(J = \sum_k\ket{a_k}\!\bra{a_k}\) and turn each vector \(a_k \in \mathbb C^d\otimes\mathbb C^{d'}\) into a map \(A_k\colon\mathbb C^d \to \mathbb C^{d'}\).

Rung 3

Write \(\ket{a_k} = \sum_i\ket i\otimes v_{ik}\) and set \(A_k\ket i = v_{ik}\). The first line of the converse is then \(\ket{a_k}\!\bra{a_k} = \sum_{ij}\ket i\!\bra j\otimes A_k\ket i\!\bra jA_k^\dagger\).

Worked solution

Idea. The Choi matrix is the channel applied to half of a maximally entangled state, so CP makes it positive. Conversely, a positive Choi matrix has a spectral decomposition, and each eigenvector, read as a matrix, is a Kraus operator; a Kraus form is CP on its face.

1, \(\Rightarrow\). \(\ket{\Phi^+_d}\!\bra{\Phi^+_d} = \tfrac1d\sum_{ij}\ket{ii}\!\bra{jj} = \tfrac1d\sum_{ij}\ket i\!\bra j\otimes\ket i\!\bra j\) is a positive operator on \(\mathbb C^d\otimes\mathbb C^d\). If \(\Phi\) is CP, then \(\mathrm{id}_d\otimes\Phi\) is positive, so \((\mathrm{id}\otimes\Phi)(\ket{\Phi^+_d}\!\bra{\Phi^+_d}) = \tfrac1dJ \ge 0\), hence \(J \ge 0\).

1, \(\Leftarrow\). (i) \(J\) is Hermitian with eigenvalues \(\lambda_k \ge 0\) and orthonormal eigenvectors \(e_k\) (spectral theorem; \(J \ge 0\)). Put \(a_k = \sqrt{\lambda_k}e_k\): \(J = \sum_k\ket{a_k}\!\bra{a_k}\). (ii) Every vector of \(\mathbb C^d\otimes\mathbb C^{d'}\) is uniquely \(\sum_i\ket i\otimes v_i\) (unit 03), so define \(A_k\colon\mathbb C^d \to \mathbb C^{d'}\) by \(A_k\ket i = v_{ik}\), where \(\ket{a_k} = \sum_i\ket i\otimes v_{ik}\). (iii) Then

\[\ket{a_k}\!\bra{a_k} = \sum_{ij}\big(\ket i\otimes A_k\ket i\big)\big(\bra j\otimes\bra jA_k^\dagger\big) = \sum_{ij}\ket i\!\bra j\otimes A_k\ket i\!\bra jA_k^\dagger .\]

Summing over \(k\) and comparing the \((i, j)\) blocks with the definition of \(J\): \(\Phi(\ket i\!\bra j) = \sum_kA_k\ket i\!\bra jA_k^\dagger\). Both sides are linear in the matrix unit, and the matrix units span \(L(\mathbb C^d)\), so \(\Phi(X) = \sum_kA_kXA_k^\dagger\) for all \(X\). (iv) For any \(k'\) and any \(Y \ge 0\) on \(\mathbb C^{k'}\otimes\mathbb C^d\): on products, \((\mathrm{id}\otimes\Phi)(S\otimes R) = S\otimes\sum_kA_kRA_k^\dagger = \sum_k(I\otimes A_k)(S\otimes R)(I\otimes A_k)^\dagger\), so by linearity \((\mathrm{id}\otimes\Phi)(Y) = \sum_k(I\otimes A_k)Y(I\otimes A_k)^\dagger\). Each term is \(BYB^\dagger\) with \(\braket{v|BYB^\dagger|v} = \braket{B^\dagger v|Y|B^\dagger v} \ge 0\). So \(\Phi\) is CP.

2. \(\operatorname{tr}_2(\ket i\!\bra j\otimes B) = (\operatorname{tr}B)\ket i\!\bra j\) (unit 04), so \(\operatorname{tr}_2J = \sum_{ij}\operatorname{tr}\Phi(\ket i\!\bra j)\,\ket i\!\bra j\). This equals \(I = \sum_{ij}\delta_{ij}\ket i\!\bra j\) iff \(\operatorname{tr}\Phi(\ket i\!\bra j) = \delta_{ij} = \operatorname{tr}\ket i\!\bra j\) for all \(i, j\), which — both sides being linear and the \(\ket i\!\bra j\) a basis — is iff \(\operatorname{tr}\Phi(X) = \operatorname{tr}X\) for all \(X\).

3. The proof of \(\Rightarrow\) used positivity of \(\mathrm{id}_d\otimes\Phi\) only. So \(\mathrm{id}_d\otimes\Phi\) positive \(\Rightarrow\) \(J \ge 0\) \(\Rightarrow\) (by \(\Leftarrow\)) \(\Phi\) is CP, i.e. \(\mathrm{id}_k\otimes\Phi\) is positive for every \(k\).

Check. On the identity channel, \(J = d\ket{\Phi^+_d}\!\bra{\Phi^+_d}\) has one nonzero eigenvalue \(d\), eigenvector \(\sqrt d\,\Phi^+_d\), i.e. \(a = \sum_i\ket i\otimes\ket i\); unfolding gives \(A\ket i = \ket i\), \(A = I\): the single Kraus operator of the identity ✓. On the transpose, \(J = \mathrm{SWAP} \not\ge 0\), so the proof correctly fails to produce Kraus operators ✓. On amplitude damping, the deck's worked example ran steps (i)–(iii) backwards.

P7 Two Kraus families for the phase flip medium

exercises: unitary freedom of Kraus operators, phase flip versus pure dephasing, a Choi matrix, a number

The phase flip with probability \(p\) has Kraus operators \(A_0 = \sqrt{1 - p}\,I\), \(A_1 = \sqrt p\,Z\).

  1. Find \(\lambda\) such that pure dephasing, \(B_0 = \mathrm{diag}(1, \sqrt{1 - \lambda})\), \(B_1 = \mathrm{diag}(0, \sqrt\lambda)\), is the same channel, for \(0 \le p \le \tfrac12\).
  2. Find the \(2\times2\) matrix \(u\) with \(A_k = \sum_lu_{kl}B_l\), and check it is unitary.
  3. Give \(\lambda\) and \(u\) numerically for \(p = 0.1\), and compute the Choi matrix of the channel.
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Two qubit channels that fix the diagonal agree iff they multiply the off-diagonal entry by the same number. \(u\) is found by matching entries.

Rung 2

The phase flip multiplies \(\rho_{01}\) by \(1 - 2p\); pure dephasing by \(\sqrt{1 - \lambda}\). Everything is diagonal, so \(A_k = u_{k0}B_0 + u_{k1}B_1\) is two scalar equations per \(k\), one per diagonal entry.

Rung 3

\(\lambda = 1 - (1 - 2p)^2 = 4p(1 - p)\), so \(\sqrt\lambda = 2\sqrt{p(1 - p)}\). The \((0, 0)\) entries give \(u_{00} = \sqrt{1 - p}\) and \(u_{10} = \sqrt p\) at once.

Worked solution

Idea. Same channel means same action; then unit 04's HJW, through the Choi matrix, promises a unitary between the families, and here it can be read off entry by entry.

1. Phase flip: \((1 - p)\rho + pZ\rho Z\) keeps the diagonal and sends \(\rho_{01} \mapsto (1 - p)\rho_{01} - p\rho_{01} = (1 - 2p)\rho_{01}\). Pure dephasing keeps the diagonal and multiplies \(\rho_{01}\) by \(\sqrt{1 - \lambda}\). For \(p \le \tfrac12\), \(1 - 2p \ge 0\), and equality needs \(\sqrt{1 - \lambda} = 1 - 2p\):

\[\lambda = 1 - (1 - 2p)^2 = 4p(1 - p), \qquad \sqrt\lambda = 2\sqrt{p(1 - p)} .\]

A linear map on \(2\times2\) matrices is fixed by its action on the four matrix units, and these two agree on all four.

2. \(A_0 = u_{00}B_0 + u_{01}B_1\): entry \((0,0)\): \(\sqrt{1 - p} = u_{00}\); entry \((1,1)\): \(\sqrt{1 - p} = u_{00}(1 - 2p) + u_{01}\cdot2\sqrt{p(1 - p)}\), so \(u_{01} = \sqrt{1 - p}\cdot2p/\big(2\sqrt{p(1 - p)}\big) = \sqrt p\). \(A_1\): \((0,0)\): \(\sqrt p = u_{10}\); \((1,1)\): \(-\sqrt p = \sqrt p(1 - 2p) + u_{11}\cdot2\sqrt{p(1 - p)}\), so \(u_{11} = -\sqrt p(2 - 2p)/\big(2\sqrt{p(1 - p)}\big) = -\sqrt{1 - p}\). Hence

\[u = \begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{1 - p} & \sqrt p\\ \sqrt p & -\sqrt{1 - p}\end{pmatrix}, \qquad u^\dagger u = \begin{pmatrix}1 - p + p & \sqrt{p(1-p)} - \sqrt{p(1-p)}\\ \cdot & p + 1 - p\end{pmatrix} = I\ \checkmark\]

— a real reflection.

3. \(p = 0.1\): \(\lambda = 0.36\), \(\sqrt{1 - \lambda} = 0.8 = 1 - 2p\) ✓; \(u = \begin{pmatrix}0.9487 & 0.3162\\ 0.3162 & -0.9487\end{pmatrix}\). The Choi matrix: \(\Phi(\ket0\!\bra0) = \ket0\!\bra0\), \(\Phi(\ket1\!\bra1) = \ket1\!\bra1\), \(\Phi(\ket0\!\bra1) = 0.8\ket0\!\bra1\), so \(J = \ket{00}\!\bra{00} + \ket{11}\!\bra{11} + 0.8(\ket{00}\!\bra{11} + \ket{11}\!\bra{00})\), eigenvalues \(1.8, 0.2, 0, 0\) — rank two, as two Kraus operators require.

Check. \(p = 0\): \(\lambda = 0\), \(u = \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}\), and \(A_1 = 0 = -B_1\) ✓. \(p = \tfrac12\): \(\lambda = 1\), \(B = \{\ket0\!\bra0, \ket1\!\bra1\}\), and \(u = \tfrac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix} = H\), the deck's example ✓.

Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Compute \(K\) on the four basis states. \(K\) acts as \(X\) on the plane spanned by \(\ket{01}, \ket{10}\) and as \(0\) on \(\ket{00}, \ket{11}\); exponentiate it on each piece.

Rung 2

\(X\otimes X\) flips both bits. For \(Y\otimes Y\) use \(Y\ket0 = i\ket1\) and \(Y\ket1 = -i\ket0\) on each factor. On the plane, \(e^{-i\theta X} = \cos\theta\,I - i\sin\theta\,X\) (unit 00's functional calculus, as \(X^2 = I\)).

Worked solution

Idea. \(K\) is the excitation-exchange coupling; running it for "angle" \(\theta\) is a complex partial swap, and the phase it introduces sits on a Kraus operator, where unitary freedom makes it invisible.

1. \(X\otimes X\) maps \(\ket{00} \leftrightarrow \ket{11}\), \(\ket{01} \leftrightarrow \ket{10}\). \(Y\otimes Y\): \(\ket{00} \mapsto (i)(i)\ket{11} = -\ket{11}\), \(\ket{11} \mapsto (-i)(-i)\ket{00} = -\ket{00}\), \(\ket{01} \mapsto (i)(-i)\ket{10} = \ket{10}\), \(\ket{10} \mapsto \ket{01}\). 2. Environment \(0\) part: \(A_0\ket0 = \ket0\), \(A_0\ket1 = \cos\theta\ket1\). Environment \(1\) part: \(A_1\ket0 = 0\), \(A_1\ket1 = -i\sin\theta\ket0\). So

\[A_0 = \begin{pmatrix}1 & 0\\ 0 & \cos\theta\end{pmatrix}, \qquad A_1 = \begin{pmatrix}0 & -i\sin\theta\\ 0 & 0\end{pmatrix}, \qquad A_0^\dagger A_0 + A_1^\dagger A_1 = \mathrm{diag}(1, \cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta) = I\ \checkmark\]

With \(\gamma = \sin^2\theta\) (\(0 \le \theta \le \tfrac\pi2\)), \(A_0\) is the deck's \(\mathrm{diag}(1, \sqrt{1 - \gamma})\), and \(A_1 = -i\cdot\sqrt\gamma\ket0\!\bra1\). 3. Each step is amplitude damping with \(\gamma_1 = \sin^2\theta\), so by the deck's composition rule the total is amplitude damping with \(1 - \gamma_{\text{total}} = (1 - \gamma_1)^2 = \cos^4\theta\). For \(\theta = \pi/6\): \(\gamma_1 = \tfrac14\), \(\gamma_{\text{total}} = 1 - \tfrac9{16} = \tfrac7{16} = 0.4375\). Check. \(\theta = 0\): no interaction, \(\gamma = 0\) ✓. \(\theta = \tfrac\pi2\): full swap up to phase, \(\gamma = 1\), reset to \(\ket0\) ✓. Many steps: \(\cos^{2n}\theta \to 0\) — a fresh environment qubit per step is the deck's picture of exponential decay.

Rung 1

Rung 2

\[2 - \tfrac{3p}2 \ \text{(once, on } \ket{\Phi^+}\text{)}, \qquad \tfrac p2 \ \text{(three times)}.\]

TP for every \(p\) (\(\operatorname{tr}_2J = (1 - p)I + pI = I\)). Idea. Coherence is an amplitude, so it decays at half the rate of the population; any extra dephasing only adds a rate. Complete positivity enforces the same inequality on any channel of this shape, model or no model.

1. Amplitude damping maps \(\rho_{01} \mapsto A_0\rho A_0^\dagger\) entry \((0,1)\), which is \(1\cdot\rho_{01}\cdot\sqrt{1 - \gamma}\) (\(A_1\rho A_1^\dagger \propto \ket0\!\bra0\) has no off-diagonal), and \(\sqrt{1 - \gamma} = e^{-t/2T_1}\). Pure dephasing multiplies by \(e^{-t/T_\varphi}\). Both are scalar factors on \(\rho_{01}\), so in either order \(\rho_{01}(t) = \rho_{01}(0)e^{-t/2T_1}e^{-t/T_\varphi}\), and \(1/T_2 = 1/2T_1 + 1/T_\varphi \ge 1/2T_1\) as \(T_\varphi \gt 0\). So \(T_2 \le 2T_1\), equality iff \(T_\varphi = \infty\).

2. Linearity and \(\Phi(X^\dagger) = \Phi(X)^\dagger\) (true for any Kraus map) give \(\Phi(\ket1\!\bra0) = \bar a\ket1\!\bra0\). Then \(J = \ket0\!\bra0\otimes\ket0\!\bra0 + \ket0\!\bra1\otimes a\ket0\!\bra1 + \ket1\!\bra0\otimes\bar a\ket1\!\bra0 + \ket1\!\bra1\otimes(\gamma\ket0\!\bra0 + (1 - \gamma)\ket1\!\bra1)\): entries \(1\) at \((00, 00)\), \(a\) at \((00, 11)\), \(\bar a\) at \((11, 00)\), \(\gamma\) at \((10, 10)\), \(1 - \gamma\) at \((11, 11)\). By Choi, \(J \ge 0\); restricting its quadratic form to \(\mathrm{span}\{\ket{00}, \ket{11}\}\) shows \(\begin{pmatrix}1 & a\\ \bar a & 1 - \gamma\end{pmatrix} \ge 0\), whose determinant gives \(1 - \gamma - |a|^2 \ge 0\). With \(|a| = e^{-t/T_2}\) and \(1 - \gamma = e^{-t/T_1}\) for all \(t\): \(e^{-2t/T_2} \le e^{-t/T_1}\), so \(2/T_2 \ge 1/T_1\): \(T_2 \le 2T_1\) again, now from complete positivity alone.

3. \(1/T_2 = 1/200 + 1/150 = 3/600 + 4/600 = 7/600\ \mu\text{s}^{-1}\), so

\[T_2 = \tfrac{600}7\ \mu\text{s} \approx 85.71\ \mu\text{s}.\]

At \(t = 50\ \mu\)s, \(x = e^{-50/85.71} = e^{-0.5833} = 0.5580\), and \(\Pr(+) = \tfrac12(1 + 0.5580) = 0.7790\). (The \(z\) coordinate, \(\gamma = 1 - e^{-0.5} = 0.3935\), does not enter an \(x\) measurement.)

4. \(2T_1 = 100\ \mu\text{s} \lt 120\ \mu\)s, which no channel of this shape allows (part 2). Either the two numbers were not measured under the same conditions (they drift over hours on real devices), or one of the fits is wrong, or the qubit is not behaving as a two-level system with decay of this shape at all. Quoting an echo \(T_2\) does not rescue the datasheet: a \(\pi\)-pulse at \(t/2\) only conjugates \(\rho_{01}\), and each half of the wait still multiplies \(|\rho_{01}|\) by at most \(e^{-t/4T_1}\), so the echoed coherence is at most \(e^{-t/2T_1}\) too. The bound is a sanity check on data.

Check. \(T_2 = 85.7 \lt 2T_1 = 200\) ✓ and \(T_2 \lt T_\varphi = 150\) ✓ (adding rates gives less than either). \(t = 0\): \(\Pr(+) = 1\) ✓; \(t \to \infty\): \(\tfrac12\) ✓.

P12 Contractivity hard

exercises: the Helstrom characterisation, the adjoint map, the partial trace as a channel, fidelity under channels via Uhlmann and Stinespring

  1. Prove \(D(\Phi(\rho), \Phi(\sigma)) \le D(\rho, \sigma)\) for every channel \(\Phi\), from the Helstrom theorem.
  2. Deduce \(D(\rho_A, \sigma_A) \le D(\rho_{AB}, \sigma_{AB})\), and show it can be strict in the extreme: give two orthogonal two-qubit states with equal reduced states.
  3. Show amplitude damping maps \(\ket0\), \(\ket1\) to states at distance \(1 - \gamma\).
  4. Using Uhlmann's theorem (cited) and a Stinespring dilation, prove \(F(\Phi(\rho), \Phi(\sigma)) \ge F(\rho, \sigma)\).
Hints · 3 rungs
Rung 1

Part 1: a test after the channel is a test before it. Part 2: the partial trace is a channel. Part 4: purifications of the inputs, pushed through a unitary, are purifications of the outputs.

Rung 2

Use \(\Phi^\dagger(E) = \sum_kA_k^\dagger EA_k\), with \(0 \le \Phi^\dagger(E) \le I\) when \(0 \le E \le I\). For part 2, the partial trace has Kraus operators \(I\otimes\bra j\). For part 4, if \(\ket\Psi\) on \(S\otimes R\) purifies \(\rho\), then \((U\otimes I_R)(\ket\Psi\otimes\ket0_E)\) is a vector on \(S\otimes E\otimes R\) whose reduced state on \(S\) is \(\Phi(\rho)\).

Rung 3

Part 1's first line: for the optimal \(E\) after the channel, \(\tfrac12(1 + D(\Phi\rho, \Phi\sigma)) = \tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(E\Phi(\rho)) + \tfrac12\operatorname{tr}((I - E)\Phi(\sigma)) = \tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\Phi^\dagger(E)\rho) + \tfrac12\operatorname{tr}((I - \Phi^\dagger(E))\sigma)\). Part 2's example: two Bell states.

Worked solution

Idea. Distinguishability is defined by what measurements can do, and every measurement on the output is a measurement on the input. Fidelity is defined by what overlaps purifications can have, and every purification of the input becomes one of the output.

1. Let \(\{E, I - E\}\) be optimal for \(\Phi(\rho)\) versus \(\Phi(\sigma)\). \(\Phi^\dagger(E) = \sum_kA_k^\dagger EA_k \ge 0\) (each term \(\braket{v|A_k^\dagger EA_k|v} = \braket{A_kv|E|A_kv} \ge 0\)) and \(I - \Phi^\dagger(E) = \Phi^\dagger(I - E) \ge 0\) since \(\Phi^\dagger(I) = \sum A_k^\dagger A_k = I\). So \(\{\Phi^\dagger(E), I - \Phi^\dagger(E)\}\) is a POVM on the input, and by cyclicity \(\operatorname{tr}(E\Phi(\tau)) = \operatorname{tr}(\Phi^\dagger(E)\tau)\) its success probability equals rung 3's left side. Helstrom bounds it by \(\tfrac12(1 + D(\rho, \sigma))\). So \(D(\Phi\rho, \Phi\sigma) \le D(\rho, \sigma)\). \(\blacksquare\)

2. \(\operatorname{tr}_B\) is linear, trace-preserving, and has Kraus operators \(A_j = I\otimes\bra j\) (unit 04's formula \(\operatorname{tr}_BX = \sum_j(I\otimes\bra j)X(I\otimes\ket j)\); \(\sum_jA_j^\dagger A_j = \sum_jI\otimes\ket j\!\bra j = I\)). So it is a channel, and part 1 applies. Example: \(\ket{\Phi^\pm} = (\ket{00} \pm \ket{11})/\sqrt2\) are orthogonal (\(\tfrac12 - \tfrac12 = 0\)), so \(D = 1\); both have \(\rho_A = I/2\) (unit 04), so \(D(\rho_A, \sigma_A) = 0\). All of the difference is in the correlations: a sign in the entanglement, invisible to either half.

3. Bloch vectors \((0, 0, \pm1)\) go to \((0, 0, \gamma + (1 - \gamma)(\pm1))\), i.e. \((0, 0, 1)\) and \((0, 0, 2\gamma - 1)\). Half the distance: \(\tfrac12(1 - (2\gamma - 1)) = 1 - \gamma\). For \(\gamma = 0.3\), \(0.7 \lt 1\) ✓ — strict contraction, and the \(T_1\) law: the distance between \(\ket0\) and \(\ket1\) decays as \(e^{-t/T_1}\), while that between \(\ket\pm\) decays as \(e^{-t/2T_1}\).

4. Uhlmann (cited): \(F(\rho, \sigma) = \max|\braket{\Psi|\Phi'}|\) over purifications on a common space, and the maximum is attained. Take optimal purifications \(\ket\Psi, \ket{\Phi'}\) on \(S\otimes R\), so \(F(\rho, \sigma) = |\braket{\Psi|\Phi'}|\). Let \(U\) be a Stinespring unitary on \(S\otimes E\) for \(\Phi\), and put \(\ket{\Psi'} = (U\otimes I_R)(\ket\Psi\otimes\ket0_E)\), likewise \(\ket{\Phi''}\). Tracing out \(E\otimes R\) from \(\ket{\Psi'}\): first \(R\) (it is untouched by \(U\), so this commutes with applying \(U\)) gives \(U(\rho\otimes\ket0\!\bra0)U^\dagger\), then \(E\) gives \(\Phi(\rho)\). So \(\ket{\Psi'}, \ket{\Phi''}\) purify \(\Phi(\rho), \Phi(\sigma)\), with purifying system \(E\otimes R\), and \(\braket{\Psi'|\Phi''} = \braket{\Psi|\Phi'}\braket{0|0}\). Uhlmann's maximum is at least this one value: \(F(\Phi(\rho), \Phi(\sigma)) \ge |\braket{\Psi|\Phi'}| = F(\rho, \sigma)\). \(\blacksquare\)

Check. Part 1 with \(\Phi\) unitary gives equality both ways (apply \(U^\dagger\)), as it must, since a unitary can be undone. Part 4 on part 3's example: \(F(\ket0, \ket1) = 0\), and after damping \(F = \sqrt{\braket{0|\Phi(\ket1\!\bra1)|0}} = \sqrt\gamma \ge 0\) ✓ (one output is the pure \(\ket0\)). Fuchs–van de Graaf: \(1 - \sqrt\gamma \le 1 - \gamma \le \sqrt{1 - \gamma}\), true for \(0 \le \gamma \le 1\) ✓.
