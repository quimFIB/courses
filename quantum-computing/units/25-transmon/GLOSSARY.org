The terms this unit introduces, in the order the deck meets them. The slides
stay the definition of record; each entry names the slide it comes from, so a
term can be looked up here and read properly there.

Running example: a 10 ns Gaussian \(\pi\)-pulse on a three-level transmon
with \(\omega/2\pi = 5\) GHz and \(\alpha/2\pi = -250\) MHz; envelope
\(\Omega_x(t) = A[e^{-(t-5)^2/2\sigma^2} - e^{-2}]\), \(\sigma = 2.5\) ns,
\(A = 0.679\) rad/ns, peak \(\Omega/2\pi = 93.4\) MHz. From \(\ket0\) it ends
with \(P_0 = 0.0253\), \(P_1 = 0.9744\), \(P_2 = 3.0\times10^{-4}\). DRAG at
\(\lambda = 1\) cuts the leakage to \(3.9\times10^{-6}\) but leaves
\(1 - P_1 = 2.8\%\), because the drive Stark-shifts the qubit by
\(\Omega_x^2/2|\alpha|\) (\(2\pi\times17.5\) MHz at the peak). A search over
the DRAG coefficient and a detuning finds \((\lambda, c) \approx (0.71,
-0.22)\), with leakage and miss both \(1.4\times10^{-5}\). Amplitude damping
costs \(t/3T_1\): \(3.3\times10^{-5}\) at 10 ns and \(6.7\times10^{-5}\) at
20 ns for \(T_1 = 100\ \mu\)s.

* A ladder, not a qubit

- transmon :: A superconducting qubit: two metal pads joined by a Josephson
  junction, behaving as a weakly nonlinear oscillator whose lowest two
  levels are the qubit. Koch et al. 2007. /Slide: An oscillator on a chip./
- LC oscillator :: A capacitor and a coil in a loop; charge sloshes between
  them at \(\omega = 1/\sqrt{LC}\), exactly as a mass on a spring moves.
- Josephson junction :: Two superconductors separated by an insulating layer
  a few atoms thick; it acts as a coil whose inductance depends on the
  current, which makes the oscillator nonlinear.
- anharmonicity :: \(\alpha\) in \(H_0 = \hbar\omega n + \tfrac{\hbar\alpha}{2}n(n
  - 1)\): how much the second gap falls short of the first. About
  \(-2\pi\times250\) MHz for a transmon, against \(\omega \approx
  2\pi\times5\) GHz. /Slide: The transmon's ladder./
- truncated lowering operator :: \(b\) on \(\mathrm{span}\{\ket0, \ket1,
  \ket2\}\) with \(b\ket1 = \ket0\), \(b\ket2 = \sqrt2\ket1\), \(b\ket0 = 0\);
  \(b^\dagger b = n = \mathrm{diag}(0, 1, 2)\). The \(\sqrt2\) makes the drive
  couple \(\ket1 \leftrightarrow \ket2\) more strongly than \(\ket0
  \leftrightarrow \ket1\). /Slide: The drive, and the √2./
- quadrature (in-phase and quadrature envelopes) :: The two slowly varying
  amplitudes \(\Omega_x\), \(\Omega_y\) of a drive
  \(\Omega_x\cos\omega t + \Omega_y\sin\omega t = \Omega\cos(\omega t -
  \varphi)\). On the qubit they become rotations about \(x\) and \(y\).

* The rotating frame

- time-dependent Hamiltonian :: A Hermitian \(H(t)\) that changes with time,
  as under a pulse. Evolution is defined by \(i\partial_tU(t, s) =
  H(t)U(t, s)\), \(U(s, s) = I\), with \(\hbar = 1\). /Slide: A Hamiltonian
  that changes in time./
- propagator :: \(U(t, s)\), the unitary taking the state at time \(s\) to
  the state at time \(t\). Unitary because \(\partial_t(U^\dagger U) = 0\); it
  composes, \(U(t, r)U(r, s) = U(t, s)\).
- piecewise-constant evolution :: Approximating \(U(T, 0)\) by
  \(\prod_{k=N}^{1}e^{-iH(t_k)\Delta t}\) with midpoints \(t_k\). The error is
  at most \(MT\Delta t/4\), \(M = \max\lVert H'\rVert\), proved from
  \(\lVert U - W\rVert \le \int\lVert H - K\rVert\) and a telescoping sum.
  /Slide: Short constant steps converge./
- time-ordered product :: The limit of the piecewise-constant products as
  \(\Delta t \to 0\), with later times on the left; it equals \(U(T, 0)\).
  When all \(H(t)\) commute it is \(e^{-i\int H}\).
- rotating frame :: The change of basis \(\ket{\tilde\psi} =
  e^{iGt}\ket\psi\), under which \(\tilde H = e^{iGt}He^{-iGt} - G\). With
  \(G = \omega n\) the drive is on resonance with \(\ket0 \leftrightarrow
  \ket1\), and populations are unchanged. /Slide: Turning with the drive./
- rotating-wave approximation :: Dropping the terms in \(e^{\pm2i\omega t}\)
  from the rotating-frame Hamiltonian, justified when \(|\Omega_x|,
  |\Omega_y|, |\alpha| \ll \omega\). It leaves \(\tilde H = \alpha\ket2\!\bra2 +
  \tfrac{\Omega_x}{2}(b + b^\dagger) + \tfrac{\Omega_y}{2}(ib^\dagger - ib)\).
  /Slide: The rotating-wave approximation, with three levels./
- Bloch–Siegert shift :: The first correction from the dropped terms, a
  shift of resonance by about \(\Omega^2/4\omega\) (unit 02, cited there):
  \(2\pi\times0.44\) MHz at the running pulse's peak.
- pulse area :: \(\theta = \int_0^T\Omega_x\,dt\). On two levels with
  \(\Omega_y = 0\) the pulse is exactly \(R_x(\theta)\) whatever its shape;
  a \(\pi\)-pulse has area \(\pi\). /Slide: On two levels: unit 02's drive./

* Leakage

- Gaussian pulse :: The envelope \(\Omega_x(t) = A[e^{-(t - T/2)^2/2\sigma^2}
  - e^{-2}]\) on \([0, T]\), \(\sigma = T/4\), offset to vanish at both ends
  and scaled to area \(\pi\). Its slope jumps at the ends: the kink that
  sets the leakage. /Slide: The Gaussian π-pulse./
- leakage :: Population outside the qubit subspace at the end of a gate,
  here \(P_2\). Worse than a qubit error of the same size, because codes
  measure Paulis on the qubit subspace and cannot see it. To leading order
  in \(1/\alpha\), \(P_2 \approx \Omega_x'(T)^2/2\alpha^4\).

* DRAG, and the Stark shift

- DRAG :: Derivative Removal by Adiabatic Gate: a quadrature \(\Omega_y =
  -\lambda\Omega_x'/\alpha\), with \(\lambda = 1\) cancelling the leading
  leakage amplitude. For \(\alpha \lt 0\), \(\Omega_y = +\Omega_x'/|\alpha|\).
  Motzoi, Gambetta, Rebentrost and Wilhelm 2009. /Slide: DRAG, derived./
- AC Stark shift :: The drive's coupling to \(\ket2\) pushes \(\ket1\) up by
  \(\Omega_x^2/2|\alpha|\) while \(\ket0\) stays, so the qubit's frequency
  rises during the pulse and a drive at \(\omega\) is left detuned. It is
  what makes a Gaussian \(\pi\)-pulse miss \(\ket1\) by \(2.6\%\). /Slide: The
  drive moves the qubit./
- effective Hamiltonian :: The qubit block, to order \(1/\alpha\), after a
  small change of basis that decouples \(\ket2\): \(\tfrac12\Omega_xX +
  \tfrac12\Omega_yY - \tfrac{\Omega_x^2}{2\alpha}\ket1\!\bra1\). With a
  detuning \(c\,\Omega_x^2n/\alpha\) added, the first-order miss is
  proportional to \((\lambda + c - \tfrac12)^2\).

* T₁ as a gate error

- average gate fidelity :: \(F_{\rm avg} = \int\braket{\psi|U^\dagger
  \Phi(\ket\psi\!\bra\psi)U|\psi}\,d\psi\) over the uniform measure on the
  Bloch sphere; \(1 - F_{\rm avg}\) is the average infidelity. The integrand
  is unit 21's \(F^2\), not \(F\). For amplitude damping over a short time
  \(t\), \(1 - F_{\rm avg} = t/3T_1\) to first order. /Slide: One number for a gate./
- six-state average :: The average over the six axis states \(\pm\hat x,
  \pm\hat y, \pm\hat z\). It equals the sphere average for any polynomial of
  degree at most 2 in the Bloch vector, so it computes \(F_{\rm avg}\)
  exactly.
